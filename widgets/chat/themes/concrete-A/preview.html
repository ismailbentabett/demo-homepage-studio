Eight lines on every ticket, dig to cure.

Each line is a step we sign off before the next one starts. You get the same list, filled in for your slab.

  1. 01Dig Topsoil and roots come out down to firm subgrade.
  2. 02Base 4 in. road base, packed with a plate compactor in lifts.
  3. 03Steel #4 rebar on an 18 in. grid, tied and set on chairs.
  4. 04Forms Straight forms staked and braced, fall checked before the truck.
  5. 05Pour 4,000 psi air-entrained mix, placed and worked into the corners.
  6. 06Screed Struck level, bull-floated, finished after the bleed water leaves.
  7. 07Joints Control joints cut every 2 to 3 times the slab depth, in feet.
  8. 08Cure Covered or sprayed for 7 days. Sand, not salt, the first winter.

The truck leaves on day 0. The slab keeps working for 28.

Day 28

  1. Day 1Walk on it. Soft shoes, no wheelbarrows.
  2. Day 7Park on it. Cars yes, delivery trucks no.
  3. Day 28Full strength. Seal it before the first freeze.

Plan · map crazing · 1 in. = 2 ft

Hairline or map cracking

Thin lines that don't move when you step on them. Usually the top dried too fast. Fix: route and seal, then resurface if the look bothers you.

Have us look at it

Plan · surface scaling · 1 in. = 2 ft

Flaking or scaling

The top layer peels off and the stones show. Often salt on young concrete or a weak surface. Fix: grind and resurface if the slab below is sound.

Have us look at it

Section · dropped panel · 1 in. = 1 ft

Sunken panel

One section has dropped below its neighbor, usually from a washed-out base. Fix: lift it level through small drilled holes, then patch them.
