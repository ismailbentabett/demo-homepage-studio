The profile under a Charlotte lawn

Four layers, four fixes. Surface work only reaches the first.

  1. 0–2 in · Turf & thatch

    Aerate and seed

    Core aeration pulls plugs through the thatch, so seed lands on soil and rain soaks in instead of running off.

  2. 2–6 in · Compacted topsoil

    Topdress with compost

    Compost goes into the open holes and over the top. It loosens the layer new roots need most and holds water through a dry July.

  3. 6–12 in · Piedmont red clay

    Test, then amend

    We pull a sample and read the pH before anything is planted. The clay gets loosened and fed, so roots push down instead of turning sideways.

  4. 12–18 in · Where water sits

    Move the water

    Regrading, swales and French drains carry standing water away from the house, so roots and crawlspaces stay dry.
