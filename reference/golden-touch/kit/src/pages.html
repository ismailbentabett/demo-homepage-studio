How a job goes, from the first visit to the hand-back.

Step 1 of 4

  1. Look at the place together

    We walk the house with you and talk through what's bothering you about it. Nothing gets priced from a phone call alone.

Two choices to make early

Neither has a right answer for every house. We'll talk through both at the visit.

Remodel

What changes
The layout or the use of the room: walls move, fixtures relocate.
Planning needed
More. Moving things affects what's behind the walls.

Renovation

What changes
The condition of what's there: worn surfaces, tired fixtures, old finishes.
Planning needed
Less, though surprises still turn up when things are opened.

How renovations run

All at once

Good for
Getting the disruption over with in one stretch.
Watch for
Living around more of the house being torn up at the same time.

In phases

Good for
Keeping most of the house usable and spreading the work out.
Watch for
Planning the order so later phases don't undo earlier ones.

Six signs the paint is giving up

[[i peel]]

Peeling

Paint lifting in flakes or sheets usually means moisture got behind it or the surface wasn't prepped.

[[i chalk]]

Chalking

Rub the wall and your hand comes away white. The binder in the paint is breaking down.

[[i sun]]

Sun-side fading

One side of the house looks a shade lighter than the rest. That's the side taking the most sun.

[[i caulk]]

Cracked caulk

Gaps around windows, doors and trim let water in behind the paint.

[[i mildew]]

Mildew

Dark patches on shady, damp walls. It has to be cleaned off before anything goes over it.

[[i wood]]

Bare wood

Exposed trim or siding soaks up water and starts to rot if it's left open.

Seeing two or more?

When a second opinion is worth having

The cheapest time to change a plan is before anyone has started. If you've been handed a scope you don't fully follow, or you're not sure the work is being done in a sensible order, an hour with a contractor can save a lot of rework.

We'll look at what you have, tell you what we'd question and explain why. If the answer is that the plan is fine, we'll tell you that too.

Consulting also suits people who aren't sure what they need yet. A house with several small problems is often one job in disguise, and knowing that changes how you'd approach it.

If you decide to go ahead with Golden Touch afterward, the work we did together carries straight into the written estimate.
