# Plumber — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
- **Who calls:** homeowners 30-80 in older Midwest and Rust Belt cities (Indianapolis, Pittsburgh, Buffalo): brick and frame houses from 1900-1960 with basements, galvanized or lead service lines, clay sewer laterals, and a water heater in the basement corner. Also landlords of doubles and duplexes, new owners who just got an inspection report full of plumbing notes, and older owners who want one plumber they trust for years.
- **What just happened:** water on the basement floor; no hot water before work; a toilet that backs up when the washer drains; a pipe froze on the coldest night; the water heater is 14 years old and rumbling; the city sent a letter about a lead service line; the home inspector wrote "galvanized supply, recommend evaluation"; water pressure dropped to a trickle upstairs.
- **Fears (their words):**
  1. "They'll charge me $400 just to show up."
  2. "They'll want to dig up my whole yard."
  3. "I don't know where my shut-off is and water is everywhere."
  4. "They'll upsell me a tankless when I just need the old one fixed."
  5. "It's going to happen again next week."
- **What makes them trust:** a price range before the visit, a camera video of the sewer line they can watch, a plumber who explains why the drain clogged, shoe covers in the house, the shut-off valve labeled before they leave, repair offered before replacement, a real local number and a text when the van is on the way.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:** cartoon plumber mascots and toilet jokes, coupon walls, "we're available 24/7" with no hours anywhere, a stock photo of a smiling man holding a wrench in a white studio, auto-play video with sound, no prices or ranges of any kind.

## 2. The job
- **Emergency vs planned:** about 50/50. Emergencies: burst or frozen pipes, sewer backups, no hot water, leaks through a ceiling, a sump pump that quit in a storm. Planned: water heater replacement, lead and galvanized line replacement, repiping, sewer lateral repair, softeners in hard-water cities, fixture swaps, pressure regulators, sump installs before spring.
- **Seasonality (Midwest / Great Lakes):**
  - Jan-Feb: frozen and burst pipes on subzero nights, water heaters working hardest and failing, sewer backups in thaws.
  - Mar-Apr: snowmelt and spring rain bring sump failures and basement water; roots start growing into clay laterals.
  - May-Jun: outdoor spigots, sewer camera work and lateral repairs, remodel plumbing.
  - Jul-Aug: storm backups during heavy rain; lead line replacement programs dig in summer.
  - Sep-Oct: winterizing spigots and irrigation, water heater replacements before winter.
  - Nov-Dec: first freezes, holiday drain clogs (grease, guests, disposals).
- **Average tickets (words):** small (drain clearing, toilet repair, faucet, spigot), medium (camera inspection, water heater repair, sump pump, pressure regulator, softener), large (water heater or tankless replacement, lead service line, partial repipe), very large (sewer lateral replacement, whole-house repipe).
- **Regional notes:** Indianapolis has hard water and many clay laterals; Pittsburgh has many lead service lines (PWSA has a replacement program), galvanized pipe and dye-test requirements on some sales; Buffalo has deep frost, old doubles with two meters, the December 2022 blizzard and Snowvember 2014. Mention these as context, never as scare copy.
- **Services that sell (from facts):** drain clearing, sewer camera inspection, water heaters (tank and tankless), lead and galvanized line replacement, repiping, sewer lateral repair, sump pumps and backup, frozen pipe thawing and repair, water softeners, pressure regulators, shut-off valve replacement and labeling, fixture repair.
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**
  | Homeowner says | Pro says |
  |---|---|
  | "the main line" | building sewer / sewer lateral |
  | "the pipe from the street" | water service line |
  | "the shut-off" | main shut-off valve / curb stop |
  | "the drain in the basement floor" | floor drain / trap |
  | "the gray pipes" | galvanized steel supply |
  | "the dull pipes that bend" | lead service line |
  | "it gurgles" | venting problem / partial sewer blockage |
  | "the snake" | drain cable / auger / hydro-jetting |
  | "the camera thing" | sewer inspection camera with locator |
  | "low pressure upstairs" | corroded galvanized / failing PRV |
  | "the pump in the pit" | sump pump with check valve |
  | "the white crust" | scale / hard water deposits |
  | "the hot water tank" | tank water heater / anode rod |
  | "the pipe burst" | freeze split / failed fitting |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Water heater life check** — inputs: tank age (or the serial-number year), type (gas/electric/tankless), signs (rumbling, rusty water, leak at the base, not enough hot water). Output: "maintain / plan / replace soon" with the reason in plain words, plus flush and anode rod notes. Leads to: replacement or repair booking.
2. **Drain trouble sorter** — inputs: which fixtures are slow or backing up (one sink vs several, basement floor drain, toilet when washer drains). Output: "one fixture clog" vs "main line likely" with what that means, and what not to pour in. Leads to: drain clear or camera inspection.
3. **Shut-off finder** — a guided picture walk: where main shut-offs usually sit in basements of that city's house types (front wall, by the meter, crawl space), how to turn a gate vs ball valve, what a curb stop is. Printable "tag your valve" card. Leads to: valve replacement and labeling.
4. **Pipe material identifier** — magnet and scratch test steps: copper (no magnet, penny color), galvanized (magnet sticks, gray), lead (no magnet, scratches shiny silver, soft), plastic. Output: what it means and next step. No claims about health. Leads to: line replacement estimate.
5. **Sewer lateral era check** — inputs: house build decade, big trees near the line, past backups. Output: likely pipe material (clay, cast iron, Orangeburg, PVC) and whether a camera look makes sense. Leads to: camera inspection.
6. **Pressure check** — how to read a $10 hose-bib gauge; output: below 40 psi / 40-80 psi normal / above 80 psi needs a regulator. Leads to: PRV or repipe booking.
7. **Freeze-risk check** — inputs: forecast low (typed by the visitor), pipes on outside walls, crawl space or unheated garage, house empty. Output: steps for tonight (drip faucets, open cabinet doors, heat on). Leads to: insulation or reroute booking.
8. **"Pipe froze?" guide** — what to do in the next 10 minutes: shut off, open faucet, gentle heat, never an open flame; when to call. Leads to: call/text.
9. **Sump readiness** — test steps (bucket of water, float, check valve, discharge line, backup). Leads to: sump service.
10. **ZIP checker** — confirms service area against `facts.biz.zips`.

All prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices`, always labelled "Sample pricing". No tool shows live wait times or live weather; any temperature is typed by the visitor.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "Every valve in your house, tagged and explained."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Wrench & Wren Plumbing · Indianapolis, IN · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Most homeowners can't find their own shut-off in a flood. Wrench & Wren ends every visit the same way: the work explained, the shut-offs found and hung with a numbered brass tag. The page shows the visit as a row of tags on a pipe rail, from the knock at the door to the last tag on the main.
- Tone lead: light-led (warm limestone white, a deep plumber's-navy text color, polished brass as the one accent)
- Type lead: slab (a sturdy slab serif with tag numerals; a plain humanist sans for body)
- Hero family: 4 stacked headline + cinematic strip (three stacked lines "Find it. / Fix it. / Tag it." above a wide, short strip photo of copper pipes and a brass valve)
- Signature seed: **Brass valve tags.** A copper pipe rail runs across the top of each section; every section hangs from a numbered brass tag (No. 01 Arrive · 02 Find the shut-off · 03 Show the problem · 04 Price it · 05 Fix it · 06 Tag it). Services are tags too, each stamped with its sample price range.
- Motion idea: Intro: the cinematic strip slides open like a valve handle turning, and the three headline lines stamp in one after another. Scroll story: each tag swings gently into place on the rail as its section arrives, and the pipe rail "fills" with color as you scroll down.
- Tools: Shut-off finder (with printable tag card), Water heater life check, Pressure check, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form (problem → photo upload optional → time → contact).
- Photo mood: warm basements with good light, copper and brass detail, hands closing a valve, a tag hanging on a pipe, clean water heaters, limestone porches; bright, honest, no faces.

### B — "Written down: every reading, every foot of pipe."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Ledgerline Plumbing · Pittsburgh, PA · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Pittsburgh houses hide old pipe: lead services, galvanized risers, clay laterals under steep yards. Ledgerline writes down everything it measures (pressure, pipe material, camera footage by the foot) and hands the homeowner the ledger. The page is that ledger, with the tools that let a homeowner take their own first readings.
- Tone lead: dark-led (iron black with steel-blue rules, an off-white ledger text and one copper-orange for "needs work")
- Type lead: mono-accent (monospaced numerals and entry labels; a clean sans for body text)
- Hero family: 2 50/50 split (left: the headline and a short ledger of three sample entries; right: a sewer camera still of a clay joint with a root, overlaid with the footage counter)
- Signature seed: **The ledger.** Every section is an entry row: entry no., date "sample", what was measured (PSI 38 / Lead, 3/4" / Camera 47 ft: root at joint), status (OK · WATCH · FIX), and the action. Services, reviews and prices all appear as ledger entries with a running total of feet of pipe "inspected" marked sample.
- Motion idea: Intro: the ledger lines type in one row at a time on the left while the camera still on the right slowly pushes forward, its foot counter ticking up. Scroll story: a vertical foot counter in the margin climbs as you scroll, stopping at named findings (joint, root, belly, cleanout).
- Tools: Pipe material identifier (magnet/scratch), Pressure check, Sewer lateral era check, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: dark basements lit by a work light, gray galvanized and dull lead pipe, sewer camera screens, gauges on hose bibs, steep Pittsburgh streets and brick row houses at dusk; moody, precise, no faces.

### C — "Buffalo winters, read like a magazine you keep by the furnace."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Thawhouse Plumbing · Buffalo, NY · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Every Buffalo family has a frozen-pipe story. Thawhouse publishes "The Thaw Issue", a local winter magazine about the city's houses, doubles and storms, with plumbing advice folded inside. The page is laid out as one issue: a cover, departments, letters and classifieds.
- Tone lead: color-led (a deep lake-blue cover field with snow white and one warm furnace red)
- Type lead: serif (a high-contrast editorial serif for the masthead and headlines; a calm sans for body)
- Hero family: 6 editorial magazine cover (masthead "THE THAW ISSUE", issue number, cover lines like "Why the Buffalo double has two shut-offs" over a snowy porch photo)
- Signature seed: **The Thaw Issue.** Departments replace sections: Field Notes (how a pipe freezes), Freeze Map (neighborhoods and house types), The Double (two-family homes, two meters), Letters (reviews, written as letters to the editor), Classifieds (services with sample prices as small ads). A page-number footer runs down the page.
- Motion idea: Intro: the cover slides up like a magazine pulled from a mailbox, snow drifting slowly across the cover photo, then the cover lines fade in one by one. Scroll story: each department opens with a page turn and the page number in the corner advances.
- Tools: Freeze-risk check (visitor types the forecast low), "Pipe froze?" guide, Sump readiness, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: snow on porches and doubles, frost on windows, a warm basement with a glowing water heater pilot, icicles on gutters, Lake Erie gray light, coffee on a cold morning; editorial, warm-inside/cold-outside, no faces.
