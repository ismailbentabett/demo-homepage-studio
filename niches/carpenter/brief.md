# Carpenter — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 35-70 in older Northeast housing stock: owners of 1850-1925 Victorians, Greek Revivals and brick
rowhouses (Portland's West End and Munjoy Hill, Albany's Center Square, Mansion District and Pine Hills), owner-occupants
and small landlords of Worcester triple-deckers, and younger buyers who just closed on an old house and got a home
inspection report full of "rot at sill / trim / porch posts". A second group: people who want something made, not
repaired (built-in bookcases, window seats, a closet that finally works, a new stair rail).

**What just happened.**
- The home inspection or the insurance inspection flagged rotten porch posts, a loose rail or soft stair treads.
- The spring thaw showed the damage: paint peeling off a window sill, a corner board that crumbles under a thumb, a
  porch floor that gives.
- A door that will not close since the winter, a stair that creaks and moves, a baluster that came out in a hand.
- They finished a renovation and the trim is missing, or the last contractor ran shoe molding over gaps.
- A tenant moves out in June and the third-floor porch must pass before the next lease (Worcester, Albany).

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
1. "He'll rip out all the old trim and put up flat pine from the big box store."
2. "I'll get a deposit taken and nobody shows up for six weeks."
3. "They'll paint over the rot and I'll be paying again in two years."
4. "The new piece won't match the old profile and everyone will see the patch."
5. "Is this porch even safe for my kids / my tenants right now?"

**What makes them trust.**
1. Photos of old-house work close up: a patched sill, a matched casing, a scarfed porch post, not a stock kitchen.
2. A plain explanation of repair vs replace, with the line drawn honestly ("this part can be saved, this cannot").
3. A written scope with measurements and a price range before the work starts.
4. Someone who names the house style and the neighborhood correctly.
5. A clear start window and a contact person who answers texts.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A stock photo of a smiling man with a level; a gallery of glossy new-build
kitchens in a city full of 120-year-old houses; "quality craftsmanship you can trust"; no phone number on the first
screen; a quote form that asks for a budget before anything else.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Almost all planned (~90%). The urgent 10%: a porch rail or stair that failed, a storm-torn
section of trim letting water in, a door that will not lock. No emergency line: `emergency: false`.

**Seasonality (Northeast: Portland ME, Albany NY, Worcester MA).**
| Month | What happens |
|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Interior: built-ins, trim, closets, stair rebuilds. Booking for spring exterior. Slowest exterior months. |
| Mar | Thaw shows rot. Calls jump. Estimates for porches and exterior trim. |
| Apr-May | Peak booking: porches, sills, corner boards, rails before summer; ice-dam rot under eaves found. |
| Jun-Aug | Peak exterior work; Worcester and Albany rental turnover (lease cycles, college move-ins late Aug). |
| Sep-Oct | Last exterior window before paint stops curing (~50°F); rush to close porches before snow. |
| Nov-Dec | Interior again: holiday built-ins, mantels, stair rails, doors that swell or shrink with heating season. |

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: a door adjusted, a stair tread replaced, a section of baseboard (a few hundred). Medium:
a run of matched casing in one room, sill and trim rot repair on a few windows, a closet system (low thousands).
Large: a full porch rebuild, a triple-decker porch stack, a library wall of built-ins, a new staircase rail and balusters
(several thousand to tens of thousands).

**The services that sell (from facts).** Custom built-ins and cabinets; crown, casing and baseboard (matched to old
profiles); rotten trim and sill repair; porch rebuilds and repairs; stairs, rails and balusters; interior and exterior
doors (hang, plane, rehang); window sash and jamb repair; shelving and closet systems; decks and back steps; framing
repairs (sistered joists, new headers); small custom furniture (benches, window seats).

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "the wood around the window is mushy" | rot at the sill and lower casing, needs a dutchman or a new sill |
| "the trim at the top of the wall" | crown molding / cornice |
| "the trim around the doors" | casing (with plinth blocks / rosettes in Victorians) |
| "the board along the floor" | baseboard, base cap, shoe molding |
| "the porch posts look rotten at the bottom" | post base rot, needs a new post or a spliced (scarfed) base |
| "the railing wobbles" | loose newel post, failed guard attachment |
| "the little posts in the railing" | balusters / spindles |
| "the stairs squeak" | loose treads, failed glue blocks, need screws from below |
| "the door rubs at the top" | door out of plumb, hinge side sagging, needs shims or planing |
| "the window won't stay up" | broken sash cord / missing weight, sash rehab |
| "they matched the old trim" | custom-ground knife, profile matched on the molder |
| "the floor dips by the wall" | sagging joist, needs sistering |
| "the gap between trim and wall" | needs scribing, not caulk |
| "the triple-decker back porches" | stacked rear porches with egress stairs |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A stock photo of a man in a tool belt giving a thumbs up, or a handshake in front of a truck.
2. "Quality craftsmanship you can trust" / "no job too small" / "attention to detail" as the headline.
3. A gallery of new-build kitchens and decks that have nothing to do with the house the visitor lives in.
4. A wall of 12 identical service icons (hammer, saw, ruler) with one-line descriptions.
5. A logo carousel of tool brands and associations.
6. "Free estimates!" in a red banner, a budget field as the first question.
7. Before/after sliders made of two unrelated photos.
8. No mention of the housing stock, the season or what a repair costs.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Repair or replace check (rot).** Learns: whether the trim, sill, post or tread can be saved. Inputs: part (sill,
   casing, corner board, porch post, stair tread), screwdriver test (goes in under 1/4 in / 1/4-1 in / through),
   how much of the length is soft, is paint holding elsewhere. Output: "Likely repairable with a patch (dutchman)" /
   "Likely a new piece, matched" / "Have us look, it may reach the framing", plus the sample price range from
   facts.proof.prices. Leads to: "Send a photo, we'll confirm" + quote form pre-filled.
2. **Molding profile picker.** Learns: what their trim is called and what matching costs. Inputs: pick a silhouette
   (Victorian casing with plinth/rosette, Greek Revival flat casing with backband, colonial crown, ogee base, etc.),
   room perimeter in feet. Output: the profile's name, whether it is stock or needs a custom knife, a sample range per
   linear foot. Leads to: "Bring a 6-inch offcut or a photo with a ruler" + call.
3. **Built-in planner.** Learns: how many shelf bays fit a wall. Inputs: wall width, ceiling height, window/radiator
   in the way. Output: a drawn elevation with bay count (bays 24-36 in wide), shelf count, typical depth (10-12 in for
   books), and the sample range. Leads to: "Get this drawn properly".
4. **Porch safety checklist.** Learns: what to look at today. Inputs: 6 yes/no checks (rail moves when pushed, post
   bases soft, balusters missing or gaps wide, treads soft or split, ledger stained, stairs pull away). Output: "Fine
   for now / Plan a repair this season / Keep people off until it is looked at". Typical guard figures shown as
   "typical", with "your town's code decides". Leads to: priority booking form.
5. **Stair fix finder.** Learns: why a stair creaks or a rail wobbles. Inputs: symptom tap-list. Output: likely cause
   in plain words, repair type, sample range.
6. **Season planner.** Learns: when to book. Input: job type. Output: best months for it in the city (exterior paint
   and glue cure windows), and the current booking lead time stated as sample.
7. **Cut list builder (A-specific).** Learns: what goes into their job. Inputs: job type. Output: a cut list of parts
   with dimensions, species and grain direction ("sill, 2 1/2 x 7 x 38, white oak, grain lengthwise").

Prices are ranges from facts.proof.prices only, always labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — Every repair is a joint you can watch being made   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Scarf & Sash Carpentry · Portland, ME · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Old-house carpentry is invisible when it is done right, so the page shows the part nobody sees: the
  scarf joint that splices new white oak into a rotten porch post, the sill dutchman, the scribed casing. The whole site
  is one repair told as a sequence of cuts, from the first measurement to the last coat of paint in a Munjoy Hill
  clapboard color.
- Tone lead: color-led (painted-clapboard palette: a deep harbor teal base, oxide red, cream trim, a brass accent;
  colors come from Portland house paint, not from "luxury").
- Type lead: serif (a sturdy text serif with old-style figures for dimensions, small caps for cut numbers).
- Hero family: 9 — horizontal filmstrip (a strip of 7 process frames: measure, scribe, cut, dry-fit, fasten, sand,
  finish; the strip drags/scrolls sideways and the headline sits above it).
- Signature seed: the page is a cut list. Every section is a numbered cut ("Cut 03 · sill · 2½ × 7 × 38 in · white oak ·
  grain ↔"), headed by a dimension line with ticks; section dividers are kerf lines; the footer is the finished joint
  where all cuts meet. The filmstrip frame numbers and the cut numbers are the same numbering system.
- Motion idea: intro — a pencil line draws the dimension line across the hero and the filmstrip slides in frame by frame
  with the cut number counting up. Scroll story — a pinned scarf-joint drawing (SVG) separates the old post and the new
  piece, slides them together, and "glues" as the visitor scrolls, ending on the painted post.
- Tools: Repair or replace check (rot) + Cut list builder + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form (problem → part →
  photo note → contact).
- Photo mood: close, hand-height, raking morning light on wood grain and chisel edges; sawdust in the air; painted
  clapboards and trim in muted coastal colors; overcast Maine light. No wide shots of crews, no new-build interiors.

### B — Measured before it is priced   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Story Pole Carpentry · Albany, NY · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: A story pole is the stick a carpenter marks every height on before cutting anything, so nothing is
  guessed. The site is the story pole for an Albany rowhouse: 11-foot ceilings, 9-inch baseboards, deep crown; every
  claim on the page is a measurement, a tolerance or a sample price, and the tools let the homeowner measure first.
- Tone lead: light-led (drafting vellum white, graphite ink, one signal color: a safety-orange pencil mark).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a clean grotesk for text with a monospaced face for every measurement, tick label and price).
- Hero family: 5 — offset framed photo with overlapping type (a tall photo of an Albany parlor doorway with deep casing,
  offset right in a thin frame; the headline overlaps its left edge; dimension callouts sit on the frame like a
  measured drawing).
- Signature seed: the story pole. A vertical ruler runs the full height of the page in the left margin; each section
  is "marked" on it at a real height of a rowhouse (0 in floor, 9 in baseboard, 36 in rail, 84 in door head, 132 in
  ceiling crown), and the section's content is about the thing at that height. Measurements in mono everywhere;
  tolerances ("gaps under 1/32 in at miters") are the proof language.
- Motion idea: intro — the ruler ticks draw down the margin and the hero frame's dimension callouts measure themselves
  (numbers count to their value). Scroll story — a scroll-linked marker slides down the story pole and each section
  "snaps" to its mark as it arrives, with the height label updating.
- Tools: Molding profile picker + Built-in planner + Repair or replace check + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright, even daylight from tall windows; architectural, straight-on, level horizons; plaster walls,
  painted trim, brick rowhouse fronts; precise and calm, a little cool. No moody shadows.

### C — Three floors, three porches, one Worcester   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Three Porches Carpentry · Worcester, MA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Worcester has thousands of triple-deckers, and each one stacks three porches facing the street and three
  more out back on the hills. The site is a triple-decker read from the sidewalk: first floor, second floor, third floor,
  each band of the page one floor of the house and one kind of work, told in the voice of the neighborhoods (Grafton
  Hill, Vernon Hill, Main South, Tatnuck) and of the owners who live on one floor and rent the other two.
- Tone lead: dark-led (night-blue/near-black base, warm porch-light amber accent, clapboard off-white text).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall, heavy condensed sans, set huge; triple-deckers are tall and narrow).
- Hero family: 3 — giant type mask over photo (the word "PORCHES" or "THREE" set full-width, a photo of stacked
  porches and clapboards shows through the letters; the rest of the hero is dark).
- Signature seed: the house is the page. Three stacked bands, labelled 1st floor / 2nd floor / 3rd floor, each with its
  own porch rail drawn as the band's top border (balusters as a repeating pattern that becomes the section rule).
  Inside each band the giant condensed type masks a photo. A small elevation drawing of a triple-decker in the corner
  lights the floor you are on.
- Motion idea: intro — the hero letters fill from the bottom up like porch lights switching on floor by floor. Scroll
  story — a pinned triple-decker elevation builds its porches one floor at a time (posts, then rail, then balusters
  drawn in), each floor bringing in its section of services.
- Tools: Porch safety checklist + Season planner (when to book a porch in Worcester) + ZIP checker + multi-step quote
  form (with an "I'm the owner / I'm a landlord" first step).
- Photo mood: dusk and early evening, porch lights on, stacked porches from the sidewalk looking up, wet clapboard after
  rain, snow on rails; strong verticals; warm light against cool blue. No faces, no house numbers.

## 7. Shot list (for the media curator)

**Hero candidates.**
- A1: a horizontal sequence-friendly close-up of hands marking wood with a pencil and square (must: landscape, hands
  only, wood grain visible; avoid: faces, power-tool logos).
- A2: chisel paring a joint in a wood beam, raking light (must: sharp edge, shavings; avoid: carving art, studio black
  backdrop).
- A3: painted clapboard house wall with white window trim (must: New England style wood siding; avoid: vinyl siding,
  non-US architecture).
- B1: tall interior doorway with deep painted casing in an old house (must: straight-on, daylight; avoid: modern flat
  trim, staged furniture).
- B2: tape measure against wood trim/molding, close (must: readable inch marks are fine, no brand on tape; avoid: metric
  only tapes).
- B3: built-in bookshelves in a bright room (must: painted wood, books; avoid: commercial library, IKEA-style units).
- C1: stacked wooden porches on a tall old house, from the street (must: US multi-family, looking up; avoid: house
  numbers, apartments of brick towers).
- C2: porch railing and balusters at dusk with warm light (must: wood rail; avoid: metal railings, faces).
- C3: painted wooden porch with columns in snow (must: residential; avoid: southern plantation look).

**Shared pool (by slot).** process steps (measure, mark with square, saw cut, chisel, dry fit, clamp/glue, sand, prime,
paint); material close-ups (oak grain, pine end grain, molding profiles, rotten wood, sawdust); tools (hand plane,
chisels, miter saw, coping saw, carpenter square, clamps, pencil); finished details (crown corner, stair balusters,
newel post, panelled door, window sill, built-in cabinet, closet shelving, wood stairs); regional homes (Victorian house,
New England clapboard, brick rowhouses, porch columns); weather/season (snow on porch, rain on wood, autumn leaves on
