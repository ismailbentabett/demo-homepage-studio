# Insulation — niche brief

Three cold-climate cities, three ways a homeowner decides who climbs into their attic:
Duluth, MN (A, process), Fargo, ND (B, proof and tools), Syracuse, NY (C, local story).

Climate zone facts used by the tools (public, label them "typical"):
- Duluth (St. Louis County, MN): IECC / DOE climate zone 7.
- Fargo (Cass County, ND): climate zone 6A on the 2021 IECC map (it was zone 7 on older maps; say "zone 6" and never argue the border).
- Syracuse (Onondaga County, NY): climate zone 5A.
- ENERGY STAR / DOE recommended attic levels are the same for zones 5, 6, 7 and 8: about **R-60 for an attic with little or
  nothing in it**, about **R-49 when there are already 3-4 inches**. Common loose-fill and batts run roughly R-2.5 to R-3.7 per
  inch (ENERGY STAR's rule of thumb: depth in inches x 3 is a fair estimate). These are the only R-value numbers a page may show,
  always labelled "typical, ENERGY STAR / DOE".
- Never on any page: energy-savings percentages, "lower your bills by", rebate or tax-credit amounts, utility program names,
  "green", "eco-friendly". Comfort, ice, frost, drafts and measured depth are the honest outcomes.

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Owners aged 35-70. Three typical households:
- Couples in their 30s-40s who bought an older house (Duluth's 1900s-1920s hillside foursquares and story-and-a-half houses,
  Syracuse's 1910s-1930s two-and-a-half-story frame houses in Eastwood, Strathmore, Westcott, Tipp Hill) and just lived through
  their first real winter in it.
- Retired owners of 1950s-1970s ranches and split-levels (south Fargo, Moorhead, North Syracuse, Hermantown) whose upstairs
  or back bedrooms never warm up and who are tired of icicles.
- Owners of 2000s-2020s builds (West Fargo, Horace, Liverpool, Clay) with one problem room: the bonus room over the garage.

**What just happened.** A row of icicles down to the ground and a brown stain on the bedroom ceiling near the outside wall.
A frost line on the basement wall where the floor joists sit. A home inspector's note: "attic insulation appears thin" or
"possible vermiculite". The kids' room is 60 °F while the thermostat says 69. The roofer said "your problem is in the attic,
not the roof". Snow melts off their roof in stripes while the neighbours' roofs stay white.

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
1. "They'll just blow more fluff on top and leave, and the ice dams will still be there."
2. "Is that gravel-looking stuff in my attic asbestos? Will they stir it up and leave it in my house?"
3. "They'll sell me spray foam for the whole house when I only need the attic done."
4. "They'll cover the vents and I'll end up with mold or a wet roof deck."
5. "Someone's foot is going to come through my ceiling and there'll be insulation all over the carpet."

**What makes them trust.**
1. Measurements and photos from *their* attic before a price (depth in inches, where the leaks are).
2. "We seal the air leaks first, then insulate" said plainly, with the reason.
3. Talking about ventilation: baffles at the eaves, bath fans vented outside, nothing blocking the soffits.
4. Honesty about vermiculite: stop, test, explain the options, do not disturb it.
5. Depth rulers left stapled in the attic so the homeowner (or the next buyer's inspector) can check the depth.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A red-and-blue thermal house photo with "SAVE UP TO 40%!". A rebate badge wall.
A smiling family on a couch holding a thermostat. No price clue at all. A popup before they have read a word. Anything that
sounds like a sales visit rather than an inspection.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** About 10-15% feels urgent (an ice dam actively leaking in January, frost dripping off attic nails
during a thaw, a new-home-purchase deadline). The rest is planned: the person who had ice dams last winter and calls in
September. Insulation is not a true emergency trade; the urgent caller needs a fast attic look and an honest "here is what to
do this week, here is what to schedule".

**Seasonality (Duluth / Fargo / Syracuse).**
- Jan-Feb: ice dams, frost in attics, cold rooms. Calls spike after every deep-cold week and every thaw. Blown-in attic work
  goes on all winter; spray foam needs warm-enough surfaces (crews heat the space).
- Mar-Apr: the thaw shows the damage: ceiling stains, wet insulation, mould on roof sheathing. Inspection-heavy months.
- May-Jun: home sales season: inspection reports name "thin attic insulation" and "vermiculite". Comfortable attics, good
  for big jobs (walls, knee walls).
- Jul-Aug: attics hit 120-140 °F; crews start at dawn. Slower, good for basements and rim joists. Hot upstairs bedrooms bring
  a second kind of call.
- Sep-Nov: the rush. Everyone remembers last winter. Duluth's first snow often arrives in October, Fargo's cold in November,
  Syracuse's lake-effect season builds from mid-November. Booking lead times stretch to weeks.
- Dec: ice-dam calls start again after the first big snow.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: an attic hatch, one bath fan vented outside, a rim joist section, an attic check with a
report. Middle: attic air sealing plus a blown-in top-up (the most common job). Large: dense-pack walls of a whole older house,
knee-wall attics of a two-and-a-half-story house, a full basement or crawl space.

**Services that sell (see facts):** attic top-up (blown-in), attic air sealing, ice dam prevention, knee walls / half-story
attics, rim joist spray foam, dense-pack walls in older houses, crawl space and porch floors, bonus room over the garage,
bath fan venting, attic hatch, a written attic check, quieter rooms (mineral wool).

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| icicles / water coming through the ceiling in winter | ice dam from heat loss into the roof deck |
| the attic fluff | loose-fill fiberglass or cellulose |
| the gravel / kitty-litter stuff in the attic | vermiculite (possible asbestos, test first) |
| drafty room | air leakage, infiltration |
| upstairs boiling, downstairs freezing | stack effect |
| frost on the nails in the attic | condensation from air leaks and humid air |
| the basement wall where the floor sits | rim joist / band joist, sill plate |
| slanted ceilings upstairs | knee walls, sloped ceiling, half-story |
| the little door in the closet ceiling | attic access, scuttle hatch |
| the room over the garage is freezing | floor over unconditioned space |
| how thick should it be | target R-value |
| my bathroom fan | exhaust duct terminating in the attic |
| the vents under the roof edge | soffit vents, need baffles / rafter vents |
| it's hollow when I knock on the wall | uninsulated wall cavity, candidate for dense-pack |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Attic ruler (R-value estimator).** Learns: roughly what R-value their attic has and how far from the typical target.
   Inputs: depth in inches (slider 0-20), material (loose grey/white fluff, loose yellow/pink fluff, batts, grey-brown pebbles,
   not sure). Output: estimated R range (depth x 2.5-3.7), the typical target for the city's zone (R-49 / R-60, ENERGY STAR,
   labelled typical), inches to add as a range. If "grey-brown pebbles": stops and shows the vermiculite note (do not disturb,
   get it tested, call us). Leads to: "We measure in six spots and send photos" + quote form pre-filled with the depth.
2. **Ice dam risk quiz.** Learns: whether their icicles come from attic heat. Inputs: 5 yes/no (icicles every winter? bare
   stripes on the roof while neighbours' are white? ceiling stains near outside walls upstairs? slanted ceilings / knee walls?
   recessed lights or a bath fan on the top floor?). Output: low / medium / high, the likely heat paths, what we check first.
   Leads to: "Book an attic look before the next snow".
3. **Heat path picker (house section).** Learns: where warm air leaves a house. Inputs: tap attic / top floor ceiling / walls /
   rim joist / crawl space or porch floor / garage ceiling. Output: what usually leaks there, how it is fixed, sample range from
   facts.proof.prices if that item is priced. Leads to: the quote form with that area checked.
4. **House type guide.** Learns: the weak spots of their kind of house. Inputs: pick a house (1900s-1930s two-and-a-half story /
   story-and-a-half with knee walls / 1950s-70s ranch / split-level / 2000s two-story with bonus room). Output: the 3 usual
   problem spots and the usual order of work. Leads to: "Is this your house? Send us two photos".
5. **Price range builder.** Learns: a ballpark before calling. Inputs: tick the items (only items in facts.proof.prices).
   Output: summed low-high range, labelled "Sample pricing, your attic decides the real number". Leads to: "Get your real number".
6. **Vermiculite check.** Learns: what to do if they see it. Inputs: 3 questions (pebble-like? grey-brown or gold, shiny flecks?
   house built before 1990?). Output: "Possibly vermiculite: do not touch, keep kids out, get a lab test; we will not blow over it
   until you know". Leads to: call. (Accurate EPA guidance: vermiculite may contain asbestos; leave it undisturbed and use a
   trained professional.)
7. **Season planner.** Learns: when to book. Inputs: month picker. Output: honest lead-time and what can be done that month
   (blown-in works in winter, spray foam needs warm surfaces, attics are brutal in July so crews start at dawn). Leads to: booking.
8. **Cold room log.** Learns: how to describe the problem. Inputs: tap rooms that are cold/hot and when. Output: a short note
   written in pro words ("north bedroom over garage, cold from Dec") that drops into the quote form.

## 6. The three directions

### A — Insulation done in the order heat escapes: top of the house down   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Stack Effect Insulation Co. · Duluth, MN · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Warm air rises and leaves through the top of a house; cold air is pulled in at the bottom (the stack effect,
  which is brutal in a three-storey hillside house above Lake Superior). The page is read like a cut through one Duluth house,
  roof peak to basement floor, and at each level it shows the six steps the crew follows there: look, measure, seal the air
  leaks first, insulate, keep the vents clear, check again. The homeowner understands why sealing comes before "more fluff".
- Tone lead: dark-led (deep night-blue, snow white, one warm amber for the light leaking out of the house)
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed sans for the level labels and headline, a plain humanist sans for text)
- Hero family: 1 — full-bleed video with left scrim (snow falling past a steep roof and lit windows at blue hour; the scrim on
  the left carries the headline and the phone)
- Signature seed: **The house section.** A vertical margin line runs the whole page as the heat path: it starts at the roof
  peak in the hero, drops through every section, and each section is a level of the same house (Roof deck, Attic, Top-floor
  ceiling, Knee walls, Walls, Rim joist, Crawl space) with a level marker ("Level 2 · Attic · steps 1-6") and a temperature tag
  showing outside and inside at that level. Steps, services, reviews and the form all hang on that line.
- Motion idea: Intro: the video fades up behind the scrim, the condensed headline rises line by line, and the margin line draws
  down from the roof peak to the first section. Scroll story: a pinned house section where amber arrows (warm air) rise and slip
  out at the attic, then each scroll step seals one leak and the arrow stops there and goes still and blue, until the last step
  shows the roof covered in even snow.
- Tools: Heat path picker (the house section is the tool), Ice dam risk quiz, Season planner + ZIP checker + multi-step quote
  form (area of the house → what you see → ZIP and timing → contact).
- Photo mood: blue hour and night, snow, warm window light, close attic work lit by a headlamp, long shadows; never sunny.

  measurement of their own attic. Inchwise sells the measurement: every visit ends with a written report (depth in inches at six
  spots, estimated R-value, leak points, photos), and the price comes from the report. The site *is* a sample report, so the
  homeowner sees the product before calling.

- Type lead: slab (a sturdy slab serif for headings and readings, a clean sans for body, tabular figures everywhere)
- Hero family: 14 — document object as hero (an attic inspection report on a clipboard: address line "Fargo, ND", findings
  F-01 to F-06 with readings, a small attic photo plate, a "SAMPLE" stamp, the phone in the report header)
- Signature seed: **The inspection report.** Every section of the page is a numbered finding (F-01 Depth, F-02 Air leaks,
  F-03 Rim joist, F-04 Ventilation, F-05 Price, F-06 Sign-off) with the same parts: reading, what it means, what we would do,
  sample range. Services are line items, reviews are "homeowner notes" in the margin, the footer is the signature block.
- Motion idea: Intro: the report header prints in, then the six readings type themselves into their boxes one after another and
  a "SAMPLE" stamp presses on (scale 1.04 to 1, no bounce). Scroll story: a vertical attic ruler pinned at the side fills from
  3.5 in to 17 in as the page scrolls, with tick marks for estimated R and the typical R-49 / R-60 lines (ENERGY STAR, zone 6),
  and each finding lights the ruler mark it refers to.
- Tools: Attic ruler (R-value estimator), Price range builder, Vermiculite check + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form (what

### C — Built for the snow belt, street by street   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
