# Pool Service — niche brief

Three pools, three waters: Tampa (screened lanais, humid heat, afternoon storms that dump rain and debris into the water every
day June to September), Orlando (big subdivisions, salt pools, families and short-term rentals that need the water right for
check-in), Scottsdale (hard water, scale lines on tile, monsoon dust storms, evaporation measured by the day, pebble finishes).
The page must look like it knows what the water in *this* pool is doing this week.

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Pool owners 35-70, single-family homes, many with a screened enclosure (Florida) or a desert backyard pool
(Arizona). Common situations:
- Just bought a house with a pool and has no idea how to run it.
- The water turned cloudy or got algae after a storm or a week away, and a party is on Saturday.
- The pump is loud, hums and will not start, or the filter pressure keeps climbing.
- The weekly guy stopped showing up or never answers texts.
- A short-term rental owner needs a reliable weekly service and a photo after each visit.
- The plaster is rough, stained or the tile has a white crust (Arizona).

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
1. "I'm paying every month and I have no idea if they actually came."
2. "They'll tell me I need a new pump when it's a $40 part."
3. "The water will turn cloudy right before the kids' party."
4. "Chemicals will eat my plaster or my kid's eyes will burn."
5. "We're losing water and nobody can tell me if it's a leak or the sun."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A visit report after every stop: time, readings, what was added, a photo of the water.
2. Ideal ranges written in plain words, and the reading next to them.
3. Repair before replace: a diagnosis with the part and the price, then the choice.
4. One technician on one route, same day of the week.
5. Local truths: storm debris and screen cages (Florida), scale and evaporation (Arizona).

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A stock photo of a resort infinity pool; "crystal clear water guaranteed"; no monthly
price; a site that sells pool construction when they want weekly cleaning; a contact form with no way to text.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** ~90% recurring weekly service and planned repairs. Cloudy water before an event and a dead pump in
August feel urgent, but they are handled as next-visit or this-week calls. `emergency: false` on all three sites.

**Seasonality.**
| Month | Tampa | Orlando | Scottsdale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Cooler water, heater and heat pump calls | Heat pump calls, rental season | Resurfacing season starts: drain and refill weather |
| Mar-Apr | Pollen and oak leaves, filter cleans | Spring break rentals, pollen | Peak resurfacing, tile cleaning before summer |
| May | Swim season ramps, pump repairs | Swim season, new owners | Heat arrives, evaporation climbs |
| Jun-Sep | Daily storms: rain, debris, algae; hurricane prep | Storms, algae, rental turnovers | Monsoon dust and haboobs, pumps run long, chlorine burns off fast |
| Oct-Nov | Hurricane cleanup, fall leaves | Steady weekly, heater checks | Good weather, renovation bookings |
| Dec | Slower, heater calls | Holiday rentals | Cool, fewer service add-ons |

**Ticket bands (words).** Weekly service is a monthly plan in the low hundreds. A cloudy or algae cleanup is mid hundreds. A
pump, heater or salt cell is low thousands. Leak detection is mid hundreds. Resurfacing is the big ticket: high thousands to
mid tens of thousands.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Weekly cleaning, chemical balance, cloudy/algae cleanup, pump and filter repair, heater and
heat pump service, salt systems, leak detection, tile cleaning (calcium), acid wash and resurfacing, opening for new owners.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "The water is cloudy" | Low free chlorine, poor filtration or high combined chlorine |
| "Slimy walls" / "it's turning" | Algae bloom |
| "The pool smells like chlorine" | Chloramines (combined chlorine), it actually needs more chlorine |
| "The little basket" | Skimmer basket / pump strainer basket |
| "The motor thing" | Pump and motor |
| "The tank" | Filter (sand, cartridge or DE) |
| "Pressure is high" | Dirty filter, backwash or clean the cartridges |
| "White crust on the tile" | Calcium scale (calcium carbonate) |
| "The rough bottom" | Etched or failing plaster, needs resurfacing |
| "Salt thing" | Salt chlorine generator / salt cell |
| "Stabilizer" | Cyanuric acid (CYA) |
| "Water level keeps dropping" | Evaporation vs leak, bucket test |
| "Drain the pool" | Drain and refill (dilution) |
| "The cage" | Screen enclosure / lanai |
| "Heater" | Gas heater or heat pump |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Cloudy water troubleshooter.** Inputs: what you see (cloudy, algae on walls, black spots, smell), recent rain, last
   service, filter pressure. Output: the likely cause in plain words and what we would do first. Leads to a cleanup booking.
2. **Plan picker.** Inputs: pool size, screen enclosure yes/no, trees, salt or chlorine, rental. Output: suggested plan with
   a sample monthly range from `proof.prices`.
3. **Test strip reader.** Inputs: the reader taps the colors or types the numbers. Output: each reading against the ideal band
   (pH 7.2-7.8, free chlorine 1-3 ppm, alkalinity 80-120 ppm, calcium 200-400 ppm, stabilizer 30-50 ppm or 60-80 for salt pools,
   salt 2,700-3,400 ppm) with "in range / low / high" and a note that we balance on site.
4. **Gallons calculator.** Length x width x average depth x 7.5. Output: gallons, used for chemical dosing and drain-and-refill
   talk. Honest rounding.
5. **Pump repair or replace.** Inputs: age, symptoms (hum, scream, leak, trips breaker), single or variable speed. Output:
   "likely a part", "worth a diagnosis" or "time to replace", with sample ranges.
6. **Bucket test leak log.** Steps for the bucket test over 24-48 hours; inputs the two drops; output "looks like evaporation"
   or "worth a leak test". Hedged: about 1/4 to 1/2 in. a day is common in desert summer.
7. **Calcium scale check.** Photo-free checklist: white crust at the waterline, rough to the touch, flakes; output a cleaning
   method and sample range.
8. **Resurfacing month planner.** Pick a month; output what the weather does to a drain and refill that month (cool months
   preferred), and when to book.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "Every week, written down."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Tileline Pool Care · Tampa, FL · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: The waterline tile is where a pool tells the truth, and the visit log is where a pool company does. The page
  walks through one weekly visit in order: skim, brush, empty baskets, check pressure, test, balance, photo, report. Tampa's
  afternoon storms are the villain of the story: every visit starts with what last night's rain did.
- Tone lead: **dark-led** (deep night-water navy, a bright aqua accent, warm white type, a tile-band strip of cobalt).
- Type lead: **grotesk** (a clean, slightly rounded grotesk for display and body, tabular figures for readings and times).
- Hero family: **1 — full-bleed video + scrim.** Slow underwater-to-surface clip of a screened pool, a dark scrim at the
  bottom-left with the headline, the monthly sample range and a "see a sample visit report" link.
- Signature seed: **The visit log.** A thin waterline runs down the left margin of the page like the tile line seen from the
  side; every section is a log entry stamped with a time (8:02 skim, 8:09 brush, 8:15 test...). A visit card in the corner
  fills in as the visitor scrolls: each section adds its line, so the page ends with a finished sample visit report.
- Motion idea: Intro: the waterline draws down the margin with a gentle ripple and the first log stamp ticks in. Scroll story:
  log lines type into the visit card one by one with their check mark; reduced motion shows the finished card.
- Tools: Cloudy water troubleshooter, Plan picker + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: dark, moody water, screen enclosure shadows, night pool light, close detail of brushes and skimmers; distance close
  to mid; storm clouds over the lanai.

### B — "Read the water, then touch it."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Clear Reading Pool Co. · Orlando, FL · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: A pool is chemistry with a swimsuit. The page opens with a test strip reader as the hero: tap your colors,
  see where each reading sits. Every claim on the page is a reading against a range. Salt pools and short-term rentals in
  Orlando suburbs are the setting.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (paper white, pale aqua, test-pad colors used only as data: yellow, coral, violet, aqua).
- Type lead: **mono-accent** (a monospace for readings, labels and units; a neutral sans for body and headlines).
- Hero family: **7 — tool-first.** The hero is a working test strip reader: six pads, each with a scale under it; the
  headline sits above and a small "readings in range: 4 of 6" result card beside it.
- Signature seed: **Gauge scales.** Every section has a horizontal scale with an ideal band shaded and a marker where "this"
  sits: price ranges sit on a scale, review stars on a scale, service times on a scale. Section dividers are blank test
  pads that fill with color as they come into view.
- Motion idea: Intro: the six pads fade from white to their sample colors one after another and the markers slide into the
  bands. Scroll story: each section's marker glides into the band as it enters the viewport.
- Tools: Test strip reader (pH 7.2-7.8, FC 1-3, TA 80-120, CH 200-400, CYA 30-50 / salt pools 60-80, salt 2,700-3,400),
  Gallons calculator, Pump repair or replace + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright, clean daylight, clear water, close-up of test kits, salt cells, cartridges, equipment pads; high clarity;
  suburban Florida houses with screened pools.

### C — "A year in a desert pool."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Low Desert Pool Co. · Scottsdale, AZ · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: A desert pool has seasons nobody in Florida knows: drain-and-refill months, scale months, monsoon months,
  evaporation months. The page is a pool almanac, one spread per season, written by someone who has scrubbed calcium off tile
  in Paradise Valley in July.
- Tone lead: **color-led** (warm terracotta field, sand paper cards, a deep pool teal accent, dark mesquite brown for text).
- Type lead: **serif** (an old-style editorial serif for display and pull quotes, a quiet sans for small text).
- Hero family: **6 — editorial cover.** The homepage opens like a magazine cover: masthead with the brand, an issue line
  ("The Desert Pool Almanac, sample issue"), a big photo of a pebble-finish pool against a mountain, cover lines for the
  sections.
- Signature seed: **The almanac.** The page is set as month spreads: each section has a folio (page number and month), a tab
  on the right edge like an almanac thumb index (Jan, Mar, Jul, Oct...), and a short "this month in your pool" note. The tabs
  stay sticky and light up as the reader moves through the year.
- Motion idea: Intro: the cover photo settles from a slight zoom while the cover lines slide in from the right edge. Scroll
  story: the thumb-index tab for the current month slides out a few pixels and the folio number counts to the next page.
- Tools: Calcium scale check, Bucket test leak log (about 1/4-1/2 in. a day in summer, hedged), Resurfacing month planner +
  ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: warm low desert light, pebble finishes, stone coping, saguaro and mountains behind, dust haze in monsoon shots,
  scale on tile close-up; editorial distance, wide and calm.
