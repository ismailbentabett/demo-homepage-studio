# Pine Straw — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 35-70 in the Deep South pine belt: two-income couples in subdivision houses with
front beds they have no weekend for, retirees who used to spread it themselves and whose backs said no this
year, landlords and agents getting a house ready to list, military families arriving or leaving on orders,
and HOA board members who need 40 common-area beds done before a walk-through. Most own a brick ranch, a
1990s two-story with a long foundation bed, or an older cottage with azaleas and a live oak out front.

**What just happened.** The beds have gone gray and flat; red clay is showing through; the HOA sent a letter
with a date on it; the house goes on the market Friday; family is coming for Easter or Thanksgiving; a
storm washed the straw off the slope and into the driveway; the neighbors just had theirs done and now the
house looks tired next to them.

**What they fear (their words).**
- "They'll throw down a thin layer and charge me for 80 bales."
- "Last guy left straw all over my driveway, in the gutters and stuck in the boxwoods."
- "It'll be gray old straw full of sticks and pine cones, or it'll be the short stuff that blows away."
- "They won't edge or pull the weeds first, they'll just bury them and the weeds come right back through."
- "Nobody shows up on the day they said, and the HOA deadline is next week."

**What makes them trust.**
- A price per bale and a bale count before anyone unloads.
- Photos of beds with a clean, tucked edge against the lawn and around the shrubs.
- Seeing that they edge and weed first, and blow off the walks and driveway after.
- A real day and arrival window, and a text when the truck is on the way.
- Long-needle straw named as such, with a reason (it holds the slope, it stays red longer).

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** No price at all; a lawn-care template that mentions pine straw once;
a photo of a commercial parking-lot island; "call for quote" with no way to count bales; a site that looks
like it has not been updated since the last owner.

## 2. The job

**Planned vs urgent.** Almost all planned. The "urgent" ones are deadline jobs: HOA violation letters,
listing photos, a party or holiday, a move-in date. Washout repairs after a big rain are the closest thing
to a call-out.

**Seasonality (Gulf Coast and south/north Georgia).**
- Jan-Feb: slow; winter cleanups, leaf removal finishing, shrub cutbacks, beds prepped for spring.
- Mar-May: the big season. Azaleas and dogwoods bloom, Easter, HOA spring walk-throughs, listing season. Two-thirds of a year's straw goes down here.
- Jun-Aug: top-ups and new-construction beds; heavy afternoon storms (Gulf) mean washout re-tucks; hurricane season starts.
- Sep-Nov: the second wave. Fall refresh before Thanksgiving, after leaves drop; HOAs do their fall round.
- Dec: holiday refreshes, then quiet.

**Average ticket (words).** A small front-bed top-up is a few hundred dollars. A full front and back yard is
mid hundreds. An HOA common-area round or an annual two-refresh program runs into four figures. Edging,
cleanup and shrub trimming add to the ticket and are where the job looks finished.

**Services that sell (facts).** Pine straw installation, delivery only, long-leaf straw, bed edging, bed
cleanup and weeding, mulch installation, seasonal refresh, HOA and common-area beds, new construction beds,
leaf removal, shrub trimming, annual programs.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner | Pro |
|---|---|
| "pine needles" | longleaf / slash / loblolly straw |
| "the bales" | 30-40 sq ft coverage per bale at 2-3 in |
| "make the edge neat" | spade edge / cut a bed line |
| "put it around the bushes" | tuck under the drip line, keep it off the stems |
| "the straw keeps sliding off" | washout on slope; tuck and pin the low edge |
| "it looks gray" | faded straw; time for a top-dress |
| "how thick?" | 3-4 in fresh, settles to 2-3 in |
| "pull the weeds" | bed cleanup, hand-pull and pre-emergent |
| "just put new on top" | top-dress vs full strip and replace |
| "the HOA letter" | common-area or violation refresh |
| "the brown needles from my trees" | volunteer straw, uneven, full of cones and sticks |
| "red stuff showing" | bare clay, thin coverage |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A generic lawn-care template where pine straw is one tile among twenty services.
2. A wide shot of a commercial office-park island as the hero.
3. "Quality service at affordable prices" and a stock handshake.
4. "Call for a quote" with no bale price and no way to estimate a count.
5. A Facebook-page-only presence with blurry phone photos of a truck bed full of bales.
6. A green-and-brown palette with a cartoon pine tree logo.
7. No mention of edging or weeding, so the buyer assumes it is not done.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Bale counter.** Learns: how many bales the beds need and the sample cost range. Inputs: bed length and
   depth (or pick "small / medium / long foundation bed"), new vs top-up. Output: bales at 3 in fresh (about
   35 sq ft per bale), a sample range from proof.prices. Leads to: "Book this count" → quote form prefilled.
2. **Bed sketcher.** Learns: the total square feet of odd-shaped beds. Inputs: tap shapes (strip, kidney,
   tree ring, corner) and rough sizes. Output: square feet and bales. Leads to: "Send this sketch" in the form.
3. **Refresh clock.** Learns: when the straw will look gray and need a top-up. Inputs: month of last spread,
   sun or shade. Output: a top-up month and the next full refresh month. Leads to: "Put me on that week".
4. **Slope check.** Learns: whether the beds will wash out. Inputs: slope (flat / gentle / steep), downspout
   onto the bed yes/no. Output: tuck depth, edge pinning or a mulch border, written as plain advice. Leads to call.
5. **Straw vs mulch compare.** Learns: which suits each bed. Inputs: slider between them on four rows
   (slope hold, color life, acid-loving shrubs, cost). Output: a plain recommendation. Leads to quote.
6. **HOA deadline planner.** Learns: the last safe booking date. Inputs: the HOA date from the letter, number
   of beds. Output: book-by date and crew-day estimate. Leads to the form with the date filled in.
7. **Needle guide.** Learns: longleaf vs slash vs loblolly. Inputs: none, a tap-to-compare card. Output:
   length, color life, slope hold, price band (sample). Leads to "Ask for longleaf".

Prices are ranges from facts.proof.prices only, always labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "Rake it out, cut the line, tuck it under. The straw goes down last."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Tuck & Rake Pine Straw · Mobile, AL · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: In Mobile the rain comes hard and often, and straw laid flat on top of an unedged bed is in the driveway by the next storm. Tuck & Rake sells the order of the work: old straw raked out, the bed line cut with a spade, weeds pulled, then the new straw shaken loose and tucked down along the edge so it locks against the cut. The page is that order, one step at a time, and nothing else comes first.
- Tone lead: dark-led (wet pine-bark brown-black after a Gulf rain, straw amber as the accent, a pale azalea-pink only for the edge line)
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed sans set big for the step verbs, RAKE · CUT · PULL · SHAKE · TUCK · BLOW; a plain humanist sans for body)
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo, scrolling text (one tall photo of a fresh-cut bed edge under an oak stays pinned while three short lines pass: "Rake the old out." / "Cut the line." / "Tuck it under."; the first line visible at load)
- Signature seed: **The bed line.** A single hand-cut edge line (drawn SVG, slightly irregular like a spade cut) runs down the left of the page. Each process step is a notch cut into it, with the step verb in condensed caps. Where the page turns to services, the line curves into bed shapes (strip, kidney, tree ring) that hold each service name.
- Motion idea: Intro: the bed line is cut top to bottom in one stroke as the hero loads, and loose needles settle along it. Scroll story: six steps, each step's photo unclips while the line "cuts" down to the next notch; on phones, stacked cards with the clip reveal.
- Tools: Slope check (the Mobile rain is the whole argument), Bale counter, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form (beds → slope → date → contact).
- Photo mood: low, close and wet: overcast or just-after-rain light, dark bed soil, reddish straw, hands and rakes cropped tight, live oaks and azaleas, raised Gulf cottages and brick ranches in the background, no faces.

### B — "Counted bales, measured beds, a number on the ticket before we unload."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Baleyard Pine Straw · Valdosta, GA · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: South Georgia is where the straw comes from, so people around Valdosta know a short bale and a thin spread when they see one. Baleyard answers with arithmetic: square feet of bed, bales at three inches fresh, needle length, the price per bale, all written on a load ticket the homeowner sees before the truck is unloaded. The page is a yard of tallies and calculators, every claim with a number beside it.
- Tone lead: light-led (bale-tag manila and paper white, graphite type, one hay-twine orange for every counted number)
- Type lead: slab (a sturdy slab serif for headlines and big counts, like a stenciled bale tag; a plain grotesk with tabular figures for the tallies)
- Hero family: 8 photo mosaic (five tiles in a tight grid on paper: one huge stacked-bales photo, four small ones of a measured bed, a bale cut open, a long needle against a tape, a finished bed; each tile carries a tally number like "35 sq ft per bale"; the headline sits on the paper beside them)
- Signature seed: **The load ticket.** Every section ends in a torn-off strip of a manila load ticket with hand-tally marks (||||) and a running total: bales counted, square feet covered, inches measured. The marks are drawn SVG strokes; the totals are real outputs of the tools the homeowner just used.
- Motion idea: Intro: the five tally strokes draw across the mosaic and the "35 sq ft per bale" number counts up. Scroll story: the bale counter is pinned while the bed outline is drawn segment by segment and the bale stack beside it grows one bale per 35 square feet.
- Tools: Bale counter (hero-adjacent, the core), Bed sketcher, Needle guide, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form (sketch → count → day → contact).
- Photo mood: bright, flat, mid-morning South Georgia light; things you can count and measure: bale stacks, a tape across a bed, needles laid side by side, clean top-down views, brick ranches and base-housing style homes, sandy soil; documentary and even, no drama, no faces.

### C — "Spring for the dogwoods, fall before Thanksgiving. Marietta, twice a year."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Two Seasons Pine Straw · Marietta, GA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: In Cobb County the straw goes down twice: once when the dogwoods and azaleas open and the HOAs walk the streets, once after the leaves come off the oaks and before family drives in for Thanksgiving. Two Seasons is built around those two weeks of the Marietta year, the red clay underneath, and the cul-de-sacs and old Square-side streets where the crews spend them. The page reads like a local almanac of two seasons on the same streets.
- Tone lead: color-led (a deep loblolly-pine green field, red-clay terracotta as the second color, dogwood cream for paper and text)
- Type lead: serif (a warm old-style serif with italics for the season names and street lines, like a town almanac; a quiet sans for body)
- Hero family: 13 shape-masked photo (the hero photo of a fresh pine straw bed curving under a dogwood sits inside a kidney-shaped bed outline, the shape every Marietta front yard has; that same bed shape returns as the frame for seasons, areas and reviews)
- Signature seed: **The two-page almanac.** The page is split into a SPRING half and a FALL half with one season ribbon at the top that turns from dogwood white to oak-leaf rust as you scroll. Each half has its own street list, its own refresh dates, its own review; the bed-shape mask from the hero repeats as the frame of each season's photo.
- Motion idea: Intro: dogwood petals drift across the kidney mask once as the photo unclips inside it. Scroll story: crossing the middle of the page, the season ribbon turns, the bed-shape photo swaps from spring bloom to fall leaves inside the same mask, and the dates on the ribbon change.
- Tools: Refresh clock (built around the two seasons), HOA deadline planner, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form (season → beds → HOA date → contact).
- Photo mood: warm and local: dogwood and azalea bloom in soft spring light, fall oak leaves and low golden afternoon, curving beds in front of 1990s two-story brick-and-siding houses, red clay at the bed edge, wide enough to feel like a street; no faces, no house numbers.
