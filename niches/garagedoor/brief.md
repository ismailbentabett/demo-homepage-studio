# Garage Door — niche brief

Three demo homepages for a residential garage door repair and installation company. All three are sample sites: fictional brands, real cities and ZIPs, 555-01NN phones, `.example` email, proof marked "Sample".

## 1. The buyer

- **Who calls:** homeowners aged 30-70 in suburban single-family houses with an attached two-car garage. Most of them use the garage door as the front door: it is how they leave for work and how the kids get home.
- **What just happened:** a loud bang from the garage (a torsion spring snapped), the door will not lift or lifts a few inches and stops, a cable is hanging loose, the door came off its track and hangs crooked, the opener hums but nothing moves, or a hailstorm dented the panels. Less urgent: the door is loud, the opener is 20 years old, the door is uninsulated and the room above it is cold.
- **Fears, in their words:**
  - "My car is stuck inside and I have to be at work at 8."
  - "Is it safe to touch? I heard those springs can kill you."
  - "They'll come out for a $300 spring and talk me into a $4,000 door."
  - "I'll pay a trip fee and they'll say they need to come back with the part."
  - "The door will fall on my kid or my dog."
- **What makes them trust:** a price range for the spring before the call; a clear "do not touch the spring" safety line; a tech who says what failed and shows the broken part; parts on the truck (fixed on the first visit); a plain repair-or-replace answer.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:** a national-chain look with a stock smiling tech; "call for price" everywhere; a pop-up coupon on load; no sign the company serves their suburb; a site that looks like it sells commercial roll-up doors.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned:** roughly 60% urgent (spring, cable, off-track, opener dead, car inside), 40% planned (new door, opener upgrade, insulation, hail replacement, tune-up).

**Seasonality:**
- **Dallas (Texas):** spring hail (March-June) brings dent and panel replacement work. Summer heat (June-September) wears out opener motors and makes uninsulated garages hot; insulated door upgrades sell. A rare hard freeze (February 2021 is remembered) brings a spike of spring breaks.
- **Cleveland (Ohio):** lake-effect winters (December-March) mean cold-snap spring breaks, rusted bottom sections from road salt and slush, and frozen bottom seals. Spring is the season for replacing rusted doors; fall is for tune-ups and weather seals.
- **Omaha (Nebraska):** the coldest mornings break the most springs (January-February below zero). Hail season runs May-July. Fall is the tune-up season ahead of winter.

**Average ticket (words, not dollars):** a spring pair or a cable repair is a small-to-mid repair; a new opener is mid-size; a new insulated 16x7 door is the big ticket; a yearly tune-up is the small entry offer. Exact sample ranges are in each facts file's `proof.prices`.

**Services that sell:** broken spring replacement, opener install and repair, cables/rollers/track, off-track door, new insulated doors, hail-dent panel replacement, sensors and auto-reverse, keypad/remote setup, weather seals, tune-ups.

**Homeowner word vs. pro word:**

| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| the big spring above the door | torsion spring (on a shaft, wound with winding bars) |
| the springs on the sides | extension springs / stretch springs (older setups) |
| the door is too heavy | the springs are out of balance / lost tension |
| the wire came off | lift cable off the drum |
| the door is crooked | door off track / racked door |
| the wheels | rollers (nylon, steel, sealed bearing) |
| the motor | opener / operator (chain, belt, jackshaft / wall-mount) |
| the beams at the bottom | photo-eye safety sensors |
| the door goes back up | auto-reverse / force setting |
| dents from the storm | hail damage, section replacement |
| the rubber at the bottom | bottom seal / astragal; side and top seal is weatherstripping / stop molding |
| how long the spring lasts | cycle rating (10,000, 25,000, 50,000 cycles) |
| an insulated door | polystyrene or polyurethane core, with an R-value |
| the emergency release | red release cord / manual disconnect |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

- A stock photo of a smiling tech in a polo holding a clipboard in front of a perfect white door.
- "$25 off" and "free service call" coupon banners and pop-ups on load.
- Red, white and blue "America's #1" chain branding and a wall of manufacturer logos.
- A grid of 24 identical service icons with no explanation of what a torsion spring is.
- "Call for price" on every service, even the spring that everyone breaks.
- A door style gallery lifted from a manufacturer catalogue that has nothing to do with local houses.
- Hero slider with five rotating photos and no clear first action.

## 5. Niche tools

1. **Spring age estimator**
   - What the homeowner learns: roughly how many cycles their springs have used and when they are likely to break.
   - Inputs: the year the springs were installed (or the house was built), opens per day (slider 2-12), spring type (standard / high-cycle / not sure).
   - Output: cycles used against the rating (10,000 standard), a bar showing used vs left, and a plain line ("you are past the usual life: a break is likely in the next cold snap"). The spring price range from facts, labelled sample.
   - Leads to the call: "Book a spring check" carries the estimate into the quote form.
2. **Car-stuck safe-release guide**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether it is safe to open the door by hand, and how.
   - Inputs: tap what they see: a gap in the spring above the door / a loose cable / the door is crooked / the opener hums / nothing.
   - Output: step-by-step for the safe case (pull the red cord with the door closed, lift with two people); a "stop, do not lift it" panel for a broken spring or a crooked door, with the phone number.
   - Leads to the call: the stop panel is the call.
3. **Symptom finder**
   - What the homeowner learns: what probably failed.
   - Inputs: sound (bang / grinding / clicking / silence), what the door does (does not move / goes up and comes back / goes up crooked / moves a few inches).
   - Output: the likely part (spring, cable, sensor, opener gear, track), how urgent it is, and the sample range.
   - Leads to the call: "Send this to us", prefilled into the form.
4. **Repair or replace calculator**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether to fix the old door or replace it.
   - Inputs: door age, sections damaged or rusted, insulation (none / some), what they plan to spend on repairs this year.
   - Output: a plain recommendation and the two sample ranges side by side (repair items vs new insulated door).
   - Leads to the call: "Get both numbers in writing."
5. **Insulation R-value compare**
   - What the homeowner learns: what an insulated door changes in the garage and the room above it.
   - Inputs: current door (uninsulated steel / polystyrene / not sure), room above the garage (yes/no), climate (fixed by site).
   - Output: a simple bar of typical R-values (no fake savings numbers), and what it means for comfort and noise.
   - Leads to the call: "See door samples."
6. **Hail dent check**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether a dented door can be fixed by section or needs a full door, and what to photograph.
   - Inputs: number of dented sections, are the dents creased or just dimpled, does the door still roll smoothly.
   - Output: section replacement or full door, the per-section sample range, and a short photo checklist.
   - Leads to the call: "Upload your photos."
7. **Winter-ready checklist** (cold climates)
   - What the homeowner learns: the 6 things to check before the first freeze.
   - Inputs: tick boxes as they check (bottom seal, rollers, springs gap, opener battery backup, sensors, lubrication).
   - Output: a score and the items that need a pro, with the tune-up sample range.
   - Leads to the call: "Book the fall tune-up."
8. **ZIP checker** (all three sites)
   - Input: a 5-digit ZIP.
   - Output: inside the service area (from `zips`) or "we'll tell you who to call".
9. **Multi-step quote form** (all three sites)
   - Steps: what is wrong (spring, cable, opener, off track, new door, hail, other); is the car stuck inside; door size and photos; timing; contact.
   - Store first, then send to GoHighLevel.

## 6. The three directions

### A — The page lifts like a garage door, one panel at a time   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)

- Brand: Brightrail Door Co. · Dallas, TX · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: the homepage is a four-panel garage door seen from inside the garage at night. Scrolling raises it section by section, and each panel that rises is one stage of a spring repair: unwind, replace, rewind (counted quarter turns), balance test, safety reverse test. The Dallas angle: two-car garages in Richardson and Plano used as the front door, spring hail in North Texas, and summer heat on openers.
- Tone lead: dark-led. Inside-the-garage night: deep charcoal and concrete gray, one warm sodium-orange light from the driveway, white type.
- Type lead: condensed. A tall condensed grotesk for headings that reads like stenciled door labels; plain sans body.
- Hero family: 4 stacked headline over a cinematic strip. The headline sits on dark paper ("Spring snapped? Car inside? We lift it today."), and under it a wide 21:9 strip shows the view from inside the garage under a half-open door, the driveway lit outside.
- Signature seed: a torsion-spring counter. A drawn spring in the left margin winds as you scroll, and its count ("quarter turns: 0 → 30") is the progress of the page; each repair step lands on a turn mark. Section edges are door-panel seams with a roller on the right edge.
- Motion idea: intro: the 21:9 strip opens upward like a door rising from the floor (clip from the bottom). Scroll story: four panels pin and rise one by one, each revealing a repair step and its sample price; with reduced motion they are four stacked cards.
- Tools: spring age estimator; car-stuck safe-release guide; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form (first question: "is your car stuck inside?").
- Photo mood: evening and night, seen from inside the garage looking out to the driveway, warm sodium or porch light, hands on springs and winding bars, close-ups of cables and drums. No faces.

### B — Every part has a number, and the page reads them out   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)

- Brand: Cycle & Coil Door Service · Cleveland, OH · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: the site is a spec plate. Every service is a part with its own reading: spring cycle rating, wire size, roller type, opener force setting, door R-value. The symptom finder is the hero: the homeowner gets an answer before they see a sales pitch. The Cleveland angle: 1950s-60s ranches and colonials in Parma and Lakewood with old stretch springs, salt-rusted bottom sections, and lake-effect winters that break springs.
- Tone lead: light-led. Galvanized steel and paper white, cool gray rules, one safety yellow for actions and warnings.
- Type lead: mono-accent. A clean grotesk for headings and body, with a monospace for every reading, part number and price (spec-plate style).
- Hero family: 7 tool-first. The symptom finder is the hero: three taps (sound, what the door does, how old), with the answer and sample range beside the promise; with JS off it is a readable list of symptoms and the phone number.
- Signature seed: spec plates. Every section has a small riveted plate in its corner with mono readouts ("SPRING 0.243 × 2.0 × 32 · 10,000 CYCLES"). Proof is shown as readings: the cycle meter of a real door, the force setting, the balance test. The facts prices sit as a parts sheet.
- Motion idea: intro: the hero's mono digits roll into place like an odometer and a cycle meter fills to the homeowner's estimate. Scroll story: a spring shown at 1:1 scale stretches across the screen as the cycle count rises, then snaps (a short shake, a bang drawn as a line) at the end of its rated life.
- Tools: symptom finder (hero); repair or replace calculator; insulation R-value compare; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: overcast daylight, mechanical close-ups: spring coils, drums, cable, rollers, rust on a bottom section, snow by a driveway. Cool, clean, honest. Hands only.

### C — Built for the coldest morning of the year   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)

- Brand: Zero Degree Door Co. · Omaha, NE · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Omaha's weather is the story. Springs break on the coldest mornings, hail comes in May and June, and bottom seals freeze to the slab. The navigation is a thermometer rail running from −8°F to 104°F; each section sits at a temperature and the problem that comes with it. Suburbs: Papillion, Elkhorn, Bellevue, Council Bluffs across the river.
- Tone lead: color-led. Deep winter blue as the main field, frost white type, and one warm amber of garage light for actions.
- Type lead: slab. A sturdy slab serif for headings (like stamped road signs and feed-store lettering), sans body.
- Hero family: 1 full-bleed video + scrim. A slow loop of a garage door rising at blue hour in snow, warm light spilling out, with the headline on a left scrim. This is the niche's one use of family 1; the idea on top is the frost.
- Signature seed: the thermometer rail. A vertical thermometer on the left edge fills and drops as you scroll; each section is labelled with its temperature ("−8°F: springs snap", "34°F: seals freeze", "88°F: hail season"). Frost forms on section edges in the cold sections and is gone in the summer ones.
- Motion idea: intro: frost clears from the headline (a mask that melts from the center out) while the door in the video rises. Scroll story: the thermometer drops from 104°F to −8°F through four pinned scenes, each showing the problem at that temperature and the sample price to fix it.
- Tools: winter-ready checklist; hail dent check; door style picker (simple: panel style + color swatches from the brand palette, no 3D); ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: blue hour and snow, warm light from inside the garage, frost on metal, hail on a driveway, Midwestern suburban houses with two-car garages. Cold outside, warm inside.
