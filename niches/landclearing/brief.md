# Land Clearing — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
Who calls: owners of 1-40 acres on the edge of town, age 35-70. Three kinds. (1) The new-land buyer who just
closed on a wooded parcel and needs a house pad and a driveway before the builder can start. (2) The
inherited-land owner: granddad's pasture or hayfield has gone to cedar, briars, yaupon or palmetto, and
they want it back in grass or ready to sell. (3) The horse or cattle owner whose fence lines and paddocks
are disappearing into brush. A fourth, smaller group calls after a storm with trees across the drive.

What just happened: closing day, a builder saying "clear it first", a county notice about overgrowth,
a fence that can't be fixed because nobody can reach it, a hurricane or ice storm, or simply walking
the land and not being able to get through.

Fears, in their words:
- "They'll bulldoze everything and I'll lose the big oaks I bought the land for."
- "They quote by the job, then charge me more once they 'find' how thick it is."
- "They'll leave piles of brush and burn holes all over the place."
- "Ruts and torn-up ground. The machine will sink, or my slope will wash into the creek."
- "Will it all just grow back by next summer?"

What makes them trust:
- Someone walks the land with them before any machine shows up, and marks what stays.
- A price per acre (or per stump, per foot) with what changes it, said up front.
- Photos of ground like theirs: the same trees, the same soil, the same slope.
- A plain answer on what happens to the debris (mulched in place, nothing hauled, nothing burned).
- Knowing the machine size, so they know it fits the gate and won't wreck the lawn.

Leaves in 5 seconds: a site full of excavators on a commercial job, no number anywhere, a form that asks
for everything before saying anything, a photo of a bare dirt moonscape, or a page that never says
which towns they work in.

## 2. The job
Mostly planned work (about 85%), booked one to six weeks out. Storm cleanup is the urgent slice.
Ticket bands: small (stump grinding, a fence line, a half-acre of brush), middle (one to three acres of
mulching, a homesite pad), large (ten-plus acres of pasture reclamation, a full lot with grading).

Seasonality by region:
- East Texas (Tyler): year-round; best ground December to March when the yaupon is dormant and the ground
  is firm; slow in wet April-May and in August heat; builders push lots in late winter.
- North Central Florida (Ocala): year-round; peak October to April (dry season, sandy ground firm); summer
  rains slow it; June-November hurricane season brings storm cleanup.
- Virginia Piedmont (Lynchburg): best November to March with the leaves down and the ground frozen or firm;
  spring rains make red clay slick; summer is for invasive follow-up and mowing; ice storms in Jan-Feb.

Services that sell: lot clearing, forestry mulching, underbrush clearing, stump grinding, pasture
reclamation, fence line clearing, trail and road cutting, tree removal, site prep and grading, storm
debris cleanup, invasive plant removal.

Homeowner words vs pro words:
- "clear the woods but keep the big trees" vs "selective clearing" / "save trees over 12-inch DBH"
- "grind it all up" vs "forestry mulching" / "masticating"
- "the thick stuff under the trees" vs "understory" / "midstory"
- "that holly that's everywhere" vs "yaupon"
- "the low palm bushes" vs "saw palmetto"
- "get the field back" vs "pasture reclamation"
- "spot for the house" vs "building pad"
- "flatten it" vs "fine grade"
- "the big chewing machine" vs "skid steer with a mulching head" / "tracked mulcher"
- "how much per acre" vs "acreage rate by density class"
- "the stuff that keeps coming back" vs "resprout" / "invasive species"
- "old fence row full of trees" vs "hedgerow"
- "tear up my yard" vs "ground disturbance" / "rutting"
- "where the property ends" vs "survey pins" / "boundary line"

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)
- A yellow excavator on a muddy commercial site as the hero, logo on the boom.
- "Professional land clearing services" headline with no town, no price, no method.
- A wall of before/after drone shots that are all brown dirt and all look the same.
- Equipment brand logos (the machine dealer's) as the proof.
- A "Get a Quote" form with 12 fields and no idea of cost anywhere.
- A Facebook-feed gallery of truck selfies and chest-up crew photos.
- Service lists copied from a template, nothing about local plants, soil or slope.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Acreage estimator**: inputs acres + density (light brush / thick understory / wooded) + slope; output a
   sample price range per acre and total from facts.proof.prices, with "what moves this number". Leads to
   "send us your parcel and we'll firm it up" → form prefilled.
2. **Keep or clear picker**: homeowner taps trees/plant types on an illustrated lot (big oak, pine, yaupon,
   palmetto, cedar, briars) and marks keep or clear; output a plain plan ("selective clear, about X% of
   cover stays") and the method. Leads to a walk-through booking.
3. **Ground reader**: pick your ground (sand, red clay, rock, wet bottom) and slope; output which machine
   fits (compact tracked vs full-size mulcher vs skid steer), when in the year it's firm, what to expect for
   ruts. Leads to call.
4. **Debris answer**: choose mulch in place / haul off / pile; output what each means for the lot, time and
   sample cost band. Leads to quote.
5. **Regrowth calendar**: by region and plant, when resprouts show and when a follow-up visit makes sense.
   Leads to booking a follow-up.
6. **Stump counter**: number of stumps by diameter band; output the sample per-stump range total.
7. **Gate and access check**: gate width, soft ground, septic or well location; output whether the machine
   fits and what to mark before the crew arrives.

Prices are always ranges from facts.proof.prices and labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — Nothing goes down that you didn't agree to on the walk.   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Flagged Tree Land Clearing · Tyler, TX · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: The job is told as one walk across an East Texas lot: walk and flag, mark the pad, mulch
  the yaupon, grind the stumps, grade, walk again. Orange flagging ribbon is the thread: every keeper tree
  gets one, and the site follows the ribbon from the gate to the finished pad.
- Tone lead: dark-led (deep pine-shade green-black, with flagging-tape orange as the only hot color)
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed grotesk for headlines and step numbers, like the stencil on a
  machine's side panel; a plain humanist sans for body)
- Hero family: 1 — full-bleed video + scrim: a slow loop of a mulching head chewing through understory, the
  headline left-aligned on a dense scrim, with one strip of orange ribbon tied across the corner.
- Signature seed: the flag ribbon system. Each process step is a tag tied to a tree trunk drawn in SVG;
  the ribbon unspools between sections as a line, and "keeper" trees are counted in the margin as you
  scroll (keep count never drops).
- Motion idea: intro, a ribbon ties itself around the first word of the headline (under 1 s). Scroll story:
  a plan-view lot drawing where understory dots clear step by step while flagged trees stay lit.
- Tools: keep or clear picker, gate and access check, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low, late afternoon light through pines; close to the machine and the ground; smoke-free,
  shaded understory, red sandy soil, mulch flecks in the air; crew only from behind or hands.

### B — You know the number per acre before anyone drives out.   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Measured Acre Land Clearing · Ocala, FL · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: The page is a ground survey sheet. Florida ground comes in classes the owner can see
  (open palmetto sand, oak hammock, old pasture, pine plantation), each with its own sample rate per acre,
  machine and timeline. The homeowner picks theirs, adds acres, and gets the same breakdown the crew uses.
- Tone lead: light-led (sun-bleached sand white, with ink and a survey-stake pink accent)
- Type lead: mono-accent (a clean grotesk for headlines and body; a monospace for every acre figure,
  rate, density class and plot label)
- Hero family: 8 — photo mosaic: four tiles of four ground classes in one grade, each tile carrying its
  class label and sample rate in mono, the promise set on plain sand-white paper beside them.
- Signature seed: the density legend. One hatch pattern per ground class (dots for palmetto, crosses for
  hammock, dashes for pasture, lines for pine) reused as section backgrounds, chart keys, price rows and
  the estimator's map, so every number on the page points back to a type of ground.
- Motion idea: intro, the four mosaic tiles draw their hatch overlay and then fade it to the photo. Scroll
  story: an acreage bar fills by class as the estimator inputs change, totals ticking in mono.
- Tools: acreage estimator, ground reader, stump counter, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright, high midday Florida light; wide and flat; white sand, palmetto, live oak and Spanish
  moss, board fences of horse country; clean, documentary, almost no people.

### C — We put old farms back the way they were, one fence row at a time.   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Split Rail Land Clearing · Lynchburg, VA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Around Lynchburg a lot of land is old tobacco and hay farms gone back to cedar and tree of
  heaven. The page is the story of one hillside through the year: what the old fence rows were, why the
  red clay slope needs mulching instead of a dozer, and what the field looks like when grass comes back.
- Tone lead: color-led (red Piedmont clay as the field color, with broomsedge gold and Blue Ridge haze blue)
- Type lead: slab (a sturdy slab serif for headlines, like a painted barn sign; a warm sans for body)
- Hero family: 11 — diagonal split: the seam runs at the angle of a Piedmont hillside, photo of a reclaimed
  slope field above it, headline on clay red below; the angle returns as section edges.
- Signature seed: the fence row timeline. A split-rail drawn in SVG runs through the page; each rail is a
  season (leaf-off clearing, spring rain, summer resprout check, fall grass), and the rails carry the
  local notes: plants, soil, which towns are in which hollow.
- Motion idea: intro, the hillside diagonal settles into place as haze lifts off the ridgeline (under 1 s).
  Scroll story: the split-rail builds rail by rail as the four seasons pass, the background shifting from
  winter grey to fall gold.
- Tools: regrowth calendar, debris answer, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: soft, hazy morning or late-fall light; rolling hills, Blue Ridge in the distance, leaf-off
  hardwoods, old split-rail and wire fences, red clay, broomsedge fields; wide and quiet.

## 7. Shot list (for the media curator)
Hero candidates (3 per direction, all different):
- A (dark, close, under pines): mulching head in understory at golden hour; wooded lot with orange flagging
  ribbon on a trunk; tracked machine from behind in a pine stand. Must: shade, depth, ground-level. Avoid:
  bare dirt moonscapes, commercial sites, burn piles, faces.
- B (bright, wide, flat Florida): palmetto scrub under live oaks in sun; cleared sandy pasture with board fence;
  oak hammock edge with Spanish moss. Must: high light, readable ground type. Avoid: swamps, beaches, palm
  trees by a pool, signs.
- C (hazy, rolling Piedmont): reclaimed hillside field with a tree line; old split-rail fence in a field with
  mountains behind; leaf-off hardwood slope in fall. Must: rolling hills, haze, autumn color or winter grey.
  Avoid: flat land, desert, snow-heavy alpine, houses up close.

materials (mulch layer, red soil, sand, roots), plants (yaupon, palmetto, cedar, briars, invasive vines),
