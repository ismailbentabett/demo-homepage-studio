# Murals & Custom Art — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
Three kinds of people call a muralist, and the page has to work for all of them in the first screen.

- **Homeowners, 30-55**, often first-time owners or long-time renters who finally can paint. A new baby on the way
  (nursery wall), a blank stucco or block wall in the yard, a garage door the street keeps tagging, a stair wall
  that is "just beige". They have a Pinterest board and no idea what it costs.
- **Small business owners, 28-65**: a taco counter, a coffee bar, a barbershop, a pizza place, a dentist's waiting
  room. They want people to stop on the sidewalk and take a photo. They are the bigger ticket and the faster decision.
- **Parents and grandparents** buying a gift wall (kids' room, playroom, a memorial wall for a family dog).

What just happened: they moved in, they opened a shop, a baby is due in 4 months, the fence got tagged again, a
landlord finally said yes.

**Fears, in their words**
1. "What if I hate it once it's on the wall? You can't just return a mural."
2. "I have no idea what this costs. Is it $500 or $20,000?"
3. "Will it fade or peel in a year?" (LA sun, DC humidity, New Haven freeze-thaw and salt air)
4. "Is it going to look like a kid's drawing / clip art / every other Instagram wing wall?"
5. "How long will someone be in my house / blocking my storefront?"

**What makes them trust**
1. Seeing a sketch or mockup on their own wall before any paint goes up.
2. A price range by wall size, said out loud, before the call.
3. Close-ups of brushwork and clean edges, not only the wide glamour shot.
4. A clear plan for the finish: what primer, what paint, what clear coat, and what happens when it gets scuffed.
5. A named owner who answers the phone and shows how the work happens.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds**: an Instagram grid with no prices, a site that is all artist statement and no
"how do I hire you", copyrighted cartoon characters on a nursery wall (feels cheap and risky), no phone number, a
slow full-screen video that never says what they do.

## 2. The job
- **Emergency vs planned**: almost all planned (95%+). The only urgent calls: graffiti over an existing mural, a
  storefront opening date, a baby's due date, a party. No emergency line.
- **Ticket bands**: small (a hand-lettered sign, a nursery accent, a restoration touch-up), medium (one interior wall,
  a garage door, a fence run), large (a full exterior wall, a restaurant interior, a storefront facade).
- **Seasonality by region**
  - Los Angeles: exterior work all year; best stretch March-June and October-November; September heat and Santa Ana
    wind days slow outdoor paint (fast drying, dust). June gloom mornings are good for long exterior sessions.
  - Washington DC: exterior April-June and September-October; July-August humidity and afternoon storms push work to
    mornings; November-March is interior season (nurseries, restaurants, offices before spring openings).
  - New Haven: exterior only May-mid October (paint needs 50°F+ nights); winter is interiors, restaurants, design
    and canvas commissions; spring calls book the summer wall.
- **Services that sell**: interior murals, exterior murals, nursery and kids' rooms, storefront and business murals,
  restaurant and cafe walls, hand-lettered signs, faux finishes, trompe l'oeil, garage and fence art, canvas
  commissions, design consultations, mural restoration.

**Homeowner words vs pro words**
| Homeowner says | Muralist says |
|---|---|
| "a painting on the wall" | site-specific mural |
| "the drawing first" | concept sketch / scaled mockup |
| "how do you get it the right size" | grid transfer / pounce pattern / projection |
| "the background color" | ground coat / base coat |
| "the shiny protective layer" | UV clear coat / anti-graffiti coating |
| "it got tagged" | graffiti strike, sacrificial coat |
| "it's chipping" | adhesion failure, efflorescence on masonry |
| "looks like a real window" | trompe l'oeil |
| "fake marble / wood look" | faux finish, glazing, graining |
| "the writing on the window" | hand lettering, sign painting, gilding |
| "how long will it last" | lightfastness, pigment rating |
| "the bumpy wall" | stucco texture / split-face block |
| "fixing an old one" | conservation / restoration, inpainting |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)
1. An Instagram-style grid of finished murals with no context, no size, no price, no process.
2. "Bringing your walls to life" / "art that speaks" headlines that say nothing.
3. A long artist bio and statement before any way to hire.
4. Copyrighted cartoon characters in the nursery gallery.
5. A full-bleed video of a spray can with no headline anyone can read.
6. "Contact for pricing" and a form with one field.
7. The selfie of the artist in front of the wall as the hero.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Wall size price range**: inputs width and height in feet, interior/exterior, detail level (simple shapes /
   illustrated / photo-real). Output: square feet, a sample price range from `proof.prices`, rough days on site,
   labelled sample. Leads to "Send me this range" form.
2. **Grid preview**: the homeowner picks a wall shape (stair wall, garage door, fence run, storefront) and sees the
   grid a muralist would chalk on it, with the square count. Teaches the process; ends with "Book a sketch".
3. **Style picker**: 6 style cards (botanical, geometric, landscape, lettering, trompe l'oeil, kids' storybook);
   picking one fills the quote form's style step.
4. **Outdoor finish check**: inputs wall material (stucco, brick, block, wood, metal door) and sun side
   (south/west/north/east). Output: the prep and coating plan in plain words (primer, paint, UV clear coat,
   sacrificial anti-graffiti coat). Leads to the call.
5. **Timeline counter**: pick a date (due date, opening day). Output: when the sketch, approval and paint days must
   happen, working back from it. Leads to "Hold my dates".
6. **Restoration triage**: photo-free checklist (fading, peeling, tags, cracks). Output: touch-up, partial repaint
   or full restoration, with the sample range.
7. **Wall walk map** (C only): a drawn map of the city's wall types and neighborhoods with the service towns.

## 6. The three directions

### A — The wall is drawn before it is painted   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Pounce Wheel Mural Co. · Los Angeles, CA · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: A pounce wheel is the little spiked roller a muralist runs along a paper pattern so charcoal dust can
  mark the outline on the wall. The site is the whole path from a sketch on paper to paint on a stucco wall in
  Highland Park: sketch, scale, grid, pounce, block in, detail, clear coat. Each step is shown as its own frame, so a
  homeowner who fears "what if I hate it" sees exactly where they say yes before paint touches the wall.
- Tone lead: color-led (sun-bleached stucco coral as the main field, jacaranda violet and palm-shadow green as
  partners, chalk white type and lines; color is the LA wall itself).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall sign-painter style condensed display for step titles, set big like hand lettering on a
  shop window; a friendly sans for body).
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo with scrolling text (a tall photo of a hand blocking in color on a chalk-gridded wall
  stays pinned; three lines scroll past: "Sketched on paper." "Gridded on your wall." "Then painted, square by square."
  The first line is visible without scrolling).
- Signature seed: the chalk grid. The whole page sits on a faint 1-foot grid; every section is a grid square with a
  coordinate (A1 sketch, B2 scale, C3 transfer...), the nav is the coordinate list, and as each section scrolls into
  view its square "gets painted" (the grid lines under it fill with a flat color block). The coordinates repeat on
  the process frames and the price tool's square-foot count.
- Motion idea: intro, a dotted pounce line traces the outline of the hero headline and then fills (≤ 1 s). Scroll
  story, a pinned wall diagram goes from blank to grid to outline to blocked color to finished detail as the seven
  steps pass.
- Tools: Grid preview + Wall size price range + Timeline counter + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form (wall → size →
  style → date → contact).
- Photo mood: bright, hard Southern California afternoon light, crisp shadows, warm stucco and painted block walls,
  palms and jacaranda in the background, close hands-and-brush framing; saturated, sunny, never gloomy.

### B — See the wall before you pay for it   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Wall Proof Mural Studio · Washington, DC · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: In printing, a proof is the copy you check before the run. Here the homeowner or shop owner gets a
  to-scale proof of the mural on a photo of their own wall, a color strip with every paint, a square-foot price and a
  dated schedule, all before a deposit. The page is the proof sheet: every claim has a number next to it.
- Tone lead: light-led (proof-paper white and pale rowhouse-brick blush, ink black type, one registration-mark cyan
  signal color for numbers and actions).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a crisp grotesk for headings and body, a monospaced face for every dimension, swatch code,
  sample price and date).
- Hero family: 7 tool-first (the wall size price tool is the hero: width and height in feet, interior/exterior,
  detail level, and the result block shows square feet, the sample range and days on site; the promise and phone
  sit beside it; with JS off it reads as a printed range table plus the phone).
- Signature seed: printer's marks. Every section is a proof page with crop marks at its corners, a registration
  target, and a color bar along one edge that holds that section's swatches; each proof carries a proof number
  ("Proof 03 · Finish") that matches the quote form step numbers, and the footer is the "approved" stamp line.
- Motion idea: intro, crop marks draw in at the four corners of the hero tool and the color bar fills swatch by
  swatch. Scroll story, a pinned mockup of a rowhouse side wall goes through three proofs (sketch overlay, color
  proof, approved) while the side text lists what the client checks at each.
- Tools: Wall size price range + Outdoor finish check + Style picker + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: even, flat daylight, overcast or north light; straight-on and level; brick party walls, painted
  interior walls, swatches, tape and measuring close-ups; clean and slightly cool, like a well-lit studio.

### C — Painted for New Haven brick, winters and all   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Trap Rock Mural Co. · New Haven, CT · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: New Haven sits between East Rock and West Rock, two red trap-rock ridges, with the harbor below and
  brick everywhere in between: pizza-place back rooms, triple-decker side walls, old factory walls in Fair Haven,
  porches in Westville. The site is a walk across that town, wall by wall, and each wall type comes with what the
  weather does to it (frost, salt air, short painting season) and how the paint is planned for it.
- Tone lead: dark-led (deep trap-rock brown-black and harbor night blue as the ground, warm brick red and a
  sodium-lamp amber accent, warm off-white type).
- Type lead: serif (a sturdy old-style serif with character for headlines and wall names, small caps for neighborhood
  labels; a quiet sans for UI).
- Hero family: 15 map / area-led (a drawn SVG map: the two ridges, the harbor, the service towns and a dotted walking
  route with numbered wall markers; the promise and phone sit on the map's legend card).
- Signature seed: the wall walk. The page is a numbered walk of wall types (Stop 1 brick party wall, Stop 2 clapboard
  garage, Stop 3 restaurant back room, Stop 4 kids' room in a two-family, Stop 5 harbor-facing fence); each stop has
  a small painted marker, a season line ("paint window: May-October" / "indoor, all winter") and the route line
  connects sections down the page.
- Motion idea: intro, the walking route draws itself across the map from the harbor to East Rock and the stop
  markers pop in. Scroll story, a side map stays pinned and a dot travels the route as each stop's section passes.
- Tools: Wall walk map + Timeline counter (season-aware) + Restoration triage + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low autumn and winter light, late afternoon or dusk, red brick and old painted wood, wet streets,
  warm lamp-lit interiors; moody and warm, never bright midday.

## 7. Shot list (for the media curator)

**Hero candidates**
- A1: hand with a brush blocking in color on a large wall with chalk grid lines (must: landscape or tall, hands/arm
  only, paint visible; avoid: faces, readable text on the wall).
- A2: muralist from behind on a ladder at a sunny stucco wall (must: from behind, hard sunlight; avoid: faces,
  brand logos, commercial billboards).
- A3: paper sketch and pencil with a wall outline, color swatches beside it (must: hand drawing; avoid: digital
  tablets, copyrighted characters).
- B1: measuring tape against a white painted wall with painter's tape lines (must: straight-on, even light; avoid:
  brand on tape, faces).
- B2: paint swatches / color chips fanned on a table beside a brush (must: neutral daylight; avoid: store displays,
  brand names).
- B3: brick side wall of a rowhouse in flat daylight (must: blank or painted brick, no signs; avoid: house numbers,
  graffiti tags with readable names).
- C1: red brick wall at dusk with a warm street lamp (must: brick, evening light; avoid: readable signs, cars with
  plates).
- C2: painted mural fragment on old brick, close, autumn light (must: no readable brand text, no characters; avoid:
  famous public murals).
- C3: rocky ridge or harbor at sunset in New England (must: landscape, red rock or harbor water; avoid: big
  city skylines, tropical scenes).

**Shared pool (by slot)**: process (sketch, grid, chalk outline, projector on wall, pounce pattern, blocking color,
detail brush, clear coat roller); materials (paint cans, spray cans, palette, brushes in a jar, masking tape,
swatches); tools (ladder, scaffold, roller pole, drop cloth); finished details (botanical mural, geometric wall,
lettering on glass, faux marble, trompe l'oeil window, kids' room mural, garage door art, fence art); places (LA
stucco walls and palms, DC brick rowhouse alleys, New England brick and clapboard, a cafe interior); weather (winter
brick, sun on stucco); hands at work; crew from behind. Must: no faces to camera, no logos, no readable brand text,
no copyrighted characters. Avoid: AI-looking renders, wallpaper/decal products, famous public murals, galleries with
framed paintings, commercial billboards.

**Video ideas (5-15 s, slow, no faces, no logos)**: 1) brush pulling a long line of color on a wall; 2) spray can
fading color onto a wall; 3) roller putting a ground coat on a big wall; 4) hand sketching a design on paper.
