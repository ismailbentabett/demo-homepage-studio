# Solar — niche brief

Three sunny cities, three different roofs: Sacramento (pitched composition-shingle roofs under a big tree canopy, two
utilities, a lot of homeowners who already got three sales pitches), Tucson (low-slope and tile roofs, extreme heat, monsoon
storms, a buyer who wants the math and the sources), Albuquerque (flat roofs behind parapets, canales, about 5,300 ft of
altitude, a windy spring, rural edges with well pumps). The page must sound like a roofer who does electrical work, not a
call centre.

**House rules for every solar page and facts file (legal, honest, current):** no promises about what a homeowner will get
back on a bill, no "$0 down", no tax incentive amounts or names, no "free panels", no lease or loan talk. Any number about a
system is a sample range and says "your real numbers come from a site visit". Production estimates are ranges with their
source named, never one exact figure.

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 40-70, owner-occupied single-family homes, many retired or close to it, many with a high summer
bill (cooling) or a plan to buy an electric car. Common situations:
- A door-to-door seller came by, and they want a second opinion from a local company.
- They are re-roofing soon and want to know if panels should go on now or after.
- The neighbour's system was installed and they wonder if their roof works.
- They already have panels: the inverter shows a red light, the app stopped reporting, pigeons nest under the array.
- They want a battery because of outages, a well pump or medical equipment.

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
1. "It's a pushy sales pitch and they'll lock me into something."
2. "The numbers they showed me were made up."
3. "They'll put holes in my roof and it will leak."
4. "The company will be gone in two years and nobody will service it."
5. "I don't understand what I'm signing."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A roof check before any panel talk: age, material, shade, structure, the electrical panel.
2. Ranges with sources and the words "sample" and "site visit", no single magic number.
3. A crew that does the roofing details (flashing, sealing) and the electrical work itself.
4. A plain list of what happens after install: inspection, utility approval, monitoring, service.
5. Local knowledge: the trees (Sacramento), the heat and dust (Tucson), the parapets and wind (Albuquerque).

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A countdown timer or "limited time" banner; a bill-cut promise with a big percentage;
a pop-up asking for the electric bill before anything is explained; stock photos of a family pointing at the sky; a site
that is just a lead form.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Nearly 100% planned: consultations, installs, batteries, service calls. A dead inverter or a roof
leak at a penetration is urgent to the owner but booked as this-week service. `emergency: false` on all three sites.

**Seasonality.**
| Month | Sacramento | Tucson | Albuquerque |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Rainy season: leak checks at old arrays, planning calls | Mild, best install weather, busy | Cold mornings, planning, roof checks |
| Mar-Apr | Pollen and dust on panels, cleaning, installs ramp | Busy installs before the heat | Windy spring: racking and critter-guard checks |
| May-Jun | Installs, battery interest before fire season | Heat arrives, crews start at dawn | Installs, longest days |
| Jul-Sep | Heat, smoke days, cleaning, inverter heat faults | Monsoon storms, dust, early starts only | Monsoon storms, hail checks |
| Oct-Nov | Outage season talk, batteries | Installs pick up again | Installs, pre-winter service |
| Dec | Slow, roof-before-solar planning | Mild, busy | Snow on parapets, slow |

**Ticket bands (words).** Cleaning and monitoring checks are low hundreds. Critter guard is high hundreds to low thousands.
An inverter is low thousands. Detach and reset for a re-roof is low-to-mid thousands. A battery is low tens of thousands. A
new system is mid tens of thousands depending on size and roof.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Solar panel install, roof assessment, battery storage, inverter service, monitoring
setup, panel cleaning, critter guard, detach and reset for re-roofs, repairs, system upgrades, consultation and second
opinions.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "How many panels do I need" | System size in kW, from annual kWh use |
| "The box on the wall" | String inverter |
| "The little boxes under the panels" | Microinverters / optimizers (MLPE) |
| "Red light on the inverter" | Ground fault or arc fault, inverter error code |
| "The app stopped working" | Monitoring offline, gateway or Wi-Fi lost |
| "Birds under the panels" | Critter guard / pest mesh around the array |
| "Taking panels off for a new roof" | Detach and reset (R&R) |
| "When can I turn it on" | Permission to operate (PTO) from the utility |
| "City paperwork" | Permit, plan review, inspection |
| "Shade from the tree" | Shade analysis, solar access, production loss |
| "Holes in the roof" | Roof attachments, flashing, penetrations |
| "Flat roof" | Low-slope roof, ballasted or attached racking, parapet setbacks |
| "Power when the grid is down" | Battery backup, critical loads panel, islanding |
| "The fuse box" | Main service panel, busbar rating, panel upgrade |
| "Selling power back" | Export, net billing, interconnection |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A huge bill-cut percentage and a countdown timer.
2. "Go solar for $0" style headlines and incentive names in every banner.
3. A family on a lawn pointing at a roof, or a sun-flare stock photo.
4. A form that asks for the electric bill before anything else.
5. Panel and inverter brand logos as the trust strip.
6. Glossy aerial renders of an array that was never installed.
7. Nothing about service, monitoring or what happens after the crew leaves.
8. Leaf and planet icons with vague environmental slogans.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Roof-fit panel count range.** Inputs: usable roof face (sq ft) or roof faces from a short picker, shade level. Output: a
   panel count range (about 18-21 sq ft per panel, ~400 W each, minus setbacks) and a kW range. Sample only; "your real
   numbers come from a site visit".
2. **Roof-ready quiz.** Inputs: roof age, material (comp shingle, tile, flat/foam, metal), shade, electrical panel size, plans
   to re-roof. Output: "ready", "check first" or "roof first", with the reason. Leads to the roof assessment booking.
3. **Questions to ask any solar seller.** A checklist the homeowner can print or keep open during a pitch: who installs, who
   services, what the inverter is, what the production estimate is based on, what happens at a re-roof. Ticking items
   reveals why each one matters.
4. **Inverter light reader.** Inputs: light colour and blink (green solid, green blink, red, orange, none) and time of day.
   Output: what it usually means and whether it can wait. Hedged: models differ; we check the code on site.
5. **12-month use to size range.** Inputs: annual kWh from the bill (or 12 monthly numbers). Output: a kW range using a
   local production band (e.g. about 1,500-1,800 kWh per kW per year in Sacramento, 1,700-1,950 in Tucson and Albuquerque),
   with that source line shown under the result.
6. **Parapet shadow calculator.** Inputs: parapet height. Output: the noon shadow length on Dec 21, Mar 20 and Jun 21 for
   Albuquerque (sun altitude about 31.5°, 54.9° and 78.3°: shadow about 1.63x, 0.70x and 0.21x the height). Shows where
   panels can sit.
7. **Battery backup planner.** Inputs: what must run in an outage (fridge, swamp cooler, well pump, CPAP, lights, internet)
   and for how many hours. Output: a kWh range and whether one or two batteries are likely, sample ranges from facts.
8. **Re-roof timeline.** Inputs: roof age and planned re-roof year. Output: install now / detach and reset later vs roof
   first, with sample ranges.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "Roof first, panels second."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Rafter Line Solar · Sacramento, CA · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: In a city of trees and old comp roofs, the job starts on the rafters, not in a spreadsheet. The page is the
  install in order: roof check, shade walk, layout, permit, attachments and flashing, wiring, inspection, utility approval,
  monitoring. Each step shows what the homeowner sees that day.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (warm off-white, graphite text, one sun-yellow accent used sparingly, cool roof grey panels).
- Type lead: **condensed** (a tall condensed sans for display and step numbers, a readable sans for body).
- Hero family: **11 — diagonal split.** The hero splits on a roof-pitch diagonal (a 5/12 slope): photo of a comp roof with
  an array above the line, headline and the roof-ready quiz entry below it.
- Signature seed: **Roof pitch and racking rails.** Every section edge is cut at the same roof pitch, alternating direction
  like a roof ridge, and a faint grid of racking rails and attachment points runs behind the content; each step of the
  process "bolts" one attachment point on the rail as it enters view.
- Motion idea: Intro: the diagonal draws from eave to ridge and the rail grid fades in behind. Scroll story: each process step
  drops a small attachment mark onto the rail line, and the step counter climbs 01-09; reduced motion shows all marks.
- Tools: Roof-ready quiz, Roof-fit panel count range + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: soft morning light, Sacramento comp-shingle roofs with tree canopy, close detail of flashing, rails, clamps,
  crew from behind on the roof, harness and ladder; mid distance, calm.

### B — "Every number has a source."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Plain Watt Solar · Tucson, AZ · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: The Tucson buyer has heard every pitch and trusts nothing without a footnote. The page treats each claim like
  a study: a number, a footnote marker, and a ledger at the bottom of each section that says where the number comes from
  (the homeowner's bill, a site measurement, a published production band, a sample price). The tools give answers before
  the call.
- Tone lead: **dark-led** (night desert charcoal, warm white type, a copper-orange accent, a muted sky blue for data).
- Type lead: **grotesk** (a sturdy neo-grotesk for headlines and body, tabular figures in every number).
- Hero family: **2 — 50/50 split.** Left half: the headline and the "12-month use to size range" tool. Right half: a photo of
  a low-slope roof array at dusk with a footnote marker on it.
- Signature seed: **Footnotes and the source ledger.** Every number on the page carries a small superscript marker; hovering
  or tapping it highlights the matching line in a "Where this number comes from" ledger that closes every section. The
  ledger is the same component everywhere, so the page reads like a well-sourced report.
- Motion idea: Intro: the split opens from the centre line and the tool's first result counts up beside its footnote.
  Scroll story: as each section ends, its ledger lines slide up one by one and the markers above flash once to connect.
- Tools: Questions to ask any solar seller, Inverter light reader, 12-month use to size range + ZIP checker + multi-step
  quote form.
- Photo mood: dusk and night desert light, dark roofs and tile, close-ups of inverters, meters, monitoring screens and
  conduit; precise, documentary, never sunny stock.

### C — "Built for flat roofs and high sun."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Parapet Solar Co. · Albuquerque, NM · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Albuquerque houses wear their roofs hidden behind parapets, with canales for the rare rain. At about 5,300 ft
  the sun is strong and the spring wind is stronger. The page follows the sun across one flat roof from east to west and
  tells the story of how a crew lays out panels around parapet shadows, roof drains and the wind.
- Tone lead: **color-led** (adobe clay field, warm plaster cream, deep turquoise accent, sun-gold for the disc, dark umber text).
- Type lead: **slab** (a sturdy slab serif for headlines and numbers, a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: **13 — shape-masked photo.** A large circle (the sun disc) masks a photo of a flat-roof array behind a parapet
  against a deep sky; the headline sits beside it on the clay field.
- Signature seed: **Stepped parapets and the sun's path.** Every section is framed by a stepped parapet edge at its top, and a
  small sun disc travels across the page from the east (left) to the west (right) as the reader scrolls, casting a short
  shadow off each parapet frame that matches the time of day. No Zia symbol or other cultural marks.
- Motion idea: Intro: the sun disc rises into the mask and the photo brightens with it. Scroll story: the sun moves east to
  west across the section frames and the parapet shadows shorten and lengthen with it; reduced motion keeps a fixed noon sun.
- Tools: Parapet shadow calculator (Dec 21 ≈31.5°, Mar 20 ≈54.9°, Jun 21 ≈78.3°), Battery backup planner (swamp cooler, well
  pump) + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: strong high-desert light, deep blue skies, flat roofs with parapets and canales, stucco, Sandia mountains at
  sunset, wind-bent grasses; bold, warm, graphic.
