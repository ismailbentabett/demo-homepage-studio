# Junk Removal — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Mostly homeowners 35-75, plus adult children handling a parent's house, landlords between tenants and
people a week from a move. Three households cover most calls:
- **The downsizer or the family after a death or a move to assisted living**: a whole house of 30-50 years of things,
  a deadline from the realtor or the lease, siblings in other states, feelings attached to every drawer.
- **The garage that won**: a two- or three-car garage nobody has parked in for years, a broken treadmill, a dead
  freezer, paint cans, a sofa "for the den" that never moved in.
- **The one big thing**: a hot tub that stopped working, a mattress after a new one arrived, a fridge the store won't
  take, a storm-soaked carpet and drywall pile at the curb, a shed falling apart.

**What just happened.** The closing date is set. The HOA sent a letter about the pile by the garage. The city's bulk
pickup said no (too much, wrong item, wrong week). A tenant left and took nothing. The new mattress came and the old
one is leaning in the hallway. A storm put a foot of water in the garage.

**What they fear (their words).**
1. "They'll quote one price on the phone and a different one once it's on the truck."
2. "They'll just dump everything, even the good stuff Mom kept for the grandkids."
3. "Two strangers in my house carrying furniture down the stairs, scraping the walls and the floors."
4. "They'll take something I meant to keep." (the box of photos in the closet, the tools Dad wanted the grandson to have)
5. "They won't show up when they said, and I took the day off."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A price range on the page, by how much of the truck the load fills, before anyone comes over.
2. A plain answer to "what happens to my stuff": what gets donated, what gets recycled, what goes to the landfill.
3. A walk-through where they point and the crew carries: "you don't lift anything".
4. A keep pile that is respected: tape, a sticker, a room the crew does not touch.
5. Photos of a swept, empty garage floor, not a truck with a logo.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A cartoon truck mascot, a price that says "call for quote" and nothing else,
a form that asks for everything before saying anything, a site that looks like a national franchise, the phone number
hidden at the bottom.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Almost all planned, but on a short clock: 70% booked within a week of the first call (a
closing, a lease end, an HOA letter), 25% planned weeks ahead (estate cleanouts, downsizing), 5% storm aftermath
(soaked carpet, drywall, fallen fence, after a hurricane or a flood). No middle-of-the-night calls.

**Seasonality by region.**
- **Daytona Beach, FL (A):** Jan-Apr busy with snowbird condos and seasonal homes being cleared or sold; May-Jun moves
  and estate work; Aug-Nov hurricane season, storm-debris spikes after a named storm (soaked carpet, drywall, fences,
  lanai screens, patio furniture); Dec slow except before the holidays.
- **Katy, TX (B):** Mar-May spring garage cleanouts and moves; Jun-Aug relocation season (families moving in and out of
  the west Houston suburbs before school), garages at 110°F so mornings fill first; Sep-Oct storm and flood debris after
  Gulf storms; Nov-Dec "before the in-laws come" cleanouts.
- **Irvine, CA (C):** Jun the big one (rental and student-housing turnover around the university, lease ends); spring
  downsizing and estate work all year in the older villages; Oct-Nov before the holidays; very little weather impact
  except the dry Santa Ana weeks, when people clear brush piles and old yard waste.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: one item or a few (a mattress, a fridge, a recliner). Medium: a quarter to a half of
a truck (a garage corner, a bedroom, a deck's worth of patio furniture). Large: a full truck (a whole garage, a
basement). Biggest: whole-house estate cleanouts and hot tub tear-outs, often more than one load or more than one day.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Junk removal by the load, furniture removal, appliance removal, garage cleanouts,
estate cleanouts, attic and closet (or basement) cleanouts, yard waste, hot tub removal, construction and storm debris,
mattress removal, donation drop-off, short-notice hauling.

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A cartoon truck or a grinning mascot holding a couch over his head.
2. A franchise look: lime-green or orange trucks, "We haul it all!" in a bubble font, a phone in a starburst.
3. "Call for a free estimate" as the only answer to price.
4. A truck photo with a logo as the hero, in a parking lot.
5. "Eco-friendly" leaf icons with no explanation of what actually gets donated or recycled.
6. A wall of stock before/after photos of two unrelated garages.
7. A list of 40 items "we take" in tiny grey type with no "we don't take" list at all.
8. Pop-up coupons on load ("$20 off!") before the page has even painted.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Load sizer by item.** Inputs: tap pictured items (sofa, mattress, fridge, boxes x10, treadmill, patio set, bags of
   yard waste). Output: the share of the truck in words ("about a quarter of the truck") and the matching sample range
   from `facts.proof.prices`. Leads to: "Book that slot" with the list prefilled.
2. **"Will you take it?" answer box.** Input: type an item (paint, tires, propane tank, piano, TV, fridge, mattress,
   hot tub). Output: yes / yes with a note (refrigerant is recovered, a piano needs two more hands) / no, and where to
   take the no items instead (county household hazardous waste drop-off, in general words). Leads to: "Add it to your
   list".
3. **Where it goes sorter.** Input: the item list (from the sizer). Output: three columns: likely donate, likely
   recycle, landfill, with plain reasons ("a sofa with no tears and no pet hair is usually accepted by donation
   centres"). No percentages promised. Leads to: the donation note in the form.
4. **Keep-pile planner.** A tickable list of what families often want set aside (photos, papers, tools, holiday boxes,
   jewelry found in drawers). Output: a short walk-through sheet ("blue tape = stays, our crew checks drawers and
   pockets before anything goes"). Leads to: estate cleanout booking.
5. **Hot tub check.** Inputs: tub size (2-4, 5-6, 7+ seats), on a deck or a slab, power disconnected yes/no, access
   width (gate, side yard). Output: whole or cut up, how long, what the homeowner must do first (an electrician
   disconnects the circuit), sample range. Leads to: a call.
6. **Arrival window picker.** Input: day and morning/afternoon. Output: a two-hour window and "we text 30 minutes
   before". Demo only; leads to the form.
7. **Storm pile check (A only).** Inputs: what got wet (carpet, drywall, furniture, fence, screens), roughly how much.
   Output: what can sit at the county curb pickup and what is quicker by truck, in plain words.
8. **ZIP checker** (every site): yes / "a little outside our usual area: call and we'll tell you honestly".

## 6. The three directions

  what the homeowner sees: point, price, lift, sort, load, sweep.

  for steps and links, near-black type).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed grotesk for the step numbers and headlines, like the stencilled bed of a

- Signature seed: **the clearing floor plan.** A top-down drawing of the garage sits in the margin, with every item as
