# Masonry — niche brief

Three demo homepages for residential masonry: repointing and tuckpointing old brick, chimney repair and rebuilds, brick and stone replacement, steps and stoops, retaining and garden walls. The work is on older brick houses and rowhouses in river cities. All three are sample sites: fictional brands, real cities and ZIPs, 555-01NN phones, `.example` email, proof marked "Sample".

## 1. The buyer

- **Who calls:**
  - Owners of older brick houses (1850s-1940s) aged 35-75: rowhouses, Italianates, foursquares, bungalows.
  - New buyers whose home inspection flagged the chimney or mortar.
  - Owners who see mortar sand on the steps, a leaning chimney, a crumbling stoop, or water in the basement wall.
  - People who care about the old house and want it done right.
- **What just happened:**
  - The home inspector wrote "repoint chimney, deteriorated mortar".
  - Bricks are spalling (faces popping off) after winter.
  - Mortar joints are sandy and recessed.
  - The chimney crown is cracked and there is a water stain on the ceiling.
  - The front steps are loose.
  - A previous "handyman" used hard gray cement, and now the bricks around it are failing.
  - A retaining wall on the hillside is bulging.
- **Fears, in their words:**
  - "They'll use the wrong mortar and ruin the brick."
  - "The patch will be a different color and look terrible."
  - "Do I need to rebuild the whole chimney or can it be repaired?"
  - "This old house will turn into a money pit."
  - "They'll power-wash or sandblast the brick."
- **What makes them trust:**
  - They understand soft brick and lime mortar.
  - Mortar matching shown with samples.
  - A clear repoint-or-rebuild answer.
  - Sample prices per sq ft and per job from facts.
  - Photos of real joints, close up.
  - A two-year mortar guarantee (from facts).
  - Local knowledge of their neighborhood's brick.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:**
  - Stock photos of new-build stone veneer McMansions.
  - "Concrete & masonry" sites that mostly pour driveways.
  - Power-washing photos.
  - Clip-art brick logos.
  - Gray cement patches shown as "after".
  - No sign they know old houses.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned:**
- About 15% urgent: a falling chimney top, loose bricks over a door, a stoop that moved, water in the wall after a storm.
- About 85% planned, often from an inspection report or a sale.

**Seasonality:**
- Mortar work needs air above about 40°F and no hard freeze while it cures, so the season runs March or April to November in all three cities.
- Winters of freeze-thaw cycles create the spring work: spalled bricks and washed-out joints.
- Fall is busy with chimney work before heating season.
- **Cincinnati:** hillside retaining walls slip after spring rain.
- **Baltimore:** rowhouse fronts and marble steps.
- **St. Louis:** parapets and cornices on the old brick flats.

**Average ticket (words, not dollars):**
- Small: a brick replacement or a chimney crown.
- Mid: a stoop rebuild, or front repointing per square foot.
- Large: a chimney rebuild above the roofline, a parapet rebuild, or a retaining wall.
- Exact sample ranges are in each facts file's `proof.prices`.

**Services that sell:** repointing / tuckpointing, chimney repair, chimney crowns, chimney rebuilds, brick replacement, lintels and arches, parapets and cornices, steps and stoops, retaining and garden walls, mortar matching, brick cleaning (gentle), paint removal from brick, formstone repair (Baltimore).

**Homeowner word vs. pro word:**

| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| fill in the cracks between bricks | repointing (Cincinnati, Baltimore) / tuckpointing (St. Louis) |
| the mortar is turning to sand | deteriorated / friable mortar joints |
| the brick face is popping off | spalling (often from hard cement mortar or water) |
| old-fashioned mortar | lime mortar (NHL or lime putty), softer than the brick |
| new gray cement | Portland cement mortar: too hard for soft historic brick |
| make the mortar match | mortar match: sand, color, joint profile |
| the way the joint looks | joint profile: concave, struck, raked, flush, tooled |
| the top of the chimney | chimney crown (and the cap / flue liner above it) |
| the chimney is leaning | chimney rebuild above the roofline |
| the beam over the window | lintel (steel or stone) / jack arch |
| the low wall on the roof | parapet (with coping on top) |
| the fake stone on the front (Baltimore) | formstone |
| the front steps | stoop / marble steps (Baltimore) |
| clean the brick | gentle cleaning: low pressure, mild cleaner, never sandblasting |

## 5. Niche tools

1. **Mortar softness check**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether their mortar is soft lime, hard cement, or failing.
   - Inputs:
     - house age;
     - mortar color (white-buff / gray);
     - does a key scratch the joint easily;
     - are the brick faces spalling next to gray patches.
   - Output: the likely mortar, why hard cement on soft brick causes spalling, and the repointing sample range.
   - Leads to the call: "Send us a close-up photo."
2. **Repoint-or-rebuild (chimney)**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether the chimney needs repointing, a new crown, or a rebuild.
   - Inputs:
     - is it leaning;
     - are bricks loose or missing at the top;
     - is the crown cracked;
     - is there a ceiling stain.
   - Output: the likely job and its sample range from facts (crown / repair / rebuild).
   - Leads to the call: "Get it looked at before winter."
3. **Mortar match**
   - What the homeowner learns: what makes a mortar match (sand, color, joint profile).
   - Inputs: pick the brick color (6 swatches from the brand palette, no images needed), pick a mortar color, pick a joint profile.
   - Output: a CSS-drawn brick panel with the chosen bond, mortar color and joint profile, plus the line "we make three test samples on your wall before we start".
   - Leads to the call: "Book mortar samples."
4. **Formstone keep, fix or remove** (Baltimore)
   - What the homeowner learns: what to do about formstone on a rowhouse front.
   - Inputs: is it cracked, is it loose or hollow-sounding, do you know what's under it, are you selling.
   - Output: keep / patch / remove-and-restore, what removal can reveal (and the risk of damaged brick under it), and sample ranges.
   - Leads to the call: "Book a facade visit."
5. **Facade-width repointing estimator**
   - What the homeowner learns: a sample range for repointing their front.
   - Inputs: facade width (rowhouse 12-20 ft), stories (2-3), share of joints that need it (some / most / all).
   - Output: an approximate sq ft and the sample range from facts per sq ft, labelled sample, with the note that a visit measures it.
   - Leads to the call: "Get the real number."
6. **Find your neighborhood's brick** (St. Louis)
   - What the homeowner learns: what their house is likely built from and what that means for repair.
   - Inputs: pick a neighborhood on the map (Soulard, Lafayette Square, Tower Grove, The Hill, Benton Park, Shaw, Dutchtown, Central West End).
   - Output: the typical brick (soft red, salmon, hard-fired face brick), its age, and what to watch for.
   - Leads to the call: "Get your brick checked."
7. **Tuckpoint timing**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether it is the right time of year to repoint.
   - Inputs: the month.
   - Output: the season window (no work below about 40°F or in a freeze), and the next bookable month.
   - Leads to the call: "Get on the spring list."
8. **ZIP checker** (all three sites)
   - Input: a 5-digit ZIP.
   - Output: served or not, from `zips`.
9. **Multi-step quote form** (all three sites)
   - Steps:
     1. what needs work (repointing, chimney, bricks, steps, wall, other);
     2. the house age;
     3. photos;
     4. timing (inspection deadline, sale);
     5. contact.

## 6. The three directions

### A — The joint, done the old way   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)

- Brand: Soft Joint Masonry · Cincinnati, OH · facts-A.json
- Concept seed:
  - The name is the philosophy: the mortar joint must be softer than the brick, so the joint fails first and the brick lasts.
  - The page walks the repointing in order:
    1. rake out the old mortar by hand to the right depth;
    2. mix lime mortar matched to the old;
    3. pack it in layers;
    4. strike it;
    5. tool the joint profile;
    6. brush.
  - Cincinnati angle: Over-the-Rhine Italianates and Mount Adams rowhouses of soft hand-made brick, limestone foundations, and hillside retaining walls in Price Hill and Clifton.
- Tone lead: light-led. Lime-mortar cream and soft limestone gray, with a warm soft-brick red as the one accent and dark iron type.
- Type lead: grotesk. A precise, slightly industrial grotesk for headings (like 19th-century trade lettering redrawn), with a humanist body.
- Hero family: 5 offset framed photo. A close photo of hands tooling a fresh lime joint sits in a frame offset from the grid, overlapping a cream panel with the headline. The frame's border is drawn like a mortar line.
- Signature seed:
  - The mortar joint is the section divider. Each divider is a horizontal joint between two drawn brick courses, and its profile changes through the page in the order of the work: raked out → packed → struck → tooled.
  - The guarantee section ends on a finished concave joint.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the framed photo unclips from the joint line outward, and the headline rises.
  - Scroll story: a large drawn cross-section of one brick joint is pinned. As you scroll it is raked out, packed in two layers, struck and tooled, each stage labelled with why it matters. With reduced motion, four static diagrams in a row.
- Tools: mortar softness check; repoint-or-rebuild (chimney); tuckpoint timing; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: soft overcast light on old soft red brick, lime mortar textures, hands with a pointing trowel and a jointer, a mortar board, limestone foundations, hillside streets of old brick houses. Warm, tactile, close.

### B — Matched, course by course   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)

- Brand: Flemish Bond Masonry · Baltimore, MD · facts-B.json
- Concept seed:
  - Proof is in the match: the mortar analysis, the test panels on the wall, before-and-after joint close-ups, and sample prices per sq ft.
  - The tools answer the Baltimore questions before the call: what do I do about formstone, can the marble steps be reset, what does repointing my front cost.
  - Baltimore angle: rowhouse blocks in Fells Point, Canton, Federal Hill, Hampden and Charles Village. Flemish bond fronts (headers and stretchers), marble steps, formstone, party walls and parapets.
- Tone lead: dark-led. A deep soot-charcoal ground with Baltimore red brick as the main accent, marble white type and one thin brass line.
- Type lead: condensed. A condensed grotesk for headlines, tall and rhythmic like a row of rowhouse fronts; plain body.
- Hero family: 8 photo mosaic. A mosaic of 6-7 tiles laid out like a Flemish bond:
  - long stretcher tiles: a rowhouse front, a repointed facade;
  - short header tiles: a joint close-up, a marble step, a mortar sample, a formstone edge.
  - The headline sits in one stretcher-sized dark tile.
- Signature seed:
  - The Flemish-bond grid is the layout system. Cards, stats and proof tiles are header-size or stretcher-size and alternate in each row, with mortar-colored gutters.
  - Numbers sit in header tiles: sample prices, years in business from facts, review count.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the mosaic tiles drop in course by course from the bottom row up (images unclip in sequence), like a wall being laid.
  - Scroll story: a rowhouse front elevation is pinned. As you scroll, the problem areas light in turn (parapet, lintels, front joints, marble steps, formstone ground floor), each with its fix and sample price. With reduced motion, a labelled elevation.
- Tools: mortar match; formstone keep, fix or remove; facade-width repointing estimator; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low warm light on red brick rowhouse fronts, white marble steps, test mortar panels, joint close-ups, formstone textures, alley walls. Rich, warm, urban. No people in frame.

### C — A city built of its own clay   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)

- Brand: Kilnside Masonry · St. Louis, MO · facts-C.json
- Concept seed:
  - After the Great Fire of 1849, St. Louis built in brick, from its own clay and its own brickyards.
  - Each neighborhood has its brick: Soulard and Lafayette Square soft reds, the Hill's brick bungalows, Tower Grove and Shaw flats with ornate cornices, Dutchtown's four-families, and the soft salmon brick of the inner layers.
  - The company is named for the old kiln yards and knows each brick.
  - Tuckpointing is the St. Louis word, so the site uses it.
- Tone lead: color-led. A deep kiln-fired brick red as the main field, with clay-buff and soot-black, and limestone cream type.
- Type lead: slab. A heavy slab serif for headings, like the stamped lettering on old brick and brickyard signs; plain sans body.
- Hero family: 15 map / area-led. The hero is a stylized map of St. Louis neighborhoods (simplified shapes, not a real tile map), each filled with its typical brick color. The headline sits over the river edge, and tapping a neighborhood shows its brick and a photo.
- Signature seed:
  - Maker's-mark bricks. Each section header is a brick face stamped with a small mark (like an old brickyard frog stamp) carrying the section number and name.
  - The neighborhood brick colors are the page's palette system.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the neighborhoods on the map fill with their brick colors one by one, from the river westward (a short sequence of fills, no bounce), and the headline rises.
  - Scroll story: a single brick is pinned and turns slowly (a CSS 2D flip of faces, no 3D library) through its life: clay, kiln, laid in 1890, a hard cement patch in 1970, spalling, tuckpointed with soft mortar now. With reduced motion, a timeline of six static frames.
- Tools: find your neighborhood's brick; tuckpoint timing; repoint-or-rebuild (chimney); ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: late afternoon sun on red brick flats and cornices, parapets against sky, soft salmon brick inside a wall, clay textures, brick alleys and gangways. Warm, proud, city-specific.
