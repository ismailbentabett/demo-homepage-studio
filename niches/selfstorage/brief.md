# Self Storage — niche brief

A self storage site is not a contractor site: nobody comes to the house. The visitor rents a room by the month
and drives to it. So "service area" means the towns people drive in from, "hours" means two clocks (the office and
the gate), and the "job" is a move-in, a season, or a few years of keeping things dry. All three businesses are on
Florida coasts (Treasure Coast, Palm Beach, Pinellas), where humidity, hurricane season, boats and snowbirds decide
what people store and why.

## 1. The buyer

**Who.** Four kinds of people, roughly in this order:
- **People between homes** (28-50): sold the house in Port St. Lucie before the new build in Tradition is done,
  a lease ending before the next one starts, a military or job move. Need a 10×10 or 10×20 for 2-6 months.
- **Downsizers and families of an older parent** (50-75): moving from a 3-bedroom ranch to a condo or to assisted
  living; the furniture is too good to sell and too big for the new place. Often climate-controlled, often long stays.
- **Snowbirds and seasonal owners** (60-80): leave for the north in April or May, need the car, the golf cart,
  the patio furniture and the hurricane shutters kept somewhere for the summer.
- **Boat, RV and trailer owners** whose HOA does not allow them in the driveway (very common in Port St. Lucie,
  Wellington and Palm Harbor deed rules). Need an outdoor or covered space by the month.
- Smaller groups: a contractor or a realtor keeping stock and staging furniture, a small online seller, a remodel
  (kitchen gutted, the dining set has to go somewhere for 6 weeks).

**What just happened.** A closing date moved. The HOA sent a letter about the boat. A hurricane watch was issued and
the garage is full. Mom is moving into a smaller place on the 1st. The renovation crew starts Monday.

**Fears, in their words.**
- "Will my stuff get mildew in there? It's Florida." (the first question for anything wooden, upholstered, paper or leather)
- "I don't know what size I need, and I don't want to pay for a unit twice as big."
- "The price online will double after two months." (the bait rate they have heard about)
- "Can I get in when I need to? I work until six." (gate hours vs office hours)
- "Is it safe? Who else can get into my unit?" / "What happens if a storm floods the place?"

**What makes them trust.**
- A real monthly price by size, with what is included, before they call.
- A clear answer on climate control: what it holds the air to, not just the word.
- Seeing the facility: the gate, the aisle, the inside of a unit, how the door opens, where the cart is.
- A person on site with a name, and a phone that gets answered during office hours.
- Plain lease terms: month-to-month, how to move out, how rent changes (said honestly).

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** No prices ("call for rates"), a "reserve" button that leads nowhere, only a
drone shot of a roof, a size chart in square feet with no picture of what fits, gate hours hidden in the footer,
a page that looks like a national chain's template.

## 2. The job

**Planned vs urgent.** Mostly planned within days: 60% book within a week of the need, 25% the same week as a
closing or move, 15% are planned months ahead (snowbirds, boat owners, seasonal businesses). The urgent case is a
hurricane watch (people need room for patio furniture, a golf cart, a second car) and a closing that slips.

**Seasonality on Florida's coasts (both Atlantic and Gulf).**
- Jan-Mar: snowbird season at its height; condo renovations before the owners leave; boat storage turns over.
- Apr-May: snowbirds leave; cars, golf carts and furniture go into storage; the busiest move-in weeks of the year.
- Jun-Jul: family moves (school is out), military moves, summer heat; climate units fill.
- Aug-Oct: peak hurricane months; people clear garages for shutters and generators; some boats come out of the water.
- Nov-Dec: snowbirds return and take things out; holiday stock for small sellers; quieter for new rentals.

**Ticket bands (in words).** A locker or a 5×5 is a small monthly line; a climate-controlled 10×10 is the core
rental; a 10×20 drive-up or a covered RV space is the big monthly ticket; long stays (a year or more) are the value
of the business. Extras: a lock, boxes and tape, a protection plan.

**The services that sell** (from `data/demo-trades.json`): climate-controlled units, drive-up units, small lockers,
vehicle and RV parking, boat storage, business storage, gated keypad access, video monitoring, online rentals,
moving supplies, truck rental help, month-to-month leases.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | The facility says |
|---|---|
| "a storage unit" | a unit, a space |
| "the air-conditioned ones" | climate-controlled, humidity controlled |
| "one I can back the truck up to" | drive-up access, exterior unit |
| "the gate code" | keypad access, access code, gate hours |
| "how big is a 10 by 10, really" | 100 square feet, about a one-bedroom apartment's furniture |
| "a little closet one" | a locker, a 5×5 |
| "somewhere for the boat" | boat and RV parking, covered or open spaces |
| "do I have to sign a year?" | month-to-month lease, rental agreement |
| "what if I'm late paying" | late fee, lien process (said plainly) |
| "insurance for my stuff" | tenant protection plan, storage insurance |
| "can I hold one" | reservation, move-in date |
| "the price goes up later" | rate change, street rate vs move-in rate |
| "the cameras" | video monitoring, recorded cameras |
| "the cart and the dolly" | carts and hand trucks at the loading area |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A drone shot of a beige roof and orange doors with the logo painted on the building.
2. "Call for rates" or a price that only appears after an email address.
3. A size guide as a grid of identical square-foot numbers with clip-art boxes.
4. A national-chain template with a "Reserve now" button that opens a third-party booking widget.
5. "Clean, safe, secure" as the whole pitch, three icons in a row.
6. Stock photo of a smiling couple holding a moving box in front of an empty orange door.
7. Gate hours and office hours mixed into one line, or missing.
8. A coupon banner ("First month $1!") on top of everything.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **What fits sizer.** Inputs: what's going in (rooms: studio, 1-3 bedrooms, a garage; or items: sofa, mattress,
   boxes, a golf cart, patio set). Output: the recommended size (locker, 5×5, 5×10, 10×10, 10×15, 10×20) drawn as a
   top-down floor plan with the items placed inside, and the sample monthly range from `facts.proof.prices`.
   Leads to: "Hold this size" with the answers prefilled.
2. **Climate or drive-up chooser.** Five yes/no questions (wood furniture? papers or photos? electronics or
   mattresses? longer than 3 months? loading by yourself with a truck?). Output: "climate-controlled" or "drive-up
   is fine", with the plain reason ("wood and fabric pick up mildew in a Florida summer without air").
   Leads to: the size step of the form.
3. **Two clocks.** The office hours and the gate hours shown side by side, live ("Office open until 6:00 · Gate open
   until 10:00"). Uses `biz.hours` for the office; the gate hours are stated in a service blurb. Leads to: call
   while the office is open, or hold online.
4. **Season planner.** Input: the month you leave and the month you return (snowbirds) or the hurricane-season
   checklist (shutters, generator, patio furniture, golf cart). Output: a dated plan and the size it needs.
   Leads to: "Hold from this date".
5. **Boat and RV fit check.** Inputs: length overall including the trailer tongue, height on the trailer, covered or
   open. Output: which space type fits, plus the sample monthly range. Leads to: "Hold a space".
6. **Move-in checklist.** The seven things to bring and do on move-in day (ID, lock, pallets for the floor, label
   boxes on two sides, heavy at the back, an aisle down the middle). Printable. Leads to: the move-in date step.
7. **Lease in plain words.** A short, honest explainer of month-to-month: how to move out, when rent is due, what
   happens if you're late. No numbers that are not in facts. Leads to: "Ask the manager" (call).
8. **ZIP checker** (every site): shows the drive time band from the visitor's ZIP ("about 10 minutes"), or "a bit far,
   but people do drive in from there for the boat spaces".

## 6. The three directions

### A — Rent it at the kitchen table, unload it by lunch   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Wide Aisle Storage · Port St. Lucie, FL · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: The page walks a move-in from the kitchen table to the locked door, one stop at a time: pick the
  size, hold it online, get the code, drive through the gate, back the truck up to the door, load heavy at the back,
  lock it. Port St. Lucie texture: families waiting on a new build in Tradition or St. Lucie West, a 1980s CBS
  ranch being sold in a hurry, the July heat at 11 a.m. that decides whether you want a drive-up door or the
  air-conditioned hallway.
- Tone lead: light-led (bright stucco white, pale concrete grey, black type, one roll-up-door blue for actions).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed grotesk for headlines and unit numbers, like stencilled door numbers; a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: 4 — stacked headline over a cinematic strip (big type on white, then a 21:9 strip that is one long
  aisle of roll-up doors; the strip sits in the first screen at 1440×900).
- Signature seed: **the unit door.** Every section is a numbered roll-up door in a row (A-101, A-102...): the
  section title is the number stencilled above the door, and the content is what is behind it. The door rolls up as
  the section enters; the move-in steps are doors along one aisle, and the final door is "Your unit", with the form
  behind it.
- Motion idea: Intro: the corrugated door in the hero rolls up in slats to reveal the strip of the aisle; the
  headline's unit number prints. Scroll story: a small truck icon drives down a drawn aisle as you scroll the seven
  move-in steps, stopping at each door and backing up to the last one.
- Tools: What fits sizer (as step 1 of the walk), move-in checklist, two clocks + ZIP checker + multi-step quote
  form ("size → climate or drive-up → move-in date → how to reach you").
- Photo mood: Bright midday Treasure Coast light, white stucco and pale concrete, hard short shadows, a corridor of
  roll-up doors, a hand truck with boxes, a pickup bed at a door. Mid-distance and clean; people only from behind.

### B — Your size and your month, measured before you drive over   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Dewpoint Self Storage · West Palm Beach, FL · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: West Palm Beach is humid eleven months a year, and the whole fear of storage here is mildew and
  surprise prices. The page is an instrument panel: the size sizer, the climate chooser and a humidity explainer
  give answers in numbers (square feet, cubic feet, monthly range, what the hallways are held at) before anyone
  calls. Context: condo owners on Flagler Drive renovating, small online sellers in Lake Worth Beach, horse-season
  tack and trailers out in Wellington.
- Tone lead: dark-led (night-blue panel, dew-cyan for readings and links, warm white text, one amber for the call).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a precise monospace for every reading, size, cubic foot and price; a clean grotesk for headlines and body).
- Hero family: 2 — 50/50 split (the left half is the headline with the size readout; the right half a cool-lit
  climate hallway photo; a drawn 10×10 floor outline crosses the seam, half on type, half on photo).
- Signature seed: **the reading.** Every section opens with a reading line in mono, like a gauge label
  ("SIZE 10×10 · 100 SQ FT · 800 CU FT", "HALL 68–78°F · RH ≤ 55%", "LEASE month-to-month"); the numbers tick into
  place, and the services, reviews and prices are all set as readings with a unit. The footer is the last reading:
  "OFFICE closed · GATE open until 22:00".
- Motion idea: Intro: the 10×10 outline draws itself, then fills with top-down item shapes from the default sizer
  answer, and the monthly range counts up. Scroll story: a humidity dial follows one wooden dresser through a year
  outside versus inside the climate hallway, the needle swinging with the months (said as a process, not a claim).
- Tools: What fits sizer (hero side), climate or drive-up chooser, lease in plain words + ZIP checker + multi-step
  quote form.
- Photo mood: Cool, even interior light; long climate-controlled hallways with metal doors, a hygrometer or
  thermostat close up, boxes on a pallet in a clean unit, a tape measure on a door frame. Graphic, cool, precise;
  no people or only hands.

### C — Clearwater's off-season lives here   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Second Season Storage · Clearwater, FL · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: In Pinellas half the storage story is the calendar: snowbirds leaving Clearwater Beach condos in
  April, boats out of the water before a storm, Dunedin and Palm Harbor HOAs that won't have a trailer in the
  driveway, beach-condo renovations in the slow months. The page is a season guide for the Gulf side: each "story"
  is a real kind of Pinellas renter (the snowbird's car and golf cart, the 22-foot center console, the shutters
  and generator in August, the Largo condo reno) and what they keep here and when.
- Tone lead: color-led (Gulf palette: warm sand, sea-glass teal, sunset coral, with deep navy text).
- Type lead: serif (a warm, characterful text serif with good italics for the cover and story headlines; a small-caps sans for months and labels).
- Hero family: 6 — editorial cover (a season-guide cover: masthead "Second Season", issue line "The off-season
  issue · Clearwater", a full-bleed Gulf-coast photo, cover lines that are real anchors: "The boat", "The car you
  leave behind", "Storm-season garage", "Condo reno, 6 weeks").
- Signature seed: **the tide calendar.** A thin calendar band of twelve months runs through the page like a tide
  line; each section is pinned to its months (Apr-May for snowbird departures, Aug-Oct for storm season), the
  current month is marked, and the band rises and falls like a tide chart. The footer closes on the month you
  come back.
- Motion idea: Intro: the cover photo settles from a slight zoom while the cover lines slide in one by one, like a
  magazine turned toward you. Scroll story: the tide calendar draws its curve as you scroll, and each story's
  months light up on the band when the story is on screen.
- Tools: Season planner (snowbird and storm season), boat and RV fit check, two clocks + ZIP checker + multi-step
  quote form.
- Photo mood: Gulf-coast late-afternoon light, warm and low: palms, boats on trailers under a covered shed, a quiet
  condo balcony with the chairs stacked, shutters stacked in a garage, Pinellas pastel houses. Wide and airy;
  people only from behind or not at all.
