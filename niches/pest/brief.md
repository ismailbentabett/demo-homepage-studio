# Pest & Termite — niche brief

Three demo homepages for a residential pest control and termite company in the coastal and Gulf South (termites first, then general pest, mosquitoes, rodents and fire ants). All three are sample sites: fictional brands, real cities and ZIPs, 555-01NN phones, `.example` email, proof marked "Sample".

**Rule for this niche:** no safety claims ("safe for kids and pets", "non-toxic", "harmless"). Say what is done instead: what we apply, where, how much, how long to keep people and pets off a treated area, and that the product label is followed and left with you.

## 1. The buyer

- **Who calls:**
  - Homeowners aged 30-75 in older raised houses, crawlspace houses and slab houses near the coast or the Gulf.
  - Buyers and sellers who need a termite letter (wood-destroying insect report) for closing.
  - Owners whose termite contract is up for renewal.
  - Families plagued by mosquitoes, fire ants in the yard, roaches, or rats in the attic.
- **What just happened:**
  - Winged insects swarmed out of a window frame or around the porch light on a warm May evening.
  - They found mud tubes on the foundation.
  - A soft spot in a door frame or baseboard.
  - The lender needs a termite letter by Friday.
  - Scratching in the attic at night.
  - The kids can't use the yard because of mosquitoes or fire ant mounds.
- **Fears, in their words:**
  - "Is the house being eaten right now? How bad is it?"
  - "They'll scare me into a $3,000 treatment I don't need."
  - "What are they spraying around my kids and my dog?"
  - "Contracts with fine print that never pay out."
  - "Flying ants or termites? I can't tell."
- **What makes them trust:**
  - An inspection report with what was seen and where.
  - A plain explanation of liquid vs bait.
  - Sample prices from facts.
  - What the retreat guarantee covers.
  - A process that names what is applied and where, and that the label is left with them.
  - An honest "these are flying ants, not termites".
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:**
  - Huge macro photos of bugs.
  - A cartoon exterminator mascot.
  - Red "DANGER" banners and fear copy.
  - "Kid and pet safe!" claims.
  - A coupon pop-up on load.
  - A commercial and restaurant pest page.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned:**
- About 30% urgent: a swarm, a closing letter, rats in the attic, a wasp nest.
- About 70% planned: quarterly service, termite contracts and renewals, mosquito season plans.

**Seasonality:**
- **Wilmington, NC:**
  - Eastern subterranean termites swarm in spring (March-May, on warm days after rain).
  - Crawlspace humidity is high all summer.
  - Mosquito season runs April-October.
  - Real-estate letters come all year, with a peak in the spring market.
  - Hurricane season brings wet crawlspaces.
- **Baton Rouge, LA:**
  - Formosan termites swarm on May and June evenings.
  - After floods (2016 is remembered), people ask for termite checks.
  - Mosquitoes and fire ants in the warm months, roaches and palmetto bugs all year.
  - Rats in attics in the cool months.
- **New Orleans, LA:**
  - The Formosan swarm season (late April-June, at dusk) is part of city life. Swarmers gather at the streetlamps and porch lights, and people turn their lights off.
  - Old raised shotgun houses and Creole cottages, camelbacks and slab houses each have their own risk.
  - Roof rats all year, and mosquitoes.

**Average ticket (words, not dollars):**
- Small: a termite letter or an inspection.
- Recurring: quarterly pest service and mosquito visits.
- Large: a full liquid or bait termite treatment.
- Yearly: a termite contract renewal.
- Exact sample ranges are in each facts file's `proof.prices`.

**Services that sell:** termite inspection, termite letter for a sale, liquid termite treatment, bait stations, contract renewal, Formosan protection, quarterly pest, mosquito yard treatment, rodents (roof rats, mice), fire ants, roaches, crawlspace moisture checks, wood damage checks.

## 5. Niche tools

1. **Termite or flying ant identifier**
   - What the homeowner learns: what swarmed.
   - Inputs: three taps:
     - waist (straight and thick / pinched);
     - wings (all four the same length / front longer);
     - antennae (straight / elbowed).
   - The choices are drawn as clean line diagrams, not photos.
   - Output: "most likely termite swarmers" or "most likely flying ants", what to do now (save a few in a bag, note where they came from), and the inspection sample price.
   - Leads to the call: "Book an inspection."
2. **Crawlspace conditions check**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether their crawlspace invites termites.
   - Inputs: tick boxes: standing water, wood touching soil, vents blocked, a musty smell, gutters dumping by the foundation.
   - Output: the list of conducive conditions they ticked, in plain words, with what fixes each. The moisture check is listed as a service.
   - Leads to the call: "Have us look under the house."
3. **Liquid vs bait compare**
   - What the homeowner learns: the two ways termites are treated.
   - Inputs: house type (slab / crawlspace / raised), wells or water nearby, will they want yearly monitoring.
   - Output: two columns. Liquid is trenched and rodded around the foundation and works as a barrier. Bait stations are monitored and eaten and carried back to the colony. Each column shows its sample range and what each visit looks like.
   - Leads to the call: "Ask which fits your house."
4. **Plan cost calculator**
   - What the homeowner learns: a sample year of service.
   - Inputs: termite plan (none / liquid / bait), renewal, quarterly pest (yes/no), mosquito visits (months picked).
   - Output: a sample yearly range built from facts prices, labelled sample.
   - Leads to the call: "Get this plan in writing."
5. **Swarm season calendar**
   - What the homeowner learns: when swarms happen in their city.
   - Inputs: the month (or today).
   - Output: a 12-month strip with the swarm window highlighted, what to watch for this month, and what to do if they see a swarm.
   - Leads to the call: "Book before swarm season."
6. **Lights-out helper** (New Orleans)
   - What the homeowner learns: why swarmers fly to lights at dusk and what to do that evening.
   - Inputs: the date (swarm season or not), lights on the porch and in windows.
   - Output: the 7:40-9:30 pm window, "porch light off, blinds down", and a reminder that swarmers at the light don't mean the house is infested. It still needs an inspection.
   - Leads to the call: "Book a swarm-season visit."
7. **House-type risk**
   - What the homeowner learns: where termites usually enter their kind of house.
   - Inputs: raised shotgun / slab / Creole cottage / camelback / crawlspace ranch.
   - Output: a simple drawing of that house with the usual entry points marked, and what the inspection checks there.
   - Leads to the call: "Check my house."
8. **Mosquito season calendar**
   - Inputs: the month.
   - Output: the visit plan by month with the sample per-visit price.
9. **ZIP checker** (all three sites)
   - Input: a 5-digit ZIP.
   - Output: served or not, from `zips`.
10. **Multi-step quote form** (all three sites)
    - Steps:
      1. what is the problem (swarm, mud tubes, letter for a sale, renewal, mosquitoes, rodents, ants, roaches);
      2. house type;
      3. closing date if a letter;
      4. photos;
      5. contact.

## 6. The three directions

### A — The inspection, station by station   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)

- Brand: Sill Plate Pest Co. · Wilmington, NC · facts-A.json
- Concept seed:
  - The page walks the actual termite inspection around and under a coastal Carolina house, in the order it is done: driveway and exterior → foundation and mud tubes → crawlspace (moisture, wood-to-soil) → the sill plate (the name: where the house sits on its foundation, where termites start) → attic → under the sinks.
  - Each station shows what is checked, what was found on a sample report (Seen / Not seen / Conducive), and what it means.
  - Wilmington angle: crawlspace houses in Forest Hills and Carolina Heights, beach houses at Wrightsville and Carolina Beach, closing letters for a hot real-estate market.
- Tone lead: light-led. Inspection-report paper white, sand and pale pine, graphite type, with one clear signal green for "not seen" and a measured amber for "conducive". No red alarm colors.
- Type lead: slab. A sturdy slab serif for headings, like a stamped report header; plain sans body and form fields.
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo, scrolling text. A photo of a crawlspace entry under a coastal house stays pinned on the right. The headline and the first stations scroll on the left; on mobile the photo sits on top and the text follows.
- Signature seed:
  - A house section diagram with numbered stations 1-6 and a dotted inspection path.
  - Each station on the page carries its number badge and a finding tag (Seen / Not seen / Conducive) like a real report line.
  - The final section is the sample report sheet with all six lines filled in.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: a flashlight cone sweeps once across the hero photo from left to right (a radial mask), revealing the crawlspace, then rests.
  - Scroll story: the house section diagram is pinned. The dotted path draws from station 1 to 6 as you scroll, and the station text changes beside it. With reduced motion, a static diagram with a numbered list.
- Tools: termite or flying ant identifier; crawlspace conditions check; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form (with "termite letter for a sale" and closing date as the fast path).
- Photo mood: daylight and flashlight, crawlspace entries, a moisture meter on a sill plate, a probe on wood, coastal Carolina crawlspace houses on piers, clipboard reports. Calm, factual, no bugs in close-up.

### B — Every station checked, every visit logged   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)

- Brand: Trap Count Pest Co. · Baton Rouge, LA · facts-B.json
- Concept seed:

  - The site shows a sample log, the liquid vs bait compare and a plan cost calculator, so the homeowner understands what a contract is before the call.
  - Baton Rouge angle: Formosan termites, after-flood checks since 2016, Mid City and Garden District raised houses, slab subdivisions in Prairieville and Central, the renewal letter every year.
- Tone lead: color-led. A deep bayou green field with bone white log sheets and one high-visibility station orange (the color of real bait station caps).
- Type lead: mono-accent. A clean grotesk for headings, with a monospace for station ids, dates, log entries and prices.
- Hero family: 11 diagonal split. A diagonal line cuts the hero. On one side, the photo of a raised Louisiana house and its yard. On the other side, the bayou-green field with the headline and the station log. The diagonal is the treated-zone line around the foundation.
- Signature seed:
  - The station ring: a house footprint (top view) with 12 numbered stations around it, used as the nav of the "how a contract works" section and the backdrop of the log.
  - Log rows (date · station · activity · action) show how proof reads. The rows are marked "Sample log".
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the diagonal line draws itself from corner to corner, then the station dots appear around it one by one (12 small rises).
  - Scroll story: the footprint is pinned. Each station lights in turn as you scroll, and its log row appears; one station shows "activity", and the page explains what happens next (bait replaced, colony tracked). With reduced motion, a static footprint and the full log table.
- Tools: liquid vs bait compare; plan cost calculator; mosquito season calendar; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: humid daylight, raised houses on piers, green yards, a technician's hands opening a bait station cap, a clipboard log, an orange station in grass. Clean and clear, no macro bugs.

### C — When the porch lights come on in May   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)

- Brand: Lamppost Pest & Termite · New Orleans, LA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed:
  - Every New Orleanian knows the Formosan swarm evenings: warm dusk in late April to June, swarmers circling the streetlamps and porch lights, and the whole block turning its lights off.
  - The page is that evening, from 7:40 pm to 9:30 pm, and what the company does about it.
  - Shotgun houses with tall transoms, Creole cottages in the Marigny, camelbacks in the Bywater, slab houses in Metairie.
- Tone lead: dark-led. A deep indigo dusk ground, streetlamp gold as the light and the accent, and wrought-iron black lines.
- Type lead: serif. A tall, high-contrast display serif for the headline (like old New Orleans signage and ironwork), with a warm sans for body.
- Hero family: 13 shape-masked photo. The hero photo of a shotgun house porch at dusk with a lit lamp sits inside an arch, echoing the arched transoms over New Orleans doors. The headline is set beside the arch on the dusk ground.
- Signature seed:
  - A dusk clock. A small clock face in the corner runs from 7:40 to 9:30 pm across the page, and the ground darkens section by section.
  - The streetlamp is the recurring mark: a thin gold line glow above each section title.
  - At 9:30 the lights go off and the guarantee and phone sit in the dark.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the arch-masked photo unclips upward like a transom opening, and a soft lamp glow warms once.
  - Scroll story: a block of four house types is pinned (shotgun, Creole cottage, camelback, slab) at dusk. As you scroll, the clock runs and the porch lights go out one by one, and each house shows where its termites get in and what the inspection checks. With reduced motion, four static cards.
- Tools: swarm season calendar; lights-out helper; house-type risk; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: dusk and night, gas lamps and porch lights, shotgun houses and Creole cottages, wrought iron, wet streets after rain, a technician's flashlight under a raised house. Atmospheric and warm. Swarmers only as soft points of light around a lamp, never in close-up.
