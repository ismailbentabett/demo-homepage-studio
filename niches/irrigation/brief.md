# Irrigation & Sprinkler Services — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 30-70 with a lawn, beds and a buried system they did not install: they bought the house
with it. Young families in new-build subdivisions (McKinney, Prosper, Eastvale) whose builder-grade system is
already failing; retirees in 1970s-90s ranch houses (Bakersfield, Riverside) with a 20-year-old controller nobody
has touched; busy two-income households who only notice the system when the water bill or the HOA letter arrives.
Mostly owner-occupied single-family homes, some landlords for rentals.

**What just happened.**
- The water bill jumped and nobody knows why.
- A brown stripe or a dead corner showed up in the lawn in the first hot week.
- A zone will not shut off; water is running down the gutter at 5 a.m.
- A geyser in the yard after the mower clipped a head.
- The city or water agency sent a backflow test notice with a deadline.
- In North Texas: a hard freeze cracked the backflow device and the yard is flooding.
- In California: a watering-day rule or a turf-rebate flyer made them rethink the whole front lawn.

**Fears, in their words.**
- "They'll dig up my whole yard to find one leak."
- "I'll get charged for a whole new system when it's one valve."
- "I don't know if the guy knows anything, or just swaps heads."
- "I'll pay and the dry spot will still be there next month."
- "I'll get fined for watering on the wrong day / for missing the backflow deadline."

**What makes them trust.**
- Being shown the problem: the cracked part, the reading, the zone on a drawing.
- A price per item before the digging, and a choice to say no to some items.
- Someone who knows the local soil and rules (clay, watering days, freeze dates, water agency forms).
- A tidy trench and sod put back; photos of finished work that look like their street.
- A real person answering, with a time window that is kept.

**Leaves in 5 seconds when:** the site is a generic "lawn care" template with a golf course photo, there is no
phone number above the fold, the service list is a wall of jargon (PVB, RPZ, GPM) with no plain words, or it
looks like a landscaping franchise that does irrigation on the side.

## 2. The job

**Split.** About 30% urgent (zone stuck on, broken main line, freeze break, sudden bill spike), 70% planned
(tune-ups, drip conversions, controller upgrades, new systems, backflow tests, winterization).

**Seasonality.**
- *Bakersfield (southern Central Valley, hot dry summers over 100°F, mild winters, heavy clay and sandy loam pockets):*
  Mar-Apr start-ups and repairs before the heat; May-Sep peak (dry spots, broken heads, bill spikes, valves stuck);
  Oct-Nov drip conversions and controller cutbacks; Dec-Feb quiet, new installs and planning.
- *Riverside (Inland Empire, hot inland summers, Santa Ana wind days, watering-day rules and conversion rebates):*
  Feb-Apr audits and turf-to-drip projects; May-Oct peak repairs and pressure problems; Oct-Nov wind-season
  controller cutbacks; winter installs while it is cool.
- *McKinney (North Texas, Blackland Prairie clay that shrinks in August and swells after rain, ice storms in winter):*
  Mar spring start-ups; Apr-Sep peak (St. Augustine dry patches, mower-broken heads, watering-day schedules);
  Oct-Nov winterization rush; Dec-Feb freeze breaks and backflow replacements after a hard freeze.

**Ticket bands.** Small: a head, a nozzle, a backflow test (under a few hundred). Medium: a valve, a leak dig, a
controller, a winterization, a drip zone (a few hundred to about a thousand). Large: a full new system or a
whole-yard turf conversion (several thousand).

**Services that sell:** sprinkler repair, valve repair, drip irrigation / conversion, smart controllers, leak
detection, backflow testing, spring start-up, winterization, new systems, water audits, rain and freeze sensors,
pressure fixes.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "a sprinkler is broken" | head failure / broken riser |
| "it sprays the street" | overspray, arc and radius adjustment |
| "this one barely comes up" | low pressure, worn wiper seal |
| "it won't shut off" | stuck valve, torn diaphragm or debris on the seat |
| "the whole zone won't come on" | solenoid failure / wire fault |
| "my bill is crazy" | leak on the lateral or mainline, meter test |
| "a brown spot in the corner" | poor head-to-head coverage, low distribution uniformity |
| "the thing by the house with the handles" | backflow preventer (PVB / RPZ / DCVA) |
| "the box in the garage" | controller / timer |
| "little hoses in the beds" | drip line, emitters, point-source |
| "it fogs / mists" | high pressure, needs a regulator |
| "blow out the pipes" | winterization, compressed-air blowout |
| "it runs when it's raining" | no rain sensor / no weather-based scheduling |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Zone symptom finder.** Inputs: what the zone does (won't start, won't stop, weak, mist, puddle, dry spot).
   Output: the two most likely causes in plain words, the part involved, and the sample price band for that fix.
   Leads to: "Call and say 'zone stuck on'": the phone with that phrase.
2. **Bill-spike checker.** Inputs: last normal bill, this bill, season. Output: whether the jump looks like a
   leak or like summer watering, plus the 3-minute meter test the homeowner can do now. Leads to: leak detection call.
3. **Catch-cup audit preview.** Inputs: lawn size band, number of zones, sun/shade. Output: what an audit measures
   (cups, pressure, run times), what the report looks like, the sample audit price. Leads to: book an audit.
4. **Drip conversion planner.** Inputs: bed count, plants (shrubs, trees, vegetables), current spray or none.
   Output: zones needed, what changes, sample price per zone. Leads to: quote form with answers pre-filled.
5. **Watering-day / season card.** Inputs: city from the service area. Output: a printable season card
   (when to start up, when to cut back, when to winterize), never inventing legal rules: it says "check your
   city's current watering days" and links the call. Leads to: book start-up or winterization.
6. **Freeze-ready checklist (Texas).** Inputs: last winterized date, backflow type photo pick. Output: a 4-item
   checklist and whether a visit is due. Leads to: book winterization or freeze repair.
7. **Controller age check.** Inputs: pick the controller look (dial, buttons, app). Output: what it can and cannot
   do, sample upgrade price. Leads to: smart controller call.

Prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices` only, always labelled Sample.
