# Appliance Repair — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
**Who calls.** Homeowners 30-70, mostly the person who runs the house. Three common households: a young family in a newer
suburb (Ankeny, Waukee, Tea, Harrisburg SD) running the washer every day; an older couple in a 1950s-60s ranch or a
Lehigh Valley rowhome with the laundry in the basement; a landlord or adult child calling for a parent's house. Most
read the site on a phone, standing in front of the broken machine, often with wet laundry or a warming fridge beside them.

**What just happened.** The fridge reads 48°F and the milk smells off. The washer stopped mid-cycle full of water. The
dryer runs but the towels come out cold. The oven takes 40 minutes to reach 350 the week before Thanksgiving. The
chest freezer with a quarter of beef in it is quiet. The freezer side of the garage fridge went soft in January.

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
- "They'll charge me $150 just to look at it and then tell me to buy a new one."
- "I'll take a day off work and they'll show up without the part, then make me wait a week."
- "They'll talk me into a repair on a machine that's going to die next year anyway."
- "Everything in the freezer is going to thaw before anyone gets here."
- "Some random guy from a national call center, not someone local."

**What makes them trust.**
- A visible price band for the visit and the common repairs, before the call.
- Being asked for the model number up front (it means the tech will bring the likely part).
- A plain rule for repair vs replace, said before anyone has a reason to upsell.
- Seeing the failed part: "he showed me the burnt fuse in his palm".
- A real local phone, hours that match their day, and a text-able number.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A wall of brand logos and "factory authorized" badges, a coupon popup, a
cartoon van, no price anywhere, a form that wants a full address before saying anything, and copy that reads like it
was written for every city in the US.

## 2. The job
**Emergency vs planned.** Roughly 75% urgent-but-not-emergency (a dead fridge, a washer full of water: they want the
next open slot, today or tomorrow), 15% planned (installs, a noisy dryer, a slow oven before a holiday), 10% "is it
worth fixing?" questions. True emergencies are rare and mostly freezers full of food. We never promise an arrival time
we cannot keep, and we never say "same-day" or "emergency".

**Seasonality (Upper Midwest: Des Moines, Sioux Falls; Lehigh Valley: Allentown).**
- Jan-Feb: garage fridges let their freezers thaw when the garage drops below freezing; dryers run longest (no line
  drying); frozen or iced dryer vent hoods; washers in unheated mudrooms. Steady, cold-driven calls.
- Mar-Apr: steady repair flow; spring move-ins start; dishwashers and disposals after the holidays settle.
- May-Jun: window AC units come out of storage and do not cool; installs for new homes and remodels.
- Jul-Aug: peak for fridges and freezers (compressors and condenser fans work hardest in hot kitchens and garages),
  ice makers, window AC units.
- Sep: move-in and install season, laundry for back-to-school.
- Oct: chest freezers get filled (deer and pheasant seasons in South Dakota and Iowa; the South Dakota pheasant opener
  is the third Saturday of October; beef bought by the quarter or half); freezer checkups.
- Nov: oven and range peak in the two weeks before Thanksgiving (igniters, bake elements, temperature).
- Dec: ovens and dishwashers under holiday load, gifts of new appliances needing install.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: a visit that ends in a cleaned filter, a reset or advice (just the visit fee).
Medium: one common part (thermal fuse, igniter, drain pump, fan motor, door switch). Large: sealed-system or tub
bearing work, where the honest answer is often "replace". Installs sit between small and medium.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Refrigerator and freezer repair, washer repair, dryer repair, dishwasher repair,
range and oven repair, over-the-range microwave repair, garbage disposal, window AC units, installation and hookup,
yearly checkup (dryer vent, fridge coils, washer hoses), chest and upright freezer repair, garage-fridge cold weather fixes.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "The fridge isn't cold but the freezer is" | evaporator fan failure or defrost system fault |
| "There's a block of ice on the back wall of the freezer" | failed defrost heater, thermostat or defrost control |
| "Water on the floor under the fridge" | clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan |
| "It hums but doesn't start" | failed start relay or overload / capacitor |
| "Washer won't drain, full of water" | drain pump obstruction or failed pump motor |
| "Washer bangs and walks across the floor" | unbalanced load, worn suspension rods / shock absorbers |
| "Loud roar when it spins" | tub bearing failure |
| "Dryer runs but no heat" | open heating element, blown thermal fuse, gas valve coils |
| "Clothes take two cycles" | restricted exhaust vent, low airflow |
| "Oven takes forever to heat" | weak bake igniter (low amperage draw) |
| "Burner won't stop clicking" | moisture in spark switch / spark module |
| "Dishwasher leaves gray water / smells" | clogged filter or sump, kinked drain hose, air gap |
| "It's showing an error code" | fault code; pull the diagnostic mode |
| "The tag with the numbers" | model and serial plate |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Repair-or-replace calculator.** Learns: whether the repair makes sense. Inputs: appliance, age in years, sample
   repair band (from facts.proof.prices). Output: "Worth fixing / Borderline / Probably replace", using typical lifespans
   (fridge ~13 years, washer ~10-14, dryer ~13, dishwasher ~9-10, range ~13-15; labelled typical) and the 50% rule of thumb
   (if the repair is over half the price of a new one and the unit is past half its life, replace). Leads to: "Book a visit,
   we'll tell you the same thing in person" with the appliance pre-filled.
2. **Symptom finder.** Learns: likely causes and what to check first themselves. Inputs: appliance + symptom chips. Output:
   2-3 likely causes in plain words, one safe self-check (clean the dishwasher filter, vacuum fridge coils, check the dryer
   vent hood flap, check the breaker), and when to stop and call. Leads to: quote form with the symptom pre-filled.
3. **Model-tag locator.** Learns: where the model/serial plate is. Inputs: tap an appliance outline. Output: a hotspot on
   the drawing ("inside the door frame, left side", "behind the kick plate", "inside the fresh-food section on the wall")
   and why it matters (the tech brings the likely part). Leads to: form field for the model number.
4. **Freezer thaw clock.** Learns: how long the food is safe. Inputs: full or half full, door/lid closed or opened. Output:
   USDA guidance (a full freezer holds about 48 hours, half full about 24, if the door stays closed), plus "don't open it".
   Leads to: call/text.
5. **Garage fridge cold check.** Learns: why the freezer thaws in winter. Inputs: month, garage temperature. Output: plain
   explanation (on many single-thermostat fridges the thermostat sits in the fresh-food side; a cold garage keeps it
   satisfied, the compressor rarely runs, the freezer warms; many manuals list a minimum room temperature, often around
   50-60°F, typical, check your manual). Leads to: "cold-weather fix" service.
6. **Dryer vent length check.** Learns: whether a long vent is causing slow drying. Inputs: total straight feet, number of
   90° and 45° elbows. Output: equivalent length vs the common code limit of 35 ft (minus 5 ft per 90° elbow, 2.5 ft per 45°;
   labelled typical, check the dryer's manual). Leads to: yearly checkup / vent cleaning.
7. **Visit prep card.** Learns: what to have ready. Inputs: appliance. Output: a 4-line checklist (model number photo,
   clear path, water shut-off location, breaker) that can be sent with the form.

Prices in every tool come from facts.proof.prices only and are always labelled "Sample pricing".
