# Tile — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

- **Who calls:** homeowners 32-68. Three main types:
  - Couples who just bought an older house and can't stand the pink 1950s bath or the cracked 1980s floor tile.
  - Empty-nesters finally redoing the primary shower they have hated for twenty years.
  - Owners whose shower has started to smell, stain or drip through the ceiling below.
  - A smaller group of landlords of two-flats and condos who need a unit turned around between tenants.
- **What just happened:**
  - A grout line went black and keeps coming back.
  - A tile sounds hollow and then cracked.
  - The ceiling under the upstairs shower has a brown ring.
  - The kitchen got new counters and the old backsplash now looks wrong.
  - They saw a herringbone floor at a friend's house and want one.
  - A water heater or washer leaked and lifted the laundry room floor.
- **What they are afraid of (their words):**
  - "Is it going to leak again in two years, behind the new tile where I can't see it?"
  - "What if the lines are crooked, or the big tiles have edges sticking up I'll stub my toe on?"
  - "They'll tear the bathroom out and then disappear for a week with no toilet."
  - "The price doubles once they open the wall and find rot."
  - "Dust everywhere in the house from cutting."
- **What makes them trust:**
  - Seeing the layers under the tile (backer board, membrane, slope to the drain), not just a pretty finished photo.
  - A drawn layout before work starts: where the cuts land, where the niche goes, what the shower will look like.
  - Named steps with real days: demo, waterproof, flood test, set, grout, seal.
  - Price ranges per job type before the call.
  - Reviews that mention a detail, like "the cuts around the toilet flange are perfect" or "no tile edges sticking up".
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:**
  - A gallery of blurry phone photos of finished bathrooms with nothing behind them.
  - "Quality craftsmanship" with no proof.
  - No prices at all.
  - A contact form that asks for everything up front.
  - A site that looks the same as the flooring store down the road.

## 2. The job

- **Emergency vs planned:** about 15% urgent (leaking shower, a cracked tile over a leak, a floor lifted by water) and 85% planned remodels and upgrades. Tile is a planned trade: people price it for weeks, then book a slot a month or more out.
- **Seasonality:**
  - **Miami:** steady all year. A spring peak (Feb-May) before hurricane season and before snowbirds leave. July-October slows a little, with humidity and storm watch. Concrete-block homes, terrazzo floors under carpet, salt air on the bay side.
  - **Oakland:** fairly flat. Peaks in March-June and September-November, with winter rain pushing leak repairs (Dec-Feb). Craftsman bungalows, 1920s Spanish-style baths with original tile, earthquake retrofit talk, small old bathrooms.
  - **Chicago:** strong indoor season Oct-April. Basements, baths and kitchens get done when the yard is frozen. Summer is booked by people doing the whole house before school. Bungalows, two-flats, vintage hex mosaic baths and freeze-thaw on front steps.
- **Average ticket bands:**
  - Small: tile repair, regrout, re-caulk.
  - Mid: backsplash, tub surround, laundry floor.
  - Large: full shower rebuild, whole-floor large-format.
  - Very large: primary bath tile, heated floor, natural stone.
- **Services that sell** (from facts): floor tile, shower tile and surrounds, shower waterproofing, kitchen backsplashes, large-format tile, natural stone, mosaic and pattern work, tub surrounds, fireplace surrounds, tile repair, regrouting, heated floors.
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**

| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|

| "The tiles stick up at the edges" | "Lippage" |
| "The floor isn't flat" | "Substrate out of plane / needs self-leveler" |
| "Hollow tile" | "Poor mortar coverage / debonded tile" |
| "Black lines in the grout" | "Mildew in porous cement grout, needs sealing or epoxy grout" |
| "The water doesn't go down the drain" | "Pre-slope / pitch to drain" |
| "Big tiles" | "Large-format (LFT), 24×48" |

| "Under the tile" | "Cement board, uncoupling membrane, thinset" |

| "Bathroom ceiling stain below" | "Flood-test failure / leak at the valve or curb" |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A slider of finished bathrooms with marble everywhere, and nothing about what's underneath.
2. "Quality craftsmanship, attention to detail," in a script font over a stock spa bath with candles.
3. A gallery grid of 60 phone photos, some sideways, with no captions.
4. Logos of tile brands and "authorized dealer" badges.
5. "Free estimates!" in a red starburst, and a contact form with 14 fields.
6. Product pages copied from a tile store: "porcelain vs ceramic", with no link to the actual install.
7. A van photo with the company name on it, plus a smiling owner with arms crossed.
8. The same gray subway-tile hero photo as every other shop in the city.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Shower layer explorer.**
   - The homeowner learns what sits behind the tile and which layer usually fails.
   - Input: tap layers (stud, backer, membrane, thinset, tile, grout).
   - Output: a short plain explanation per layer and "what we check" on a leak.
   - Lead: "Shower leaking? Send a photo" opens the form with *Shower waterproofing* preselected.
2. **Tile count and layout estimator.**
   - Inputs: room length and width, tile size (12×24, 24×48, 3×6 subway, hex), pattern (straight, offset, herringbone).
   - Output: square feet, a waste factor (10% / 15% / 20% for herringbone), boxes to buy, and a sample price range from facts.
   - Lead: prefills size and pattern into the quote form.
3. **Leak symptom check.**
   - Input: 4 questions (stain below? loose tile? grout cracks at corners? smell?).
   - Output: "likely grout/caulk", "likely pan or membrane" or "likely valve: call a plumber first", with honest next steps.
   - Lead: call or text a photo.
4. **Lippage and flatness explainer.**
   - A slider shows two big tiles with edges 0 to 1/16" apart and what that looks like in raking light.
   - Output: why large-format needs a flat floor and leveling clips.
   - Lead: "Measure my floor" CTA.
5. **Grout color preview.**
   - Pick a tile (white subway, gray hex, terracotta, marble look) and a grout (white, warm gray, charcoal, matched).
   - Output: a live CSS swatch with a note on how each ages.
   - Lead: prefill into the form.
6. **Repair or redo quiz.**
   - Inputs: tile age, how many cracked, leak yes/no, do you like the tile.
   - Output: "regrout and reseal", "spot repair" or "rebuild", with sample price ranges.
   - Lead: prefill the service.
7. **Heated floor cost and run-time card.**
   - Inputs: room size and hours per day.
   - Output: the install range from facts plus a plain note on daily use.
   - Lead: quote form.

Prices are always ranges from `facts.proof.prices`, labelled Sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — Every shower is built in five layers, and you see each one before the tile goes on   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)

- Brand: Pitch to Drain Tile Co. · Miami, FL · facts-A.json
- Concept seed:
  - Miami showers fail from water, not from looks. Humidity, block walls and slabs mean a leak shows up as mold in the next room.
  - The site is a cutaway of one shower wall, drawn from the stud outward. Each section is one layer (demo, backer and slope, membrane plus a 24-hour flood test, set, grout and seal), with how long it takes and what you see that day.
  - The finished tile comes last, as the reward.
- Tone lead: dark-led. Deep wet-slate ground, warm white type, one membrane-orange accent (the color of a liquid membrane drying), with a pale grout-gray light band.
- Type lead: grotesk. A tight, technical neo-grotesk for headings with tabular figures for the layer numbers and days, and a plain humanist sans for body.
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo, scrolling text. A tall shower photo stays put while three short statements scroll past: "Flat walls." "Water goes to the drain." "Then the tile." The first is visible without scrolling.
- Signature seed:
  - A layer stack ruler runs down the left margin of the whole page. It is five colored bands (stud, backer, membrane, thinset, tile) that fill as you reach each layer section, with a thickness reading in fractions of an inch.
  - Section dividers are cross-section edges of a wall, not lines.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the five layer bands slide in from the left edge one after another (≤ 1 s), then the headline appears.
  - Scroll story: one shower wall cutaway is pinned. As you scroll, each layer is drawn on in order and a water line drops to the drain at the flood-test step. With reduced motion, it is five stacked labeled drawings.
- Tools: shower layer explorer; leak symptom check; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: close and wet. Water beading on glazed tile, a trowel combing thinset, orange membrane rolled on, low warm light in a humid bathroom, crew hands from behind. Some late-afternoon Miami light through a frosted window. Never a spa with candles.

### B — Flat to a sixteenth: the numbers on your floor before and after   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)

- Brand: Feeler Gauge Tile Works · Oakland, CA · facts-B.json
- Concept seed:
  - Big tiles show every flaw, and old Oakland houses are never flat. The site works like an inspection sheet.
  - It shows the measurements a good setter takes (floor flatness over 10 feet, lippage between tiles, slope to drain, mortar coverage) and what each should read, with tools to price your room.
  - Proof is numbers and checks, not adjectives.
- Tone lead: light-led. Porcelain white and pale concrete, graphite type, one bright level-vial green accent, a deep ink band for the price table.
- Type lead: mono-accent. A clean grotesk for headings with a monospaced face for every measurement, tolerance and price, like gauge readouts.
- Hero family: 8 photo mosaic. Four photos set in a tight grid with real grout-width gaps, one huge (a long large-format floor in raking light), each with a mono spec tag ("lippage ≤ 1/32", "95% coverage"). The headline sits on paper beside it.
- Signature seed:
  - Every section sits on a 1/16"-gap tile grid, with the grout lines drawn as the page gutters.
  - Each claim carries a small check readout (target / measured / tool), like a punch-list ticket that ticks off as you scroll.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the mosaic tiles drop into the grid and a straightedge slides across the big photo, leaving a "flat ✓" readout (≤ 1.2 s).
  - Scroll story: a raking-light floor where the lippage gap closes from 1/8" to flush as you scroll, with the readout counting down. With reduced motion, it is a side-by-side static pair with numbers.
- Tools: tile count and layout estimator; lippage and flatness explainer; repair or redo quiz; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: clean, even daylight, cool and exact. Large-format porcelain floors, a straightedge and level on tile, leveling clips, a laser line, mortar on the back of a tile. Bay Area overcast window light, no harsh shadows, mid distance.

### C — Chicago bathrooms have been tile for a hundred years. We keep the old ones going and set the new ones to last as long   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)

- Brand: Two-Flat Tile & Mosaic · Chicago, IL · facts-C.json
- Concept seed:
  - Chicago's bungalow belt and two-flats are full of 1920s white hex mosaic floors, subway walls and glazed fireplace tile. Half are worth saving, half are past it.
  - The site is a walk down a bungalow block: each house is a room (the vintage bath, the basement with a floor drain, the kitchen backsplash, the fireplace, the winter-salted entry) and what the crew does there.
  - Every story is set in a Chicago season.
- Tone lead: color-led. Deep bungalow-brick red and a warm cream, with a glazed green band (the color of old Chicago fireplace tile) and near-black type.
- Type lead: serif. A sturdy 1920s-flavored serif for headings (an inscriptional, slightly old-style cut), and a friendly sans for body.
- Hero family: 13 shape-masked photo. The hero photo sits inside a single large hexagon (the shape of a vintage mosaic tile), with the headline wrapped around it on cream. The hex returns as the signature.
- Signature seed:
  - A hex mosaic floor map: the service area drawn as a field of small hexagons (one per neighborhood and suburb), filled in brick, cream and green like an old bath floor.
  - Hovering or tapping a hex shows the town, its usual house type and the kind of tile job common there. The same hex frames every photo on the page.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the hex mask grows from one small tile to the full hero photo (≤ 1 s).
  - Scroll story: the hex floor map fills in tile by tile as you scroll through the four seasons of Chicago tile work (winter basements, spring baths, summer kitchens, fall fireplaces). With reduced motion, the map is complete and the seasons are a plain list.
- Tools: repair or redo quiz; grout color preview; heated floor cost and run-time card; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: warm, homey, a little nostalgic. Vintage white hex mosaic, subway tile with a black liner, glazed fireplace tile, a brick bungalow street in snow or fall. Warm interior light against blue winter dusk outside. Hands at work, never faces.
