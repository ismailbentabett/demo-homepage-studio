# Drywall — niche brief

Three demo homepages for a drywall and plaster finisher. All three are sample sites: fictional brands, real cities and ZIPs, 555-01NN phones, `.example` email, proof marked "Sample".

## 1. The buyer

- **Who calls:** homeowners aged 30-70 with one specific wall problem, not a remodel. The most common reasons are a hole from a doorknob or a moved TV mount, a water stain after a leak, cracks in old plaster, nail pops after the first heating season, a patch that "never looks right", and popcorn ceilings in a house they just bought.
- **Second group:** people finishing a basement, attic or addition who have done the framing themselves (or had a GC do it) and need hang-tape-finish only. Landlords and flippers turning over a unit fast.
- **What they fear:** dust everywhere; a patch they can still see when the light hits it; a texture that does not match; being told to tear out a whole ceiling; asbestos in an old ceiling; a small job being ignored or overpriced; a stranger working in an occupied home.
- **What they want to see before calling:** a rough price for their size of job, proof the finisher can match their texture, how many visits it takes (mud has to dry between coats), how dust is controlled, and photos of finished walls under real light.
- **Decision speed:** small patches are booked within days. Water damage waits on the leak fix and the insurance adjuster. Popcorn removal and skim coats are planned weeks ahead, often before move-in or a paint job.
- **Who else is involved:** the painter (who often refers the job), the plumber or roofer who fixed the leak, the insurance adjuster, and, for old ceilings, a testing lab.

## 2. The job

**What the work actually is:** cut back damaged board to sound material, add backing (blocking or a backer strip), hang new board, tape the seams (paper or mesh), apply three coats of joint compound with drying time between them, sand, check under a low side light, prime, and match the texture (smooth, orange peel, knockdown, skip trowel). Old plaster adds its own steps: re-attaching loose plaster to the lath with plaster washers, bonding agent, and skim coats. Finish quality is described by the Gypsum Association's Levels of Finish (GA-214), 0 to 5.

**Seasonality:**
- **Late fall to winter (heating season):** dry indoor air shrinks framing. Nail pops, seam cracks and plaster cracks show up in December to February. This is especially true in New England and Pennsylvania.
- **Late winter to spring:** ice dam stains on top-floor ceilings in snowy regions, with snowmelt and spring storms behind them. Ceiling repairs follow the roof fix.
- **Spring and summer:** basement and attic finishing, pre-sale and move-in popcorn removal, and repairs ahead of painting.
- **Wet climates (Pacific Northwest):** damp winters bring bathroom and laundry water damage and mold behind board. Repairs come all year after leaks.
- **After floods and big storms:** cut-back and rebuild work, for example the Susquehanna floods around Harrisburg (Agnes in 1972, Tropical Storm Lee in 2011). Copy may mention that floods happened; it must never claim the business was there.

**Homeowner word vs. pro word:**

| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| hole in the wall | patch / California patch / hot patch |
| the white stuff / spackle | joint compound / mud (all-purpose, topping, setting-type "hot mud") |
| sheetrock | gypsum board / drywall (Sheetrock is a brand) |
| bumps on the wall | nail pops / screw pops |
| crack along the ceiling | tape crack / seam crack / failed joint |
| the bumpy ceiling | popcorn / acoustic / cottage cheese ceiling |
| the texture | orange peel, knockdown, skip trowel, smooth (Level 5) |
| making it smooth | skim coat / Level 5 finish |
| the metal on corners | corner bead (metal, paper-faced, vinyl, flexible for arches) |
| the wood strips behind old walls | lath (wood or metal), with plaster "keys" |
| plaster coming loose | delaminated plaster / lost keys, re-attached with plaster washers |
| the stain keeps coming back | bleed-through, which needs a stain-blocking primer |
| the moisture-resistant kind | moisture-resistant board / cement board in showers |
| sanding dust | dust control: poly walls, zip doors, vacuum sanders |
| it shows when the sun hits it | critical lighting / raking light / flashing |

## 4. Competitor clichés

- A stock photo of a smiling man in a hard hat holding a sheet of drywall over his head, on a commercial office site.
- "No job too big or too small", with a list of 30 services in a grid of identical icons.
- A before/after slider where the "after" is a different room, or is plainly a painted wall shot in perfect light.
- Pages buried in trust-badge rows and "call now" banners with nothing about how the work is done.
- Prices hidden behind "call for a quote", with no sense of whether a patch costs $150 or $1,500.
- Texture named once ("we match any texture") with no proof or examples.
- Popcorn removal advertised with no mention of testing older ceilings first.
- Gray-and-orange construction palettes and heavy condensed fonts that look like a lumber yard flyer.

## 5. Tools

1. **Texture matcher**
   - What the homeowner learns: what their texture is called, and how a match is made (a sample board first).
   - Inputs: tap one of four close-up swatches (smooth, orange peel, knockdown, skip trowel), then wall or ceiling, then the rough room age.
   - Output: the texture's name, how it is applied, how hard it is to blend, and a note that a test board is sprayed before the patch.
   - Leads to the call: "Send a photo of your wall with the light from the side." This prefills the quote form with the texture.
2. **Patch sizer with sample price band**
   - What the homeowner learns: roughly what their repair costs, and why one hole can take three visits.
   - Inputs: hole size (coin, fist, plate, door-sized), wall or ceiling, textured or smooth, painted after or not.
   - Output: a "Sample range" from the facts file's prices, the number of visits (coats plus drying), and dust notes.
   - Leads to the call: "Book this size of repair", which carries the size into the form.
3. **Popcorn ceiling "test first" checker**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether to test before scraping, and what the order of steps is.
   - Inputs: the year the house was built (or the decade), whether the ceiling has been painted, and whether it has been tested before.
   - Output: for homes built before about 1980, "Test first: a small sample goes to a lab before anyone scrapes." It also lists the steps (sample, result, then the scrape or a plan for a licensed abatement firm) and the sample test price.
   - Hedge: rules on asbestos surveys differ by state. In Oregon, DEQ sets survey requirements for many renovation jobs. The copy must say "check the current rules on Oregon DEQ's website" and never state the rule as settled fact.
   - Leads to the call: "Book a sample visit."
4. **Finish level picker (GA-214 Levels 0-5)**
   - What the homeowner learns: what Level 3, 4 or 5 means, and which one their room needs.
   - Inputs: the room, the window direction or a big window, the paint sheen (flat, eggshell, semi-gloss), and whether texture is going on top.
   - Output: the recommended level and why ("west window and eggshell paint: Level 5 so seams do not show at sunset").
   - Leads to the call: the level is written on the quote.
5. **Crack and nail pop timer (seasonal)**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether to fix now or wait until after the heating season.
   - Inputs: the type of problem (nail pop, seam crack, plaster crack, stain), when it appeared, and whether it is growing.
   - Output: fix now, or wait until spring once the framing settles, with the reason; plaster cracks get a "pin it, do not just caulk it" note.
   - Leads to the call: "Put me on the spring list" or "Book now."
6. **Wall-era identifier (plaster or drywall?)**
   - What the homeowner learns: what their wall is made of, and whether it can be saved.
   - Inputs: the house's decade, a knock test (hollow or solid), a thumbtack test (goes in easily or not), and whether the cracks are long and branching.
   - Output: horsehair plaster on lath, gypsum plaster on rock lath, or drywall, with the repair approach for each.
   - Leads to the call: "Ask us to look at it."
7. **ZIP checker** (all three sites)
   - Input: a 5-digit ZIP.
   - Output: inside the service area (from `zips`) or "we'll tell you who to call".
8. **Multi-step quote form** (all three sites)
   - Steps: what is wrong (patch, water damage, texture, popcorn, plaster, new rooms); size and photos; wall or ceiling, and texture; timing; contact.
   - Store first, then send to GoHighLevel.

### A — The page is built up coat by coat, the way a wall is   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)

- **Brand:** Knife & Hawk Drywall (Knife & Hawk), Harrisburg, PA. Owner Gil. Phone (717) 555-0161.
- **Concept seed:** the homepage is one wall being finished. Each section is one stage: cut back, back-block, tape, first coat, second coat, skim, sand, prime, texture match. The page's own surface changes as you scroll: raw gray board and paper tape at the top, wet gray mud in the middle, feathered edges and sanding light, then smooth primed white at the bottom, where the quote form lives. The Harrisburg angle:
  - River-town water damage after Susquehanna floods.
  - 1920s-50s brick rowhouses in the city (Midtown, Uptown).
  - Split-levels and ranches in Camp Hill and Mechanicsburg.
  - Copy may name the floods, but never claims the business was there for them.
- **Tone lead:** light-led. Warm off-white "primed" ground, gypsum gray, wet-mud blue-gray, one strong accent (deep blue-green or brick red) for actions only.
- **Type lead:** grotesk. A tight, confident grotesk for headings, with set-width numerals for the coat counter and the price ranges. Quiet body text.
- **Hero family:** 12, sticky photo with scrolling text. A tall photo of a hand and knife spreading mud stays pinned while the opening text scrolls past it in three short beats: "Cut it square." "Tape it." "Three coats, then it disappears." The photo dims slightly and gains a "coat 1" label as the text moves.
- **Signature seed:** a coat counter in the left margin ("Coat 0 → Coat 5 / Primed") that ticks up as sections pass. Each section's background shifts one step from board gray toward primer white, and section dividers are drawn as feathered knife strokes rather than lines. The facts' prices sit as a "what each stage costs" ledger.
- **Motion idea:** as each section enters, a soft knife-stroke wipe reveals it, left to right like a pass of mud. In the sanding section, a slow raking glow passes once. With reduced motion, the stage just changes color with no wipes.
- **Tools:** the patch sizer with sample price band (visits = coats + drying); the texture matcher; the crack and nail pop timer; the ZIP checker; the multi-step quote form, styled as the "final coat".
- **Photo mood:** daylight in occupied homes, close crops of hands, knife, hawk and tape, and wet mud texture. Rowhouse fronts and a basement finished in one shot. Calm, practical, never dramatic water damage.
