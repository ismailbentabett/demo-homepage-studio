# Demolition (homeowner interior demo and small structures) — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** This is not wrecking-ball demolition. It is the messy first week of somebody else's project:
- **The remodeling homeowner (about 50%).** 35-65, owns an older house (1920s-1970s), has a kitchen or bathroom plan, and either has a contractor who "doesn't do demo" or wants to save money by hiring the tear-out separately and handing a clean shell to the builder. Often doing part of the finish work themselves.
- **The outdoor-structure owner (about 30%).** A rotted deck, a collapsing detached garage, a shed that came with the house, an above-ground pool, a hot tub nobody has run since 2019, a carport. Usually prompted by a home sale, an insurance letter, a city notice or a new fence plan.
- **The landlord, flipper or estate (about 20%).** A rental after a bad tenant or a leak, a flip that needs a basement taken to the studs, an inherited house with a finished basement that smells. Wants a fast, clean, documented job.

**What just happened.** The kitchen cabinets are ordered and arrive in five weeks. The basement flooded and the insurance adjuster said "the drywall has to come out to four feet". The buyer's inspector flagged the deck. The contractor's bid had one vague line, "demo and haul", and they want a second number. A tree came down on the shed.

**Fears, in their words.**
1. "They'll cut into something they shouldn't: a pipe, a wire, a wall that holds up the house."
2. "The whole house will be covered in dust for a month."
3. "It's an old house. What if there's asbestos in the floor tile or lead in the paint?"
4. "They'll leave a dumpster in my driveway for six weeks and crack the concrete."
5. "The bid will double once the walls are open."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A walk-through plan that says what stays (cabinets they want to keep, the window trim, the cast-iron tub) and how it is protected.
2. Dust control spelled out: plastic walls, zipper doors, floor protection, a negative-air fan, the HVAC returns covered.
3. A plain "we test first when the house is old enough to need it" line, with what happens if the test comes back positive (the work stops and the right crew takes that part).
4. Shut-offs explained: water, gas and power to the area are shut off or capped before any wall opens, and nothing structural is removed without the builder's or engineer's say-so.
5. Where the debris goes: a trailer that leaves each day, or a dumpster with boards under it, and metal and clean wood separated.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A hero photo of an excavator knocking down a commercial building. "Demolition services" with no mention of houses. Sledgehammer selfies. No mention of dust. A form that asks "project budget" before anything else.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Planned. Water-damage tear-outs are urgent (within days, for mold) but they are arranged with an adjuster, not a 2 a.m. call. `emergency: false` on every site.

**Seasonality (Akron, Toledo, Roanoke).**
- Jan-Feb: interior work: kitchens, baths and basements ahead of spring remodels; frozen-pipe and ice-dam water damage tear-outs in Ohio.
- Mar-Apr: spring thaw, flooded basements (Toledo and Akron clay soils), the first deck and shed removals once the ground firms up.
- May-Jul: peak outdoor season: decks, sheds, above-ground pools, hot tubs, detached garages, concrete patios; home sales trigger "remove it before closing" calls.
- Aug-Sep: kitchen tear-outs before the holidays, pool removals at the end of summer, storm-damaged sheds and fences after thunderstorms.
- Oct-Nov: last outdoor removals before frost, basement finishes for winter projects; Roanoke leaves and early cold.
- Dec: slow; interior basement and bath work only.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: a hot tub, a small shed, one bathroom gutted, a stretch of carpet and pad, a single wall opened. Medium: a full kitchen tear-out to the studs, a 300-400 sq ft deck, a finished basement after water. Larger: a detached one- or two-car garage with its slab, a whole-floor gut for a flip, an above-ground pool plus the deck around it.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Kitchen tear-out, bathroom gut, basement tear-out after water, flooring removal (tile, carpet, glued wood), wall opening for an open plan (non-structural, or with the builder's plan), deck removal, shed and detached garage removal, hot tub and above-ground pool removal, concrete patio and walk breakup, debris haul-away.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Demo crew says |
|---|---|
| "rip out the kitchen" | tear-out to the studs, gut |
| "the dust" | containment, poly walls, zipper door, negative air |
| "the wall I want gone" | load-bearing vs non-load-bearing, header, temporary shoring |
| "the old tile floor" | tile on mortar bed, mastic, thinset, 9-inch tile (test first) |
| "the dumpster" | roll-off, 10 / 20 yard container, dump trailer |
| "the junk from the job" | C&D debris, mixed load, clean wood, metal for scrap |
| "turn off the water" | shut-off and cap, make safe |
| "the old paint" | pre-1978 paint, lead-safe work practices (test first) |
| "the shed" | outbuilding, accessory structure |
| "the slab under the garage" | slab removal, breakup, rebar, haul-off |
| "what's behind the wall" | rough-in: plumbing, wiring, ductwork |
| "a permit" | demolition permit, utility disconnect letter (for structures) |
| "clean up after" | broom-clean, magnet sweep for nails |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

2. "We tear it down so you can build it up!" as the headline.
3. A guy in a hard hat swinging a sledgehammer at drywall for the camera, no mask, no plastic.
4. Lists of 25 services with every commercial term ("selective demolition, total demolition, industrial dismantling").

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Tear-out sizer.** Inputs: the room (kitchen / bath / basement / whole floor) or the structure (deck / shed / garage / pool / hot tub), size (sq ft or "small / average / large" with a picture), what is in it (cabinets, tile floor, plaster walls, a tub). Output: the crew-days in words ("about 2 days"), the debris in trailer loads or dumpster yards, and the sample price range from `facts.proof.prices`. Leads to: "Book a walk-through" with the answers prefilled.
2. **Layer peeler.** A drawn cross-section of a wall or floor: tap to peel each layer (paint, plaster, lath, insulation, studs; or vinyl, mastic, underlayment, subfloor, joists). Each layer says what it is, how it comes off, and which are "test first if the house is old enough" (some floor tile and mastic, some plaster and textured ceilings, old paint). Output: a list of layers in their room. Leads to: "Tell us your house's year" step of the form.
3. **Debris load estimator.** Inputs: the job type and size. Output: "about 3 trailer loads or one 20-yard container", with a short honest note on which is cheaper in their case (a dumpster that sits for a week vs a trailer that leaves each day), no dollar amounts beyond facts.
4. **What stays checklist.** A tickable list (cabinets to keep, trim, doors, a sink, the radiator, a light fixture) that becomes the walk-through sheet with "protect" or "remove and set aside" notes. Leads to: attached to the quote request.
5. **House-year check.** Input: the year the house was built (or "not sure"). Output: plain words: "Houses built before 1978 can have lead paint, and some older floor tile, mastic and pipe wrap can contain asbestos. We test the materials we'll disturb before we start, and if a test comes back positive, that part waits for a crew that does that work." No scare, no certification claims.
6. **Dust plan picker.** Inputs: does the family stay in the house during the work, are there pets, kids, HVAC in the area. Output: the containment level ("plastic walls + zipper door + negative air fan + returns covered") in words, with a small diagram.
7. **Space-back ruler (structures).** Inputs: the size of the shed, deck or garage. Output: the square feet of yard given back, drawn to scale against a car, a garden bed and a patio set.
8. **ZIP checker** (every site): yes / "a bit outside our usual area: call and we'll tell you honestly if we'll make the trip".
