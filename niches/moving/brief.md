# Moving & Junk Removal — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Three people, and the page has to serve all three without looking like three businesses:
- **The local mover (about 55%).** 24-45, renter or first-time buyer, moving across town or to the next town: apartment to a starter house, a duplex to a bigger rental, a lease that ends on the 31st. Often a couple with one car, a toddler and no friends who still own a pickup. A smaller group is 60-80, downsizing from the family house to a condo or a parent's in-law suite.
- **The haul-away caller (about 35%).** A homeowner or landlord with a pile: the garage nobody can park in, a basement after a leak, a tenant who left a couch and a mattress, a parent's house after a move to assisted living, a kitchen torn out by a weekend DIY project.
- **The helper buyer (about 10%).** Already has a rental truck or a pod and needs two strong people for two hours to load it, or a piano or gun safe moved down three steps.

**What just happened.** The lease end date is now 19 days away. The closing got moved. The landlord sent a move-out checklist. A sibling said "we need to empty Mom's house by the end of the month". The rental truck company only has a 26-footer left. The couch didn't fit through the new door.

**Fears, in their words.**
1. "They'll quote me three hours on the phone and it'll take seven." (the hour-creep fear)
2. "They're going to hold my stuff hostage until I pay more." (the fear behind moving-scam news stories)
3. "Somebody's going to gouge the hallway or drop the dresser my grandpa made."
4. "The junk guys will just dump it in a ditch, and it'll come back to me."
5. "I'll be standing in an empty apartment at 9 p.m. because the crew never showed."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A number before the truck rolls, built from what they actually own (a room count or an item list), with the hourly rate and the minimum said plainly.
2. A short, specific day plan: arrival window, how many people, which size truck, what gets wrapped.
3. Where the junk goes: donation drop first, recycling for metal and electronics, landfill last, said in that order.
4. Photos of real-looking work: blankets on furniture, shrink-wrapped mattresses, a ramp at a porch step. Not a fleet lineup.
5. Reviews that name a building, a staircase, a piece of furniture, a town.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** "Get a free quote" with no number anywhere. A stock photo of a smiling couple holding a cardboard box. A form that asks for email, phone and move date before it says anything. A page that says "nationwide" when they need a two-mile move. "Best movers in [city]" in the H1.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Almost all planned, but on short notice: most local moves are booked 1-3 weeks out, and junk pickups 1-5 days out. A real "today" call exists (closing fell through, eviction, a landlord turnover) but it is not an emergency trade. `emergency: false` on every site.

**Seasonality (Durham, Fort Collins, Boulder: college towns with lease cycles).**
- Jan-Feb: slowest month for moves. Junk removal holds up: post-holiday cleanouts, basement purges, estate cleanouts after the holidays. Snow days in Colorado push moves.
- Mar-Apr: spring cleaning, garage cleanouts, first home sales close, tax-refund furniture swaps.
- May: graduation. Duke and UNC (Durham, Chapel Hill), CSU (Fort Collins) and CU (Boulder) students leave; furniture left at the curb; landlords call for haul-aways.
- Jun-Jul: peak home-sale closings; family moves before school starts; the hottest crews of the year (Durham humidity, Front Range sun).
- Aug: the big one. August lease turnover in Boulder and Fort Collins (many leases end in late July and start mid-August), Duke move-in, apartment-to-apartment swaps. Crews book out.
- Sep-Oct: second home-sale wave, downsizing before winter, estate cleanouts.
- Nov-Dec: holiday slowdown, end-of-year office moves, year-end cleanouts; first snow on the Front Range.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: a single item, labor-only loading, a pickup-truck load of junk. Medium: a 1-2 bedroom local move, a quarter to half truck of junk, a garage cleanout. Larger: a 3-4 bedroom house with packing, a full estate cleanout, a move plus a haul-away of what didn't make the cut.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Local moves, packing and wrapping, furniture and heavy items, junk removal by the truckload, single-item pickup, donation drop-offs, estate and rental cleanouts, labor-only loading, appliance haul-away, short-term storage.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Mover says |
|---|---|
| "how many guys do I need" | crew size, two-person vs three-person crew |
| "how long will it take" | estimated hours, load time + drive time + unload time |
| "the truck" | 16 ft / 20 ft / 26 ft box truck, cube |
| "pad everything" | blanket wrap, moving pads, shrink-wrap |
| "the travel fee" | drive time, shuttle charge |
| "can you take my junk too" | haul-away, add-on load |
| "how much junk is that" | quarter / half / full truckload, cubic yards |
| "the heavy stuff" | specialty items: piano, gun safe, hot tub, treadmill |
| "take it apart" | disassembly / reassembly (bed frames, tables, cribs) |
| "the stairs fee" | long carry, stair carry, elevator reservation |
| "the old fridge" | appliance disposal, refrigerant handling |
| "my mom's whole house" | estate cleanout, sort-donate-haul |
| "just loading" | labor-only, customer-supplied truck or container |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A smiling young couple in a white room holding one cardboard box (nobody's move looks like that).
2. A lineup of branded trucks shot in a parking lot, with a giant logo on each.
3. "Stress-free moving!" and "No job too big or too small" as the headline.
4. A quote form with nine fields and no number at the end, just "we'll be in touch".
5. Junk removal pages that show a landfill or a dumpster overflowing, which is the fear, not the answer.
6. Fake urgency: "Only 2 slots left this week!" counters.
7. "Licensed and insured" badges and a wall of association logos as the only proof.
8. Interstate and "nationwide" language on a company that does two-mile moves.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Room-by-room move sizer.** Inputs: home type (studio / 1-4 bed / house with garage), floor and stairs at each end, an elevator yes/no, how much is boxed already. Output: suggested crew size, truck size and a sample hour range, then a sample price range built only from `facts.proof.prices` (hourly rate × hours, with the minimum). Leads to: "Hold a date" quote step with the answers prefilled.
2. **Truck-fill gauge (junk).** Tap the items in the pile (couch, mattress, fridge, bags, boxes, a hot tub). Each item adds to a drawn truck bed that fills in eighths. Output: "about 3/8 of a truck", with the sample load ranges from facts. Leads to: "Send a photo of the pile" step of the form.
3. **Keep / donate / haul sorter.** A three-column drag board for a downsizing or estate cleanout: items dropped into Keep (move it), Donate (we drop it off), Haul (recycle or dispose). Output: the move list and the haul list as one plan, printable. Leads to: a combined move + haul quote.
4. **Move-day planner.** Input: the move date. Output: a countdown checklist (book the elevator, reserve parking, defrost the fridge 24 h before, pack a first-night box), with the dates filled in. Leads to: "Book the crew for this date".
5. **Stairs and long-carry check.** Inputs: floors at each end, distance from door to truck, narrowest door or turn. Output: plain-words notes ("the sofa may need its legs off", "a third person saves an hour on three flights") and how it moves the sample hours. No dollar amounts beyond facts.
6. **Where it goes map.** A simple diagram (not a live map) of the junk stream: donation partners (described generically: "a furniture bank", "a building-materials reuse store"), metal and electronics recycling, landfill last. Output: the visitor sees their items sorted into those streams.
7. **Lease-turnover calendar (college towns).** Shows the busy weeks of the local lease cycle and graduation, so a visitor sees why booking early matters. Static, honest: "these are the weeks most leases change", no fake availability.
8. **ZIP checker** (every site): yes / "a bit outside our usual area: call and we'll tell you the drive time honestly".

## 6. The three directions

### A — Every piece tagged, wrapped, loaded, placed   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Tag & Lift Moving · Durham, NC · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: The move told as the journey of one household's things, from the moment each piece gets a numbered tag to the moment it stands in the right room of the new place. Durham texture: Trinity Park and Old West Durham porches with steep front steps, mill-house conversions in Golden Belt, apartment stairwells near Ninth Street, a July thunderstorm forcing the ramp under a tarp. The point the page proves without adjectives: nothing gets lost because everything gets a tag and a room.
- Tone lead: light-led (warm paper white, kraft-box tan, black ink, one tag-orange for numbers and links).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed grotesk for the tag numbers and headlines, set like stencilled box labels; a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: 9 — horizontal filmstrip (a sideways strip of move-day frames: tag, wrap, ramp, truck, hallway, placed; the headline sits above the strip, the strip drifts and can be dragged).
- Signature seed: **the inventory tag.** Every section is a numbered tag (No. 001, 002...) with a room code and a condition line, exactly like a household inventory sheet: "No. 004 · Kitchen → Kitchen · wrapped, 2 pads". The tag number in the sticky header counts up as you scroll, the services are tags on a tag board, the reviews are tags signed at the new house, and the footer is the last tag: "No. 112 · First-night box · placed".
- Motion idea: Intro: a paper tag swings in on its string, the number prints, then the filmstrip slides in from the right. Scroll story: one dresser travels across the page as you scroll (pads wrap it, it rides the ramp, it lands in a floor-plan room labelled "Bedroom 2").
- Tools: Room-by-room move sizer, stairs and long-carry check, move-day planner + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form ("from → to → what's moving → date → how to reach you").
- Photo mood: Bright, humid Carolina daylight through tall windows, close hands-and-pads detail (blankets, tape, shrink-wrap), mid-distance crews from behind on porch steps, kraft and wood tones. No staged smiles.

### B — The number before the truck rolls   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Even Load Moving & Junk · Fort Collins, CO · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: The page is a load ledger. Everything a mover usually keeps in their head (how many cubic feet a two-bedroom really is, how many hours three flights cost, how much of a truck a garage pile fills) is put on the page as a running number the visitor builds themselves. Fort Collins context: CSU students on August leases, Old Town bungalows with narrow side doors, new builds east of I-25 in Timnath and Windsor, snow in October.
- Tone lead: dark-led (truck-box charcoal, load-strap yellow for numbers, cool grey text; the tools glow like a dash readout).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a monospaced face for every figure, cubic feet, hours and ranges; a sturdy grotesk for headlines and body).
- Hero family: 7 — tool-first hero (the move sizer is the hero: three taps and a drawn truck fills, with the sample range and the phone number beside it; the photo is secondary, a strip on the side).
- Signature seed: **the running load meter.** A slim truck outline lives in the sticky header and fills as the visitor uses any tool on the page; every section adds a line to a ledger ("+ 2 flights: +0.5 h", "+ couch to haul: 1/8 truck"), and the closing section prints the ledger as a ready-to-send quote. Reviews are shown as ledger entries ("estimated 4 h · actual 3 h 40").
- Motion idea: Intro: the truck outline draws itself in one line, the cargo box fills to the visitor's default answer, the numbers tick up. Scroll story: a "where the hours go" section splits a sample 4-hour move into load, drive and unload bars that grow and shrink as you change the stairs or distance inputs.
- Tools: Room-by-room move sizer (hero), truck-fill gauge for junk, stairs and long-carry check + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: Clean, high-altitude light, crisp shadows; overhead shots of strapped loads inside a truck box, measuring tape on a doorframe, dollies and straps laid out like tools. Graphic, cool, a little technical.

### C — Boulder moves on a lease clock   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Kestrel Moving & Haul · Boulder, CO · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: In Boulder half the city seems to move in the same two weeks of August, and the rest of the year is downsizers in Table Mesa ranches, families leaving a Lafayette starter home, mountain moves up the canyon to Nederland where the truck can't turn around. The page is a map of the moves the town makes: the Hill to North Boulder, a Martin Acres split-level to Louisville, a Gunbarrel condo cleanout, a foothills cabin with a 200-foot driveway. Each route on the map opens a short story with the street-level detail that decided the plan.
- Tone lead: color-led (Front Range palette: sandstone red, spruce, high-sky blue on off-white; each route line has its own color).
- Type lead: serif (a characterful text serif for headlines and route names, with a small-caps sans for distances and labels).
- Hero family: 15 — map / area-led hero (a stylised, hand-drawn map of Boulder County with five route lines drawn in; the headline sits in the plains to the east, the phone on the mountains to the west).
- Signature seed: **the route lines.** Every section is a route drawn from one place to another, with a start pin, an end pin and a distance; the line continues from section to section down the page like a long trip, services are "stops" on the route, the haul-away section ends at the reuse store and the recycling yard, and the footer is "End of route: you're home".
- Motion idea: Intro: the five route lines draw themselves on the map one after another, each ending with a small pin drop. Scroll story: the canyon move section climbs: the route line steepens, the elevation readout climbs from about 5,400 ft to 8,200 ft, and the truck icon swaps to a smaller box truck for the narrow road.
- Tools: Lease-turnover calendar (August), keep / donate / haul sorter for downsizers, truck-fill gauge + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: Colorado light at its warmest (early morning and late afternoon), red-rock and foothill backdrops behind ordinary houses, ponderosa pines, a loaded dolly on a sunny sidewalk, snow on the Flatirons in a winter frame. Wide and airy, people only from behind.
