# Landscaper — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 32-68. Three main types: (1) a young family two to five years into a suburban house whose builder-grade yard has given up (thin fescue, red clay showing, a bed of dead shrubs by the porch); (2) empty-nesters in an older neighborhood who want a garden they can sit in, not mow; (3) people about to list a house or who just bought one and see the yard as the first thing the street judges. Most are dual-income and short on weekends. Many are reading on a phone in the evening, standing in the yard.

**What just happened.**
- A summer burned the lawn out (Austin, Charlotte) or the fall overseeding window is closing (Charlotte, late September).
- A storm or a heavy rain sent water toward the foundation, or the backyard stayed a puddle for three days.
- A neighbor redid their front yard and theirs suddenly looks tired.
- The city or the HOA sent a letter (overgrown beds, dead turf, watering-day violation in Austin).
- They are hosting something (graduation, wedding in the courtyard, a sale open house) in six to ten weeks.

**What they fear (in their words).**
- "They'll plant it, leave, and half of it will be dead by August."
- "I'll pay for sod and it'll wash out / turn brown because nobody fixed the soil."
- "I don't know what any of this costs, and I don't want to be talked into a $30k patio."
- "The crew will wreck my irrigation lines / my lawn getting the equipment in."
- "They'll pick plants that look good in the nursery and don't survive here."

**What makes them trust.**
- Seeing the plan before the dig: a drawing, a plant list with names, a schedule.
- Honest plant knowledge for *their* place (native, drought-tough, shade-tolerant, deer-resistant where it matters).
- A clear "what you'll need to do after we leave" (watering the first weeks, mowing height).
- A price range for a project like theirs before the visit.
- Photos of planting details (edges, mulch depth, drip lines), not just wide glamour shots.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A carousel of stock mansions with pool lawns; "Your Outdoor Oasis Awaits"; no prices or any sense of scale; a form that asks for budget first; a page that feels like the same national franchise template; plants that obviously do not grow in their region (palms in Charlotte, lush ferns in full Austin sun).

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** About 95% planned. The "urgent" 5% is drainage after a big rain, storm cleanup (limbs, debris), or a hard deadline (an event, a closing). No emergency line is needed; fast scheduling for cleanup is enough.

**Seasonality by month (Southeast + Central Texas).**
| Month | Charlotte (fescue, red clay) | Austin (Hill Country, drought) | Savannah (coastal, humid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Planning, pruning, hardscape, NC soil tests (peak-season fee Dec-Mar) | Pruning, bed prep, tree planting, hardscape | Planting trees and shrubs, camellias in bloom, pruning |
| Mar-Apr | Bed planting, mulch, pre-emergent, spring cleanup | Prime planting, irrigation start-up and audits | Azalea season, spring cleanup, mulching, irrigation checks |
| May-Jun | Mulch, warm-season sod (zoysia/Bermuda) | Drip conversions, xeriscape, watering-day schedules start (May 1) | Heat starts, lighting installs, maintenance |
| Jul-Aug | Survival mode, irrigation repairs, design for fall | Peak drought stress, hardscape early mornings, irrigation repair | Storms, heavy rain, drainage calls, hurricane prep |
| Sep-Oct | **Fescue aeration + overseeding window (the busiest month)**, fall planting | Second planting season, natives in, fall cleanup | Hurricane-season cleanup, fall planting begins |
| Nov-Dec | Leaf cleanup, planting trees, lighting (holidays) | Planting, freeze prep (covers), lighting | Leaf/moss cleanup, planting, lighting |

**Average ticket bands.**
- Small: seasonal cleanup, mulch refresh, irrigation repair, aeration + overseed (hundreds).
- Medium: front-yard bed redo, sod install, drip conversion, landscape lighting (low thousands to high single thousands).
- Large: full yard design-build with patio, walls and irrigation (five figures).

**Services that sell (from facts).** Lawn renovation / overseeding, garden design, planting beds, mulch and edging, sod installation, irrigation and drip, drainage fixes, patios and walkways, retaining walls, landscape lighting, seasonal cleanup, shrub and small-tree care, xeriscape conversion (Austin), courtyard gardens (Savannah).

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "my grass keeps dying" | turf decline, compaction, low pH, wrong cultivar for the site |
| "the yard turns into a lake" | poor surface drainage, negative grade, needs a French drain or swale |
| "that orange dirt" | Cecil clay / heavy clay subsoil |
| "the white rock under the dirt" (Austin) | caliche layer |
| "brown patches in summer" | drought dormancy / brown patch fungus (fescue) |
| "the sprinklers water the sidewalk" | overspray, poor head coverage, needs head adjustment or drip |
| "bushes by the porch" | foundation planting |
| "the little wall" | segmental retaining wall, seat wall |
| "stone path" | flagstone walkway on a compacted base, dry-laid or mortared |
| "lights in the yard" | low-voltage landscape lighting, uplights, path lights, transformer |
| "plants that don't need water" | xeric / native / drought-adapted palette |
| "the gray stuff in the trees" (Savannah) | Spanish moss (an epiphyte, not a parasite) |
| "fresh dirt on top" | topdressing with compost |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A drone shot of a giant estate lawn with stripes that no buyer owns.
2. "Transform your outdoor space into an oasis" / "Your vision, our passion" headlines.
3. A grid of six generic icons (leaf, shovel, sprinkler) with three-word captions.
4. A truck and trailer photo with the logo as the main proof.
5. Before/after sliders using photos from different houses.
6. Plant names never mentioned; no sense of the local climate at all.
7. A "Get a free estimate" form above everything, with no prices anywhere.
8. Stock photos of tropical resort gardens in a city where those plants die.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Water budget calculator** (Austin-leaning). Inputs: yard area (sq ft, with S/M/L presets), how much is lawn vs beds, sun. Output: estimated weekly water need for turf vs a native bed, shown as a simple bar, and how a drip conversion shifts it. Method shown openly (typical inches-per-week rule of thumb, labelled estimate). Leads to: "price a drip conversion" with the sample range from facts.
2. **Watering-day finder.** Input: house number (odd/even) and irrigation type. Output: the allowed days and hours *as a sample of how the rule works*, with a note to check the utility's current stage. No live data.
3. **Soil-first lawn quiz** (Charlotte-leaning). 5 questions (bare spots? orange clay visible? standing water? shade hours? last soil test?). Output: a likely cause (compaction, pH, drainage, shade) and the matching service, plus the NC soil test timing tip.
4. **Overseeding window countdown.** Input: none (uses today's date). Output: days until/through the fall fescue window (mid-Sept to mid-Oct), plus the steps that happen before it. Static date logic only.
5. **Plant picker by light.** Inputs: sun hours, wet/dry, deer pressure. Output: 6 plant cards suited to the city (names only, from a fixed list in the page), with height and bloom month.
6. **Project size-to-range estimator.** Inputs: project type (bed redo, sod, patio, lighting, drainage) and size. Output: the sample range from facts.proof.prices, clearly labelled sample. Leads to the quote form pre-filled.
7. **Season planner.** Input: month. Output: what is planted, pruned, and installed this month in this city. Leads to "book the next window".
8. **ZIP checker** + **multi-step quote form** (every site).

## 6. The three directions

### A — The yard starts underground   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Tilth & Tine Landscape · Charlotte, NC · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Charlotte lawns fail in the red clay, not in the grass. Tilth & Tine shows its work from the soil up: test, loosen, amend, grade, plant, mulch, water in. The page reads like a dig: you scroll *down* through the ground and each section is the work done at that depth, so the homeowner understands why the cheap "sod on clay" job dies.
- Tone lead: **color-led** (Piedmont red clay terracotta as the dominant field, with deep fescue green and pale straw text panels; color does the talking, not black or white).
- Type lead: **slab** (a sturdy slab serif for display, like stamped garden-tool handles and seed sacks; a plain humanist sans for body).
- Hero family: **4 — stacked headline above a cinematic strip** (a big three-line headline, then one wide strip photo that is a ground-level cross-section feel: turf edge, roots, soil).
- Signature seed: **the page is a soil profile.** A depth gauge runs down the left edge (0 in, 2 in, 6 in, 12 in, 18 in); every section is a soil horizon with its depth tag, what lives there, and the step of the job done at that depth (turf and mulch at 0-2 in, compost amendment at 2-6 in, aeration cores at 3 in, drainage pipe at 12-18 in). Section dividers are wavy horizon lines in shifting earth tones; the footer is bedrock.
- Motion idea: intro: the headline settles and the strip photo "lifts" a soil layer away from the top, revealing the first horizon line. Scroll story: the depth gauge fills as you scroll; at each horizon the layer color washes in and a small cross-section illustration adds its element (core holes, compost, pipe), so by the bottom the full healthy profile is drawn.
- Tools: soil-first lawn quiz, overseeding window countdown, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low, ground-level, early morning or late afternoon side light on soil and turf; hands in dirt, tools in soil, close and tactile; muted greens, rich red-brown; nothing glossy.

### B — Every gallon accounted for   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Half Inch Landscape Co. · Austin, TX · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: In Austin the real question is water: watering days, summer bills, a lawn that browns anyway. Half Inch answers with numbers before the call. The water budget tool is the hero: enter your yard, see what turf costs in water vs a native bed with drip, then see the price range for the swap.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (bleached Hill Country limestone white, sun-washed; ink in deep juniper green, accents in agave blue-gray).
- Type lead: **mono-accent** (a clean grotesk for text, with a monospaced face for every number, unit, gauge reading and label, like an irrigation controller's display).
- Hero family: **7 — tool-first hero** (the water budget calculator is the hero; the headline sits above it and a small photo of a native bed beside it).
- Signature seed: **the page is an irrigation controller.** Every section is a "zone" with a zone number (Z1 Front lawn, Z2 Beds, Z3 Patio...), a run time, a gallons-per-week readout and an on/off toggle look; services are zones, reviews are "zone notes", the price list is the controller's schedule screen. Numbers are always in mono with units.
- Motion idea: intro: the controller "boots": zone numbers tick on one by one and the calculator's default values count up from zero. Scroll story: a thin water line runs down the page like a drip line; at each zone section it "emits" a small dot pattern and the gallons readout settles to its value.
- Tools: water budget calculator (hero), watering-day finder (sample rule), project size-to-range estimator, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright, high midday Texas light, crisp shadows; limestone, decomposed granite, agave, salvia, muhly grass; drip emitters close-up; clean and airy, never lush-tropical.

### C — Gardens for the shade of the old oaks   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Square & Shade Garden Co. · Savannah, GA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Savannah gardens live under live oaks, in humid air, behind ironwork, in small courtyards and on marsh-edge lots. Square & Shade tells the story of gardening *here*: dappled shade, sandy soil, salt wind on the islands, azaleas in March, storms in August. Each section is a place in the city's garden life (a courtyard, a porch bed, a marsh-edge yard), not a sales pitch.
- Tone lead: **dark-led** (deep live-oak shade green-black as the base, Spanish-moss gray-green, warm lantern-gold accents, cream text).
- Type lead: **serif** (a high-contrast old-style or transitional serif with italics for plant names; a quiet sans for small labels).
- Hero family: **13 — arch / shape-masked photo** (the hero photo sits inside a tall arch, echoing courtyard gates and the arch the oak limbs make over the streets; the headline wraps the arch).
- Signature seed: **every section is a botanical plant marker.** Section headers are drawn like the embossed garden tags of old botanical gardens: common name, Latin name in italics, where in Savannah it thrives, light and bloom month. Services carry their own markers ("Courtyard garden — *hortus conclusus* — downtown squares, part shade"), reviews hang on small tags, the service-area list is a marker per neighborhood.
- Motion idea: intro: the arch mask opens from a narrow slit to full arch as the headline letters fade in, like stepping through a gate. Scroll story: dappled-light shadows (CSS gradient spots) drift slowly across the dark sections, and each plant marker "presses" in (slight scale and emboss shadow) as it enters.
- Tools: plant picker by light (shade-first list for Savannah), season planner, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: dappled shade, soft late-day gold light through moss, humid haze; ironwork, brick paths, ferns, azaleas, palmetto, courtyard fountains; close and intimate, no wide estate lawns.
