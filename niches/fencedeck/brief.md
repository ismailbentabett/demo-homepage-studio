# Fence & Deck — niche brief

Three sites, three yards: Boise (dry summers, cold winters, fast-growing subdivisions with HOA fence rules and new-build
backyards with nothing in them), Denver (hail, intense UV at 5,280 ft, freeze-thaw that heaves posts, older bungalow
alleys), San Diego (canyon-rim lots, slopes, salt air near the coast, wildfire zones inland). The page must feel like it
was built by someone who has dug post holes in *this* dirt.

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 30-65, single-family homes. Common situations:
- A new build with a bare dirt backyard: needs a privacy fence before the dog arrives and a deck before summer.
- An old fence is leaning, a section blew down in a windstorm, or the neighbor asked to split a replacement.
- The deck is grey, splintery, has soft boards or a wobbly railing, and an inspector or a guest noticed.
- A pool went in and the city requires a compliant barrier and self-closing gate.
- Selling the house: a tired fence and a peeling deck are the first things buyers see out back.
Often a couple; one partner wants it pretty, the other wants to know if the posts are set in concrete.

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
1. "The posts will rot or lean in three years and the whole fence will wave in the wind."
2. "They'll build it on the wrong side of the property line and the neighbor will make me tear it out."
3. "The HOA or the city will reject it after it's built."
4. "The deck will look great and then the boards will cup and the stain will peel by next summer."
5. "The gate will sag and drag on the ground."

**What makes them trust.**
1. Post depth, post spacing, post material and how posts are set, written on the quote.
2. A clear answer about the property line, the 811 call before digging and who pulls permits.
3. A real gate detail: hinge side post size, anti-sag kit or steel frame.
4. For decks: joist spacing, joist tape, fastener type and ledger flashing explained in plain words.
5. Photos of their kind of house and yard, not a ranch in Kentucky with white rail fence.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A stock photo of a white picket fence with a golden retriever; "Quality Fencing Solutions";
no prices at all; a gallery that is all commercial chain link; a slow site that does not say which towns it covers.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** ~95% planned. The only urgent jobs are a fence section blown down (dog containment) or a broken
gate. These are handled as next-week repairs, not emergencies. `emergency: false` on all three sites.

**Seasonality.**
| Month | Boise | Denver | San Diego |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Frozen ground slows posts; deck design and bids | Bids; posts only on thaw days | Steady work; rainy weeks pause stain jobs |
| Mar-Apr | Phones ramp; wind-damaged fences | Spring wind repairs, deck planning | Busy: spring deck season starts |
| May-Jun | Peak: new-build backyards, decks before July 4 | Peak; hail can damage stain and vinyl | Peak; "May gray" mornings, good stain weather |
| Jul-Aug | Peak; hot afternoons, early starts | Peak; afternoon storms | Busy; inland heat, fire-season prep |
| Sep-Oct | Staining season, fence before winter | Staining season, last decks | Fire-season talk: ignition-resistant decking, Santa Ana winds |
| Nov-Dec | Slows; gate repairs | Slows; snow days | Good build weather, holidays slow |

**Ticket bands (words).** Gate repairs and a few replaced boards are low hundreds. A stain and seal job is mid hundreds to low
thousands. A full backyard privacy fence is mid thousands to low tens of thousands. A new composite deck is the biggest ticket:
mid to high tens of thousands for a large one with stairs and railings.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Wood privacy fences, vinyl fences, horizontal modern fences, gates, chain link (backyards and
dog runs), pool barriers, deck building (wood and composite), deck repair and board replacement, railings, staining and sealing.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "The fence is leaning" | Post failure: rot at grade, frost heave or shallow footing |
| "The gate drags" | Gate sag; undersized hinge post, no diagonal brace or anti-sag cable |
| "Fence boards" | Pickets |
| "The bars along the back" | Rails / stringers |
| "The bottom board" | Rot board / kickboard |
| "Fancy top" | Cap and trim, lattice topper |
| "The boards are curling" | Cupping (moisture difference between faces) |
| "Grey wood" | UV weathering, lignin breakdown |
| "Soft spot on the deck" | Rotted decking or joist decay |
| "Where the deck meets the house" | Ledger board and ledger flashing |
| "Wobbly railing" | Post-to-rim connection; missing blocking or lag-only attachment |
| "Plastic deck" | Composite or PVC decking |
| "Hidden screws" | Hidden fastener clips |
| "The see-through fence by the pool" | Pool barrier: height, gaps, self-closing self-latching gate |
| "Call to check for pipes" | 811 utility locate |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. White picket fence with a dog or kids running: no one in these towns has a picket fence out back.
2. "Quality craftsmanship" and "your vision, our expertise" headlines.
3. A gallery of 60 near-identical fence photos with no captions, towns or materials.
4. Material brand logos as a trust strip (composite and vinyl brands).
5. A "Get a quote" form that asks for nothing about the yard (no length, no height, no material).
6. Stock wood grain backgrounds behind every section.
7. Hiding the price until the "free consultation".

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Fence estimator.** Inputs: linear feet (or draw on a simple yard grid), height (4/6/8 ft), material, number of gates.
   Output: post count (one every 8 ft + ends + gate posts), a sample price range from `proof.prices`, and "call 811 before
   anyone digs". Leads to the form with the numbers prefilled.
2. **Fix or replace quiz.** 5 questions: age, how many posts lean, rot at the base, gate drags, boards missing. Output: "repair
   a section", "replace posts, keep panels" or "replace". Leads to a photo-upload step in the form.
3. **Deck size and board counter.** Inputs: deck width × depth, board width, joist spacing (12 or 16 in.). Output: square feet,
   approximate board count and joist count, a sample range. Hand-off to the quote.
4. **Material compare.** Pressure-treated, cedar, composite, PVC side by side: upkeep per year, hail/UV notes, look; slider for
   "how much time will you spend on upkeep". No brand names.
5. **Permit checklist.** Questions: deck height above ground, attached to house, fence height, front or back yard, pool.
   Output: "usually needs a permit / usually does not; we check your city" (hedged, no legal claims).
6. **Fire-zone checklist** (San Diego). Static, honest: what ignition-resistant decking and ember-resistant details are, a link
   out to the CAL FIRE hazard map, and "we build to what your city requires".
7. **Stain schedule.** Input: wood type, sun exposure, last stain date. Output: next stain window (season), a sample range.
8. **Neighbor split helper.** Input: shared length, both households' share. Output: per-household sample range and a printable
   sheet to hand the neighbor.

Prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices` only, always labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "A straight fence starts with a tight string."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Stringline Fence & Deck · Boise, ID · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Every good fence starts with a mason's string pulled tight between two corner stakes, and every post is set to
  it. The homepage shows the job as that line: stake the corners, call 811, dig, set posts, hang rails, hang pickets, hang the
  gate, walk it with you. Boise new-build backyards are the setting: bare dirt to finished yard.
- Tone lead: **color-led** (sage field color, cedar orange accent, off-white type and cards, deep pine for text).
- Type lead: **grotesk** (a sturdy, wide grotesk for headlines, the same family for body; numerals tabular for feet and counts).
- Hero family: **9 — horizontal filmstrip.** Under the headline, a slow strip of frames runs left: stakes and string, a post hole,
  a post in concrete, rails, pickets going on, the finished fence at golden hour. Pauses on hover/focus.
- Signature seed: **The string line.** A thin taut line (bright cedar orange) runs across the whole page, section to section; post
  marks sit on it every "8 ft", each section is a post. A small counter at the line's end reads "ft built" and climbs as you
  scroll. Section labels sit on the line like tags on stakes.
- Motion idea: Intro: the string snaps tight from left to right (a small overshoot, 600 ms) and the first post drops onto it.
  Scroll story: as the visitor scrolls, posts appear on the line at even spacing and the "ft built" counter rises to the length
  of an average Boise backyard fence.
- Tools: Fence estimator (posts every 8 ft, 811 reminder), Fix or replace quiz + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low golden light, clean new subdivisions with foothills behind, close detail of string, stakes, post holes, fresh
  cedar color; distance mid to close; no faces.

### B — "Count it before you pay for it."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Tallyboard Fence & Deck · Denver, CO · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: A deck or fence is a pile of countable things: posts, joists, boards, screws, hours. Tallyboard counts them in
  front of you. The page keeps a running pencil tally of what your job needs, and every number has the lumber grade stamp it
  came from. Denver's hail and UV are the reason the counts include upkeep.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (raw lumber paper, graphite pencil grey, one stamp-ink blue accent, a small hail-white).
- Type lead: **slab** (a sturdy slab serif for display, like stencil on lumber; a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: **8 — photo mosaic.** Four photos in a tight grid: one huge (a finished composite deck behind a Denver bungalow),
  three small (stacked boards with grade stamps, a hidden-fastener clip, a tight gate latch). Headline on paper beside it.
- Signature seed: **The tally and the stamp.** Pencil tally marks (four strokes and a slash) count things throughout the page:
  reviews, posts, boards. Every section label is a lumber grade stamp (an ink box with a code, a species and a mill-style
  number, all fictional). The tools add to a running tally in a side card that becomes the quote request.
- Motion idea: Intro: tally strokes draw in one by one and the fifth slash crosses them as the headline settles. Scroll
  story: the stamp labels "thunk" onto each section (scale 1.08 to 1 with a short ink spread).
- Tools: Deck size and board counter, Material compare (with hail and UV notes), Permit checklist ("decks over 30 in. or attached
  usually need a permit; we check your city") + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright even daylight, close-up of material detail, clean, high clarity, light lumber tones, Denver brick and
  bungalow backyards, alleys with garages.

### C — "Built for the canyon edge, from four o'clock to lights on."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Torrey Rail Fence & Deck · San Diego, CA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: San Diego backyards sit on slopes and canyon rims, and the deck is where the evening happens. The page follows
  one canyon-rim deck from 4 PM to 10 PM: afternoon sun on the boards, the marine layer coming in, dinner, the deck lights on.
  Each hour is a section and a local truth (salt air, slope, fire zone, privacy from the neighbor above).
- Tone lead: **dark-led** (dusk navy-charcoal base, warm lamp amber accent, sage scrub green, warm white type).
- Type lead: **serif** (a refined high-contrast serif for display, a quiet sans for body).
- Hero family: **4 — stacked headline above cinematic strip.** Big serif headline on dark paper, then a wide 21:9 strip of a deck
  over a canyon at golden hour.
- Signature seed: **The evening clock.** Each section is stamped with an hour (4 PM, 5 PM, 6 PM...) and divided from the next by a
  thin slope profile line (the canyon edge drawn as a single stroke). The page background darkens slowly from section to section
  and small deck-light dots switch on along the dividers as you go down.
- Motion idea: Intro: the strip warms from blue to gold and the headline rises line by line. Scroll story: the background
  shifts from late afternoon to night; the deck lights along the slope lines turn on one by one (disabled with reduced motion,
  the final state shown).
- Tools: Fire-zone checklist (CAL FIRE map link, ignition-resistant decking, honest and static), Material by exposure (coast vs
  inland: salt air means stainless fasteners), Fence estimator + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: golden hour to dusk, warm lamps, canyon chaparral, ocean haze in the distance, decks on slopes, Spanish and
  mid-century houses; medium-wide distance.
