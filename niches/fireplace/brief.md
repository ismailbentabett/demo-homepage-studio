# Fireplace & chimney (sweeps, inspections, stoves, inserts, gas logs) — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Three kinds of people, all thinking about the first cold night:
- **The wood burner (about 50%).** 35-70, owns a house with a masonry fireplace or a wood stove and burns a cord or more a winter. Knows a sweep is "something you should do" and has put it off. Often has a smell, a smoky room or a cracked crown they noticed from the ladder while cleaning gutters.
- **The new owner (about 30%).** Just bought a house with a fireplace nobody has used in years. The home inspector wrote "recommend Level 2 chimney inspection before use", or the insurer asked about the stove. Wants to know if it is safe to light, and what it costs if not.
- **The upgrader (about 20%).** Tired of an open fireplace that sends the heat up the chimney. Wants an insert, a freestanding stove or gas logs, and needs someone to say which one fits the opening, the flue and the way they live.

**What just happened.** The first fall morning below freezing. A smell of old campfire in the living room on a humid day. Smoke rolled into the room when they lit the first fire. A bird or a squirrel is in the chimney. Bits of black, crunchy flakes fell onto the grate. A chunk of mortar landed on the roof. They are listing the house and the buyer's inspector asked for a camera inspection.

**Fears, in their words.**
1. "Is there going to be a chimney fire in my house?"
2. "They'll put a sheet down, then tell me I need a whole new liner for thousands of dollars."
3. "They'll get soot all over the white carpet."
4. "Is my wood stove even installed right? The last owner did it himself."
5. "I don't know the difference between a sweep and an inspection, so I'll pay for the wrong one."

**What makes them trust.**
1. Before-and-after photos from inside *their* flue, from a camera on a rod, sent with the visit notes.
2. A plain explanation of what a sweep removes (soot and creosote) and what an inspection looks at (the liner, the smoke shelf, the crown, the cap, clearances).
3. Drop cloths, a vacuum running at the firebox opening during the whole sweep, and a promise of a clean hearth.
4. Findings written as "fine", "keep an eye on it", "fix before you burn", with the reason for each, and never a pressure line.
5. Straight answers on stove vs insert vs gas logs, including "keep your open fireplace" when that is the honest answer.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A stock photo of a family in Christmas sweaters in front of a roaring fire. Mary Poppins chimney sweep cartoons. A wall of safety badges and "certified" logos. A "chimney fire!" scare banner in red. No mention of what a visit includes.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Planned. A chimney fire is a 911 call, not a sweep call; an animal in the flue or a smoky room is booked within days. `emergency: false` on every site.

**Seasonality (Missoula, Bend, Burlington).**
- Jan-Feb: mid-season sweeps for heavy burners, smoky-room and draft calls during cold snaps; Missoula and Bend inversions and burn-restriction days, when stoves sit cold.
- Mar-Apr: spring sweeps for people burning all winter, cap and crown repairs once the roof is dry, birds and squirrels nesting in uncapped flues.
- May-Jul: the best months to book: sweeps without a waitlist, liner replacements, stove and insert installs ahead of fall, masonry and crown work, chimney leaks after spring rain.
- Aug-Sep: the rush begins: everyone who "will do it before winter" calls, firewood season (Burlington and Missoula woodpiles), first cords stacked; book now or wait.
- Oct-Nov: peak: sweeps, inspections, first-fire smoke problems, gas log service before the holidays.
- Dec: last-minute "can you come before Christmas" calls, gas log ignition problems, smoky-room fixes.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: a sweep of one flue, a camera look for a home sale, a new cap. Medium: a crown seal or rebuild, a damper replacement, gas log installation, a liner for an older masonry chimney. Larger: a wood stove or insert with a new stainless liner, a full chimney rebuild above the roofline.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Chimney sweep, chimney inspection with camera, wood stove sweep and service, wood stove and insert installation, stainless steel liner, chimney cap and damper, crown repair, gas log installation and service, smoky-room and draft fixes, animal and nest removal.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Chimney pro says |
|---|---|
| "clean the chimney" | sweep the flue, remove creosote and soot |
| "the black flaky stuff" | creosote (stage 1 soot, stage 2 flaky, stage 3 glazed) |
| "the inside of the chimney" | flue, flue tiles, liner |
| "the little door that opens" | damper (throat damper, top-sealing damper) |
| "the ledge behind the damper" | smoke shelf |
| "the top of the chimney" | crown, cap, spark arrestor |
| "a camera look" | Level 2 inspection, video scan |
| "it smokes into the room" | poor draft, negative pressure, short flue, cold flue |
| "that smell in summer" | creosote odor, damp flue, missing cap |
| "fireplace insert" | insert with a full-length stainless liner |
| "fake logs" | gas log set, vented or vent-free |
| "the metal box chimney" | factory-built (prefab) fireplace, class A chimney pipe |
| "the bricks are falling apart" | spalling, mortar joints, repointing |
| "the stuff over the opening" | lintel, firebox, hearth extension |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Symptom checker.** Inputs: tap what is happening (smoke into the room, smell in summer, flakes falling, sounds in the flue, water stain by the chimney, the stove won't stay lit, haven't used it in years). Output: what it usually means in plain words, how soon to deal with it ("before your next fire" / "this season" / "next visit"), and which visit to book (sweep, camera inspection, cap, draft fix). Leads to: the quote form with the symptom prefilled.
2. **Creosote stage explainer.** Three tiles with a drawn texture: dusty soot, crunchy flakes, shiny glaze. Tap the one that looks like what fell on the grate. Output: what it is, why it forms (cool fires, wet wood, a cold flue), and what a sweep can and cannot remove. Honest: glazed creosote may need a second visit or a special treatment, shown as words, never as a price.
3. **Firewood dry-check.** Inputs: the month the wood was split and stacked, the wood type (pine, fir, larch, birch, maple, oak), covered or not, or a moisture meter reading if they have one. Output: "ready around next October" or "should be ready now", with the rule that dry wood reads under 20% on a fresh split face. Leads to: "burning wet wood builds creosote faster; book your sweep for the end of the season".
4. **Sweep interval planner.** Inputs: what they burn in (open fireplace / insert / freestanding stove / gas logs), how often (weekends / most evenings / heat the house), cords per winter. Output: the suggested sweep timing (once a year at least for the inspection; heavy burners twice), drawn as a dot on a season calendar. Leads to: "book the next opening before the fall rush".
5. **Stove, insert or gas logs picker.** Inputs: what they want (heat the room / heat the house / the look / no hauling wood), the fireplace type, whether power goes out often, how much wood they can get. Output: a short ranked answer with the trade-off of each and the sample price ranges from `facts.proof.prices`, labelled sample. Leads to: a measuring visit.
6. **Visit-includes card.** Not interactive; a drawn cross-section of the chimney with what is checked at each level (firebox, damper, smoke shelf, liner, crown, cap, roof flashing), used as the "what you get" section on every direction.
7. **ZIP checker.** Inputs: a ZIP. Output: "We cover 59803. Sweeps there run on Tuesdays and Thursdays" or "That's outside our usual area; call and we'll tell you who we'd send you to". Reads `facts.biz.zips` only.

## 6. The three directions

### A — From the hearth to the cap   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Hearth to Cap Chimney Co. · Missoula, MT · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: A sweep is a climb. The page starts at the hearth with the drop cloth going down and rises through the chimney the way the brush does, one part at a time, until it comes out at the cap and the Missoula sky. Missoula texture: Rattlesnake and University District bungalows with brick chimneys, wood stoves in Target Range and Lolo, the valley inversions when the county asks people not to burn, larch and fir stacked under eaves, Mount Sentinel's "M" above the rooftops.
- Tone lead: dark-led (the warm black of a firebox and soot, ember orange as the accent for steps and links, brick reds and flue-tile buff in photos, cream type).
- Type lead: serif (a sturdy old-style serif with real character for headlines, like an old stove maker's plate; a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: 13 — arch / shaped frame (the hero photo is shown through the arched shape of a firebox opening; the headline sits on the hearth below it).
- Signature seed: **the chimney cross-section.** A drawn chimney runs up the edge of the page and each section is one level of it: firebox, damper, smoke shelf, liner, crown, cap. A small brush marker climbs the flue as you scroll and the current level is labelled ("Level 3 · smoke shelf · where the creosote hides"). Services sit at the level where they happen (a damper at the damper, a cap at the top); the footer is the cap with the sky behind it.
- Motion idea: Intro: an ember glow warms up inside the arch, then the photo fades in behind it. Scroll story: the brush marker rises through the flue and the soot on the drawn flue walls is wiped clean as it passes; at the crown, the page brightens to daylight.
- Tools: Symptom checker, creosote stage explainer, visit-includes card + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form ("what you burn in → what's happening → when it was last swept → your address area → how to reach you").
- Photo mood: Warm, low light inside a firebox and around a hearth: brick, flue tile, a brush and rods on a drop cloth, soot texture, then daylight and a chimney cap against a mountain sky. Hands and backs only; no cozy-family scenes.

### B — The draft note   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Draft Note Chimney & Stove · Bend, OR · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Every visit ends with a written note, numbered findings with photos from inside the flue, each marked OK, watch or fix. The page is that note, written for the reader's house: what is checked, how it is graded, what it usually costs to fix, and what is fine to leave. Bend texture: ponderosa pine and juniper smoke, high-desert cold nights even in June, pumice soil, wood stoves in Tumalo and La Pine, gas inserts in newer westside builds, smoke season in late summer.
- Tone lead: light-led (paper white and pumice gray, ink-black type, one signal color per finding: a muted green-gray for OK, ochre for watch, brick red for fix).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a clean grotesk for headlines and body, with a typewriter-style monospace for finding numbers, measurements and labels).
- Hero family: 14 — document / report (the hero is a visit note on paper, with a flue photo clipped to it and the first three findings readable; the phone number is the "questions?" line on the note).
- Signature seed: **numbered findings.** Every section of the page is one finding on the note ("Finding 04 · Cap · Watch · the mesh is bent on the north side"), with its status chip, a photo slot and a line of what it means. The services list is the "what we can fix" column; reviews are signed at the bottom like notes from past visits; the footer is "Finding 12 · All done · next sweep: October".
- Motion idea: Intro: the note slides up from the bottom and the status chips stamp on one by one. Scroll story: as each finding comes into view, a camera-scope circle wipes across the photo from top to bottom like the rod moving down the flue, and the status chip settles.
- Tools: Sweep interval planner, firewood dry-check, stove, insert or gas logs picker + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form ("visit type → appliance → last sweep → photos (optional) → how to reach you").
- Photo mood: Clean, even daylight, documentary: an inspection camera screen showing flue tile, a stove on a hearth pad, a stainless liner coil, a cap on a metal chimney, a moisture meter in a split log. Precise, uncluttered, nothing cozy-staged.
