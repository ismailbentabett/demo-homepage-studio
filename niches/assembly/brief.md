# Furniture Assembly — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
- **Who calls:** renters and new owners aged 26-45 in apartments, condos and townhouses; new parents with a crib
  in a box and a due date; people who work from home and just bought a standing desk; older homeowners (60+) whose
  kids live far away and who should not be on a ladder; landlords and building managers turning over a unit.
- **What just happened:** the delivery came. Six boxes are standing in the hallway, the move-in date was last week,
  the old bed went to the curb, a 65-inch TV is leaning against the wall, or the baby arrives in three weeks.
  Often they already tried: the dresser is half built, a cam lock spun out, and there are four screws left over.
- **What they are afraid of (their words):**
  1. "They'll strip the particleboard and the drawers will never close right."
  2. "The TV will fall off the wall, or they'll drill into a pipe or a wire."
  3. "They'll show up without the right tools, or not show up at all."
  4. "It'll cost more than the furniture did."
  5. "My building wants an insurance certificate and an elevator booking, and nobody tells me that until the day."
- **What makes them trust:** a price per item or a clear hourly rate before booking; a photo checklist ("send us
  the box label and the wall"); someone who brings the drill, the level, the stud finder and the wall anchors; a
  promise to anchor tall pieces to the wall; boxes and foam taken away at the end.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:** a site that hides prices behind a form; a cartoon of a man buried in
  boxes; a phone number that only appears in the footer; "handyman services" with no word about furniture; a
  booking calendar that shows nothing for three weeks.

## 2. The job
- **Planned vs urgent:** almost all planned, but with a deadline (move-in, a baby, guests, a lease start, the
  first workday at the new desk). Same-week is what people want; nobody needs a midnight call.
- **Seasonality (East Coast, Arlington and Jersey City):** Jan: home gym gear and treadmills after the holidays.
  Feb-Mar: quiet, office furniture, bookcases. Apr-May: patio sets, grills, playsets and sheds. Jun-Aug: the moving
  peak (government and military moves in Arlington, lease turnovers in Jersey City), beds, dressers, wardrobes
  every day. Late Aug-Sep: dorm and kids' rooms, Sept 1 lease starts. Oct: steady. Nov-Dec: TVs, gifts, bikes,
  guest beds before the holidays.
- **Seasonality (West Coast, San Jose):** dry and mild most of the year, so outdoor work runs March to October;
  rainy November to March moves work indoors (office setups, anchoring, home gyms). Spring is playset season.
- **Ticket bands:** small (one nightstand, one TV mount), medium (a bedroom: bed, two dressers, a mirror), large
  (a whole move-in: 10-20 boxes, a wardrobe system, a desk, TVs), project (a backyard playset, a home gym, a full
  office of desks and chairs).
- **Services that sell:** flat-pack furniture assembly, beds and cribs, office desks and chairs, TV and wall
  mounting, anchoring tall furniture to the wall, outdoor furniture and playsets, gym equipment, box and packing
  haul-away, taking furniture apart before a move.
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**
  | Homeowner says | Pro says |
  |---|---|
  | "the drawers won't close straight" | out of square carcass, drawer slides misaligned |
  | "the little round lock things" | cam locks and cam bolts |
  | "the wooden pegs" | dowels |
  | "the thin back board" | hardboard back panel, needs to be squared and nailed |
  | "hang the TV" | wall mount, fixed / tilt / full-motion bracket |
  | "find the beam in the wall" | locate the stud, toggle into drywall, sleeve anchor in concrete |
  | "hide the wires" | in-wall cable kit, power bridge, cord cover |
  | "stop it falling on the kids" | anti-tip kit, furniture anchoring to a stud |
  | "the swing set" | playset, wood swing set kit, 4x4 posts, ground anchors |
  | "the bike that doesn't go anywhere" | stationary bike, rower, treadmill deck and console |
  | "that closet system" | wardrobe frame with hinged doors, rail and soft-close hinges |
  | "it wobbles" | loose fittings, uneven floor, needs leveling feet or shims |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)
1. A clip-art man buried under boxes, or a cartoon hex key as the logo.
2. "Quality you can trust" headlines over a stock photo of a smiling couple and a sofa.
3. A big price-request form with 14 fields and no number anywhere on the page.
4. Photos of a brand's showroom or packaging with the logo showing.
5. "Handyman services" as a long list of every trade, with furniture as one bullet.
6. A booking widget that sends you to a third-party marketplace.
7. Before/after pairs that are clearly two different rooms.
8. A wall of "5 stars!!" reviews with no job, no item and no town.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Item counter** — learn: a sample time band and sample price for the whole order. Inputs: tap items (bed,
   dresser, desk, wardrobe, TV, crib, playset) and a count. Output: "about 3-4 hours, sample $280-$420". Leads to:
   "Book this list" button that fills the quote form.
2. **Wall checker** — learn: what holds a TV on their wall. Inputs: wall type (drywall, plaster, brick, concrete,
   glass-and-beam), TV size. Output: the anchor method, mount type and a sample range. Leads to: "Send a photo of
   the wall" by text.
3. **Box-label reader** — learn: what the installer needs to know before coming. Inputs: number of boxes, weight
   label, floor and elevator. Output: a short "what to tell us" note and whether two people are needed. Leads to: SMS
   with the note pre-filled.
4. **Tip-over check** — learn: which of their pieces should be anchored. Inputs: height of the piece, kids or pets,
   wall type. Output: anchor yes/no and how. Leads to: "Add anchoring to my visit".
5. **Move-in day planner** — learn: what order things get built. Inputs: move-in date, rooms. Output: a day plan
   (beds first, then dressers, then TV and desk). Leads to: booking that date.
6. **Building checklist** — learn: what an apartment building needs (elevator booking, insurance certificate,
   work hours). Inputs: building type. Output: a checklist with what the company sends. Leads to: the call.
7. **Playset sizing** — learn: how much yard a kit needs and how long it takes. Inputs: kit footprint, ground slope.
   Output: a clearance sketch and a sample day count. Leads to: booking a spring date.

Prices in every tool are ranges from facts.proof.prices only, always labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — From six boxes in the hall to a room you can sleep in, step by step   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Dowel & Cam Assembly · Arlington, VA · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Every flat-pack box hides the same honest process, and most people only see the end of it. Dowel &
  Cam shows the visit as numbered steps, the way a good manual should have been written: open and count the parts on
  a blanket, square the frame, set the cam locks, hang and adjust the doors, anchor it to a stud, take every box and
  every bag of foam away. Arlington moves a lot (summer moves, short leases, condo towers along the Orange Line), so
  the promise is a bedroom that is done on move-in day, not a weekend lost.
- Tone lead: light-led (instruction-sheet white and a warm unbleached-paper cream, graphite line drawings, one
  cam-lock zinc gray and a single signal color, a clear tomato red, for step numbers and actions).
- Type lead: grotesk (a plain, well-spaced grotesk for headlines and steps, tabular figures for part counts and
  times; nothing decorative, like a manual that respects you).
- Hero family: 12 — sticky photo, scrolling text (one tall photo of a half-built dresser on a blanket with its
  parts sorted in rows stays pinned while three lines scroll past: "Six boxes in the hall." "Parts counted on a
  blanket." "Built, anchored, boxes gone." The first line is visible without scrolling).
- Signature seed: **the page is the manual that should have come in the box.** Every section is a numbered step with
  a small hand-drawn exploded view in the margin (a frame, a drawer, a door hinge, a wall anchor) and a hardware
  tally like "bag C: 12 cam bolts, 12 dowels", only these tallies count the visit (rooms, hours, boxes out). The last
  step is the empty hallway with the call button.
- Motion idea: intro: the exploded-view drawing of a dresser in the margin slides together part by part as the
  headline appears. Scroll story: as you pass each step, its line drawing assembles itself (DrawSVG) and the step
  number gets a small check.
- Tools: move-in day planner, tip-over check, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: soft daylight in apartment bedrooms and living rooms, close-ups of hands with hex keys and a drill,
  parts sorted neatly on a rug or blanket, half-built frames, plain walls; calm, orderly, top-down and three-quarter
  views, no faces, no brand packaging.

### B — Every item has a price and a time before anyone rings the buzzer   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Piece Count Assembly · Jersey City, NJ · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Jersey City people live in towers along the river and in walk-up brownstones in the Heights, and
  both come with rules: concrete walls that eat drywall anchors, freight elevators that need booking, managers who
  ask for an insurance certificate. Piece Count answers all of it before the call: tap your items and see the sample
  time and price, pick your wall and see how the TV goes up, pick your building and see the checklist. The proof is
  the numbers and the answers, not adjectives.
- Tone lead: dark-led (a deep tower-night blue-black, concrete gray panels, one sharp signal yellow for numbers,
  totals and actions, white type).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed sans for headlines, item names and big numbers, like labels on a stack of
  boxes; a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: 7 — tool-first (the item counter is the hero: a dark board of tappable items, bed, dresser,
  desk, wardrobe, TV, crib, with a running total "about 3-4 hours, sample range" beside the promise "Price your list
  in 30 seconds." With JS off, it is a readable price list and the phone number).
- Signature seed: **the receipt that writes itself.** One running receipt lives at the right edge of the page (bottom
  sheet on phones): as you use any tool (items, wall type, building checklist, haul-away), lines print onto it with
  time and sample price. At the end of the page it tears off as the quote form, already filled in.
- Motion idea: intro: the item tiles slide up in a quick stagger and the receipt prints its header line. Scroll
  story: each section's answer prints one line onto the receipt as it enters, with tabular numbers ticking up.
- Tools: item counter, wall checker, building checklist, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: city apartments at evening and night, warm lamp light against cool windows, concrete and brick walls,
  TVs mounted level with no wires showing, a drill and a level on a box, stacked flat-pack boxes in an elevator lobby
  or hallway; sharp, graphic, no faces, no brand logos, no readable building signs.

### C — Built for the house on the fault line: anchored, mounted, ready for the next shake   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Post & Beam Assembly Co. · San Jose, CA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: San Jose is Eichlers with glass walls and beams instead of studs, ranch houses in Cambrian and
  Willow Glen, and townhouses full of home offices. It is also earthquake country, where a tall bookcase or a TV
  that is not anchored is a real risk for a toddler. Post & Beam tells that local story room by room around the
  house: the home office (standing desk, monitor arm), the kids' room (crib, bunk bed, anchored dresser), the living
  room (a TV on a post-and-beam wall), the garage gym, and the dry-season backyard (the playset).
- Tone lead: color-led (a mid-century palette carried as big fields: warm redwood, an olive green, a sun-faded
  mustard, with cream paper and a deep charcoal for text).
- Type lead: serif (a warm mid-century display serif with character for headlines and room names; a clean sans for
  body and numbers).
- Hero family: 11 — diagonal split (the seam is the low pitch of an Eichler gable roof: the headline sits under
  the roofline on redwood, and the photo of a sunlit glass-walled room with a mounted TV and an anchored bookcase sits
  on the other side).
- Signature seed: **the house plan around the atrium.** The page is laid out as a mid-century floor plan with an
  open atrium in the middle: each section is a room on the plan (office, kids' room, living room, garage, yard) and a
  small plan in the corner shows where you are. Every tall item in every room carries a little drawn strap mark, the
  anchoring promise made visible.
- Motion idea: intro: the roofline seam draws itself across the hero and the photo slides in under it. Scroll story:
  the floor-plan nav lights each room as you enter, and a tall bookcase drawing leans, then a strap snaps it upright,
  once, when the anchoring section arrives (static with reduced motion).
- Tools: tip-over check, playset sizing, wall checker (with "glass and beam" as a wall type), + ZIP checker +
  multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: California daylight, clean mid-century rooms with wood beams and big glass, warm wood tones, home
  offices with a desk by a window, backyards with dry grass and a wooden playset, golden afternoon; no faces, no
  readable signs, no snowy or tropical scenes.
