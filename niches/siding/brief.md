# Siding — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 35-75 who own the house for the long run, plus some about to sell. Four main types: (1) owners of 1950s-1990s houses with faded, cracked or wavy vinyl or hardboard; (2) owners after a hail or wind storm with dents, holes or panels blown off (often with an insurance claim open); (3) owners of older New England houses (cedar clapboard, shingles, triple-deckers) where paint keeps failing and boards rot; (4) people who want lower heating bills and a quieter house and have heard about insulated siding or new wrap.

**What just happened.**
- A spring hail storm dented one side of the house and the neighbors are getting new siding.
- A strong wind tore off a few panels and the house wrap (or bare sheathing) is showing.
- The painter said the clapboards are too far gone to paint again.
- Ice dams last winter left water stains inside, and the trim and the bottom courses are soft.
- They are selling next year and the faded siding is the first thing buyers see.

**What they fear (in their words).**
- "They'll cover rotten wood with new siding and it'll rot underneath."
- "It'll look cheap and wavy, like a rental."
- "The insurance check won't cover it and they'll want cash for the rest."
- "They'll leave nails and pieces of old siding in the yard for months."
- "It'll take weeks and the house will be wrapped in plastic all that time."

**What makes them trust.**
- A clear sequence: tear-off, check the sheathing, wrap, flashing, then siding and trim.
- Honest talk about the wood they find under the old siding, priced before it starts.
- Real material comparisons (vinyl, insulated vinyl, fiber cement, engineered wood, cedar) with prices per square foot.
- Close-ups of their work: straight courses, tidy corners, flashing at windows.
- Sample colors and profiles they can see on houses near them.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A stock photo of a brand-new suburban mansion; manufacturer logos all over; "lifetime" claims in big type; no prices or ranges; a form that asks for their insurance details first; storm-chaser language ("we're in your area after the storm, act now"); a gallery of other trades' work.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Mostly planned, 2-8 weeks out. After storms, repairs of torn or dented panels get scheduled first, and the site says how quickly they can come look; no emergency service is offered. Insurance work is planned too: they inspect, write the scope and the owner talks to the insurer.

**Seasonality by month.**
| Month | Minneapolis (hail, ice dams, deep cold) | Hartford (four seasons, mixed housing) | Providence (salt air, nor'easters, triple-deckers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Estimates and planning; vinyl gets brittle in deep cold, so little install work | Estimates, small repairs on mild days | Estimates; nor'easter repairs on mild days |
| Mar-Apr | Ice-dam damage shows up; scheduling for the season | Install season starts; storm repairs | Install season starts; shingles and trim after winter |
| May-Jun | Hail season: inspections and repair scopes; full re-sides start | Full re-sides; color consults | Full re-sides, cedar shingles |
| Jul-Aug | Peak installs, hail claims | Peak installs | Peak installs, summer salt air |
| Sep-Oct | Push to finish before frost | Peak installs, fall colors for consults | Push to finish before nor'easter season |
| Nov-Dec | Last jobs, quick repairs before snow | Slowing, repairs | Storm repairs, planning for spring |

**Average ticket bands.**
- Repairs: a few panels, a corner, a dented side or a rotten trim board, around $450-$2,500.
- Soffit, fascia and trim jobs: around $1,200-$7,500.
- Full re-side of a typical house: vinyl around $14,000-$28,000, insulated vinyl around $18,000-$34,000, fiber cement around $24,000-$48,000; a Providence triple-decker around $28,000-$55,000; cedar shingles and clapboard sit at the top of the range per square foot.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Full re-sides in vinyl, insulated vinyl, fiber cement, engineered wood and cedar; hail and wind repair; soffit and fascia; window and door trim; rotten sheathing and trim replacement; house wrap and flashing; color consults; cedar shingle and clapboard repair on older houses; triple-decker re-sides.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "the boards on the outside" | siding, cladding, courses |
| "how much siding" | squares (100 sq ft of wall), takeoff |
| "the plastic stuff" | vinyl lap, Dutch lap, insulated vinyl |
| "the cement board" | fiber cement, lap plank |
| "the wood under it" | sheathing (plywood, OSB, boards) |
| "the paper / the wrap" | house wrap, weather-resistive barrier (WRB) |

| "the part under the roof edge" | soffit, fascia |
| "the corners" | outside corner posts, corner boards |
| "how wide the boards look" | reveal, exposure (e.g. 4 in., 7 in.) |
| "the shingles on the walls" | cedar shingles, sidewall shingles, coursing |
| "the fancy round shingles" | fishscale (fancy-cut) shingles |
| "the dents from the storm" | hail impact damage, storm scope |

**Honest limits to say on every site.** Prices are typical ranges by size and wall condition; rotten sheathing is priced after tear-off shows it. On insurance work, the homeowner talks to the insurer; the contractor writes the scope and photos. Older houses (pre-1978) may have lead paint on trim and clapboards, which changes how old paint is handled; the site mentions it plainly without claiming a credential.

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A hero photo of a brand-new house in a showroom-bright suburb that looks nothing like the local streets.
2. Manufacturer logos in a row as the main proof.
3. "Lifetime" promises in huge type and starbursts.
4. No prices, not even per square foot.
5. Storm-chaser urgency: "Hail in your area! Act now!"
6. A gallery of roofs, windows and gutters mixed in with the siding.
7. Nothing about the sheathing, wrap or flashing behind the siding.
8. A color chart image with no help choosing.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Squares calculator.** Inputs: house footprint or wall lengths, height (stories), gable ends, number of windows and doors. Output: wall area, squares (100 sq ft) with a waste allowance, and a sample price range per material from facts.proof.prices only, labelled "sample".
2. **Material compare.** Vinyl, insulated vinyl, fiber cement, engineered wood, cedar side by side: price per sq ft (from facts, "sample"), how it handles hail, salt air and cold, upkeep (paint or not), and the look. Five plain criteria, no brand names.
3. **Elevation picker.** Click the side of the house (front, back, left, right, gable) on a simple drawing to mark damage or what to replace; it builds the request list for the quote form.
4. **Course and reveal visualizer.** A wall drawing that switches between 4 in., 5 in. and 7 in. reveals, Dutch lap, board and batten and shingles, so the owner sees the look.
5. **Shingle pattern book.** Cedar shingle patterns (straight, staggered, fishscale, diamond, hexagon) shown by house era, with where each was used on local houses.
6. **Storm damage checklist.** What to photograph after hail or wind (each side, close-ups with a coin for scale, the date), what to tell the insurer, what to hold off on. Plain and calm.
7. **Color by street.** A few sample color combinations (body, trim, accent) shown on a drawn house of the local type (rambler, colonial, triple-decker).
8. **ZIP checker** + **multi-step quote form** (every site).

## 6. The three directions

### A — Courses go on in order, and you see every layer   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Coursework Siding Co. · Minneapolis, MN · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Minneapolis walls take hail in summer, ice dams in winter and a 120-degree swing in temperature over the year. Coursework's story is the wall itself, built up layer by layer: tear-off, sheathing checked and replaced where soft, wrap lapped like shingles, flashing at every window and at the base, starter strip, courses one by one, then trim. The homepage builds a wall from bare studs to finished siding as you scroll, and each layer is a section with what they check and why it matters in a Minnesota winter.
- Tone lead: **dark-led** (deep blue-charcoal like a winter evening as the field, white and pale frost-gray text, a single warm accent like a lit window, and the siding colors as the "material" in drawings).
- Type lead: **grotesk** (a strong, plain grotesk for headlines and labels, set large and tight; a readable sans for body).
- Hero family: **11 — diagonal split** (one side a dark photo of a house at dusk with straight new siding, the other side a drawn wall section; the split runs on the same slope as a roof line, with the headline on the drawn side: "Built in courses. Checked in layers.").
- Signature seed: **the page is built in 7-inch lap courses.** Every section sits on a band that reads like a siding course: a thin shadow line under its top edge (the drip of the course above), a row of tiny nail marks at the top where the next course will cover them, and a fixed 7-inch rhythm (scaled) to the spacing. Services, reviews and prices sit "on" courses; the process section shows the wall layers (studs, sheathing, wrap, flashing, starter, courses, trim) stacked in that same rhythm. The footer is the top course with the trim board.
- Motion idea: intro: the diagonal split opens and the drawn wall builds from studs to siding in a quick sequence. Scroll story: each new section slides up like a course being set and its shadow line falls into place under the one above; the nail marks disappear as the next course covers them.
- Tools: squares calculator, storm damage checklist (calm, no urgency), course and reveal visualizer, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: houses at dusk with lit windows, crisp new siding in straight lines, winter light, frost; close-ups of wrap, flashing and corners; crews from behind on scaffolds; no faces, no logos, no house numbers.

### B — Five tests, every material, written down   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Fieldtest Siding Co. · Hartford, CT · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Hartford owners compare: their street has vinyl colonials, cedar capes and old Victorian two-families side by side. Fieldtest answers the question "which siding for my house?" like a lab report: each material is scored on five plain criteria (hail and impact, cold and moisture, upkeep, look, price per square foot), the visitor measures their house with a squares calculator and clicks the sides they want done on an elevation drawing. Every number is labelled "sample" and comes from the price list.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (clean report white and pale gray as the field, ink text, one signal color for scores and the "result" marks, small material swatches as the only other color).
- Type lead: **mono-accent** (a monospace for every reading, score, measurement and price; a clear sans for headlines and body).
- Hero family: **6 — editorial cover** (like the cover of a test report or a magazine issue: a big headline "Which siding for a Hartford house? We tested five.", a single strong photo of a siding wall close-up, issue-style labels for the five materials and the sample price range).
- Signature seed: **the page is a material test report with 5 criteria.** Every section is a report page: a header row (test no., material, criterion), a reading in monospace, and a small bar out of 5. The material compare is the core table; services are "test results" for each job; reviews are "field notes" with the house type and town; prices are the report's price sheet, labelled "sample". The footer is the report's sign-off: "Next step: measure your house."
- Motion idea: intro: the cover label lines type in one after another, and the five score bars fill. Scroll story: report pages stack and lift like sheets on a clipboard; score bars fill when each criterion row enters view.
- Tools: squares calculator, material compare (five criteria), elevation picker, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: close, even daylight on siding textures (vinyl lap, fiber cement, cedar, engineered wood) side by side, measuring tapes, swatches on a wall, New England colonials and capes; calm, precise, no faces.

### C — The pattern book of a Providence street   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Fishscale & Clapboard Co. · Providence, RI · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Providence is a city of triple-deckers, Victorians with fishscale shingles in the gables, Greek Revival clapboard and shingle-style houses near the bay. Salt air off Narragansett Bay and nor'easters wear the paint, split the shingles and rot the bottom courses. Fishscale & Clapboard knows the patterns: what was used in which era, how to match a run of shingles on a Fox Point cottage, how to re-side a triple-decker without making it look flat. The homepage is a pattern book of the city's walls by era, with the fixes each needs.
- Tone lead: **color-led** (a deep harbor teal or painted-lady sage as the field, with cream, salmon-pink and a weathered cedar color as the house palette; the colors of real painted Providence houses).
- Type lead: **serif** (a refined serif with some 19th-century character for headlines and era titles; a simple sans for body and labels).
- Hero family: **3 — type mask** (the word "Clapboard" or "Fishscale" set huge, with a photo of shingles or clapboard showing through the letters, and the headline below: "Old Providence walls, matched course by course.").
- Signature seed: **the page is a shingle pattern book by era.** Each section is a plate in the book with an era title (1850s Greek Revival clapboard, 1880s Queen Anne fishscale, 1900s triple-decker, 1920s shingle style) and a drawn pattern swatch (straight courses, fishscale, diamond, staggered, clapboard reveal) as its background band. Services are matched to the plates, reviews come from the house of that era, prices are the plate's caption, labelled "sample". The footer is the book's index with the ZIP checker.
- Motion idea: intro: the masked word fills in with the photo, then the pattern swatches fade in under it. Scroll story: each plate's pattern draws in row by row, like shingles being laid from the bottom up, and the era title turns like a page.
- Tools: shingle pattern book (era and pattern explorer), color by street (triple-decker and Victorian palettes), squares calculator, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: painted Providence-style houses in soft coastal light, fishscale gables, weathered cedar shingles, crisp new clapboard, triple-deckers on a hill, salt-gray mornings by the bay; no faces, no house numbers or readable signs.
