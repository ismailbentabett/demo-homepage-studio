# Roofer — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
- **Who calls:** homeowners 35-70 in hail country (Kansas, North Texas, central Oklahoma). Mostly owners of 1950s-1990s brick or siding ranch houses and newer tract homes with cut-up hip roofs. Two big groups: (a) a family that just had a storm roll through and sees granules in the downspout splash, a dented vent, or a neighbor's dumpster; (b) an older owner with a 20+ year roof and a ceiling stain who has been putting it off.
- **What just happened:** a hail or wind event (April-June is peak), a water stain after a heavy rain, a door-knocker in a pickup who said "you have damage", an insurance renewal letter asking the roof's age, or a home sale inspection.
- **Fears (their words):**
  1. "Some storm chaser from out of state takes my deposit and disappears."
  2. "They'll tell me I need a whole roof when it's one leak."
  3. "Will insurance even pay, and what if they deny it after the roof is torn off?"
  4. "Nails all over my driveway and my kid's bike tires."
  5. "They'll cover up rotten wood and I'll never know."
- **What makes them trust:** photos of *their* roof (not a brochure), a local phone number and a local office/town named, someone who explains the claim process without pushing to sign, a clear written price with squares counted, the crew cleaning up with a magnet, a named person who answers after the job.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:** "Storm damage? Call now!!!" banners and countdown timers, stock photo of a handshake or a smiling crew in hard hats, no prices or even ranges, "serving all 50 states" energy, a form that asks for the insurance claim number before saying anything useful, no phone number visible on mobile.

## 2. The job
- **Emergency vs planned:** roughly 40/60. Emergencies are active leaks, blown-off sections and limbs through the deck after storms (tarping first, repair later). Planned work is replacement at end of life (shingles last ~15-22 years in this sun and hail), insurance-funded replacement after a hail claim, and pre-sale repairs.
- **Seasonality (Southern Plains):**
  - Jan-Feb: ice and freeze; ice storms bring limbs down (central Oklahoma had a severe one in late Oct 2020); slow sales, inspections, leak calls after thaw.
  - Mar: wind season starts; lifted tabs, ridge caps off; hail can start late March.
  - Apr-Jun: peak hail and severe storms; phone volume 3-5x; storm chasers flood the area; claims filed.
  - Jul-Aug: heat; crews start at dawn; replacements from spring claims; attic heat and ventilation talk.
  - Sep-Oct: second smaller storm window; best installing weather; people rushing to finish before winter.
  - Nov-Dec: last replacements, leak repairs, gutter and ice-and-water work.
- **Average tickets (words):** small (pipe boot, a few shingles, flashing reseal), medium (leak repair with decking, one slope re-roofed, skylight), large (full tear-off and replacement), very large (impact-resistant or metal roof on a big hip roof). Insurance claims push many jobs into the large band.
- **Services that sell (from facts):** roof inspection, leak repair, hail and wind damage repair, storm tarping, full replacement, impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles, metal roofing, flashing/chimney work, skylights, low-slope coating, ventilation, gutters with a new roof.
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**
  | Homeowner says | Pro says |
  |---|---|
  | "leak in the ceiling" | flashing failure / pipe boot cracked |
  | "the little rocks in the gutter" | granule loss |
  | "dents from hail" | hail bruising / fractured mat |
  | "shingles flapping" | unsealed tabs / wind lift |
  | "the wood under the roof" | decking / sheathing (OSB or plank) |
  | "the black paper" | underlayment (felt or synthetic) |
  | "the metal along the edge" | drip edge |
  | "the hump on top" | ridge / ridge cap / ridge vent |
  | "the part where the roof meets the wall" | step flashing / counterflashing |
  | "the rubber thing around the pipe" | pipe boot / plumbing vent flashing |
  | "new roof" | tear-off and re-roof / replacement |
  | "how big is my roof" | how many squares (1 square = 100 sq ft) |
  | "hail-proof shingles" | Class 4 impact-resistant (UL 2218) |
  | "the money insurance holds back" | depreciation / recoverable depreciation |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)
1. Full-width stock photo of a smiling crew in hard hats and a "Quality You Can Trust" headline.
2. Storm-panic banner: red bar, siren icon, "FREE INSPECTION — CALL NOW".
3. A row of association and manufacturer badges and "#1 roofer" claims that nobody can check.
4. Pickup truck with the logo wrap as the hero.
5. Drone shot of a generic suburb with a tilted horizon.
6. Before/after slider of two unrelated stock roofs.
7. "We handle everything with your insurance!" with no explanation of what that means.
8. A 12-field form that asks for the claim number before giving any information.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Roof size and sample price estimator** — learns roughly how many squares the roof is and the sample price band. Inputs: house footprint (sq ft) or bedrooms, roof shape (gable/hip/cut-up), pitch (walkable/steep). Output: squares range (footprint × pitch factor × waste 10-15%) and the matching sample range from `facts.proof.prices`. Leads to: "Get your real count: we measure on site" → quote form prefilled with the squares.
2. **After-the-storm ground check** — learns what to look for safely from the ground. Inputs: a checklist (granules at downspouts, dented gutters/vents, shingles in the yard, ceiling stains, dented car/AC fins). Output: a "worth an inspection / probably fine / call now" verdict with the reasons. Leads to: inspection request with the ticked items attached.
3. **Repair or replace sorter** — inputs: roof age, number of leaks, curling/missing shingles, last storm. Output: "repair likely", "on the edge", "plan replacement", each with why. Leads to: call or form with the answer attached.
4. **Material compare** — slider or tabs between architectural shingle, Class 4 shingle, standing-seam metal: lifespan band, hail behavior, look, sample price band from facts. Leads to: "price my roof in this material".
5. **Insurance claim timeline** — a step-by-step explainer (storm → inspection → claim filed → adjuster meeting → first check minus depreciation → build → final invoice → depreciation released). Honest, no promises. Texas version notes that contractors may not waive or pay a deductible (Texas Insurance Code ch. 707). Leads to: "Book the inspection before you call your insurer".
6. **Roof age quiz** — 5 visual questions (curling, granules, moss/algae streaks, nail pops, last replaced). Output: age band and what to watch.
7. **Hail season calendar** — month picker showing what happens to roofs that month in this region and what to check. No live weather data.
8. **ZIP checker** — confirms service area against `facts.biz.zips`.

All prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices`, always labelled "Sample pricing".

## 6. The three directions

### A — "A roof is a stack of layers; we show you every one before it is covered."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Felt & Flashing Roof Co. · Wichita, KS · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Most roofs fail at the layers nobody sees: rotten decking left in place, missing drip edge, flashing reused from 1994. Felt & Flashing photographs every layer as it goes on and hands the homeowner the stack. The page is built the way the roof is: from the deck up.
- Tone lead: color-led (a saturated barn-red/oxblood field, like fresh felt-paper stamping, with warm cream type and tar-black details)
- Type lead: condensed (tall narrow display, like lumber stamps and shingle-bundle wrappers; plain grotesk for body)
- Hero family: 9 horizontal filmstrip (a strip of seven frames, deck → drip edge → ice and water shield → underlayment → starter → shingles → ridge, advancing sideways under the headline)
- Signature seed: **The cross-section.** A vertical roof cross-section rides the left edge of the page; each section of the page *is* one layer in build order ("Layer 01 · Deck", "Layer 03 · Ice & water shield"...) and the cross-section adds that layer as you scroll. Services, process, reviews and the quote form are all "layers"; the footer is the ridge cap.
- Motion idea: Intro: the filmstrip advances frame by frame like a contact sheet being pulled, then settles with the shingle frame in the center. Scroll story: each layer of the side cross-section slides in from the right and locks onto the stack as its section reaches the top.
- Tools: Repair or replace sorter, Roof size and sample price estimator, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form (roof type → problem → photos optional → contact).
- Photo mood: hard Kansas midday light, close and mid distance, texture first (felt, nails, flashing edges), wide prairie sky in the hero strip; no faces, hands and backs only.

### B — "Counted before you sign: squares, findings, prices, deductible."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Squarecount Roofing · Fort Worth, TX · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Fort Worth homeowners get three bids with three different roof sizes. Squarecount shows the count: squares measured, every finding numbered with its photo, every line priced. The page reads like the inspection report they will receive.
- Tone lead: light-led (report paper white, graphite text, one red-pen accent for findings and stamps)
- Type lead: mono-accent (a clean sans for reading, monospace for measurements, finding numbers and stamps)
- Hero family: 14 document object as hero (an inspection report sheet: roof diagram with numbered pins, three photo thumbnails, a "Findings: 7 / Fix: 3 / Watch: 4" summary and the headline written as the report title)
- Signature seed: **The inspection report.** Every section is a numbered finding (F-01, F-02...) with a pin on a small roof plan, a photo, a measurement in mono and a status stamp (OK / WATCH / FIX). Services are findings, the price table is the "line items" page, reviews are "signed-off" notes, the form is the "request a report" cover sheet.
- Motion idea: Intro: the report slides up from below like paper from a printer, pins drop onto the roof plan, then the stamps land with a short press. Scroll story: as each finding enters, a thin leader line draws from its pin on a sticky roof plan to the section's photo.
- Tools: Roof size and sample price estimator (the hero tool's little brother), Insurance claim timeline (Texas deductible note), Material compare, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: overcast or soft morning light, documentary close-ups (hail bruises with a chalk circle, a measuring tape on shingles, a cracked pipe boot), top-down and square-on framing, nothing glamorous.

### C — "Built for the sky over central Oklahoma."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Hailward Roofing · Oklahoma City, OK · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Central Oklahoma roofs live through hail in spring, straight-line wind, summer heat and ice that brings trees down. Hailward tells that year honestly, month by month, and shows what each season does to a roof and what they do about it. The page is an almanac of the sky over the metro.
- Tone lead: dark-led (storm-slate blue-black with a cold hail-white and one warm prairie-gold accent for the "clear sky" moments)
- Type lead: serif (an almanac serif with sturdy italics for month names; small grotesk for labels)
- Hero family: 15 map / area-led hero (a stylized map of the OKC metro, Edmond to Norman, Yukon to Midwest City, with the service towns named and a seasonal ring around it; headline set over the open prairie edge)
- Signature seed: **The almanac.** The page is a year of central-Oklahoma weather: each section is a month band (MAR wind · APR-JUN hail · JUL heat · OCT ice · DEC freeze) with what that weather does to a roof, the photo of it, and the matching service. A month ring in the corner shows where you are in the year.
- Motion idea: Intro: a soft storm-cell shadow drifts across the map from southwest to northeast, and the town names light up behind it. Scroll story: the month ring turns one notch per section and the page background shifts from storm slate to clear gold and back.
- Tools: After-the-storm ground check, Hail season calendar, Material compare (Class 4 focus with "ask your insurer about a premium discount"), ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: big skies, late-afternoon storm light, dark anvil clouds over brick ranch houses, wet shingles after rain, hail stones in a palm (hand only), ice-glazed branches; wide and moody, never disaster porn.
