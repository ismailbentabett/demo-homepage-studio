# Tattoo & Piercing — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
**Who looks.** Mostly 19-45. Three kinds: the first-timer who has saved a drawing on their phone for two years
and is nervous about everything; the collector with four pieces already, judging line work and healed photos in
seconds; the parent booking a first lobe piercing for a 10-year-old, or a teen's helix with a parent's signature.
Plus the cover-up client: a name, an old tribal band, a faded script they want gone.

**What just happened.** A birthday, a breakup, a baby, a death in the family, a trip coming up, a tax refund. Or
an old tattoo in the mirror they cannot look at anymore. Or a piercing that went bad at a mall kiosk and they want a
studio this time.

**Fears, in their words.**
- "Is it clean? Are the needles new? I don't want hepatitis from a back room."
- "Will the artist actually draw MY idea, or push their own thing, or copy something off Pinterest?"
- "What will it cost and why do they want a deposit before I've even seen a drawing?"
- "Will it blur in five years? Fine line looks great on Instagram the day it's done."
- "Can you even cover this? I've been told no twice."
- "Will they make me feel stupid for asking questions / for how my body looks / for crying?"

**What makes them trust.**
- Healed photos (not just fresh, shiny, filtered ones) of the exact style they want.
- A plain hygiene answer: single-use needles opened in front of you, an autoclave with a test log, gloves changed.
- A clear booking path: consult, deposit amount, how the deposit is used, how long the drawing takes, reschedule rule.
- An artist who names what they will not do (too small, too fine, too close to an old piece) and why.
- Piercing done with a needle, not a gun, with implant-grade jewelry named by material.

**Leave in 5 seconds when.** Instagram embed that won't load, a black page with skull clip art, no prices at all,
"DM to book" with no other path, autoplay metal music, no word about cleanliness, a gallery of fresh red swollen work.

## 2. The job
**Planned vs urgent.** Nearly all planned (consult, then a date 2-8 weeks out). The only "today" jobs are flash
walk-ins, lobe piercings and a piercing that needs a jewelry downsize or a check (swelling, a bump). Nothing is an
emergency; a site that sounds urgent reads wrong.

**Season by month (Northeast: Brooklyn, Philadelphia).** Jan-Mar busiest for large pieces (long sleeves, no sun,
no swimming, new-year money and resolutions); April-May booking for summer pieces; June-Aug walk-ins, small pieces
and piercings up, big sessions down (sun and beaches slow healing); late Aug back-to-school lobe and cartilage
piercings; Oct-Nov sleeves and back pieces start again; Dec gift cards and the holiday booking rush.
**Southern California (Long Beach).** Steady year round; the beach is the healing problem all year (no ocean or
pool for 2-3 weeks, sun on fresh ink); summer leans to small flash and piercings, winter to bigger pieces.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: a lobe piercing, a tiny flash piece, a touch-up (the shop minimum). Mid: a palm-size
custom piece, a fine-line script, a cartilage piercing with titanium jewelry. Large: a cover-up, a half sleeve in
several sittings. Top: a full sleeve or back piece, sold by the hour or the day over months.

**Services that sell.** Custom tattoos, fine line, black & grey realism, color, cover-ups, touch-ups, flash walk-ins,
ear piercing, body piercing, jewelry upgrades, consultations, aftercare checks.

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)
1. A black page, a gothic blackletter logo, a skull or rose clip art, and red accents.
2. An Instagram feed embed as the whole portfolio (fresh, filtered, glossy work; nothing healed).
3. "Walk-ins welcome!" with no hours, no prices, no minimum.
4. A wall of 300 thumbnails with no style labels and no artist names.
5. Hygiene buried in an FAQ, or missing.
6. "Book now" goes to a form with 20 fields and no deposit explanation.
7. A photo of the artist face-on with arms crossed as the hero.
8. Aftercare as a downloadable PDF nobody opens.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Size & sitting estimator.** Inputs: placement (forearm, calf, ribs...), size (palm, hand, half sleeve), style.
   Output: a band of sittings and a sample price range from `proof.prices`, plus "fine line under 2 cm is not
   advised". Leads to: "Book a consult" with the answers pre-filled.
2. **Cover-up check.** Inputs: how dark the old piece is, its size, colors used, what they want. Output: one of three
   honest answers ("cover in one go", "needs a few laser sessions first", "rework instead of cover"), never a promise.
   Leads to: "Text us a photo".
3. **Healing timeline / aftercare day counter.** Input: session date (or piercing type). Output: what day 1, 3, 7,
   14, 30 look like, when to swim, when the touch-up window opens. Leads to: a check-in visit.
4. **Piercing picker (ear map).** Tap a spot on a drawn ear: name of the piercing, heal time, jewelry used (material),
   sample price, age rule. Leads to: walk-in hours or a booking.
5. **Deposit explainer.** A small ledger: deposit amount, what it holds (the date + drawing time), how it comes off the
   final price, reschedule rule. Leads to: confidence to book.
6. **Hygiene log.** The studio's sterilization steps as a checklist with sample test dates. Leads to: trust.
7. **Style finder.** Pick 3 of 9 swatches (fine line, bold traditional, black & grey, color...); output is which
   service fits and what to bring to the consult. Leads to: booking with the style named.
8. **Flash board.** Today's flash designs with size and a fixed sample price; tap to hold one for a walk-in slot.

## 6. The three directions

### A — Your idea, drawn three times before it touches skin   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Vellum & Needle Tattoo · Brooklyn, NY · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Brooklyn clients come in with a saved screenshot and leave with something nobody else has. Vellum &
  Needle shows the custom process as layers of tracing paper: the rough idea, the refined drawing, the stencil
  placed on the body, the finished line work, and the healed piece at the 6-week check. Each layer is a section of the
  page, and the visitor sees the drawing get more exact as they scroll, which answers "will you actually draw MY idea".
- Tone lead: light-led (warm tracing-paper white and pale vellum gray as the field, graphite and stencil-purple
  line work, a single deep ink black for type).
- Type lead: serif (a sharp, high-contrast display serif with a fine-line feel for headlines; a quiet grotesk for
  body and labels).
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo, scrolling text (one healed fine-line forearm piece stays put while three short
  statements scroll past: "Drawn for you." "Placed on you." "Healed with you.").
- Signature seed: **stacked tracing sheets.** Every section is a sheet of semi-transparent vellum layered over the one
  before, each with a small pencil register mark and sheet number (01 idea, 02 drawing, 03 stencil, 04 lines,
  05 healed). The stencil-purple line is the only accent and it appears only where something is "transferred".
- Motion idea: intro: a stencil-purple line draws itself across the first sheet, then the sheet settles over the
  photo. Scroll story: in the process section, five vellum sheets stack one onto the next, and the drawing beneath
  sharpens from sketch to clean line with each layer.
- Tools: size & sitting estimator, healing timeline, deposit explainer, + ZIP checker + multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: soft north-window daylight, pale and quiet; close crops of hands drawing on tracing paper, stencils on
  skin, fine line work, healed pieces on forearms and shoulders. Never shiny fresh blood, never a face.

### B — Clean you can check: every needle, every cycle, every price on the page   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Cycle Log Tattoo & Piercing · Philadelphia, PA · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Philadelphia clients ask the blunt question first: is it clean, and what will it cost. Cycle Log
  answers both with records instead of reassurance: the autoclave steps, the single-use needle opened in front of
  you, the implant-grade titanium list for piercings, the deposit math, and sample prices by size. The page reads
  like the studio's own log book, open on the counter.
- Tone lead: dark-led (deep graphite steel as the field, surgical-tray stainless highlights, an autoclave-indicator
  stripe color (the dark stripe that appears after a cycle) as the accent; white type).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a precise monospace for every reading, cycle number, gauge, price and time; a firm grotesk
  for headlines and body).
- Hero family: 14 document object (the hero is a session sheet: date, artist, placement, size band, needle lot "opened
  in front of client", deposit applied, sample total; each line is an anchor to the section that explains it).
- Signature seed: **indicator tape.** A strip of sterilization indicator tape runs across every section break; its
  diagonal stripes darken as the section is read, like tape after a cycle. Proof blocks (reviews, prices, hygiene
  steps) are logged as numbered entries with a stamped check.
- Motion idea: intro: the session sheet's lines fill in one by one like a pen writing the form, then the "checked"
  stamp lands. Scroll story: the hygiene section runs one autoclave cycle (load, heat, hold, dry, log) with a gauge
  dial and the indicator stripe darkening as it completes.
- Tools: piercing picker (ear map with materials and heal times), size & sitting estimator, hygiene log, deposit
  explainer, + ZIP checker + multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: cool, clinical, crisp: steel trays, sealed needle packs, gloved hands, ink caps in a row, a piercing
  needle and titanium jewelry on a tray; even, bright light on a dark ground. No blood, no faces, no brand names on
  packs.

### C — Flash off the wall, a block from the water   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Crane Light Tattoo & Piercing · Long Beach, CA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Long Beach is a port town with sailor flash in its history, a long beach where people show their
  work all year, and a mix of neighborhoods from Belmont Shore to Signal Hill. Crane Light leans into the walk-in:
  a flash wall that changes each week, bold traditional and color pieces made to read from across a boardwalk, and
  aftercare written for people who live near salt water and sun.
- Tone lead: color-led (a sun-faded harbor teal field with flash-sheet red, mustard yellow and cream, like an old
  flash sheet under glass; black outlines).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed sans for headlines, like hand-painted shop lettering and dock stencils; a
  plain sans for body).
- Hero family: 8 photo mosaic (a tight grid of flash-wall tiles: a swallow on a calf, a ship on a forearm, ink caps,
  a pinned flash sheet, one big healed color piece; the headline sits on a cream tag in the grid).
- Signature seed: **the flash wall.** Every section is a flash sheet pinned to the wall with push pins, with a hand-
  numbered design code and a size/price tag; services are designs, reviews are notes tucked under the sheets, the
  booking form "holds" a design. A small harbor-crane silhouette marks the top of each sheet.
- Motion idea: intro: the mosaic tiles pin up one by one with a tiny tilt and settle straight. Scroll story: a row of
  flash sheets slides along the wall as you scroll (horizontal rail on desktop, stacked on phones) and each sheet's
  price tag flips over to show size and sitting time.
- Tools: flash board (hold a design for a walk-in), healing timeline written for beach and sun, cover-up check,
  + ZIP checker + multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: warm Southern California light, saturated color, bold traditional work on sun-browned skin (healed),
  flash sheets, the beach and harbor in soft late-afternoon haze; relaxed, outdoors-friendly. No faces, no shop signs.
