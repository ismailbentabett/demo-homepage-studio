# Windows & Doors — niche brief

Three Florida coasts, three reasons to change a window: Sarasota (Gulf barrier islands, salt air, mid-century and Old Florida
houses, impact glass that still has to look right), Naples (big glass, sliders to the lanai, hurricane season planned around,
homeowners who read the product label), Jacksonville (old wood windows in Riverside, Avondale and Springfield, oak canopy,
river and beach neighborhoods that each need a different answer). The page must sound like someone who has measured a lot of
openings on this coast and knows what the label on the glass means.

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 40-75, single-family homes and some condos. Many are seasonal or recent arrivals. Common situations:
- Hurricane season is coming and they are tired of putting up panels or have no shutters at all.
- A slider is hard to roll, leaks in sideways rain or has fogged glass between the panes.
- The insurance company or a home inspection asked about the openings (wind mitigation).
- They are selling or just bought, and the windows are original single-pane aluminum.
- A historic wood window is rattling, painted shut or rotting at the sill (Jacksonville).
- The AC runs all day and the west rooms are hot by 3 pm.

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
1. "They'll quote me a number and then the price goes up when they 'find something'."
2. "I'll pay a deposit and wait six months for windows that never show up."
3. "Impact windows will make my house look like an office building."
4. "They'll rip out my old wood windows when they could have been fixed."
5. "I don't know what any of the numbers on the sticker mean, so I can't compare quotes."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A measured quote: each opening listed with its size, product and price (a window schedule, not a lump sum).
2. Plain explanations of the label: design pressure, impact rating, U-factor, SHGC.
3. Honest lead times, with an order-by date for hurricane season.
4. Permits pulled and inspection passed, said plainly.
5. Repair offered before replace, especially for old wood windows.
6. Install photos of real homes like theirs: stucco, block, frame, historic.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** "Buy one get one" banners and a countdown clock; a giant stock photo of a glass skyscraper;
a mandatory "in-home consultation" with no prices at all; a site that only talks about one window brand; nothing about permits.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Almost all planned: measure, order, wait for manufacture, install. A broken slider glass or a door that
will not lock gets a quick board-up or repair call, but it is not the business. `emergency: false` on all three sites.

**Seasonality.**
| Month | Sarasota | Naples | Jacksonville |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Mar | Peak season: snowbirds ordering before they leave | Peak season, condo boards approve projects | Cool, good install weather, historic repairs |
| Apr-May | Last orders that can arrive before storms | Order-by window for hurricane season | Pollen, spring remodels |
| Jun-Nov | Hurricane season: storm-prep calls, shutter vs impact questions | Same; installs continue between storms | Storm season, afternoon rain, leak calls |
| Post-storm | Board-up, glass and seal repairs, then replacement surge | Replacement surge, longer lead times | Leak and rot repairs |
| Dec | Owners return, quotes for next year | Quotes, condo planning | Holiday lull, quotes |

Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. Impact products often take about 6 to 14 weeks from order to install (hedged:
manufacturers and permits vary), so a site should show an order-by date.

**Ticket bands (words).** A seal, glass or roller repair is a few hundred. One impact window is low thousands per opening. An
impact slider or French door is mid to high thousands. A whole house of impact openings is tens of thousands. Wood sash
restoration is mid hundreds per window.

**Services that sell.** Impact window replacement, impact sliding glass doors, entry doors, French doors, storm windows or
shutters compared with impact glass, wood window repair and restoration, glass and seal repair (fogged glass), slider rollers and
tracks, energy glass upgrades for hot west walls, custom sizes for older homes.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "Hurricane windows" | Impact-rated windows (large or small missile) |
| "The sticker" | NFRC label and product approval label |
| "How strong is it" | Design pressure (DP), positive and negative |
| "Fogged glass" | Failed insulated glass unit seal |
| "Slider is sticking" | Worn rollers, dirty or damaged track |
| "Water comes in when it rains sideways" | Water infiltration, sill pan, weep holes |
| "The hot room" | West or south exposure, high solar heat gain |
| "Tinted" | Low-E coating, low SHGC |
| "Old wavy glass" | Original cylinder glass in a wood sash |
| "The ropes are broken" | Broken sash cords, balance system |
| "Window size" | Rough opening vs unit size |
| "The frame is rotten" | Sill rot, replacement vs full-frame install |
| "Shutters" | Accordion, roll-down or panel storm protection |
| "Insurance discount paperwork" | Wind mitigation inspection, opening protection |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. "Buy one get one 40% off" banners, countdown timers, "sale ends Sunday".
2. A glass high-rise or a stock living room with a white sofa and floor-to-ceiling glass nobody here has.
3. A wall of manufacturer logos instead of their own installs.
4. "Call for a free in-home consultation", and not one price on the page.
5. Hurricane imagery used to scare: storm radar, smashed glass, flooded streets.
6. One generic page for every house type; nothing about historic wood windows, condos or block homes.
7. No mention of permits, inspections or lead times.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Measure your opening.** Step-by-step: width at top, middle, bottom; height left, center, right; use the smallest. Output: the
   rough size to text us, plus "we measure again before ordering". Shows a simple opening diagram.
2. **Glass by wall direction.** Inputs: which way the wall faces, shade trees, room use. Output: plain advice on low-E and SHGC
   (west and south walls in Florida: lower SHGC helps), with a note that the label tells you the numbers.
3. **Label explainer.** The visitor taps parts of a sample label: DP rating (positive and negative pressure), impact rating
   (large missile: a 9-lb 2x4 fired at 50 ft per second, then 9,000 pressure cycles), U-factor (lower holds heat or cool better),
   SHGC (lower blocks more sun heat), visible transmittance. Plain words for each.
4. **Shutters vs impact glass compare.** Inputs: how many openings, who puts the shutters up, how often you are away in season.
   Output: a side-by-side of the work, the look and sample ranges, with no claim about insurance numbers.
5. **Order-by timeline.** Pick the month you would sign. Output: a hedged timeline (measure, permit, manufacture 6-14 weeks,
   install, inspection) against hurricane season June 1 to November 30.
6. **Repair or replace (wood windows).** Inputs: rot at the sill (soft / not), glass broken, cords broken, painted shut, house
   age, historic district. Output: "restore the sash", "repair parts" or "replace", with sample ranges.
7. **Style by house age.** Pick the decade the house was built. Output: window styles that fit (1920s bungalow: wood double-hung
   with divided upper sash; 1950s ranch: horizontal sliders or awnings; 1990s stucco: single-hung with simple grids).
8. **Count and price estimator.** Inputs: number of windows by type and doors. Output: a sample whole-house range from
   `proof.prices`, "your measured quote lists each opening".

## 6. The three directions

### A — "Measured twice, listed opening by opening."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Sill & Sash Window Co. · Sarasota, FL · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Architects keep a window schedule: every opening gets a tag (W1, W2, D1) with its size, type and notes. That is
  the whole process: measure each opening, list it, order it, set it in a sill pan, seal it, inspect it. The page walks the
  visitor through one house in Sarasota, opening by opening, from the first tape measure to the final inspection sticker.
- Tone lead: **color-led** (warm coral field color, sea-glass teal accent, sand-white cards, deep ink-navy text).
- Type lead: **serif** (a refined transitional serif for display and tags, a calm humanist sans for body).
- Hero family: **5 — offset framed photo.** A large photo of a mid-century Sarasota house with new impact windows sits off
  center inside a thick frame drawn like a window jamb profile; the headline overlaps the frame edge, and a small schedule tag
  ("W1 · 36 x 60 · impact single-hung") hangs off the corner like a label.
- Signature seed: **The window schedule.** Every section is an opening in the schedule: a tag (W1 Measure, W2 Order, W3 Remove,
  D1 Install, W4 Seal, W5 Inspect) in a ruled table margin, and each section's photo sits in a jamb-profile frame (sill at the
  bottom, head at the top, drawn in thin lines). The price section is a real-looking schedule table with sample rows.
- Motion idea: Intro: the jamb frame draws around the hero photo (head, then jambs, then sill) and the tag swings in on its
  string. Scroll story: schedule rows tick off as each section passes; reduced motion shows the finished table.
- Tools: Measure your opening, Glass by wall direction + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright Gulf daylight, mid-century and Old Florida houses, coral and teal accents found in real homes, close detail
  of sills, caulk lines and tape measures, installers from behind; calm, warm, never storm-themed.

### B — "Read the label before you sign."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Shutterless Window & Door · Naples, FL · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Every impact window carries a label with the numbers that matter, and almost nobody explains them. The page
  is the label explained: design pressure, impact rating, U-factor, SHGC, each tied to the house in front of you. Proof here is
  the spec, the order-by date, and a comparison with putting up shutters every storm.
- Tone lead: **dark-led** (deep night-lanai charcoal, warm white type, one clean signal-yellow accent used like label ink, a cool
  Gulf blue for data).
- Type lead: **mono-accent** (a monospace for spec values, ratings and dates; a neutral grotesk for body and headlines).
- Hero family: **12 — sticky photo with scrolling text.** A tall photo of a Naples lanai slider at dusk stays pinned on one side
  while the opening lines scroll past on the other: "DP 50 means...", "Large missile means...", "SHGC 0.25 means...", each
  ending in a small spec plate.
- Signature seed: **Spec plates.** Every section ends in a product-label style plate: a bordered box with rows (DP +50/-50, MISSILE
  LEVEL D, U-FACTOR 0.45, SHGC 0.24, SAMPLE), set in mono. Prices, reviews and the timeline all use the same plate. Small
  rivet dots at the corners; a thin label-yellow rule along the top.
- Motion idea: Intro: the pinned photo slowly brightens from dusk to porch light; the first spec plate's rows fill in one value at
  a time. Scroll story: each plate's values count up as it enters; reduced motion shows the values at once.
- Tools: Label explainer (large missile: a 9-lb 2x4 at 50 ft per second plus 9,000 pressure cycles), Shutters vs impact glass
  compare, Order-by timeline (June 1 to November 30) + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: dusk and night lanais, big sliders, warm interior light through glass, close-ups of frames, locks, labels and
  glass edges; high contrast, quiet, never storm damage.

### C — "One river, a hundred years of windows."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Oak Canopy Window & Door · Jacksonville, FL · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Jacksonville's houses follow the St. Johns River: 1910s and 1920s wood windows in Riverside, Avondale and
  Springfield, brick and mid-century in San Marco, ranch homes in Mandarin, salt air at the Beaches. The page travels the river
  from neighborhood to neighborhood, and each stop shows what its windows need, from restoring a wavy-glass sash to an impact
  slider near the ocean.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (Spanish-moss pale grey-white, river blue ink, oak-bark brown, one brick-red accent for the stops).
- Type lead: **condensed** (a tall condensed sans for the big statement and stop names, a readable sans for body).
- Hero family: **10 — centered statement + marquee.** A centered, eight-word-max statement ("Every Jacksonville window, from
  Riverside to the Beaches.") over pale paper, with a slow marquee of neighborhood house photos and their window types gliding
  along the bottom.
- Signature seed: **The river route.** A blue river line winds down the page like the St. Johns on a map, with stops (Riverside /
  Avondale, Springfield, San Marco, Mandarin, the Beaches); each section sits at a stop with its name in condensed caps, a
  house-age line ("Built 1915-1930") and the window that fits. The current stop's dot fills brick red.
- Motion idea: Intro: the marquee starts slowly and the river line draws down from the statement to the first stop. Scroll story:
  the river line follows the reader, each stop's dot fills as it arrives; reduced motion shows the full line.
- Tools: Repair or replace (wood windows), Style by house age, Count and price estimator + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
  Note for historic districts: approval may be needed before changing windows; hedge it and say we check.
- Photo mood: soft daylight under oaks with Spanish moss, historic wood windows and porches, brick, river light, beach cottages;
  warm and lived-in, close detail of sash cords, glazing putty and old glass.
