# House Cleaning — niche brief

Three demo homepages for recurring residential house cleaning (every week, every other week, monthly) plus deep, move-in and move-out cleans. This is house cleaning for families, not janitorial or office cleaning. All three are sample sites: fictional brands, real cities and ZIPs, 555-01NN phones, `.example` email, proof marked "Sample".

## 1. The buyer

- **Who calls:**
  - Two-income households aged 30-55 with kids or pets, who are out of time.
  - Retirees who want help with the heavy parts.
  - Military families moving on PCS orders (Huntsville / Redstone Arsenal).
  - People selling, buying or renting out a house.
  - Hosts getting ready for holiday guests.
- **What just happened:**
  - The weekend went to cleaning again.
  - A move-out inspection or closing date is coming.
  - The in-laws arrive in two weeks.
  - The last cleaner stopped showing up.
  - Spring pollen coated everything.
  - They just moved in and the house is not clean.
- **Fears, in their words:**
  - "A different stranger every time in my house."
  - "They'll skip the baseboards and charge me anyway."
  - "I don't know what's included."
  - "They'll quote one price on the phone and another at the door."
  - "Will they be okay with the dog? Do I need to be home?"
- **What makes them trust:**
  - A clear checklist per room.
  - A sample price by house size before the call.
  - The same cleaner or team each visit.
  - How long a visit takes.
  - What happens if something is missed (re-clean promise from facts).
  - How keys and entry work.
  - Insured (as stated in facts).
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:**
  - A stock photo of a smiling woman in yellow gloves holding a spray bottle to camera.
  - Sparkle emojis and "spotless!" everywhere.
  - Office and commercial cleaning on the same page.
  - "Call for a free quote" with no price.
  - A chat pop-up on load.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned:**
- Almost all planned.
- About 25% one-time (deep, move-in/out, holiday) and 75% recurring.
- The first visit is usually a deep clean, and then the recurring schedule starts.

**Seasonality:**
- **Greenville, SC:** spring pollen (March-April) coats porches, sills and screens with yellow dust, so spring cleaning spikes. Summer is move season. November-December is holiday guest prep.
- **Huntsville, AL:** PCS moving season (May-August) brings move-out cleaning with inspection checklists. Spring pollen. A steady recurring base from tech and Arsenal families.
- **Chattanooga, TN:** river-valley fog and humid summers bring mildew in baths. Spring pollen. Weekend and mountain houses on Signal and Lookout Mountain get cleaned before and after guests, and the holidays are busy.

**Average ticket (words, not dollars):**
- Small, regular: a recurring visit.
- Larger, one-time: a first deep clean or a move-out.
- Add-ons (oven, fridge, inside windows) are small.
- Exact sample ranges are in each facts file's `proof.prices`.

**Services that sell:** every-other-week cleaning, weekly, monthly, first deep clean, move-out and move-in, kitchen and bath detail, inside windows and blinds, pollen clean, after-renovation dust, holiday prep, oven and fridge add-ons.

**Homeowner word vs. pro word:**

| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| regular cleaning | recurring service (weekly / biweekly / monthly) |
| every two weeks | biweekly / EOW (every other week) |
| a really good clean | deep clean / initial clean |
| cleaning when I move out | move-out clean (often against a landlord's or base housing checklist) |
| the top of the cabinets and the door frames | high dusting / detail dusting |
| the little edges on the floor | baseboards |
| the shower glass gets cloudy | soap scum / hard water buildup |
| the black stuff in the grout | mildew on grout lines |
| the extra stuff | add-ons (inside oven, inside fridge, inside windows, laundry) |
| how long they're here | visit time / labor hours (team of 2 × hours) |
| leave a key | entry method: lockbox, garage code, key on file |
| they missed a spot | re-clean request (within 24-48 h) |
| the same person | dedicated cleaner / assigned team |
| top to bottom | top-down, dry-to-wet method |

## 5. Niche tools

1. **Visit-time estimator**
   - What the homeowner learns: how long a visit takes for their house.
   - Inputs: bedrooms, bathrooms, sq ft band, pets (yes/no), visit type (recurring / first deep).
   - Output: an estimated visit length for a team of two, shown on a visit timeline (kitchen 0:00-0:50, baths, bedrooms, floors last), and the matching sample price range from facts.
   - Leads to the call: "Book this visit."
2. **Deep-vs-recurring chooser**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether they need a deep clean first.
   - Inputs: when the house was last professionally cleaned, are there baseboards/blinds/grout that have not been done in months, are they moving.
   - Output: "start with a first deep clean, then every other week" (or the reverse), with both sample ranges.
   - Leads to the call: "Start with this plan."
3. **Quote builder by rooms**
   - What the homeowner learns: a sample price for their house, built line by line.
   - Inputs: bedrooms, bathrooms, frequency, add-ons (oven, fridge, inside windows).
   - Output: a running receipt with each line, the sample range total, and the line "sample — final price after a walk-through or photos".
   - Leads to the call: "Send this quote request."
4. **Frequency quiz**
   - What the homeowner learns: weekly, every other week or monthly.
   - Inputs: people at home, pets, how much they cook, how they feel by day 10.
   - Output: the recommended frequency and why, with the sample price for it.
   - Leads to the call: "Book every other Friday" (or the matching frequency).
5. **Checklist explorer**
   - What the homeowner learns: exactly what is done in each room on each visit type.
   - Inputs: tap a room (kitchen, bath, bedroom, living, floors) and a visit type.
   - Output: the checklist for that room, with deep-only items marked.
   - Leads to the call: "That's the list we follow."
6. **Season planner**
   - What the homeowner learns: what to add when (pollen in spring, windows in fall, ovens before Thanksgiving).
   - Inputs: their recurring frequency and the month.
   - Output: a 12-month strip with suggested add-ons per season and sample add-on prices.
   - Leads to the call: "Add this to my next visit."
7. **Guest-ready countdown**
   - What the homeowner learns: when to book so the house is ready before guests arrive.
   - Inputs: the arrival date, the house size.
   - Output: the latest good booking date, a two-visit plan (deep clean, then a touch-up the day before) and sample ranges.
   - Leads to the call: "Hold these dates."
8. **ZIP checker** (all three sites)
   - Input: a 5-digit ZIP.
   - Output: served or not, from `zips`.
9. **Multi-step quote form** (all three sites)
   - Steps: visit type; house size (beds, baths); frequency; pets and entry method; preferred day; contact.

## 6. The three directions

### A — The visit, minute by minute   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)

- Brand: Ticklist House Cleaning · Greenville, SC · facts-A.json
- Concept seed:
  - The page is the actual checklist a two-person team works on a 3-bed, 2-bath house, from 0:00 to 3:10.
  - They go top-down (high dusting first) and dry-to-wet (dust before mop), and finish at the front door.
  - Each item gets ticked as you scroll.
  - Greenville angle: spring pollen on porches and sills, Augusta Road and North Main bungalows, newer houses in Simpsonville and Greer.
- Tone lead: color-led. A fresh pollen-free sage green as the main field, paper white checklist cards, and one deep ink-blue for type and ticks.
- Type lead: mono-accent. A friendly grotesk for headings, and a monospace for times, checklist items and prices (clipboard style).
- Hero family: 14 document object. The hero is the clipboard checklist itself: a paper sheet titled "Visit checklist — 3 bed / 2 bath", with rooms, times and a few ticked lines. The headline and phone sit beside it.
- Signature seed:
  - A visit timeline from 0:00 to 3:10 runs down the page edge.
  - Each section is a room with its start time and its list. Ticks draw themselves as the section enters, and the arrow "top-down, dry-to-wet" is drawn once on the first room.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the clipboard sheet rises, and three lines tick themselves one by one (stroke draw).
  - Scroll story: the timeline is pinned on the left. The clock runs from 0:00 to 3:10 as rooms pass on the right, and a small house plan highlights the room being cleaned. With reduced motion, a static list with times.
- Tools: visit-time estimator; deep-vs-recurring chooser; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: soft morning light in real Greenville houses, hands wiping counters, a microfiber cloth on a baseboard, a vacuum line on carpet, sills and screens with pollen before and after. Clean and calm, never staged.

### B — You see the price and the list before anyone comes over   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)

- Brand: Every Other Friday Cleaning · Huntsville, AL · facts-B.json
- Concept seed:
  - The name is the promise: the same team every other Friday, so the weekend is free. The site answers the price before the call with a quote builder that prints a running receipt, and shows the exact checklist.
  - Huntsville angle: Redstone Arsenal and tech families, PCS moves with base-housing move-out checklists, Madison and Harvest subdivisions.
- Tone lead: light-led. Bright paper white and soft warm gray, with one cheerful Friday-orange accent and charcoal type.
- Type lead: grotesk. A clean, round-ish geometric grotesk for headings and body; the figures are tabular.
- Hero family: 10 centered statement + photo marquee. A big centered line ("Every other Friday, the house gets done.") and a slow marquee of clean-room photos beneath it. It pauses on hover and stops with reduced motion.
- Signature seed:
  - A running receipt. The quote builder prints line items (beds, baths, frequency, add-ons) onto a receipt strip with a torn edge.
  - The same receipt look carries proof: "visits this year", the reviews as receipt notes, and the checklist as a long itemised slip.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the statement words rise one by one, and the marquee fades in.
  - Scroll story: a calendar month is pinned. Every other Friday lights up as you scroll, and each lit Friday shows what got done that visit (rotating add-ons: oven, fridge, windows). With reduced motion, a static calendar with notes.
- Tools: quote builder by rooms; frequency quiz; checklist explorer; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright daylight, tidy family kitchens and living rooms in suburban Alabama houses, folded towels, a made bed, a dog's bed by a clean floor (dog from behind), moving boxes for PCS. Cheerful and real.

### C — A year of care for Chattanooga houses   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)

- Brand: Riverfog Housekeeping · Chattanooga, TN · facts-C.json
- Concept seed:
  - Morning fog on the Tennessee River, mildew in old bathrooms on the North Shore, pollen in March, and weekend houses on Signal and Lookout Mountain.
  - The site is a house almanac: each season has its spread (what the house needs, what the team adds).
  - Old-house care (plaster walls, clawfoot tubs, heart pine floors) is shown with respect.
- Tone lead: dark-led. River-fog charcoal and deep evergreen dark ground, warm cream paper inserts, and a soft brass accent.
- Type lead: serif. An editorial old-style serif for headlines and the almanac entries, with a quiet sans for UI.
- Hero family: 6 editorial cover. The hero is a magazine-like cover: the masthead "Riverfog Housekeeping — The House Almanac, Spring edition", a big cover photo of fog over a riverside neighborhood at morning, cover lines ("Pollen week: what we add", "Old tubs, gently") and the phone.
- Signature seed:
  - A season dial. A small circular dial in the corner shows the four seasons and turns as you scroll through the almanac spreads.
  - Each spread opens with a dateline ("Late March, Signal Mountain") and a short note on the house in that season.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the fog photo slowly clears (a soft white overlay fades from 40% to 0%) as the cover lines rise.
  - Scroll story: the four season spreads in order, with the dial turning a quarter at each spread and the palette warming from fog gray to summer green to autumn rust to winter cream. With reduced motion, four static spreads.
- Tools: season planner; guest-ready countdown; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: fog over the river and ridges at morning, old-house interiors with plaster and wood, clawfoot tubs, linen, a mountain house porch, seasonal light. Quiet, editorial, warm inside.
