# Septic — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
- **Who calls:** homeowners 30-70 on private systems outside city sewer: young families who bought a country lot in the last
  five years and have never had the lid open, retirees in a 1980s brick ranch whose tank was last pumped "when the kids
  were small", and buyers or sellers in the middle of a closing where the lender or agent asked for a septic inspection.
- **What just happened:** the toilet gurgles and the tub drains slow; there is sewage smell over the backyard after rain;
  a soggy, too-green stripe of grass sits over the field; an alarm box on the side of the house is beeping and blinking
  red; in Louisiana, the aerobic plant's air pump went quiet; or the agent says "we need the septic letter by Friday".
- **Fears, in their words:**
  - "Is this going to be a whole new system? I heard that's twenty grand."
  - "Nobody knows where the tank even is. Are they going to dig up the whole yard?"
  - "They'll pump it and tell me it's fine, and it backs up again Thanksgiving with a full house."
  - "Is it safe for the kids and the dog to be out there?"
  - "The inspection is going to kill the sale."
- **What makes them trust:** a plain explanation of what was found (sludge depth, baffle condition, field test) with
  photos of their own tank; a price range before the truck rolls; someone who knows the local ground (red clay, karst
  limestone, gumbo clay with a high water table); a crew that puts the sod back; a phone answered by a person.
- **Leave in 5 seconds:** a cartoon poop emoji or toilet joke, a franchise-style site with no prices, a stock photo of a
  smiling man in a clean polo next to a truck, a "call for quote" wall, or a site that only talks to restaurants about
  grease traps.

## 2. The job
- **Split:** about 35% urgent (backup, alarm, smell, wet yard), 65% planned (routine pump-out every 3-5 years, real-estate
  inspections, riser installs, maintenance contracts, new systems for new builds and failed fields).
- **Seasonality (Southeast and mid-South):**
  - Jan-Feb: holiday-load backups (more people, more laundry, grease from cooking), saturated ground after winter rain.
  - Mar-May: wettest stretch; drain fields flood and surface; real-estate season starts, inspection calls peak (Augusta's
    spring is also when the ground finally firms up for field repair).
  - Jun-Aug: steady pump-outs, new installs on dry ground; in Lafayette, hurricane season begins and flooded aerobic units
    need restarts; in Bowling Green, summer storms open sinkholes near fields.
  - Sep-Oct: hurricane and tropical-storm aftermath on the Gulf; "pump before the holidays" calls.
  - Nov-Dec: pre-Thanksgiving pump-outs, cold snaps freezing exposed lines in Kentucky.
- **Ticket bands:** small (locate, lid dig, alarm reset, chlorine tablets), standard (pump-out, inspection, riser and lid),
  big (baffle and line repair, pump replacement, jetting), major (drain field repair or replacement, full new system).
- **Services that sell:** septic tank pumping, septic inspections, real-estate inspections, tank locating, riser and lid
  installation, line jetting, septic repair (baffles, lines, distribution box), drain field repair, pump and alarm repair,
  aerobic maintenance plans, new system installation.
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**
  | Homeowner says | Pro says |
  |---|---|
  | "the toilets gurgle" | partial blockage / venting or outlet restriction |
  | "the yard is squishy over the pipes" | effluent surfacing / hydraulic failure of the absorption field |
  | "it smells out back" | off-gassing at the lid or a cracked riser seal |
  | "the beeping box" | high-water alarm on the pump tank |
  | "get the tank cleaned" | pump-out and sludge removal |
  | "the pipes in the yard" | lateral lines / drain field trenches |
  | "the part between the tank and yard" | distribution box (D-box) |
  | "the little wall inside the tank" | inlet / outlet baffle or sanitary tee |
  | "the filter thing" | effluent filter |
  | "the sprinkler heads that spray in the yard" | aerobic treatment unit spray discharge |
  | "the hockey pucks" | chlorine tablets in the chlorinator |
  | "how much stuff is in there" | sludge and scum layer depth |
  | "the lid is under the grass" | buried access port, needs a riser to grade |
  | "it's backing up into the shower" | main line or inlet restriction |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Pump-interval calculator.** Inputs: people in the house, tank size (or bedrooms if unknown), garbage disposal yes/no,
   year last pumped. Output: a range of years between pump-outs and the month it is probably due, with a sample price.
   Leads to: "Book the pump-out" prefilled with tank size and household.
2. **Backup triage.** Inputs: what you see (gurgle, slow drains, smell, wet yard, alarm), where (one drain or all), after
   rain or not. Output: plain read ("probably the tank is full" / "probably the line" / "call now") and what to do in the
   next hour. Leads to: call button, or form prefilled with the symptom.
3. **Find-my-tank helper.** Inputs: year the house was built, where the main stack leaves the house, any lids seen.
   Output: a sketch of where tanks usually sit for that house type, and when a locate visit is the better bet.
   Leads to: "Locate and dig" booking.
4. **Real-estate inspection checklist.** Inputs: closing date, buyer or seller, lender type. Output: what the inspection
   includes, how long the report takes, the latest day to book. Leads to: inspection form prefilled with closing date.
5. **Sludge-layer gauge.** Interactive cross-section: drag the sludge and scum layers to see when the tank needs pumping
   and why the baffles matter. Leads to: pump-out booking.
6. **Alarm decoder.** Inputs: light color, beeping or not, aerobic or conventional. Output: what it means, what to try
   (breaker, silence button), and when to call. Leads to: call.
7. **Drain-field check.** Inputs: soggy spots, smell, grass greener over lines, standing water after rain days. Output:
   field risk (low / watch / call), and the sample range for repair. Leads to: inspection booking.

Prices on every tool are ranges from facts.proof.prices only, labelled "Sample pricing".

## 6. The three directions

### A — Lid up, layer by layer: you see every inch of your tank   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Tee & Baffle Septic · Augusta, GA · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: A septic visit is invisible to most homeowners: a truck comes, a hose goes into the grass, a bill
  arrives. This site walks you down through the tank: locate, dig to the lid, measure the scum and sludge with a
  sludge stick, pump to the floor, check the inlet and outlet baffles, look at the field, put the sod back. The reader
  scrolls down the way the hose goes down.
- Tone lead: dark-led (the dark of an open tank and wet red Georgia clay, a lamp-yellow work-light accent, cream type).
- Type lead: slab (a heavy slab serif for headlines and step numbers, like stencil on a tank lid; a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo with scrolling text (a photo of an opened concrete lid in red clay stays pinned while
  three short lines scroll past: "Find the lid." "Measure what's in it." "Pump it to the floor.").
- Signature seed: the depth stick. A vertical ruler runs down the left edge of the whole page, marked in inches like a
  sludge-judge pole; each section sits at a depth (0 in. lid, 6 in. scum, 40 in. effluent, 52 in. sludge, floor) and the
  marker slides as you scroll. The process steps are readings on the stick.
- Motion idea: intro, the lid outline lifts a few pixels and lamp-light falls into the hero; scroll story, the depth
  stick fills from top to floor as the seven steps pass, each step stamping its reading.
- Tools: sludge-layer gauge, backup triage, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low, close and earthy: red clay, concrete lids, hoses, gloved hands, late-afternoon light in a backyard,
  tight crops; nothing glossy.

### B — When it's due, in years and gallons, before you call   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Gallon Count Septic · Bowling Green, KY · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Most people have no idea when their tank is due. This site answers in numbers: household size and tank
  gallons give a pump interval, an inspection report shows the readings it records, and every service carries a sample
  range. On karst ground, where a failed field can reach a sinkhole and the groundwater, the numbers are the pitch.
- Tone lead: light-led (clean report white and limestone gray, graphite text, one signal cobalt for readings and actions).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a calm grotesk for headings and body with a monospace for every reading, gallon count, interval
  and price).
- Hero family: 7 tool-first (the pump-interval calculator is the hero: people, tank gallons, last pumped; the answer
  "Due about: spring 2027" and a sample range appear beside the promise; with JS off it is a readable table and the phone).
- Signature seed: the field report. Every section is a row on a printed inspection report with a reading column
  (sludge 14 in., scum 3 in., baffles OK, D-box level, field probe dry), so the page itself shows what you get after a visit.
- Motion idea: intro, the calculator's numbers tick into place like a meter; scroll story, the sample report fills its
  reading column one line at a time as each row enters.
- Tools: pump-interval calculator, real-estate inspection checklist, drain-field check, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright, documentary daylight: open tanks shot straight down, measuring tools, clipboards, green Kentucky
  pasture and limestone, crisp and clear, a little clinical.

## 7. Shot list (for the media curator)
**Hero candidates**
- A (dark, close, earthy): an opened septic or concrete access lid in a backyard lawn with soil around it; a vacuum hose
  running across grass into an open tank; gloved hands lifting a heavy concrete lid. Must: low angle, dirt and grass
  visible, no faces, no truck logos. Avoid: city sewer manholes in streets, clean studio shots, smiling workers.
- B (bright, documentary): a top-down view into an open tank or riser; a clipboard inspection sheet on a tailgate over a
  green lawn; a rural Kentucky home on a wide lawn with pasture behind. Must: clear daylight, readable composition. Avoid:
  readable text with brands, faces, commercial plants.
- C (humid, green): a raised home on a flat green lot with live oaks; Spanish moss over a still bayou; a wet backyard
  after rain under a gray sky. Must: Gulf South vegetation, flat land. Avoid: mountains, snow, desert, palm-beach resort.

**Shared pool (must / avoid per slot in media-queries.json)**: lid and riser details, hose and pump truck parts (no logos),
probe and shovel, gloved hands, digging in clay, drain-field trenches and gravel, PVC pipe and fittings, distribution box,
alarm panel, aerobic air pump and spray head, chlorine tablets, sod being replaced, the regions' houses (Georgia brick
ranch, Kentucky farmhouse and pasture, Louisiana raised home), weather (Georgia summer thunderstorm, Kentucky limestone
creek and sinkhole country, Louisiana rain on a ditch), seasons (fall leaves on a lawn, spring rain).

**Videos (5-15 s, slow, no faces, no logos)**: a hose pulsing as a tank is pumped; rain falling on a green backyard lawn;
a shovel cutting into clay; Spanish moss moving over a slow bayou.

Avoid in every slot: faces toward the camera, logos and readable signs, restaurant grease traps or industrial plants,
city sewer work in streets, toilets as jokes, snow and desert landscapes.
