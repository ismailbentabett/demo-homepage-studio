# Gutter — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
- **Who calls:** homeowners 35-80 in rainy, tree-heavy cities (Memphis, Seattle, Portland): ranches and bungalows under big oaks, sweetgums, maples and Douglas firs, often with old 5-inch gutters, too few downspouts and a basement or crawl space that gets damp. Also people who just bought a house and saw water marks on the foundation, older owners who no longer want to climb a ladder twice a year, and landlords tired of tenant calls about overflowing gutters.
- **What just happened:** water poured over the gutter like a waterfall in the last storm; a downspout came loose and is dumping water against the foundation; the fascia board is soft and the gutter sags; there are plants growing out of the gutter; the crawl space smells musty; the basement corner leaked; the ice storm pulled a section off; the inspector wrote "gutters undersized, regrade soil, extend downspouts".
- **Fears (their words):**
  1. "They'll sell me expensive guards that don't work under my trees."
  2. "Someone will fall off a ladder on my property."
  3. "They'll just blow everything onto my flower beds and leave."
  4. "The new gutters will look cheap and sag in a year."
  5. "I'm not sure the gutters are even the problem."
- **What makes them trust:** before-and-after photos of their own gutters, a crew that cleans up and bags the debris, a plain explanation of slope and downspout size, an honest answer about which guard works under which tree, color samples matched to their trim, a price range per foot before the visit, and a text when the crew is on the way.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:** "never clean your gutters again" promises, gimmick guard videos with water poured from a bucket, pressure-sales "today only" pricing, clip-art houses, a stock photo of a smiling man on a ladder in a studio, no prices or ranges of any kind.

## 2. The job
- **Emergency vs planned:** almost all planned. Even storm damage (a section torn loose, a downspout knocked off) is a repair booked within days, not a same-night call, so these sites do not promise emergency service. Planned work: seasonal cleaning, seamless gutter replacement, downspout additions and extensions, guards, fascia and soffit repair, re-pitching sagging runs, underground drain lines, rain barrels and downspout disconnection where the city allows it.
- **Seasonality:**
  - Memphis (humid South, about 53 in of rain a year): spring storms Mar-May bring overflow calls; sweetgum balls, oak tassels and pollen clog gutters in spring; heavy leaf fall Nov-Dec; summer thunderstorms test downspout size.
  - Seattle (about 150 rain days a year, light steady rain): fall cleaning Oct-Nov is peak; moss and fir needles build up all winter; atmospheric-river storms find every leaking seam; summer is the window for replacements.
  - Portland (wet winters, big trees): bigleaf maple and oak leaves Oct-Dec, Douglas fir needles all year, moss on north roofs; occasional ice storms (January 2024) tear gutters loose; dry summers are replacement season.
- **Average tickets (words):** small (one-story cleaning, downspout reattach, leak seal), medium (two-story cleaning, downspout additions, re-pitching a run, partial guard install), large (full seamless replacement on a house), very large (seamless replacement plus guards plus fascia repair and underground drains).
- **Regional notes:** Memphis has loess soil that erodes and shifts when water dumps at one corner, and many 1950s-60s ranches with long roof runs. Seattle runs the RainWise program for rain gardens and cisterns in some neighborhoods. Portland's Downspout Disconnection Program (1993-2011) disconnected roughly 56,000 homes' downspouts from the combined sewer, so many Portland downspouts now drain onto the yard and need proper splash blocks or extensions. Use these as context, never as scare copy or as claims about the business.
- **Services that sell (from facts):** gutter cleaning (one- and two-story), seamless aluminum gutters, oversized 6-inch gutters, downspout additions and extensions, gutter guards matched to the tree, fascia and soffit repair, re-pitching and re-hanging, underground drain lines, leak sealing and end caps, rain barrels and disconnection, storm repair.
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**
  | Homeowner says | Pro says |
  |---|---|
  | "the gutters overflow" | undersized trough / clogged outlet / poor pitch |
  | "the pipe that comes down" | downspout / leader |
  | "the board behind the gutter" | fascia |
  | "the underside of the roof edge" | soffit |
  | "the gutter is tilted wrong" | pitch / slope (about 1/4 in per 10 ft) |
  | "the hole where water goes down" | drop outlet |
  | "the bend at the bottom" | elbow / kick-out |
  | "the thing it splashes on" | splash block |
  | "covers" | gutter guards: screen, micro-mesh, reverse curve |
  | "the gutter is coming off" | failed hangers / spikes / rotted fascia |
  | "the ones with no seams" | seamless gutter, formed on site |
  | "the drain in the yard" | underground downspout drain / pop-up emitter |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Gutter size check** — inputs: roof area of one side (or footprint length × depth), roof pitch (low/medium/steep), local heavy-rain intensity (low/medium/high, picked by the visitor). Output: "5-inch is usually enough / 6-inch is the safer choice" with the reason. No live weather data. Leads to: seamless gutter estimate.
2. **Tree debris sorter** — inputs: which trees overhang the roof (oak, maple, sweetgum, pine, Douglas fir, cottonwood, none). Output: what that tree drops and when, which guard type tends to handle it (screen vs micro-mesh vs reverse curve vs none), and a suggested cleaning rhythm. Leads to: guards or a cleaning plan.
3. **Runoff calculator** — inputs: roof footprint in square feet, inches of rain (typed by the visitor). Output: gallons off the roof (sq ft × inches × 0.623). Example: 1 in on 1,000 sq ft is about 623 gallons. Leads to: downspout or drain booking.
4. **Downspout count** — inputs: total gutter length per run, gutter size, number of corners. Output: suggested downspouts (roughly one per 30-40 ft of gutter) and where. Leads to: downspout additions.
5. **Guard compare** — a side-by-side of screen, micro-mesh, reverse curve and foam: what each stops, what slips through, upkeep. Sample price ranges only from `facts.proof.prices`. Leads to: guard estimate.
6. **Downspout disconnection check** — inputs: distance from the foundation to the property line, slope away from the house, basement yes/no. Output: whether a splash block, an extension or an underground line suits the spot, with a note to check local rules. Leads to: drain line booking.
7. **Cleaning calendar** — inputs: trees, roof height. Output: a 12-month calendar with the months to clean. Printable. Leads to: a recurring cleaning plan.
8. **ZIP checker** — confirms service area against `facts.biz.zips`.

All prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices`, always labelled "Sample pricing". No tool shows live rain, forecasts or wait times; any rainfall number is typed by the visitor or a fixed example.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "A quarter inch every ten feet, and the water goes where you want it."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Quarter-Inch Gutter Co. · Memphis, TN · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Most gutter trouble is slope and outlets, not the gutter itself. Quarter-Inch is named for the pitch it hangs every run at. The page is one long gutter: a thin slope line runs down the whole page, falling a little with every section toward a downspout at the bottom, and each step of the visit is a fitting on that run.
- Tone lead: light-led (warm magnolia white, a deep river-mud brown text color, one copper accent like a new downspout)
- Type lead: slab (a sturdy slab serif for headlines and measurements; a plain sans for body)
- Hero family: 11 diagonal split (the page split by a line pitched like a gutter run: headline on the high side, a photo of a seamless gutter and downspout against a brick ranch on the low side)
- Signature seed: **The slope line.** A thin copper line runs down the page, falling about a quarter inch per "ten feet" of scroll. The steps sit on it as fittings: End cap (we look) · Hanger (we measure) · Seam (we form it on site) · Outlet (we place the drop) · Elbow (we turn it away) · Splash block (we check where it lands). Services hang below the line like brackets.
- Motion idea: Intro: the diagonal split draws itself like a chalk line snapped along the fascia, and a single drop of water rolls down the line to the photo side. Scroll story: the drop keeps rolling down the slope line as you scroll, pausing at each fitting, and lands in a splash block in the footer.
- Tools: Gutter size check, Downspout count, Tree debris sorter (sweetgum and oak lead), ZIP checker, multi-step booking form (house height → problem → photo upload optional → time → contact).
- Photo mood: brick ranches and 1960s homes under oaks, copper and bronze seamless gutters, crews forming gutter on site, magnolia and sweetgum leaves, bright humid afternoons; honest, warm, no faces.

### B — "623 gallons fall on 1,000 square feet in one inch of rain. We count them."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Gallonworks Gutters · Seattle, WA · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Seattle rain is steady, not dramatic, and it adds up. Gallonworks sizes every job by the water it has to carry: square feet of roof, inches of rain, gallons per downspout. The page is a running rain gauge with the tools that let a homeowner do the same math.
- Tone lead: color-led (a saturated deep evergreen field, rain-gray rules, and one signal yellow for numbers)
- Type lead: mono-accent (monospaced numerals and labels for gallons and feet; a calm sans for body)
- Hero family: 10 centered statement + marquee (a centered statement "Every inch of rain on your roof has to go somewhere." over a slow marquee of sample numbers: "1,000 sq ft · 1 in · 623 gal · 2 downspouts")
- Signature seed: **The gallon count.** A rain gauge tube sits in the margin and fills as you scroll; every section reports what it handles in gallons (a 30 ft run, a 2x3 vs 3x4 downspout, a guard under fir). Reviews and services carry a small "sample" gallon tag. The runoff calculator writes its answer into the gauge.
- Motion idea: Intro: the marquee drifts in slowly like rain on a window and the gauge fills to "1 in". Scroll story: the gauge level rises section by section, each tick labeled with the gallons that section talks about; the numbers count up only when the section is in view.
- Tools: Runoff calculator, Gutter size check, Guard compare, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: rain on gutters and downspouts, moss on shingles, Craftsman houses in Ballard and West Seattle in soft gray light, water flowing from a downspout into a rain garden, wet evergreens; cool, precise, no faces.

### C — "Every Portland roof drops something different. Here's the field guide."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Firneedle Gutter Co. · Portland, OR · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Portland's trees decide what clogs a gutter: Douglas fir needles all year, bigleaf maple leaves in fall, moss on the north side, cottonwood fluff in June, and the odd ice storm. Firneedle writes the city's gutter field guide, one specimen at a time, and matches the cleaning and the guard to the specimen.
- Tone lead: dark-led (deep forest black with moss-green accents, bone-white text and one rust orange)
- Type lead: grotesk (a tight grotesk for headlines and specimen names; the same family for body)
- Hero family: 1 full-bleed video with left scrim (slow rain running through a gutter under fir branches, a dark scrim on the left holding the headline)
- Signature seed: **The field guide.** Sections are specimen plates: a numbered specimen (No. 1 Douglas fir needle, No. 2 bigleaf maple leaf, No. 3 moss, No. 4 cottonwood fluff, No. 5 ice), drawn as a simple line illustration with when it falls, what it does to a gutter, and which service handles it. Reviews read like field notes from neighborhoods.
- Motion idea: Intro: rain runs through the gutter in the hero video while the specimen number "No. 1" fades in beside the headline. Scroll story: each specimen plate draws its line illustration as it enters, and a seasonal ring in the margin turns to the months when that specimen falls.
- Tools: Tree debris sorter (Portland species), Downspout disconnection check, Cleaning calendar, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: Douglas firs over old Portland houses, maple leaves in gutters, moss on shingles, a gutter in steady rain, the January 2024 ice on branches (no damage close-ups), dark wet streets with warm windows; moody, green, no faces.
