# Electrician — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
- **Who calls:** homeowners 30-75 in Columbus, San Antonio and Kansas City: owners of 1910-1970 houses with old panels, two-prong outlets and a few modern loads bolted on; families adding an EV charger, a heat pump or a hot tub; new owners holding an inspection report that says "Federal Pacific panel, recommend evaluation"; older owners who want lights that stop flickering and outlets that hold a plug.
- **What just happened:** a breaker trips every time the microwave and the toaster run together; half the house lost power; an outlet is warm or scorched; the lights dim when the AC kicks on; they bought an EV and the dealer said "you'll want a 240-volt charger"; the insurer won't renew without a panel change; a winter storm knocked the power out for days and they want a generator plan.
- **Fears (their words):**
  1. "Is this going to start a fire tonight?"
  2. "They'll tell me I need a whole new panel when I don't."
  3. "They'll cut holes in every wall of my plaster house."
  4. "I don't know if my house can even handle an EV charger."
  5. "Nobody will pull the permit and it will bite me when I sell."
- **What makes them trust:** a plain explanation of what tripped and why, a price range before the visit, the panel labeled when they leave, a permit and inspection mentioned up front, small plaster cuts patched, photos of the problem, a real local number and a text when the van is on the way.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:** lightning-bolt clip art and a mascot, "we do it all" lists of 60 services, a stock photo of a man in a hard hat pointing at a breaker box he isn't touching, no hours, no ranges of any kind, a chat pop-up that covers the phone number.

## 2. The job
- **Emergency vs planned:** about 30/70. Emergencies: partial power loss, burning smell or a scorched outlet, a breaker that will not reset, storm damage to the service mast, a tree on the drop. Planned: panel replacement, EV chargers, heat pump and generator circuits, rewiring old houses, GFCI/AFCI updates, lighting, surge protection, inspections for sales and insurance.
- **Seasonality:**
  - Jan-Feb: space heaters overload old circuits; ice storms take down service drops (San Antonio Feb 2021, Kansas City ice); generator interest peaks.
  - Mar-Apr: spring storms and lightning; surge protectors; outdoor GFCIs before the season.
  - May-Jun: AC circuits, pool and hot tub wiring, ceiling fans, landscape lighting.
  - Jul-Aug: heat loads; dimming lights when the AC starts; panels running hot; EV deliveries.
  - Sep-Oct: generator interlocks and standby units before winter; holiday lighting circuits.
  - Nov-Dec: holiday loads, outdoor outlets, heat pump add-ons.
- **Average tickets (words):** small (outlet or switch repair, GFCI, fixture swap, troubleshooting), medium (EV charger, surge protector, generator interlock, a dedicated circuit, inspection), large (panel replacement, service upgrade, partial rewire), very large (whole-house rewire, standby generator).
- **Regional notes:** Columbus has many 1920s-1950s houses (German Village brick, Clintonville bungalows) with Federal Pacific or Zinsco panels still in service; San Antonio remembers Winter Storm Uri (Feb 2021) and CPS Energy outages, and has many 1950s-1970s ranches with aluminum branch wiring; Kansas City has 1910s-1930s bungalows and Tudors in Brookside and Waldo with knob-and-tube wiring and plaster walls. Use these as context only.
- **Services that sell (from facts):** panel replacement and service upgrades, EV chargers, troubleshooting and repairs, GFCI/AFCI updates, whole-home surge protection, generator interlocks and standby generators, rewiring, knob-and-tube replacement, two-prong to grounded outlets, lighting and ceiling fans, electrical inspections.
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**
  | Homeowner says | Pro says |
  |---|---|
  | "the breaker box" | panel / load center |
  | "the fuse keeps blowing" | breaker tripping / overloaded circuit |
  | "the outlet in the bathroom with buttons" | GFCI receptacle |
  | "the old two-hole outlets" | ungrounded two-prong receptacles |
  | "the wires on little white knobs" | knob-and-tube wiring |
  | "the big switch" | main breaker / service disconnect |
  | "the line from the pole" | service drop / service entrance |
  | "the car charger" | Level 2 EVSE on a 240 V circuit |
  | "it's a 100-amp house" | 100 A service |
  | "the lights flicker" | loose neutral / voltage drop |
  | "a plug-in generator" | portable generator with an interlock / inlet |
  | "the whole-house one" | standby generator with automatic transfer switch |
  | "the shiny wires" | aluminum branch circuit wiring |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **Old panel identifier** — a picture walk: brand label inside the door, breaker look (FPE "Stab-Lok" red-tipped breakers, Zinsco colored breakers, fuse boxes, split-bus panels with no main). Output: what it is, what it means, next step. Careful, plain words, no scare numbers. Leads to: panel evaluation.
2. **EV / heat pump load check** — inputs: service size (100/150/200 A or "not sure"), big loads already in the house (electric range, dryer, AC, water heater, hot tub), charger wanted (Level 2 at 32/40/48 A). Output: "likely fits / may need a load calculation / likely needs an upgrade or load management" with the reason. Leads to: EV charger quote.
3. **Generator sizer** — inputs: what to keep running in an outage (fridge, furnace blower, well pump, AC, medical device, lights). Output: a rough watt range and "portable + interlock" vs "standby" with the trade-offs. Leads to: interlock or standby quote.
4. **EV charger planner** — inputs: where the car parks, panel location, distance estimate, hardwired vs plug-in. Output: what affects the price (run length, panel space, wall type) shown against the sample price range. Leads to: charger booking.
5. **Wiring-era decoder** — inputs: decade the house was built, outlets (two-prong, three-prong), what's in the basement ceiling (cloth wires on knobs, metal cable, plastic sheathed). Output: likely wiring type and what an inspection would look at. Leads to: inspection.
6. **Two-prong options** — the three legal-style options in plain words (run a ground, GFCI protection with "no equipment ground" label, replace with like) and when each fits. Leads to: outlet update booking.
7. **Breaker trip sorter** — inputs: when it trips (right away, after a few minutes, when one appliance starts), which room. Output: overload vs short vs ground fault, what to unplug, when to stop resetting. Leads to: troubleshooting visit.
8. **ZIP checker** — confirms service area against `facts.biz.zips`.

All prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices`, always labelled "Sample pricing". No tool claims a code ruling for a specific house; outputs say "an electrician confirms on site". No live outage maps or live utility data.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "From the pole to the plug, one line at a time."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Lineside Electric · Columbus, OH · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Electricians draw a one-line diagram before they touch anything: utility, meter, main, circuits, devices. Lineside walks every homeowner through their own house the same way, so a panel change or an EV charger stops being a mystery. The page is that one-line diagram, drawn as you scroll.
- Tone lead: color-led (a deep signal teal field with chalk white and one bright copper orange for the live current)
- Type lead: grotesk (a tight neo-grotesk for headlines and symbol labels; the same family lighter for body)
- Hero family: 5 offset framed photo (a framed photo of a clean labeled panel set off-center to the right, the headline overlapping its left edge, the one-line diagram's first symbol peeking out below the frame)
- Signature seed: **The one-line.** A single vertical line runs down the page with real diagram symbols at each stop: 01 Utility (pole/transformer) → 02 Meter → 03 Main → 04 Circuits → 05 Devices → 06 Test. Each stop is a section. Services sit at the symbol where they belong (service upgrade at Meter, panel at Main, EV charger at Circuits, GFCI at Devices, inspection at Test).
- Motion idea: Intro: the framed photo slides in from the right and the line draws down from the top of the frame to the first symbol. Scroll story: a small copper pulse travels down the line as you scroll, and each symbol lights when the pulse reaches it.
- Tools: Old panel identifier, EV / heat pump load check, Two-prong options, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form (what's wrong → photo optional → time → contact).
- Photo mood: clean modern panels with neat labels, copper and colored wire close-ups, brick German Village homes, Clintonville porches, bright daylight basements; crisp and graphic, no faces.

### B — "Your panel schedule, filled in."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Torque & Tag Electric · San Antonio, TX · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: The inside of most panel doors is a smudged list of "kitchen?" and "??". Torque & Tag torques every lug to spec and fills in the panel schedule for every circuit before leaving. The page is a panel schedule: numbered circuits on two columns, and each one opens into a service, a tool or a review.
- Tone lead: dark-led (panel-gray charcoal with off-white labels, a breaker-handle black and one amber "ON" color)
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed sans for headlines and circuit labels; a plain sans for body)
- Hero family: 8 photo mosaic (a grid of small photos laid out as a panel's two columns of breakers: wire, lug, meter, charger, ranch house, generator; the center spine carries the headline)
- Signature seed: **The panel schedule.** Two columns of numbered circuits (1, 3, 5… / 2, 4, 6…) with amperage and a label written in. Flipping a breaker handle opens that circuit's detail: services carry their sample price range, tools sit on their own circuits, reviews are filed under the circuit they fixed. A "main" at the top is the call button.
- Motion idea: Intro: the mosaic tiles click on one by one like breakers being reset, top to bottom, then the headline lights. Scroll story: as each section enters, its breaker handle flips from OFF to ON with a short click-like snap and the label fills in by hand.
- Tools: Breaker trip sorter, Generator sizer, EV / heat pump load check, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: charcoal panels with neat wiring, torque screwdrivers on lugs, 1960s San Antonio ranch houses under live oaks, a generator beside a stone wall, hot Texas evening light; precise, dark, no faces.

### C — "A 1925 bungalow, rewired room by room."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Bungalow Current Electric · Kansas City, MO · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Brookside and Waldo are full of 1920s bungalows and Tudors with plaster walls and knob-and-tube still in the attic. Bungalow Current rewires them a room at a time, fishing new wire through small cuts and patching the plaster. The page is a tour through one Kansas City bungalow, each room its own chapter.
- Tone lead: light-led (warm plaster cream with oak brown text, a muted brick red accent and a soft brass)
- Type lead: serif (a warm old-style serif for headlines and room names; a humanist sans for body)
- Hero family: 13 arch (the hero photo of a lamp-lit bungalow front room set inside a Tudor arch shape, the headline below it like a carved lintel)
- Signature seed: **Room by room.** Each section is a room behind its own arched doorway: Front porch (service and meter), Living room (two-prong outlets, plaster patching), Kitchen (dedicated circuits, GFCI), Basement (panel), Attic (knob-and-tube), Garage (EV charger). A small floor plan in the margin marks where you are.
- Motion idea: Intro: the arch opens like a door swinging inward and the lamp in the photo warms up. Scroll story: walking through each arch, the floor plan highlights the next room and a soft lamp glow fades in on each room photo.
- Tools: Wiring-era decoder, Two-prong options, EV charger planner, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: lamp-lit bungalow interiors, plaster walls, old brass switch plates and push-button switches, Tudor arches, Brookside porches, autumn trees; warm, homey, no faces.
