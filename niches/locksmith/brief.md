# Locksmith — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Two very different people:
- **The locked-out caller (about 60% of calls).** 22-65, renter or homeowner, standing on a porch, in a parking lot or in a stairwell, phone often low on battery. It is dark, cold or wet. They have one hand free and maybe 90 seconds of patience. Often a woman alone at night, a parent with a child or dog inside, a nurse leaving for a shift, a student near campus, an older homeowner who stepped out for the mail.
- **The planned buyer (about 40%).** New homeowners after closing, tenants moving in, landlords between leases, someone after a breakup or a lost purse, a family after a break-in on the block, a military family arriving on new orders. They have time, compare two or three companies and read the page.

**What just happened.** The door clicked shut with the keys inside. The key snapped in the lock. The lock froze. The keys are lost and the purse had the address in it. They closed on a house and have no idea who else holds a key. The ex still has one. The deadbolt stopped turning when the door swelled.

**Fears, in their words.**
1. "They'll say $29 on the phone and charge me $300 at the door." (the classic bait-and-switch)
2. "Some random guy in an unmarked car shows up and I can't tell if he's legit."
3. "He'll just drill the lock and sell me a new one."
4. "If they open my door without checking anything, they'll open anyone's door."
5. "I'll wait two hours in the cold and nobody will tell me anything."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A price range said out loud before anyone drives, written the same way on the page.
2. The company name said when the phone is answered, and a text with the tech's first name, the vehicle and an arrival window.
3. Being asked for ID or proof of residence: the check reassures them that the company won't open a stranger's door for someone else.
4. "We try to open it without damage first" said plainly, with what happens if that fails.
5. A real local area: neighborhoods they recognise, a phone number with the local area code.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** No phone number above the fold. A page that looks like a national call-center template ("serving all of [city] in 15 minutes!"). Stock photo of a smiling man holding a key ring. Prices like "from $19". A pop-up before the phone number. Text in grey on grey that cannot be read on a phone in sunlight.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Roughly 55-65% urgent (house lockouts, car lockouts, broken keys, failed locks after a break-in), 35-45% planned (rekeys, deadbolts, master keying for rentals, safes, smart locks). Urgent calls make the phone ring; planned jobs make the money per visit.

**Seasonality (northern US: Milwaukee, Rochester, Tacoma).**
- Jan-Feb: frozen locks and frozen car door seals in Milwaukee and Rochester, keys locked in running cars on remote start mornings, lockouts after shoveling. Tacoma: wet-season swollen doors, deadbolts that bind.
- Mar-Apr: thaw, real estate listings start, rekey requests begin; tax-refund deadbolt upgrades.
- May-Aug: peak move season. Closings and lease turnovers (June 1 and Sept 1 leases in Rochester near the colleges), summer PCS season at Joint Base Lewis-McChord (May-August), landlord turnover rekeys, car lockouts at beaches and parks.
- Sep: college move-ins (University of Rochester, RIT), September 1 leases, back-to-school key copies.
- Oct-Nov: daylight saving ends, more evening lockouts, security upgrades after break-ins, first frozen locks in the Midwest, Tacoma doors swell with the rains.
- Dec: holiday travel lockouts, lost keys, safes opened after a family member dies (estate safes).

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: key copies, a single lock repair. Medium: a house or car lockout, a rekey of 2-4 locks, a deadbolt installed. Larger: whole-house rekey plus new hardware, rental master-key systems, safe openings, smart-lock installs with new deadbolts.

**Services that sell (from facts).** House lockouts, car lockouts, rekeying, deadbolt installation, lock repair, move-in / turnover rekeys, master keying for duplexes and rentals, key copies, safe opening, sticking deadbolt adjustment, frozen lock thawing, smart lock setup.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Locksmith says |
|---|---|
| "I'm locked out" | lockout, non-destructive entry |
| "change the locks" | rekey (re-pin the cylinder) or replace the lockset |
| "make my old keys stop working" | rekey to a new bitting |
| "the key won't turn" | binding cylinder, worn pins, misaligned strike |
| "the lock is frozen" | ice in the keyway / cylinder, needs dry lubricant |
| "the bolt won't go in" | bolt not throwing, strike misalignment, door sag |
| "one key for all my doors" | keyed alike |
| "landlord key that opens every unit" | master key system |
| "the inside lock with the big old key" | mortise lock, skeleton key |
| "the key broke in the lock" | broken key extraction |
| "a stronger lock" | Grade 1 / Grade 2 deadbolt, reinforced strike, 3-inch screws |
| "the code lock" | keypad / electronic deadbolt |
| "they picked it" | picking, bypass, or under-door tool |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A stock man in a polo holding a ring of 40 keys, smiling at the camera.
2. "From $19!" or "$15 service call" in a burst graphic, with no word about the opening price.
3. "Fast, reliable, affordable" plus "licensed, bonded and insured" as the only proof.
4. A national template with the city name swapped into every H2 ("Best Locksmith in Milwaukee | Milwaukee Locksmith Services in Milwaukee").
5. A van photo with a fake wrap and a giant padlock icon.
6. Twenty-four service tiles, each with a clip-art padlock, including things they never do.
7. A "15-minute arrival" promise that no honest company can keep at 5 p.m. on a snowy Friday.
8. Dark-web hacker aesthetic (glowing padlocks, binary code), which reads as threat, not safety.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Price-it-before-we-drive card.** Inputs: what is locked (house / car / safe / storage), time of day (daytime / evening / night), anything broken (key snapped, lock damaged). Output: the sample range from `facts.proof.prices` for that item, plus "what can push it to the top of the range" in plain words. Leads to: "Call and we say the same range out loud" button.
2. **Rekey or replace?** Inputs: do the locks work smoothly (yes / sticky / broken), do you like how they look, do you want one key for all doors, how many outside locks. Output: a recommendation (rekey / replace some / replace all) with the sample range: first lock + each additional from facts. Leads to: quote form preset to "Rekeying".
3. **Lock counter.** A visual house outline: tap the doors you have (front, back, side, garage entry, storm door, mailbox, basement / shared hall for duplexes). Output: the number of cylinders, the sample rekey range computed from first + additional prices, and "one key for all of them" option. Leads to: quote form with the count prefilled.
4. **Scam checklist ("Before you let anyone touch the door").** Six yes/no checks: did they say a company name, did they give a range for the whole job, is the vehicle marked or did they text a description, did they ask for ID, do they try to open without drilling first, do you get a written receipt. Output: a score with honest wording ("Two answers worried us: here is why"). Written as consumer advice, never naming real companies. Consumer agencies like the FTC warn about low phone quotes that grow at the door.
5. **The arrival text preview.** Shows the exact sample text the customer will receive ("Hi, this is {{owner}}'s team. Your tech is Sam, grey van with our name on the side, arriving 8:40-9:05 p.m. They will ask for ID before opening."). Static demo, no fake live data, no tracking map.
6. **Frozen lock / sticking bolt self-check.** Three steps a homeowner can try safely (warm the key, don't force it, check if the bolt binds only with the door shut), then "if that fails, here is what we do". Leads to a call.
7. **Move-in rekey checklist.** Doors, garage keypad codes, mailbox, previous owner's spare keys, smart lock codes. Output: a printable list and the rekey count to price.
8. **ZIP checker** (every site): yes / "outside our usual area, call and we'll tell you honestly".
