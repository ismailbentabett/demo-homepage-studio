# Farm — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who comes.** Three kinds of people, all within a 40-minute drive:
- **Parents of young kids (28-45)** in the newer subdivisions around the city (Lexington and Blythewood SC, Madison and Brandon MS, Enterprise AL). They want a Saturday outing: strawberries in April, pumpkins in October, a hayride, a bathroom that exists.
- **Cooks and planners (35-70)** who buy a CSA share, a quarter of beef or eggs every week. Often retired, often one partner gardens and gave up on tomatoes after the third hornworm year.
- **Restaurant and caterer buyers** (small, independent kitchens in town) who need a weekly list and a text when something is short.

**What just happened.** A friend posted a strawberry bucket photo. The grocery tomatoes tasted like water again. A new baby and a decision to "know where food comes from". A chest freezer was bought and now needs filling. A CSA they tried last year sent ten pounds of kale.

**Fears, in their words.**
1. "We drove 40 minutes and the field was picked out." (the number one PYO complaint)
2. "Is it actually theirs, or did they buy it at the produce terminal and put it on a table?"
3. "The CSA box will be all greens I don't know how to cook, and I can't skip a week when we're at the beach."
4. "A quarter cow, where do I even put it, and what cuts do I get?"
5. "Is it open today? Their Facebook says one thing and the sign says another."

**What makes them trust.**
1. A dated "picked this morning / ripe this week" list that is clearly kept up.
2. Photos of the actual rows, the actual hens, the barn, not a stock basket of perfect vegetables.
3. Plain rules: what a share holds, how skipping works, what a bucket costs, cash or card.
4. A grower they can talk to: a first name, a phone that gets a text back.
5. Hours that match the season, with the stand's closed days said out loud.

**Why they leave in 5 seconds.** No hours on the first screen. A slideshow of sunsets. "Check our Facebook for updates." A PDF menu. A page that still says "Strawberry season is here!" in October.

## 2. The job

**Planned vs urgent.** 100% planned, but time-sensitive: "is it ripe *this* weekend" is the real question. Nothing is an emergency; a sell-out is the closest thing.

**Seasonality (Deep South, Midlands SC / central MS / Wiregrass AL):**
| Month | What's happening |
|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Collards, turnips, cabbage, eggs slow down. Beef and pork orders for spring. Strawberry plants under row covers on frost nights. |
| Mar | First strawberries late month (earlier in Dothan, later in Columbia). Asparagus a little. CSA sign-ups. |
| Apr | Peak strawberries, PYO weekends packed. Lettuce, spring onions. |
| May | Strawberries end; blackberries, new potatoes, squash, first sweet corn (Dothan first). |
| Jun | Peaches (Midlands SC ridge), blueberries (central MS), sweet corn, tomatoes, watermelon starts. |
| Jul | Tomatoes, okra, field peas, butter beans, watermelon, cantaloupe. Heat: stand opens early, closes early. |
| Aug | Muscadines and scuppernongs, okra, peppers, boiled peanuts (Wiregrass, fresh-dug peanuts). |
| Sep | Muscadines, sweet potatoes, fall planting, peanut harvest in the Wiregrass. |
| Oct | Pumpkin patch, corn maze, hayrides, school field trips, sweet potatoes. |
| Nov | Greens, pecans, cane syrup days (AL), Thanksgiving pre-orders, beef pickup. |
| Dec | Christmas trees (Leyland cypress, Virginia pine), wreaths, greens, eggs. |

**Ticket bands.** Small: a dozen eggs, a jar of honey, a gallon of berries. Medium: a pumpkin patch family visit, a monthly egg share. Large: a season CSA share. Largest: a quarter or half of grass-fed beef, a restaurant's weekly standing order.

**Services that sell (from facts):** farm stand, CSA shares, pick-your-own, seasonal produce, pasture-raised eggs, grass-fed beef, pastured pork and poultry, raw honey, pumpkin patch, Christmas trees, farm tours and field trips, wholesale for restaurants.

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A cartoon barn logo and a Wix template with a header photo of a sunset over someone else's field.
2. "Farm fresh, locally grown, from our family to yours" in script on top of a basket of grocery-perfect produce.
3. Hours that live only on a Facebook post from last March.
4. A "Products" page with a grid of fruit clip art and no prices, no season.
5. Pick-your-own info buried in a PDF: rules, bucket sizes, "no pets", read nowhere.
6. A chicken photo with a person grinning at the camera holding it.
7. A CSA "sign up" button that opens an email draft with no explanation of what is in a box.
8. A season banner that is three months out of date.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Ripe this week board.** Learns: what is in, coming, done. Inputs: none (or pick a week). Output: list with a status per crop (picked today / coming / done for the year). Leads to: "Text us before you drive" button.
2. **Picked-out check (PYO).** Learns: whether the field is open this weekend and the best arrival time. Inputs: day. Output: open / limited / closed, with "best before 10 am". Leads to: call or text to confirm.
3. **Share sizer.** Learns: half or full CSA share. Inputs: people at home, how often they cook, whether they like greens. Output: the share size and a sample week's box. Prices from facts only. Leads to: reserve form.
4. **Freezer math (beef).** Learns: what a quarter / half fits in, what cuts come. Inputs: quarter / half, freezer cubic feet. Output: approx. take-home pounds, fits / tight / too big, sample price range from facts. Leads to: reserve a pickup date.
5. **Bucket math (PYO).** Learns: what a family will spend and carry. Inputs: kids, adults, gallon or flat. Output: buckets, approx. pounds, sample price. Leads to: plan the visit.
6. **Season wheel.** Learns: the whole year at a glance, by month for this region. Inputs: tap a month. Output: crops + events. Leads to: "remind me" form (demo).
7. **Drive-time map (drawn SVG).** Learns: how far from their town. Inputs: town from areas. Output: minutes + which day the delivery van comes. Leads to: delivery-point signup.
8. **Field trip planner.** Learns: whether a class fits. Inputs: number of kids, grade, month. Output: what the visit covers and the best month. Leads to: call to book.

Prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices` only, always labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — You know what left the field this morning, and how it got to the stand by ten   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Sixteen Rows Farm · Columbia, SC · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: The Midlands summer is a race against heat: picking starts at first light because a peach or a tomato cut at 2 pm is soft by supper. The site is the farm's morning clock, 5:45 picking, 8:00 cooled and washed, 10:00 on the stand, so a Lexington parent understands why the stand opens early and closes at six, and why it tastes like that.
- Tone lead: dark-led (pre-dawn blue-black, the stand's chalkboard)
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed grotesk in chalk-white for the times and crop names, a humanist sans for reading)
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo, scrolling text — a still frame of rows at first light holds while "5:45 Picking", "8:00 Cooled", "10:00 On the stand" scroll past; the first statement is visible without scrolling.
- Signature seed: **The morning clock.** A thin clock rail on the left edge whose time advances as you scroll (5:45 to 18:00); each section is stamped with the hour it happens on the farm (eggs gathered 7:15, CSA boxes packed 9:30, stand closes 18:00). The stand's chalkboard "Ripe this week" list hangs at 10:00.
- Motion idea: Intro: the sky behind the hero lightens from night to dawn in 1.2 s while the first time stamp chalks on. Scroll story: the clock rail ticks forward and the background warms section by section from pre-dawn to afternoon.
- Tools: Ripe this week board (as a chalkboard), Share sizer + ZIP checker (delivery points) + multi-step reserve form.
- Photo mood: blue hour and first light, low sun, dew on leaves, hands and crates close up, harvest bins, no midday glare. Darker grade, rich greens and reds.

### B — Every question answered before you get in the car   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Halfbushel Farm · Jackson, MS · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Central Mississippi families drive 30-45 minutes for berries in a humid April and leave angry if the field is picked out. Halfbushel answers the four questions before the drive: is it open, is it picked out, what will a bucket cost, and what fits in my freezer. Each answer is a tagged, dated crate label you can read in a glance.
- Tone lead: light-led (white kraft paper, produce-tag manila, ink-black text)
- Type lead: mono-accent (a clean grotesk for reading, a monospace for weights, dates, bucket counts and prices like a scale ticket)
- Hero family: 8 photo mosaic — five crate tiles shot from above (strawberries, blueberries, eggs, okra, honey) in one grade, one huge, each tile carrying a tag with its status this week ("in", "coming", "done"); type on paper beside.
- Signature seed: **The crate tag.** Every fact on the page is a string-tied manila tag with a punched hole: status, weight, date checked, sample price. Tags flip over on tap to show the "how we know" side (e.g. "Field walked Friday 6 pm").
- Motion idea: Intro: tags drop on their strings onto the mosaic tiles and settle (no bounce, a short ease-out). Scroll story: the freezer-math tool fills a drawn chest freezer with cut-labelled blocks as the quarter/half toggles.
- Tools: Picked-out check, Bucket math, Freezer math + ZIP checker + multi-step reserve form.
- Photo mood: overhead flat-lays of crates and produce on wood, bright overcast daylight, clean and even, kraft paper and tags; whole-field shots at mid-morning with soft light.

### C — A Wiregrass farm, its year told by the roads and the people on them   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Sand Road Farm · Dothan, AL · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: The Wiregrass has sandy loam, a long season and a calendar set by peanuts: strawberries in March, sweet corn by Memorial Day, boiled peanuts in August, cane syrup and trees by December. Sand Road tells that year as a drawn county-road map of the Wiregrass, with the farm, the delivery stops in Enterprise and Ozark, and what is coming out of the ground each month.
- Tone lead: color-led (warm sand and red-clay terracotta, pine-green and cane-syrup amber accents)
- Type lead: serif (a warm old-style serif with character, set large for the place names and months; a plain sans for reading)
- Hero family: 15 map / area-led — an illustrated SVG map of the Wiregrass county roads with the farm marked and the towns it reaches, the promise beside it; a month dial on the map shows what is in the ground now.
- Signature seed: **The Wiregrass almanac.** The page is a twelve-month almanac strip: each section opens with its month's page (crop, weather line, the job on the farm that month), and the current month is found and highlighted on load.
- Motion idea: Intro: the county roads draw themselves in from the edges to the farm pin (DrawSVG, 1.4 s). Scroll story: a horizontal almanac rail moves from March to December as you scroll, the map tint shifting with the season.
- Tools: Season wheel (as the almanac), Drive-time map, Field trip planner + ZIP checker + multi-step reserve form.
- Photo mood: wide and warm, late afternoon golden light, flat sandy fields, longleaf and pines at the field edge, peanut rows, dirt roads, clay color, a truck bed from behind; distance over close-up.
