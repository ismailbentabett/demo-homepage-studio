# Auto Body — niche brief

Body shop + cosmetic restoration. Collision repair is the door in (insurance pays, the buyer is shaken);
restoration (paint correction, ceramic coating, headlight and gel coat work) is the work people choose and
come back for. The three sites must sell both, with the insurance claim as the first worry answered.

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Two people.
- *After a crash* (60% of calls): 25-60, commuter or parent, one car the household cannot spare for long. Just
  rear-ended at a light, backed into a pole, side-swiped in a parking lot, or hit by a deer or a pothole. They
  have a claim number from their insurer, a "preferred shop" list in the app, and a photo estimate from the app
  that looks low. They are on a phone in a parking lot or at work on a lunch break.
- *By choice* (40%): 30-70, owns a car they plan to keep 10 years, or a boat at the dock. The paint is dull,
  swirled, chalky or peeling; the headlights are yellow; the hull has gone chalky white from sun. They want it to
  look like new before selling it, before a trip, or just because they are tired of looking at it.

**Fears, in their words.**
1. "Will my insurance make me go to their shop?" (no: in most states you choose; the shop must say so plainly)
2. "The app said $1,400. Is this going to turn into $4,000 and who pays the difference?"
3. "Will the new paint match, or will I see that door forever?"
4. "How long will I be without my car, and do I get a rental?"
5. "Are they going to buff through my clear coat / scratch my boat?"

**What makes them trust.**
1. A plain list of the claim steps with who does what (you, the insurer, the shop) and how long each takes.
2. Photos of the work in the order it happens: teardown, masking, primer, booth, polish, not just a shiny car.
3. A paint-match promise in daylight, with the reason it works (spray-out card, blend into the next panel).
4. A real person answering, a written estimate, and the supplement process explained before it happens.
5. For restoration: a test spot first, and a gloss or paint-depth reading before and after.

**Leave in 5 seconds.** A red sports car with flames and a chrome logo, "Collision experts you can trust",
no phone above the fold, a form that wants the VIN before anything else, a stock handshake with an adjuster,
or no word on insurance anywhere.

## 2. The job

**Split.** Not emergency work (towing is someone else's); urgent-planned. Most collision jobs start within
2-7 days of the crash; restoration is booked 1-3 weeks out.

**Seasonality by region.**
- *Detroit (A):* Dec-Mar is peak: ice rear-enders, slide-offs, road salt eating rocker panels, pothole season
  in Mar-Apr (bent wheels, cracked bumpers). Oct-Nov deer strikes on the suburban edges. Summer is paint and
  rust-repair season (booth runs dry and warm; people fix the winter's damage before selling).
- *El Paso (B):* sun all year, 290+ clear days: clear coat fails on roofs, hoods and trunks (peeling, chalky),
  headlights yellow in 3-4 years. Spring wind and dust (Mar-May) pit paint and windshields; occasional
  late-spring and monsoon hail (Jun-Sep) brings dent work. I-10 and Loop 375 fender benders all year.
- *Fort Lauderdale (C):* boat season Nov-Apr: owners want hulls and topsides restored before winter guests and
  the boat show; summer is sun and salt season (oxidation, water spots on cars, faded paint). Hurricane season
  (Jun-Nov) brings tree-limb dents and flood-claim cars. Snowbirds book coatings in Nov when they come back.

**Ticket bands (words, not $).** Small: headlight pair, one scratch, one or two door dings (under a car payment).
Medium: bumper repair and refinish, one panel paint match, paint correction (a few car payments). Large: a
collision claim with parts and frame measuring, full ceramic coating, a full boat hull compound and wax (often
paid by insurance or saved for).

**Services that sell** (from data/demo-trades.json, rewritten in facts): collision repair, dent repair,
paintless dent removal, paint matching and refinishing, bumper repair, scratch and chip repair, frame
straightening, headlight restoration, ceramic coating, gel coat and boat restoration, insurance claim help.
Plus paint correction (swirls and haze), which every restoration buyer asks for by some other name.

**Homeowner words vs shop words.**
| They say | The shop says |
|---|---|
| "the paint is peeling on the roof" | clear coat delamination / UV failure |
| "swirly scratches in the sun" | marring, holograms, needs paint correction |
| "a little dent with no paint damage" | PDR candidate |
| "my bumper is pushed in" | bumper cover repair, absorber and reinforcement check |
| "the door doesn't look the same color" | color mismatch; needs a blend into the adjacent panel |
| "the insurance estimate is too low" | the supplement after teardown |
| "is the frame bent?" | unibody measured against factory dimensions |
| "the headlights are cloudy" | oxidized polycarbonate lens, UV coat failed |
| "the boat is chalky / faded" | gel coat oxidation, needs wet sanding and compounding |
| "the shiny protection stuff" | ceramic coating, cured layer over corrected paint |
| "rock chips on the hood" | stone chips, touch-up and level |
| "it pulls since the crash" | alignment and suspension check after collision |
| "do I have to use their shop?" | right to choose your repair facility |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A glossy red sports car or a supercar render as the hero (the buyer drives a 2017 crossover).
2. "Quality collision repair you can trust" over a stock photo of a smiling man in a polo with a clipboard.
3. A row of manufacturer and paint-brand logos as the only proof.
4. A slideshow of before/after photos with no story of what happened between them.
5. A huge "Get an estimate" form asking for VIN, year, make, model, insurer and claim number up front.
6. Insurance mentioned only as a logo strip of carriers.
7. Restoration (coating, correction) on a separate "detailing" page with a neon wet-look car under LED lights.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Claim path.** Learns: who does what next and how long. Inputs: what happened (hit by someone / my fault /
   hail or tree / parking lot, no other car), "do you have a claim number yet". Output: 4-6 numbered steps with
   who acts (you, insurer, shop) and typical days, plus "you choose the shop". Leads to: call or send photos.
2. **Photo estimate.** Learns: what we need to price it. Inputs: 4 guided photo slots (whole car at a corner,
   damage straight on, damage at an angle, odometer/VIN plate optional). Output: "we reply with a range by
   phone or text"; with JS off, a text-and-call line.
3. **Dent check.** Learns: PDR or body-and-paint. Inputs: size (coin / palm / larger), paint cracked?, on a
   body line or edge?. Output: "likely paintless" or "likely body and paint", with a sample range from prices.
4. **Paint-match explainer.** Learns: why a blend is not overcharging. Input: slider across a door and fender.
   Output: the spray-out card, the blend zone, and the daylight check.
5. **Sun-damage sorter (El Paso).** Learns: polish, refinish, or coat. Inputs: what the paint looks like (dull /
   chalky / peeling / bare). Output: the fix and its sample range.
6. **Restoration planner.** Learns: correction, coating or both, and how long it lasts. Inputs: car or boat,
   kept outside or garaged / lifted or in the water, goal (sell, keep, show). Output: a plan with steps and a
   sample range.
7. **Hull size calculator (boats).** Inputs: boat length in feet, oxidation level (light/medium/heavy). Output: a
   sample range from the per-foot price.
8. **Days without a car.** Inputs: damage size and parts needed (guessed by type). Output: typical days in the shop
   and the rental question to ask the insurer.

Prices only from facts.proof.prices, always labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — From teardown to final wash: every repair shown in the order the shop does it   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Booth Nine Collision · Detroit, MI · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: A Detroit commuter's car comes in after a salt-season rear-ender, and the page follows it through
  the shop's nine stations: check-in photos, teardown, the supplement call, measuring, metal and plastic repair,
  prime and block, the booth, polish, and the final wash and walk-around. Each station says what happens, what you
  hear from us, and what the insurer does at that moment, so the claim stops feeling like a black box.
- Tone lead: dark-led (spray-booth night: deep gunmetal and booth-wall grey, one masking-tape yellow accent for steps
  and links, booth-light white type).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall industrial condensed sans for station numbers and headlines, like stencils on a
  booth door; a plain sans for body).
- Hero family: 9 — horizontal filmstrip (under the headline "Nine stations. You hear from us at every one.", a
  slow strip of frames runs left: masking, sanding, primer, booth, polisher; it pauses on hover and focus and stops
  with reduced motion).
- Signature seed: **the masking-tape strip.** Every section opens with a strip of yellow masking tape written on in
  marker ("Station 4 · Measure · Day 2 · You get a text"), the way panels are labelled in a real shop; on scroll the
  tape peels back to reveal the finished panel colour beneath. Only this site labels its life in tape.
- Motion idea: intro, the filmstrip eases in and the first tape strip lays itself across the headline; scroll story,
  a pinned station counter (1-9) ticks while a single panel goes from bare metal to gloss in four photo steps.
- Tools: claim path, days without a car, dent check + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form (with photo upload step).
- Photo mood: inside the shop at night or under booth lights: hard white tube light, gunmetal shadows, close and
  mid distance on hands, sanders, tape and panels; winter grit and salt on lower panels for the local shots.

### B — Your paint, measured: answers in numbers before you call   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Basin Light Body & Paint · El Paso, TX · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: In El Paso the sun is the biggest body shop customer, so this site leads with readings, not
  adjectives: a sun-damage sorter that tells you polish, refinish or coat; a dent check that says paintless or not;
  and a claim path with the days each step takes. Every claim on the page is backed by a measured number (paint
  depth in mils, gloss before and after, days in shop, sample range), set like a test sheet.
- Tone lead: light-led (high-desert noon: chalk white and caliche sand paper, ink-black type, one clear-coat blue
  for readings and actions; shadows hard and short).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a precise monospace for every reading, mil value, day count and sample price; a firm
  neutral grotesk for headlines and body).
- Hero family: 7 — tool-first (the hero is the sun-damage sorter: four swatches, dull / chalky / peeling / bare,
  each tap shows the fix, a paint-depth reading and the sample range; beside it the promise and phone; with JS off
  it is a readable four-row list with the phone).
- Signature seed: **the gauge readout.** A small paint-depth gauge display ("4.6 mils · factory range") sits in the
  corner of every section and changes with it: factory paint, sun-thinned clear, after correction, after coat. It
  is the site's proof system: numbers you could check with the same gauge at the counter.
- Motion idea: intro, the gauge digits count up from 0.0 to the factory reading and settle; scroll story, a hood
  photo goes from chalky to corrected as the reading and the gloss bar move in step.
- Tools: sun-damage sorter, dent check, claim path + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright, hard desert daylight; close macro of paint surfaces, gauges, polisher pads and test spots;
  sun-faded roofs and hoods, Franklin Mountains haze in the distance for the local frames; no golden-hour glamour.

### C — Salt, sun and the same shine for the car in the driveway and the boat at the dock   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Saltline Finish & Collision · Fort Lauderdale, FL · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: In Fort Lauderdale the same salt air and sun fade the car under the carport and the hull at the
  canal dock behind the house. A shop trained in collision repair brings both back: paint correction and ceramic
  coating for cars, wet sanding and compounding for chalky gel coat, and honest collision work when a storm drops a
  limb. The page follows the South Florida year: boat season, snowbird return, rainy season, storm season.
- Tone lead: color-led (Intracoastal teal as the main field, sun-bleached gel-coat white, a coral-red boot-stripe
  accent and deep navy text; each season band takes its own tint).
- Type lead: serif (a crisp high-contrast serif with nautical-chart character for headlines; a clean sans for body).
- Hero family: 13 — shape-masked photo (a boat-hull bow silhouette masks a photo of a gel coat hull half restored,
  the line between chalky and glossy running through the shape; the headline sits beside it on teal).
- Signature seed: **the boot stripe.** A double stripe (navy and coral, like the waterline stripe on a hull) runs
  along every section; the area above it is the "after" finish and below it the "before" oxidation, and it is the
  rule that divides each section's car side from its boat side. The bow shape from the hero returns as the
  section marker.
- Motion idea: intro, the boot stripe draws left to right under the hero and the chalky half of the photo wipes to
  gloss; scroll story, a season calendar (Nov to Oct) slides horizontally with the jobs each month brings.
- Tools: restoration planner, hull size calculator, claim path + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright coastal light, morning or late afternoon, white hulls against blue water, palms and canal docks
  in the background; close macro of gel coat, polisher on a hull, water beading on coated paint; never yachts-for-
  the-rich glamour, always working docks and driveways.
