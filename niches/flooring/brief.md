# Flooring — niche brief

Three demo homepages for residential flooring companies: hardwood refinishing, new floors (hardwood, engineered, luxury vinyl plank, tile) and floor repair. All three are sample sites: fictional brands, real cities and ZIPs, 555-01NN phones, `.example` email, proof marked "Sample".

## 1. The buyer

- **Who calls:**
  - Homeowners aged 30-70, often just after buying a house (pulling up carpet and finding oak underneath), getting a house ready to sell, or finally replacing worn carpet and vinyl.
  - Parents and pet owners asking for something that survives kids and dogs.
  - People with water damage from a dishwasher, a slab leak or a storm.
- **What just happened:**
  - They pulled up carpet and found hardwood.
  - The finish is gray and worn in the traffic lanes.
  - Boards are cupping near the sink.
  - The dog scratched the floors.
  - They are about to list the house.
  - They saw a "waterproof" floor at a big-box store and want to know if it is right for a slab.
- **Fears, in their words:**
  - "They'll sand right through my old floors."
  - "The stain will look nothing like the little sample."
  - "We'll be out of the house for two weeks and the dust will be everywhere."
  - "The cheap vinyl will peel at the seams in a year."
  - "They'll lay the new floor on a wet slab and it'll buckle."
- **What makes them trust:**
  - Stain samples on their own floor.
  - A per-square-foot sample range before the visit.
  - A moisture reading before any install.
  - Clear days-out-of-the-room numbers.
  - Close-up photos of real grain and seams.
  - An honest "your floor can be refinished, you don't need new" answer.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:**
  - A showroom catalogue site with 400 SKUs.
  - Glossy 3D-rendered rooms.
  - "Free estimate!" banners and no prices.
  - A carpet-store look.
  - Photos of commercial lobbies.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned:** about 10% urgent (water damage, a soft spot, a closing date) and 90% planned. Lead time is usually 1-4 weeks.

**Seasonality:**
- **Knoxville, TN:** spring and fall are the busy refinishing seasons, because windows can open and the finish cures well. Summer humidity slows waterborne finishes. Before Thanksgiving there is a rush to "get it done before family comes".
- **Atlanta, GA:** steady all year. The busy season is spring listings and new homeowners. Summer humidity matters for slab moisture before LVP or engineered installs. Holiday-deadline jobs come in October-November.
- **Norfolk, VA:** nor'easters and king tides bring water-damage repair work (fall-winter). Humid summers mean crawlspace moisture checks before hardwood goes down. Older houses in Ghent and Colonial Place have heart pine to restore, and spring is the season for it.

**Average ticket (words, not dollars):**
- Small: a screen-and-recoat or a single board repair.
- Mid: a full sand-and-refinish of a main floor.
- Large: new hardwood or engineered on a whole floor, or tile in baths and laundry.
- Exact sample ranges per sq ft are in each facts file's `proof.prices`.

**Services that sell:** sand and refinish, screen and recoat, stain sampling, board repair and lacing-in, new hardwood, engineered hardwood, LVP, tile, stairs, leveling, moisture testing, water-damage repair.

**Homeowner word vs. pro word:**

| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| redo the floors | sand and refinish (full sand to bare wood) |
| just make them shiny again | screen and recoat (buff and new topcoat) |
| the color | stain (and the topcoat sheen: matte, satin, semi-gloss) |
| the clear coat | finish: waterborne poly, oil-modified poly, hardwax oil |
| the floor is wavy | cupping (edges up) or crowning (middle up) |
| the gaps between boards | seasonal gapping |
| patch in new boards | lacing or weaving in boards |
| the vinyl planks | LVP / rigid core (SPC, WPC), wear layer in mil |
| real wood on top | engineered hardwood (veneer / wear layer over plywood core) |
| the floor bounces | deflection / subfloor fastening |
| the floor isn't flat | out of flat: leveling, self-leveling underlayment |
| is the concrete dry | slab moisture test: calcium chloride or RH probe |
| the strips at doorways | transitions, thresholds, reducers |
| the sanding marks | chatter marks / swirl marks from the edger |

## 5. Niche tools

1. **Stain sample picker**
   - What the homeowner learns: what common stains look like on their wood type, and which sheen.
   - Inputs: wood (red oak / white oak / heart pine / maple / not sure), the light in the room (north / south / mixed), stain swatch (6 CSS-drawn swatches over a photo of raw oak), sheen (matte / satin).
   - Output: the swatch over the grain, a note on how red oak pulls pink and white oak pulls gray, and the line "we lay real samples on your floor before you choose".
   - Leads to the call: "Book a sample visit" with the picked stains prefilled.
2. **Refinish-or-recoat quiz**
   - What the homeowner learns: whether the floor needs a full sand, a screen and recoat, or new boards.
   - Inputs: is the finish worn through to gray wood, are there deep scratches or pet stains, has it been sanded before (count, or not sure), any cupping.
   - Output: the likely job, why, and the sample per-sq-ft range.
   - Leads to the call: "Get it checked."
3. **Square-foot estimator**
   - What the homeowner learns: a sample price range for their rooms.
   - Inputs: rooms (add length × width, or pick a common size), service (from facts prices), stairs count.
   - Output: total sq ft, the sample range (low × sq ft to high × sq ft) labelled sample, and days out of the room.
   - Leads to the call: "Send these numbers."
4. **Material compare**
   - What the homeowner learns: LVP vs engineered vs solid hardwood vs tile for their room.
   - Inputs: room (kitchen / bath / basement / bedroom / living), pets (yes/no), slab or crawlspace.
   - Output: a four-column comparison (water, scratch, can it be refinished, sample price per sq ft, feel underfoot), with the fit for that room highlighted.
   - Leads to the call: "See the samples at your house."
5. **Slab moisture explainer**
   - What the homeowner learns: why the concrete is tested before wood or vinyl goes down.
   - Inputs: slab age, a ground-floor room (yes/no), any past water.
   - Output: what the test is (RH probe), what happens if it reads high (vapor barrier, a different product), and the leveling sample range.
   - Leads to the call: "Book a moisture test."
6. **House-age floor guide**
   - What the homeowner learns: what is probably under their carpet.
   - Inputs: the year the house was built (slider 1890-2020), neighborhood type.
   - Output: the likely floor (heart pine before 1920, oak strip 1920s-60s, plywood or slab after) and what can be done with it.
   - Leads to the call: "Pull a corner and send us a photo."
7. **Flood-recovery checklist**
   - What the homeowner learns: what to do in the first 48 hours after water reaches a wood floor.
   - Inputs: tick boxes (water source stopped, standing water removed, fans running, crawlspace checked, photos taken for insurance).
   - Output: the next steps, what not to do (do not sand a wet floor), and the water-damage sample range.
   - Leads to the call: "Book a damage check."
8. **ZIP checker** (all three sites)
   - Input: a 5-digit ZIP.
   - Output: served or not, from `zips`.
9. **Multi-step quote form** (all three sites)
   - Steps: service; rooms and approximate sq ft; current floor and any water; photos; timing (move-in date, listing date); contact.

## 6. The three directions

### A — Sixty grit to finish coat, in order   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)

- Brand: Third Coat Floor Works · Knoxville, TN · facts-A.json
- Concept seed:
  - The refinish is told in its real order, and the page gets finer as it goes. The first section is rough and saw-cut (36 grit: old finish off), then 60, then 100 (smooth), then stain, then first coat, second coat, third coat.
  - The name is the last step.
  - Knoxville angle: Fountain City and Sequoyah Hills oak strip under 1970s carpet, and Smoky Mountain humidity that sets finish cure times.
- Tone lead: light-led. Raw maple and oak paper tones, warm white, dark walnut type, one deep finish-amber accent.
- Type lead: serif. A warm book serif for headings (crafted, calm), simple sans for body and the grit numbers.
- Hero family: 9 horizontal filmstrip. A horizontal strip of five frames, one per stage (worn floor → 36 grit → bare wood → stain → satin finish). The headline sits above, and the strip is scrollable by swipe and arrow buttons. It is not autoplay.
- Signature seed:
  - A grit sequence runs through the page. Each section header carries its grit number (36 · 60 · 100 · stain · coat 1 · 2 · 3), and the background noise texture gets finer from section to section.
  - Dividers are the edge of a sanded pass: a rough band, then a smooth one.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the filmstrip frames unclip left to right, like a sander's pass across the floor.
  - Scroll story: one floor photo is pinned. As you scroll it goes from worn to sanded to stained to finished (a crossfade of four photos under a moving "sander line"), with the grit counter ticking. With reduced motion, it is four stacked photos with captions.
- Tools: stain sample picker; refinish-or-recoat quiz; square-foot estimator; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: soft window light across oak strip, raw sanded wood, sawdust in light, a drum sander's path, stain being wiped by hand, satin finish reflecting a window. Warm, calm, close.

### B — Every floor has a spec, and you see it before you choose   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)

- Brand: Twelve Mil Flooring · Atlanta, GA · facts-B.json
- Concept seed:
  - The site is a sample board. Each floor is shown as a sample card with its spec: wear layer in mil (the name is the thick wear layer they recommend for pets), thickness, core, water rating, refinish count and sample price per sq ft.
  - The material compare and moisture test answer "which floor" before the call.
  - Atlanta angle: slab-on-grade houses in Smyrna and Tucker, 1950s ranches with oak in Decatur and Brookhaven, dogs, and humidity.
- Tone lead: color-led. A deep clay-terracotta field (Georgia red clay, softened) with bone white and charcoal; sample cards sit on it like a showroom board.
- Type lead: grotesk. A precise neo-grotesk for headings and spec rows, figures tabular.
- Hero family: 2 50/50 split. Left: the promise and a stacked sample board (4 cards). Right: a full-height photo of a finished floor, edge-lit.
- Signature seed:
  - Sample cards everywhere. Each service is a physical-looking sample chip with a cut edge, a spec strip (wear layer · thickness · water · refinishable), and a tiny cross-section drawing (wear layer / core / backing) that draws itself.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the sample cards fan out from a stack in the hero (rise and unclip, no bounce).
  - Scroll story: a cross-section of a plank is pinned. Layers peel apart as you scroll (wear layer, print layer, core, backing), each labelled with what it does and why the mil number matters for a dog. With reduced motion, a static labelled diagram.
- Tools: material compare; slab moisture explainer; square-foot estimator; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: crisp daylight, close-ups of plank edges and seams, cross-sections, sample boards laid on a slab, a dog's paw on LVP (no face), finished kitchens in real Atlanta ranches. Clean, bright, material-first.

### C — The floors under Norfolk's old houses   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)

- Brand: Heart & Tide Floor Co. · Norfolk, VA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed:
  - Norfolk's older houses in Ghent, Colonial Place, Larchmont and Park Place sit on heart pine milled more than a century ago, and on crawlspaces that breathe the tide.
  - The story is the wood and the water: heart pine to bring back, nor'easters and king tides that warp floors, crawlspace moisture that cups boards, and waterproof floors where water will come again.
- Tone lead: dark-led. Deep tide-navy and wet-slate dark ground, heart-pine amber as the warm accent, pale sand type.
- Type lead: condensed. A tall condensed sans for headlines like harbour warehouse lettering; humanist body.
- Hero family: 3 type mask. The huge condensed word "HEART PINE" is a mask filled with a close-up of century-old heart pine grain. The promise and phone sit under it on the dark ground.
- Signature seed:
  - A tide line. A thin horizontal rule rises and falls between sections like a tide gauge (marked in feet, "king tide 3.8 ft").
  - In the water sections the tide line crosses into the photo and the floor beneath darkens. Heart-pine growth rings are used as a section motif.
- Motion idea:
  - Intro: the grain inside the "HEART PINE" mask slowly drifts (a parallax of 20 px), and the headline rises.
  - Scroll story: a house cross-section (floor, joists, crawlspace) is pinned. The tide line rises on scroll, and the problems appear in order (crawlspace humidity → cupping → standing water), each with the fix and sample price. With reduced motion, three stacked diagrams.
- Tools: house-age floor guide; flood-recovery checklist; ZIP checker; multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low raking light on old heart pine, porch floors of old Norfolk houses, rain on windows, a crawlspace with a moisture meter, boards being laced in by hand. Moody, warm wood on dark.
