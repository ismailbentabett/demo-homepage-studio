# Cannabis Shop — niche brief

Compliance frame for all three sites (non-negotiable, the builder and copy chief read this first):
adults 21+ only, with a 21+ confirmation gate that still lets the page read with JS off (a plain "You must be 21+
to shop" line and no product imagery above it). No health, medical or effect claims ("helps you sleep", "for pain",
"relieves", "calms", "heals"): formats are described by how they are used, how strength is labelled and how long they
take to notice, never by what they treat. No cartoon, candy, toy or bright-gummy imagery, no youth slang, no
consumption shown. No delivery promise: pickup and curbside are described as a process. Licence number appears only
through the token and labelled "Licence # (sample)". Every price is a sample range from facts.

## 1. The buyer
**Who looks.** Adults 21-75, in three groups:
- **The first-timer or returner**: 35-70, last tried it in college or never, now curious because it is legal in the
  state. Often a parent, a retiree, a couple. Nervous about the door, the jargon and looking clueless.
- **The regular**: 25-45, knows what they like, shops by price per gram, test date and what is in stock. Wants the
  menu, the hours and a pre-order button, nothing else.
- **The visitor**: in Flagstaff on the way to the Grand Canyon, in Santa Cruz for the weekend from the Bay Area,
  in Kalamazoo for a game or a parents' weekend. Wants to know: can I buy with an out-of-state ID, where do I park,
  what am I allowed to do with it after.

**What just happened.** A friend said "just go to a shop, they'll explain". A road trip through a legal state. A
coupon in the mailbox. Their usual shop raised prices or ran out. The first warm weekend, the first snow day, a
holiday weekend with family in town.

**Fears, in their words.**
- "I don't know what to ask for and I'll look stupid at the counter."
- "Edibles hit me way too hard last time. How much is actually in one?"
- "Is this stuff tested? What is actually in a vape cart?"
- "What's the real price after tax? The menu said one thing and the register said another."
- "Can I even use my out-of-state license? Do I need cash?"
- "Is it going to feel sketchy walking in?"

**What makes them trust.**
- A calm, bright, organized store: glass cases, jars, clear price tags, a person who explains.
- Test dates and strength printed on the menu, not hidden in a QR code.
- The out-the-door price, or at least how tax is added, shown before they drive over.
- A clear "first visit" walk-through: ID at the door, what the counter looks like, cash or debit, how long it takes.
- Hours that are right, a pickup lane that is where the site says it is.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A neon-green leaf logo and stoner jokes. A site that looks like a candy
shop. An age gate that blocks everything and then a menu that does not load. No hours above the fold. "Call for
prices". Ads for a 4/20 sale that ended two years ago.

## 2. The job
**Walk-in vs planned.** About 55% walk in after work or before a weekend; 45% pre-order online and pick up at the
counter or curbside. Pre-order share grows with weather: snow in Flagstaff and Kalamazoo, rain in Santa Cruz.

**Seasonality by region.**
- Flagstaff (7,000 ft, ponderosa forest): busiest late May to early September (Grand Canyon and Route 66 traffic,
  summer cabin people from Phoenix), a second bump in ski season (Dec-Mar) and around snow days; monsoon afternoons
  (July-August) push people to pre-order. Quiet in April and November.
- Kalamazoo (lake-effect snow belt, two college campuses, Portage suburbs): steady all year; spikes on Fridays,
  game weekends, Thanksgiving week, 4/20 and before long weekends. Lake-effect snow days (Dec-Feb) send people to
  curbside pickup.
- Santa Cruz (coastal fog, redwoods, beach town): tourist spike Memorial Day to Labor Day and every sunny weekend;
  the fog season (June-August mornings) means slow mornings and busy late afternoons; winter storms on Highway 17
  cut the mountain traffic for a few days at a time.

**Ticket bands (words, not dollars).** A single pre-roll or a small edible pack is the small ticket; an eighth of
flower plus a cartridge is the typical one; an ounce, a stock-up for a long weekend or concentrates are the big
ticket. First-timers usually buy small and come back.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Flower, pre-rolls, edibles, vapes and cartridges, concentrates, tinctures,
topicals, CBD products, accessories, online pre-order, curbside pickup, first-time guidance.

**Customer words vs shop words.**
| Customer says | Shop says |
|---|---|

| "a joint" | pre-roll |
| "gummies", "a chocolate" | edibles, infused products |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)
- A neon-green leaf on black with a smoke cloud behind the logo.
- An age gate that is a full-screen modal with a stock photo of a joint and a "YES I'M 21" button in Comic-style type.
- An embedded third-party menu iframe that loads slowly, then shows 400 products with no way to start.
- "Premium cannabis, exceptional experience" headlines that say nothing.
- Stoner puns, "high" jokes, cartoon mascots.
- A wall of brand logos of the products they carry.
- Deals banners that are out of date, prices "before tax" with no hint of what tax adds.
- Photos of people smoking on a beach or a couch.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **First-visit walk-through.** Learns: exactly what happens from the door to the bag. Inputs: none, or "first
   time / been a while / regular". Output: 5 steps (ID check, the counter, the questions a budtender will ask,
   paying, the sealed bag) with the time each takes. Leads to: "Pre-order your first visit" or call.
2. **Price-per-gram compare.** Learns: whether an eighth, a quarter or an ounce is the better buy. Inputs: pick two
   sizes from the sample price ranges. Output: price per gram side by side, labelled sample. Leads to: pre-order form
   prefilled with the size.
3. **Lab sheet reader.** Learns: how to read a COA. Inputs: tap a part of a sample lab sheet (THC, CBD, terpenes,
   test date, contaminant panel). Output: one plain sentence per line. Leads to: "Ask us for the sheet of any jar".
4. **Format explainer (start-low card).** Learns: how each format is used and how long it usually takes to notice
   (inhaled minutes, edibles up to two hours), and "start low, go slow". Inputs: pick a format. Output: a card with
   serving size labelling and timing. No effect or health claims. Leads to: first-time guidance booking.
5. **Pickup planner.** Learns: when the order will be ready and where to pull in. Inputs: pick-up day and time
   window, counter or curbside. Output: a pickup ticket with "bring your ID" and the order steps. Leads to: the
   multi-step pre-order form.
6. **Out-the-door estimate.** Learns: roughly what tax adds. Inputs: a sample cart total. Output: "tax is added at
   the register; here is how it is calculated in this state" (rate shown as sample, never a promised total).
   Leads to: call or pre-order.
7. **Visitor checklist.** Learns: what an out-of-state adult needs (valid ID, 21+, keep it sealed and out of reach
   while driving, not on federal land). Inputs: "visiting from out of state?" toggle. Output: checklist. Leads to: call.
8. **Open-now + pickup lane.** Learns: open or closed, how long until close, whether curbside is running. Inputs:
   none (uses facts hours). Output: status line. Leads to: call or pre-order.

The ZIP checker on these sites answers "is this our pickup area / how far is the drive", never "do we deliver".

## 6. The three directions

### A — From the cure room to your hand, every step shown   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Night Jar Cannabis Co. · Flagstaff, AZ · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Flagstaff is a dark-sky town at 7,000 feet, and the shop treats a visit like a slow night walk: one
  lit stop at a time. The page follows a jar of flower through the shop (received, checked against its lab sheet,
  stored dark and cool, opened at the counter, weighed, sealed) and then follows the customer through the same
  counter (ID, questions, pay, sealed bag), so a first-timer sees exactly where they fit before they arrive.
- Tone lead: dark-led (deep pine-night blue-black field, warm amber lamplight as the light source, a frosted
  trichome white for type, ponderosa-bark rust as the one accent).
- Type lead: serif (a calm, warm old-style serif with good italics for headlines and step names; a quiet humanist
  sans for body and labels).
- Hero family: 9 horizontal filmstrip (a slow strip of frames under the headline: jar on a shelf, lid off, flower on
  a scale, sealed bag, pickup window at dusk; it pauses on hover/focus and stops with reduced motion).
- Signature seed: **lamp stops.** Every step of the page is a small pool of amber light on the dark field, numbered
  like lanterns on a path (01 Door, 02 Case, 03 Counter, 04 Scale, 05 Seal, 06 Pickup); only the current stop is
  lit, the rest glow faintly, and the step's photo lives inside its pool of light.
- Motion idea: intro: the page starts in darkness and the first lamp warms up behind the headline in under a second.
  Scroll story: in the visit section, the lamps light one after another down the path while the filmstrip frame for
  each step slides into the lit pool.
- Tools: first-visit walk-through, format explainer (start-low card), visitor checklist, + ZIP checker + multi-step
  pre-order form.
- Photo mood: low, warm, intimate light on dark grounds; close and mid shots of jars, flower on a scale, glass cases
  lit from inside, hands (no faces) at a counter, pine branches with snow at dusk. Never daylight-bright, never smoke.

### B — Read the jar before you buy it   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Batch & Date Cannabis · Kalamazoo, MI · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Michigan has some of the lowest shelf prices in the country, so Kalamazoo shoppers compare hard:
  strength, test date, price per gram. Batch & Date puts every number on the page before the drive: the lab sheet
  explained line by line, sample price-per-gram math, how tax is added, and the date each batch was tested, so the
  regular can decide in a minute and the first-timer learns what the numbers mean.
- Tone lead: light-led (lab-white and pale paper field, graphite type, a test-tube amber accent for numbers, a cool
  sample-bag blue for tags; no green leaf palette).
- Type lead: mono-accent (a precise monospace for every number, batch code, date and price; a clean grotesk for
  headlines and body).
- Hero family: 14 document object (the hero is a shelf tag / lab-sheet card for a sample jar: batch code, test date,
  THC %, top terpenes, sample price per gram, "sheet on request"; each line is an anchor to the section that explains
  it).
- Signature seed: **batch tags.** Every section is filed under a batch tag with a code (B-01 Strength, B-02 Test
  date, B-03 Price per gram, B-04 Tax, B-05 Pickup) and a printed date; numbers are set in mono and underlined in
  amber when the page explains them, like a highlighter on a lab report.
- Motion idea: intro: the hero tag prints line by line like a label printer, ending with the batch code. Scroll story:
  the price-per-gram section runs a counter that recalculates as the sizes step from an eighth to an ounce, with a
  bar that shrinks per gram.
- Tools: lab sheet reader, price-per-gram compare, out-the-door estimate, open-now + pickup lane, + ZIP checker +
  multi-step pre-order form.
- Photo mood: bright, even, clean daylight or light-box light; flat lays and close-ups of flower on white, glass jars
  in rows, a digital scale, sample tubes, a magnifier on buds, trichomes in macro. Cool and precise, never moody.

### C — A shop on the fog line, open after the sun breaks through   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Poppy & Fog Cannabis · Santa Cruz, CA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: In Santa Cruz the day runs on the marine layer: gray mornings, the sun burning through by early
  afternoon, the redwoods dripping on Highway 17. Poppy & Fog builds the page around that day and the places people
  come from (the Westside, Live Oak, Capitola, the mountain towns): when the fog lifts, when the counter is quiet,
  which pickup window suits a drive down from Felton, all told in the voice of a neighbor who has lived here a while.
- Tone lead: color-led (a fog teal-gray field warming to California-poppy orange, with redwood brown and sea-glass
  cream; the color shifts warmer down the page like the day clearing).
- Type lead: condensed (a tall condensed sans for headlines, like hand-painted beach-town shop lettering; a plain sans
  for body).
- Hero family: 13 shape-masked photo (a round "sun through fog" disc masks the hero photo of coastal fog and redwoods,
  with the headline set beside it; the disc returns as the signature).
- Signature seed: **the fog dial.** A round dial (the same disc as the hero) sits at the edge of each section and turns
  from morning fog to afternoon sun as the visitor scrolls; it reads the shop's day (quiet morning counter, busy
  late afternoon, last pickup) from the facts hours, and every section is tinted by its time of day.
- Motion idea: intro: a soft fog layer drifts off the hero disc and the poppy color comes up underneath. Scroll story:
  the "our day" section moves the dial from open to close, and the area map's towns light in the order the fog
  clears them (coast first, mountains last).
- Tools: pickup planner, open-now + pickup lane, first-visit walk-through, + ZIP checker + multi-step pre-order form.
- Photo mood: coastal and soft; marine-layer fog over the coast, redwood trunks in mist, California poppies, late
  afternoon gold light on wooden counters and glass jars, a cannabis plant in filtered outdoor light. Muted mornings,
  warm afternoons, no beach party scenes, no surfing-and-smoking.
