# Excavation — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 30-65 on a half-acre to ten-acre lot at the edge of town: a couple building a house on
land they bought two years ago, a retired man whose yard turns into a pond every April, a family that just bought a
1970s ranch with a gravel drive that washes into the ditch, a woman adding a shop building or an in-ground pool and
told by the builder "you'll need your own dirt guy". Also small builders and remodelers who need a basement or footing
dug and do not want to babysit an operator.

**What just happened.**
- Water came into the basement or crawlspace after a three-inch rain, or stood against the foundation for days.
- The driveway rutted out, the culvert at the road clogged, or gravel washed into the street again.
- The builder, pool company or septic installer said "get the site graded and the pad ready before we come".
- They bought land and need a house pad, a driveway in and a trench for power and water.
- A neighbour's runoff now crosses their yard after a new house went up uphill.

**Fears, in their words.**
- "They'll tear up the whole yard getting the machine back there and leave it a mud pit."
- "They'll hit the water line or the gas line and I'll be the one paying for it."
- "I'll pay to fix the drainage and the water will just go somewhere else in the yard, or into my neighbour's."
- "The price will double the minute they hit rock." (Ozark chert, Arkansas sandstone ledges.)
- "They'll dig it and disappear for three weeks with a hole in my yard."

**What makes them trust.**
- They explain where the water will go, with a sketch or flags in the ground, before any digging.
- They say out loud that they call 811 for utility locates and wait for the paint.
- A written number that says what happens if they hit rock, and what it costs per hour or per load.
- Photos of trenches with straight walls, compacted backfill, a finished grade with straw and seed down.
- A real phone answered by the person who will run the machine, and a start date they keep.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.**
- A wall of yellow-iron photos with no idea of what the job will be for *their* yard.
- "Commercial and residential site development solutions" copy that reads like a road contractor.
- No idea of price at all, or a contact form with 14 fields.
- Stock photos of a highway job or a mining dump truck.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** About 15 % urgent (water in the basement, a washed-out driveway, a broken line that needs
digging up), 85 % planned (house pads, footings, driveways, drainage, utility trenches, ponds, pool digs).
The "urgent" work is still scheduled within days, not hours.

**Seasonality.**
- *Springfield MO (Ozarks):* March-May is the rush: spring rain shows every drainage problem and builders start
  foundations. June-August dry and steady (ponds, pads). Sept-Nov second peak before freeze; Dec-Feb only when the
  ground is not frozen or soup.
- *Fayetteville AR (Northwest Arkansas):* steady new-home building all year; heavy spring storms (April-June) bring
  driveway washouts and wet-yard calls; autumn is the best grading weather; winter slows on wet weeks.
- *Macon GA (Middle Georgia):* red clay works year round except right after rain; summer thunderstorms (June-Aug)
  drive French drain and grading calls; late autumn-winter is pond and land-clearing-to-pad season.

**Ticket bands (words).** Small: a day of backhoe work, a short trench, a load of gravel spread. Medium: a French drain
run around the house, a regraded back yard, a gravel driveway with a culvert. Large: a full basement dig with
backfill, a house pad plus driveway plus utility trenches, a stock pond.

**Services that sell** (from data/demo-trades.json): foundation excavation, trenching, backhoe work by the hour,
site grading, driveway installation, drainage solutions, French drains, utility line trenching, pond digging,
retaining wall prep, gravel delivery and spreading, demolition debris removal.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "water sits against the house" | negative grade / reverse slope at the foundation |
| "make the yard slope away" | finish grade at 5 % for the first 10 ft |
| "dig for the basement" | mass excavation, over-dig for footing forms |
| "fill the dirt back in" | backfill in lifts, compacted |
| "the ditch for the water line" | utility trench, below frost depth |
| "gravel road" | aggregate base, crusher run / SB2, with a crown |
| "pipe under the driveway" | culvert, sized for the ditch flow |
| "a rock drain" | French drain: perforated pipe in clean stone, fabric wrapped |
| "flat spot for the house" | building pad, compacted to spec |
| "a big rock in the way" | ledge rock / chert / boulder, needs a breaker |
| "a dip for the water to run" | swale |
| "where the water comes out" | daylight outlet / pop-up emitter |
| "how much dirt" | cubic yards, bank vs loose (swell) |
| "they marked the yard with paint" | 811 locate, utility marks |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

2. A list of 30 services in one gray block ("Site Work · Grading · Hauling · Clearing · ...") with no explanation.
3. "Residential & Commercial" everywhere, with photos of road jobs and dump trucks that scare a homeowner.
4. A gallery of 80 phone photos of mud with no captions.
5. "Call for a free estimate" and nothing about price, rock or weather delays.
6. A Facebook feed embed as the "reviews" section.
7. Logos of equipment brands as if they were credentials.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Dirt calculator (cubic yards).** Inputs: length, width, depth (or "basement / driveway / pad" presets).
   Output: bank cubic yards, loose yards after swell, about how many truckloads. Leads to the call: "Bring these
   numbers when you call, we'll check them."
2. **Where does the water go? (drainage picker).** Inputs: where water stands (against the house / middle of yard /
   driveway / basement), soil feel (clay / rocky / sandy), roof gutters yes/no. Output: the likely fix (regrade,
   swale, French drain, downspout lines, culvert) with a sample price range from facts. Leads to "Price it".
3. **Gravel driveway estimator.** Inputs: length, width, new or re-top. Output: tons of base and top rock, loads,
   sample range. Leads to the quote form pre-filled.
4. **Utility trench planner.** Inputs: what goes in (water, power, gas, conduit for a shop), distance. Output: typical
   depth for the line, trench length, what we need from you (locate ticket, inspector). Leads to the call.
5. **Machine access check.** Inputs: gate width, slope, septic in the yard, fence. Output: which machine fits (mini
   excavator, skid steer, backhoe), and how the yard is protected (mats, plywood). Leads to "send us a photo by text".
6. **Rock clause explainer.** A slider: how much rock we hit (none / some / ledge). Output: what changes in the
   price, in plain words, using the facts price ranges. Leads to trust and the call.
7. **Season planner.** Month picker: what work fits that month locally (ground frozen, soup, dry), how far out the
   schedule is in a typical year (stated as a pattern, not a promise).

Prices are ranges from facts.proof.prices only, always labelled sample.

## 6. The three directions

### A — Every dig is staked, cut, checked and closed   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Batter Board Excavating · Springfield, MO · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: In the Ozarks the ground is chert, clay and sinkholes, and the homeowner's fear is a hole that never
  gets finished. The site walks one job from the first wooden stake to the last bale of straw: locate, stake, cut,
  set, backfill in lifts, grade, seed. Each step shows what the homeowner sees in their yard that day.
- Tone lead: dark-led (night-blue soil section with chert-white type and one survey-ribbon orange)
- Type lead: grotesk (a hard industrial grotesk for headlines and step numbers, tabular figures for depths)
- Hero family: 11 diagonal split (the seam is the 1:1 side slope of a foundation cut: type sits on the "ground",
  the photo fills the dug side; the angle is the batter of a real dig)
- Signature seed: **grade stakes.** Every section is headed by a drawn wooden grade stake with flagging ribbon and
  the cut or fill mark surveyors write on it ("C 2.4" = cut 2.4 ft; "F 0.6" = fill); the stake's mark tells how
  deep into the job the section is, and the last stake reads "0.0 · on grade".
- Motion idea: intro: the diagonal seam slices in like a bucket pass and the headline settles on the bank. Scroll
  story: a cross-section of the yard fills step by step (cut, footing, backfill lifts in alternating bands, topsoil,
  seed) as the seven steps pass.
- Tools: rock clause explainer, utility trench planner, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: overcast or early-morning flat light, close and low: the edge of a fresh cut, bucket teeth in rocky clay,
  stakes and string lines, compactor on a lift. Dark, earthy, honest. Wooded Ozark lots, no city skylines.

### B — Know the yards, the loads and the price before we start   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Rod Reading Excavation · Fayetteville, AR · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: Northwest Arkansas is full of new houses on sloped, rocky lots, and buyers are engineers and nurses who
  want numbers. The site is a field book: measure your job, get cubic yards, truckloads and a sample range, then see
  the three checks we run on every job (locate, laser grade, compaction) as readings, not adjectives.
- Tone lead: light-led (surveyor's field-book paper, graphite, one laser red)
- Type lead: mono-accent (a calm grotesk for text, a monospace for every reading, yard count and range)
- Hero family: 7 tool-first (the dirt calculator is the hero; the promise sits beside it, and a laser-red level line
  runs across the whole hero at the reading the tool returns)
- Signature seed: **the rod reading.** A thin laser-red line crosses the page like a laser level's beam; every section
  header is a reading taken off a grade rod at that line ("4.62 ft · driveway crown", "5.10 ft · pad"), and the page
  footer closes the loop: "benchmark 0.00 · call to check".
- Motion idea: intro: the laser line sweeps across the hero once and the calculator's numbers tick to their default.
  Scroll story: a grade rod drawn on the right edge slides up and down to each section's reading as you scroll.
- Tools: dirt calculator (hero), gravel driveway estimator, drainage picker, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright mid-morning, clean and precise, medium distance: a finished gravel drive with a crown, a laser
  level on a tripod, a tape across a trench, a compacted pad. Crisp shadows, light gravel tones, green hills behind.

### C — Red clay holds the water until somebody gives it a way out   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Swale & Culvert Excavating · Macon, GA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Middle Georgia's red clay sheds summer thunderstorms straight into brick ranch basements, low
  backyards and dirt drives. The site is about the water's route across a Macon-area lot: from the roofline to the
  clay, along a swale, under the drive through a culvert and out to daylight, told through the towns and the storms.
- Tone lead: color-led (Georgia red clay ground with pine-green and creek-water accents)
- Type lead: serif (a warm, sturdy book serif with italics for the water's voice, small caps for town names)
- Hero family: 9 horizontal filmstrip (a slow strip of frames under the headline: wet red clay, a swale with fresh
  straw, a culvert mouth, a pine-edged yard, a dry finished slope)
- Signature seed: **the soil color chip.** Every section is keyed to a soil color card chip (like a soil scientist's
  color book): "Red clay · wet", "Red clay · dry", "Sandy loam · Byron", "Creek bottom gray"; the chip sets that
  section's background, and the ZIP checker answers with the soil chip of that area.
- Motion idea: intro: the filmstrip drifts in from the right as a thin water line draws itself under the headline.
  Scroll story: a single blue water line runs down the page from roof to daylight outlet, turning at each section like
  water following a swale.
- Tools: drainage picker, season planner (storm months), machine access check, ZIP checker, multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: humid late afternoon after rain, warm low sun on red soil, wet sheen, pine trees and brick ranch homes,
  close-to-medium distance. Saturated earth tones, never dusty-desert.
