# HVAC — niche brief

## 1. The buyer
- **Who calls:** homeowners 30-75 in hot cities (Las Vegas, Houston, Phoenix), in tract homes from the 1980s-2000s with a split system in the garage or attic, or a rooftop package unit on a flat-roofed block house. Many are retirees home all day in the heat, young families with a baby, and landlords with a tenant texting them at 9 p.m. A smaller group plans ahead: a 15-year-old system and a summer bill that doubled.
- **What just happened:** the house is 86°F at 4 p.m. and climbing; the outdoor unit is humming but the fan is not spinning; water is dripping from the attic or the closet; the air smells musty; the thermostat is blank; or the last tech quoted a whole new system and they want a second opinion.
- **Fears (their words):**
  1. "They'll tell me the whole system is dead when it's a $20 part."
  2. "It's 112 out and nobody can come until Thursday."
  3. "They'll sell me a giant unit I don't need and my bill won't change."
  4. "Some guy is going to be in my attic and break something else."
  5. "I don't understand any of the numbers: SEER, tons, refrigerant."
- **What makes them trust:** a diagnostic visit with a clear flat price range before anyone shows up, a tech who explains the reading and shows the burnt part, repair offered before replacement, a load calculation instead of "same size as the old one", texts with arrival time and the tech's first name, booties and a drop cloth.
- **What makes them leave in 5 seconds:** smiling-family-on-a-couch stock photo with a thermostat, flashing coupons and "$49 tune-up!!!" spinners, chatbots that pop up before the page loads, no visible phone number on mobile, brand logos everywhere and no explanation, "comfort solutions" copy that says nothing.

## 2. The job
- **Emergency vs planned:** about 55/45 in the desert and Gulf. Emergencies: no cool (capacitor, contactor, frozen coil, refrigerant leak, clogged drain shutting off the unit), no heat on the rare freeze nights, water leaking from the air handler. Planned: replacement of 12-20 year old systems, heat pump upgrades, ductless rooms, duct sealing, tune-ups before summer, whole-home dehumidifiers on the Gulf.
- **Seasonality (hot-climate US):**
  - Jan-Feb: heat calls on cold snaps (Houston's freezes, desert nights near 30°F), tune-ups, replacements scheduled while crews have room.
  - Mar-Apr: pre-season tune-ups; the first 95°F day brings the first wave of "it's blowing warm".
  - May-Jun: volume climbs fast; capacitors and contactors fail in the first long heat; Phoenix and Las Vegas pass 105°F.
  - Jul-Aug: peak. Desert days of 110-120°F (Las Vegas hit 120°F on 7 July 2024; Phoenix had 31 straight days at or above 110°F in July 2023); Gulf humidity and storms (Hurricane Beryl knocked out Houston power for days in July 2024); monsoon dust and power blips in Phoenix (season runs 15 June-30 September).
  - Sep-Oct: still hot in the desert; replacement decisions after a rough summer; Gulf storm season tails off.
  - Nov-Dec: heat-pump heat checks, duct work, quieter weeks.
- **Average tickets (words):** small (diagnostic, capacitor, drain line clear, thermostat), medium (blower motor, coil cleaning, refrigerant leak search and repair, ductless head), large (full split system or heat pump replacement, rooftop package unit), very large (heat pump with new ducts, multi-zone ductless, whole-home dehumidifier added to a new system).
- **Refrigerant note:** new systems from 2025 use A2L refrigerants (R-454B, R-32) as R-410A is phased down; older R-22 systems are costly to recharge. Explain in plain words, never as scare copy.
- **Services that sell (from facts):** AC repair, AC replacement, heat pump install, furnace/heat repair, tune-ups, ductless mini-splits, duct cleaning and sealing, thermostat install, drain line clearing, dehumidifiers, rooftop package units, indoor air (filters and UV).
- **Homeowner words vs pro words:**
  | Homeowner says | Pro says |
  |---|---|
  | "it's blowing warm air" | low charge / failed capacitor / compressor not starting |
  | "the outside unit hums but the fan doesn't spin" | failed run capacitor / condenser fan motor |
  | "ice on the pipes" | frozen evaporator coil (low airflow or low charge) |
  | "water dripping from the closet" | clogged condensate drain / float switch tripped |
  | "the freon" | refrigerant (R-410A, R-454B) |
  | "how big is my AC" | tonnage (1 ton = 12,000 BTU/h) |
  | "the efficiency number" | SEER2 / HSPF2 |
  | "the inside unit" | air handler / furnace and evaporator coil |
  | "the box on the roof" | package unit |
  | "the vents" | supply registers and return grilles |
  | "it's sticky inside" | high indoor humidity / latent load |
  | "the ducts in the attic" | flex duct runs / plenum |
  | "a check-up" | maintenance tune-up / PM visit |
  | "the AC keeps turning on and off" | short-cycling |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)
1. **"What's it doing?" symptom sorter** — learns the likely cause and whether it is safe to wait. Inputs: pick a symptom (blowing warm, fan not spinning, ice on lines, water inside, short-cycling, noise type: buzz/click/rattle/squeal). Output: 2-3 likely causes in plain words, what the homeowner can check (filter, breaker, drain float, thermostat batteries), and "call now / book soon / watch it". Leads to: booking with the symptom attached.
2. **Filter reminder** — inputs: filter size, pets yes/no, dusty season. Output: change interval and a downloadable .ics calendar reminder created in the browser. Leads to: "add a tune-up to the reminder".
3. **Repair or replace sorter** — inputs: system age, repair quote band, refrigerant type (R-22 / R-410A / don't know), number of repairs in 2 years. Output: "repair makes sense / on the edge / plan replacement" with the reason (the old 5,000 rule explained honestly as a rule of thumb). Leads to: second-opinion booking.
4. **Dew point and comfort calculator** — inputs: indoor temperature and humidity from a cheap hygrometer. Output: dew point and where the room sits against a comfort band (dew point roughly 50-60°F feels comfortable); explains why a cold-but-clammy house needs humidity control, not a lower setting. Pure math, no live data. Leads to: dehumidifier or tune-up booking.
5. **Size sanity check** — inputs: square feet, ceiling height, sun exposure, insulation era. Output: a broad tonnage band and a clear note that only a Manual J load calculation sizes a system. Leads to: "book a load calculation with your replacement quote".
6. **Hot-day plan** — what to do while waiting for the tech (close blinds, check breaker, don't run the AC frozen, fan only to thaw, water for pets). Printable. Leads to: booking.
7. **Monsoon and storm checklist** — after dust storms and power blips: rinse the condenser, reset the breaker correctly, check the drain, what a surge protector does. Leads to: tune-up.
8. **ZIP checker** — confirms service area against `facts.biz.zips`.

All prices are ranges from `facts.proof.prices`, always labelled "Sample pricing". No tool shows live wait times, live tech locations or live weather.

## 6. The three directions

### A — "A good visit is a timeline you can read: arrival, reading, cause, fix, proof."   (axis: PROCESS)
- Brand: Saltbush Heating & Air · Las Vegas, NV · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: When the AC dies at 115°F, people want to know exactly what will happen next. Saltbush runs every call the same way and logs it like a ticket: on the way, arrived, readings taken, cause found, options priced, fixed, readings again. The page is one service ticket, read top to bottom.
- Tone lead: dark-led (night-desert charcoal with a sage-green saltbush accent and one hot sodium-orange for readings)
- Type lead: grotesk (a tight neo-grotesk for everything; tabular figures for times and readings)
- Hero family: 12 sticky photo + scrolling text (one tall photo of a condenser at dusk stays pinned; the ticket lines scroll past it)
- Signature seed: **The service ticket.** A vertical clock rail runs down the page with timestamps (2:14 PM On the way · 2:41 Arrived · 2:52 Reading · 3:05 Cause...). Each section is one ticket entry; the pinned photo crops and zooms to the part the entry is about (the fan, the capacitor, the coil, the drain). Readings are shown as small mono-style chips labelled "sample reading".
- Motion idea: Intro: the ticket header prints in line by line (job no., address withheld, outside temp "sample") while the pinned photo fades up from black. Scroll story: the clock rail fills as you scroll and the pinned photo pans and zooms to each part named in the current entry.
- Tools: "What's it doing?" symptom sorter, Hot-day plan, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form (symptom → system type → best time → contact).
- Photo mood: desert dusk and night, sodium and blue-hour light, block walls and gravel yards, close details of condenser fins, a capacitor in a gloved hand, gauges on lines; no faces.

### B — "Sticky house, cold air? Check the dew point before you buy anything."   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS)
- Brand: Wetbulb Heating & Cooling · Houston, TX · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: In Houston the problem is often water in the air, not heat. Wetbulb leads with a dew point calculator and shows every recommendation as a reading on a comfort chart, so the homeowner sees why a bigger unit can make a house clammier. Every claim on the page is a number the visitor can check.
- Tone lead: light-led (paper white with humid-teal lines, a graphite text color and one coral for "outside the band")
- Type lead: slab (a friendly slab serif for headings, like a field notebook; plain sans for body)
- Hero family: 7 tool-first hero (the dew point calculator is the hero: two inputs, a live dot on a small psychrometric chart, the headline answering the result)
- Signature seed: **The comfort chart.** A simplified psychrometric chart is the page's grid: each section plots one situation as a dot (a sticky bedroom, a short-cycling oversized unit, after a tune-up, with a dehumidifier) and moves it into or out of the shaded comfort band. Services and prices sit beside the dot they fix.
- Motion idea: Intro: the chart's curves draw in from the bottom-left, then the hero dot drops into the visitor's default reading and settles. Scroll story: one dot travels across a sticky mini-chart in the margin, section by section, ending inside the band at the booking form.
- Tools: Dew point and comfort calculator (hero), Size sanity check, Repair or replace sorter, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: humid Gulf light, soft overcast, condensation on a glass and on a window, damp attic insulation, clean close-ups of a hygrometer, a coil being cleaned, a drain pan; bright and documentary.

### C — "Phoenix lives on the shade line; so do we."   (axis: LOCAL STORY)
- Brand: Shadeline Heating & Cooling · Phoenix, AZ · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: In Phoenix every life choice follows the shade: where you park, when you walk the dog, when a tech can safely work on a roof. Shadeline tells the story of a Phoenix summer day from 5 a.m. to midnight and what each hour does to a house and its AC, with the monsoon and the 2023 heat wave as chapters.
- Tone lead: color-led (a saturated adobe terracotta field with a deep shade-indigo and sunlit cream)
- Type lead: condensed (very tall condensed display that holds the temperature numerals; small grotesk for body)
- Hero family: 3 giant type mask (a huge "115°" in condensed type, with a sunlit rooftop package unit photo showing through the numerals)
- Signature seed: **The shade line.** Every section is split by a diagonal sun/shade line: the sunlit side states the problem at that hour, the shaded side gives the fix. As you scroll, the line moves across the section like the sun crossing the yard (5 AM rooftop work, 2 PM peak, 6 PM monsoon, 11 PM still 95°).
- Motion idea: Intro: the 115° mask fills with the photo from bottom to top like a thermometer rising, then the shade line sweeps across the hero. Scroll story: the shade line angle and position track a clock in the corner from dawn to midnight.
- Tools: Monsoon and storm checklist, Filter reminder (dust season), Repair or replace sorter, ZIP checker, multi-step booking form.
- Photo mood: hard white midday sun and long dawn shadows, flat roofs with package units, block walls, palo verde and saguaro silhouettes, monsoon dust walls and lightning at dusk; saturated, high-contrast, no faces.
