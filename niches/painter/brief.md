# Painter — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who calls.** Homeowners 30-72. Four main types: (1) owners of older houses (pre-1978 rowhouses, foursquares and Victorians in Richmond's Fan and Church Hill, Charleston's peninsula) whose exterior paint is peeling and who worry about lead; (2) suburban families in Raleigh-Cary-Apex builder homes ready to replace flat builder beige, one room or the whole inside at a time; (3) people getting a house ready to sell or just moved in; (4) owners who want kitchen cabinets refreshed instead of replaced.

**What just happened.**
- Paint on the south side or the porch is flaking, and bare wood is showing at the sills and the bottom of the siding.
- They bought a house and every wall is the same flat beige with scuffs, and the move-in date is in three weeks.
- A listing agent told them to paint before photos.
- They priced new cabinets and nearly fell over.
- A storm or the humid summer left mildew on the north wall and the porch ceiling.

**What they fear (in their words).**
- "They'll paint right over the peeling stuff and it'll be flaking again in two years."
- "Paint on my floors, my hardwood, my outlets, my shrubs."
- "It's an old house, is the old paint lead, and will they make a mess of it in front of my kids?"
- "They'll stretch one coat and call it two."
- "The quote will double once they start and 'find' rot."

**What makes them trust.**
- A written scope that names every step: wash, scrape, sand, prime bare wood, caulk, two coats, and the product and sheen.
- Clear lead-safe practice talk for older homes (plastic, containment, cleanup) without scare language.
- Knowing what happens to the furniture, the floors and the shrubs, and when they can use the room again.
- Close-up photos of cut lines, sanded trim and primed wood, not glamour shots.
- A price range by room or by house size before anyone visits.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** A smiling painter in white overalls holding a roller at the camera; a rainbow of paint swatches as the hero with no idea what the company actually does; "Quality you can trust" and "No job too big or small"; no word about prep; a form that wants their budget first; photos of commercial office walls.

## 2. The job

**Emergency vs planned.** Almost 100% planned. Urgency comes from deadlines (a listing date, a move-in, a party, an HOA notice), not emergencies. No emergency line.

**Seasonality by month (Mid-Atlantic and Carolinas).**
| Month | Richmond (old housing stock) | Raleigh (suburban growth) | Charleston (coastal, salt and humidity) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Interiors, cabinets, planning spring exteriors | Interiors, cabinets, whole-home repaints in vacant homes | Interiors; exterior on dry mild days (Charleston winters allow it) |
| Mar-Apr | Exterior season opens once nights stay above about 50F; booking rush | Exteriors open, deck staining starts, pollen season (wash first) | Prime exterior season, porch ceilings, shutters |
| May-Jun | Exteriors, porches, trim; pre-listing repaints | Exteriors, decks, pre-listing paints | Exteriors early mornings; humidity watch |
| Jul-Aug | Exteriors (heat: work the shade side), interiors in heat waves | Interiors grow, exteriors early, storms delay | Afternoon storms and heat: interiors and shady sides; hurricane season |
| Sep-Oct | Second exterior window, the steadiest weather | Best exterior months, deck stain | Exteriors resume, mildew wash before painting |
| Nov-Dec | Interiors for the holidays, last mild exterior days | Interiors before holidays, cabinets | Interiors and some exteriors on dry days |

**Average ticket bands.**
- Small: one room, a door and trim, porch ceiling, pressure wash (hundreds to low thousands).
- Medium: several rooms, a whole interior, cabinets, a deck stain (low to high thousands).
- Large: full exterior of an older or historic house with carpentry repair (high thousands to tens of thousands).

**Services that sell (from facts).** Interior painting, exterior painting, cabinet refinishing, deck and fence staining, drywall repair before paint, color help, pressure washing, wallpaper removal, porch and trim painting, wood repair before paint, historic exterior repaints.

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "the paint is peeling off the wood" | adhesion failure, moisture behind the film |
| "the edges where the wall meets the ceiling" | cut-in line |
| "the shiny paint" / "the flat paint" | sheen: gloss, semi-gloss, satin, eggshell, flat/matte |
| "the old paint might be lead" | pre-1978 coating, EPA RRP lead-safe work practices |
| "black spots on the porch ceiling" | mildew, needs a wash and a mildewcide primer |
| "filling the cracks" | caulking joints, spackling, patching |
| "the boards are soft at the bottom" | wood rot, Dutchman repair or epoxy consolidant |
| "the white coat first" | primer, spot-prime bare wood, stain-blocking primer |
| "paint the cabinets" | degrease, scuff-sand, bonding primer, sprayed enamel |
| "blue porch ceiling" | haint blue |
| "it looks streaky" | lap marks, flashing, uneven film build |
| "how many cans" | spread rate, coverage per gallon |

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **Paint calculator.** Inputs: room length, width, wall height, doors and windows count, coats. Output: wall area and gallons needed (about 350-400 sq ft per gallon, shown as the method), primer gallons if going dark-to-light. Leads to "or let us price the whole room" with the sample range.
2. **Sheen picker.** Inputs: room type (bath, kitchen, bedroom, hall, trim, ceiling), kids/pets yes/no. Output: the usual sheen and why (wipeability vs hiding flaws), in plain words.
3. **Room quote builder.** Inputs: rooms by size (S/M/L), ceilings yes/no, trim and doors yes/no, color change dark-to-light. Output: a sample range built from facts.proof.prices only, with each line shown.
4. **Coat schedule / dry-time planner.** Inputs: interior or exterior, start day. Output: a day-by-day plan (wash, dry, scrape and sand, prime, coat 1, recoat time, coat 2) with typical dry and recoat times labelled typical, and when the room can be used again.
5. **Old-house paint check.** Inputs: year built (before or after 1978), peeling yes/no, kids under 6 at home. Output: what lead-safe prep looks like (plastic, containment, wet scraping, HEPA cleanup) and that testing is done before work; plain, not alarming, no claim of any credential.
6. **Exterior weather window.** Inputs: month. Output: typical temperature and humidity notes for the city and why painters watch dew and night lows; uses a fixed table in the page, labelled typical.
7. **Historic color explorer.** Inputs: house style (Charleston single house, Victorian, Craftsman). Output: 4-6 color families typical of that style, shown as swatches with names written by us (no paint brand codes).
8. **ZIP checker** + **multi-step quote form** (every site).

## 6. The three directions

### A — Paint fails in the prep, so we show the prep   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Cutline Painting Co. · Richmond, VA · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: Richmond's old houses (Fan rowhouses, Church Hill porches, foursquares) peel because someone painted over the last failure. Cutline sells the boring part: wash, scrape, sand, prime bare wood, caulk, then two real coats. The page is the coat schedule a crew follows, so the homeowner sees what two coats over proper prep actually means and how long each step needs to dry.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (fresh primer white and warm plaster off-white as the field, ink in deep navy, one crisp accent of brick red for cut lines and stamps).
- Type lead: **grotesk** (a precise neo-grotesk at large sizes, tight tracking, like painter's tape lettering; tabular figures for times).
- Hero family: **12 — sticky photo, scrolling text** (one photo of a freshly cut trim line stays fixed while three short statements scroll past: "Wash. Scrape. Prime.", "Then two coats.", "Then we leave.").
- Signature seed: **the page is a coat schedule.** Every section is one coat in order (00 Wash, 01 Scrape and sand, 02 Spot-prime, 03 Caulk, 04 Coat one, 05 Coat two, 06 Walk-through), each with a stamped dry-time ("dry to touch 1 h, recoat 4 h, labelled typical"), the tools and products used, and a painted stripe that thickens one film-layer per section. Services, reviews and prices are filed under the coat they belong to. Older-house sections explain lead-safe practice for pre-1978 homes in plain words (no claim of a credential).
- Motion idea: intro: a piece of painter's tape pulls away across the headline to reveal a razor-straight cut line. Scroll story: the page's edge stripe builds up coat by coat as you scroll, each section adding one translucent layer until the last section shows full, even color; dry-time stamps tick from "wet" to "dry".
- Tools: coat schedule / dry-time planner, old-house paint check, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: clean daylight, close and exact: scraped clapboard, sanded sills, primer on bare wood, a brush cutting a line, tape and drop cloths; old Richmond porches and brick rowhouses; calm, no glamour.

- Photo mood: bright, even interior daylight; fresh rooms in modern suburban homes, cabinets sprayed smooth, deck boards being stained; color is the subject, with clean walls and crisp edges.
