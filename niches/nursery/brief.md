# Plant Nursery — niche brief

## 1. The buyer

**Who comes.** Mostly homeowners 30-70. Three types make most of the trips:
- The new-house owner (30-45) facing a bare builder yard, a dead strip of sod and an HOA letter. Wants to "make it look like somebody lives here" without killing a few hundred dollars of plants.
- The settled gardener (50-70), often retired, who knows what a perennial is and drives past the box store to a place where someone can name the bug on the leaf. Shops weekly in spring.
- The one-job buyer: a tree to replace the one that came down, a hedge for privacy from the new neighbor, starts for a first vegetable bed, a houseplant that will not die in a north window.

**What just happened.** Spring warm-up or the first cool fall weekend; a tree fell or died; they moved in; a neighbor's yard looks good and theirs does not; the box-store plants they bought last year are dead; the kids want tomatoes; the water bill or a drought rule made them rethink the lawn.

**What they are afraid of (their words).**
- "I'll spend $300 on plants and half of them will be dead by August."
- "I don't know what grows here. The tag says full sun, but our sun is not their sun."
- "That tree is going to get huge and lift the driveway in ten years."
- "Is it in stock, or am I driving out there for nothing?"
- "I'll look stupid asking a basic question."

**What makes them trust.**
- Plants grown or hardened off here, in this weather, not trucked in last night.
- Someone who answers the actual question: "for that corner, by the fence, with the sprinkler that hits it."
- Clear sizes and prices (4-inch, 1-gallon, 5-gallon, 15-gallon) before the drive.
- A plain replacement promise for plants that fail in the first season.
- Photos of real benches and real roots, not a glossy bouquet.

**What makes them leave in 5 seconds.** No hours on the page; no idea whether it is retail or wholesale; a stock photo of a smiling woman holding a pot; "contact us for pricing"; a site that looks like a wedding florist; nothing about what grows in *their* town.

## 2. The job

**Planned vs urgent.** Almost all planned and seasonal. The "urgent" cases: a tree down after a storm, an HOA deadline, a dead hedge before a sale, a planting weekend that has to happen this Saturday. No emergency line; long Saturday hours matter more.

**Seasonality by region.**
- *Sonoma County (Santa Rosa):* Mediterranean. Rain November-March, dry May-October. Fall (Oct-Nov) is the real planting season for natives and shrubs; spring (Mar-May) is the busy retail rush for perennials and vegetable starts; bare-root roses and fruit trees January-February; summer is watering-and-deer questions. Clay soils, deer pressure, wildfire defensible-space questions since the 2017 fires.
- *Polk County (Lakeland):* humid subtropical, sandy soil, citrus country. Two planting windows: October-March (dry and cool, the best months) and the summer rainy season (June-September) when new plantings water themselves but heat and bugs are hard. Rare frost nights in January. Hurricane season June-November: trees that stand up to wind, palms, hedges after storm loss. Lawn is St. Augustine or Bahia; sand drains instantly.
- *Stanislaus County (Modesto):* Central Valley. Tule fog December-February (bare-root fruit trees, roses), almond bloom in February, spring rush March-May, 100°F+ in July-August (shade trees and survival watering), fall planting September-November. Ranch houses from the 1950s-70s with old mulberries and sycamores, alkaline soil, irrigation-district water, big backyard vegetable gardens.

**Ticket bands.** Small: a flat of annuals, a few 4-inch perennials, a bag of compost. Medium: a bed's worth of 1- and 5-gallon shrubs, a fruit tree, a patio pot planted up. Large: 15-gallon and box trees with delivery and planting, a full front-yard planting list, a privacy hedge run.

**Services that sell (from facts).** Perennials · annuals and hanging baskets · shrubs · trees (shade, fruit, privacy) · vegetable and herb starts · native and water-wise plants · houseplants · succulents and cactus · soil, mulch and compost · pots and planters · delivery and planting · garden advice (bring a photo or a leaf).

**Homeowner words vs pro words.**
| Homeowner says | Pro says |
|---|---|
| "the plant got yellow leaves" | chlorosis, often iron in alkaline soil or overwatering |
| "it just wilted and died" | transplant shock / root rot / root-bound at planting |
| "how big does it get" | mature height and spread |
| "plants that don't need water" | drought-tolerant once established, low-water |
| "plants from around here" | California natives / Florida-friendly / regionally adapted |
| "the small pot / the big pot" | 4-inch, 1-gallon, 5-gallon, 15-gallon, 24-inch box |
| "a tree with the dirt still on" | balled and burlapped, container-grown |
| "the dirt" | soil, potting mix, amendment, compost |
| "deer keep eating everything" | deer-resistant palette |
| "will it survive winter" | hardiness, frost-tender, USDA zone, chill hours (fruit) |
| "it flowered once and quit" | deadheading, bloom cycle, sun hours |
| "bugs on the leaves" | aphids, scale, whitefly, chilli thrips |
| "a tree that won't wreck the pipes" | non-invasive root system, right tree right place |
| "getting the plant used to outside" | hardening off |

## 4. What every competitor site does (so we do not)

1. A hero carousel of generic flowers or a bouquet that could be a florist or a wedding venue.
2. "Welcome to our garden center, family-owned since…" paragraphs and a wall of vendor brand logos (soil, fertilizer, pottery brands).
3. "Contact us for current availability" with no list, no sizes, no prices.
4. A Facebook feed as the main content, out of date by three months.
5. Event-calendar clutter (workshops, Santa photos) above the basic facts.
6. Generic plant tips copied from national sites ("zones 5-9") with nothing about the local soil or season.
7. Hours hidden in the footer, no map logic, no idea whether delivery covers your town.

## 5. Niche tools (pick from, each site uses 2+)

1. **"Will it live in my yard?" check.** Inputs: ZIP, sun hours (2/4/6/8+), soil (clay / sand / loam / don't know), watering (hose / drip / none), deer or not. Output: a short list of plant groups that suit it (from facts.services), with a sample size and price range per group. Leads to: "Bring a photo or text one, we'll fill in the names" → call/text.
2. **Planting calendar for this town.** Inputs: what you want to plant (tree, perennial, vegetable, native). Output: the month window for this region and what is on the benches now. Leads to: "It's the window now: price a delivery".
3. **Pot size explainer / size-to-price.** Inputs: a slider from 4-inch to 15-gallon. Output: what the plant looks like at that size, how long to fill in, sample price range from facts. Leads to the quote form ("how many and which sizes").
4. **Delivery and planting estimator.** Inputs: number of plants by size, ZIP, plant or drop-off. Output: a sample range from facts.proof.prices. Leads to the multi-step form.
5. **Mature-size check.** Inputs: the spot (by a wall, under a wire, near the drive), distance. Output: which tree/shrub heights fit. Leads to "Ask which ones we have in that size".
6. **Bring-a-leaf / photo help.** A text line: "Send a photo of the sick plant". Output: what we'll look at (leaf, roots, soil), the hours someone is on the bench. Leads to sms:.
7. **Hardening-off / weeks-to-ready tracker.** Shows how many weeks a plant has spent outside on the benches before sale. Output: a timeline. Leads to "Reserve a flat".
8. **Hedge counter.** Inputs: fence length, plant type. Output: how many plants at what spacing, sample range. Leads to the form.

Prices are always ranges from facts.proof.prices, labelled "Sample pricing".

## 6. The three directions

### A — Nothing leaves the bench until it has lived outside here   (axis: PROCESS — how the work is done, shown step by step)
- Brand: Hardening Bench Nursery · Santa Rosa, CA · facts-A.json
- Concept seed: The reason box-store plants die in Sonoma County is that they never met its weather: cold wet winter nights, dry 95°F afternoons, clay. Hardening Bench shows the process a plant goes through before you can buy it: cutting or seed, potted up, moved out of the hoop house, weeks on the open bench in real sun and wind, checked for roots, then tagged ready. The page follows one flat of plants down the bench, week by week.
- Tone lead: **dark-led** (the inside of a propagation house before dawn: deep soil-black and wet-slate as the base, misted glass gray, one warm accent the color of a grow lamp, small notes in chalky white).
- Type lead: **mono-accent** (a monospace for week numbers, bench rows, sow and pot-up dates, sizes and sample prices; a calm humanist sans for headlines and body).
- Hero family: **9 — horizontal filmstrip** (under the headline, a slow strip of frames moves left: seed tray, rooted cutting, 4-inch pot, gallon pot on the open bench, root check, a tagged plant ready. Pauses on hover/focus, static with reduced motion).
- Signature seed: **the bench card.** Every section is a row on a numbered growing bench with its own bench card: "Row 3 · Week 6 · Hardening off · 41°F last night". Services are bench rows (perennials Row 1, natives Row 2...), each with a week count from potting to ready; reviews are clipped to the end of the row like grower notes; the price list is the ready-to-sell rack. A running week counter in the margin advances as you scroll, from Week 0 to "Ready".
- Motion idea: intro: the filmstrip fades up frame by frame from the dark, like grow lights clicking on along a row. Scroll story: a thin bench rail runs down the left margin; the week counter ticks forward at each section and the plant icon at its head grows from seedling to gallon pot.
- Tools: weeks-to-ready tracker (the bench timeline), planting calendar for Santa Rosa (fall native window, bare-root window), + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: low-key and close: dawn or overcast light in a greenhouse or under shade cloth, mist and condensation, seed trays, roots, hands potting up. Dark backgrounds, one warm light source, shallow focus. No sunny retail displays.

### B — Know before you drive   (axis: PROOF & TOOLS — numbers, checks, answers before the call)
- Brand: Sun Hours Nursery · Lakeland, FL · facts-B.json
- Concept seed: In Polk County the tag lies: "full sun" in Ohio is not full sun on Lakeland sand in July. Sun Hours lets the homeowner answer the real questions before getting in the car: how many hours of sun the spot gets, sand or not, sprinkler or not, which plant groups fit, in which pot size, for roughly what price, and whether they can deliver and plant it. The site is a check, then a list, then a call.
- Tone lead: **light-led** (bright Florida midday: sun-bleached sand white, clean paper, deep palmetto ink for text, citrus-leaf and hot-sun yellow only as data highlights).
- Type lead: **grotesk** (a wide, sturdy grotesk for headlines and big numbers like "6 HRS"; the same family for body; tabular figures for sizes and prices).
- Hero family: **7 — tool-first** (the "Will it live in my yard?" check is the hero: four big tap choices for sun hours, soil, watering and wind, with a live shortlist; with JS off it reads as a plain list of plant groups by sun hours plus the call button).
- Signature seed: **the sun-hours dial.** One drawn dial (a sun path over a house roofline) appears across the site: in the hero it sets the input; each service shows the sun hours it needs on a mini dial; the price table rows show pot sizes as rings on the same dial; the hours block shows the nursery's open hours as a sun arc ("open now" lit). No other site in the library uses a dial of daylight.
- Motion idea: intro: the sun traces its arc across the dial and stops at the default 6 hours while the shortlist counts in. Scroll story: as you scroll, the dial in the corner moves from morning to evening, and sections pick up a sharper or softer shadow to match the time of day.
- Tools: "Will it live in my yard?" check (hero), size-to-price slider, hedge counter, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: bright high sun, hard short shadows, crisp color: plants on outdoor benches under open sky, palms and citrus leaves against blue, sand-colored ground. Clear and airy, never dark or moody.

### C — The valley's garden year   (axis: LOCAL STORY — the place, the weather, the streets, the people)
- Brand: Fog Season Nursery · Modesto, CA · facts-C.json
- Concept seed: Modesto gardening runs on a calendar nobody outside the valley knows: tule fog and bare-root fruit trees in January, almond bloom in February, the spring rush, 104°F in July, the second planting season in fall. Fog Season tells that year as the story of the town's yards: the old ranch-house mulberries, the backyard tomato rows, the fruit trees every grandparent planted. Each section is a season of the valley, and what the nursery sells and advises in it.
- Tone lead: **color-led** (warm orchard colors as the main fields: almond-blossom pink, dry-summer straw gold, fog gray-blue, with deep orchard-bark brown for text; color carries each season).
- Type lead: **serif** (an old-style serif with warm italics for plant varieties and month names, like a seed catalog or a fruit-crate label; a plain sans for small labels and forms).
- Hero family: **8 — photo mosaic** (four seasonal photos in a tight grid, one large: fog over bare orchard rows big, with blossom, summer shade and a harvest basket smaller; the headline sits on warm paper beside it, one color grade across all four).
- Signature seed: **the valley almanac ribbon.** A horizontal ribbon of the twelve months runs across the top of each section, with the current month marked and the section's season shaded (Fog / Bloom / Rush / Heat / Second spring). Services, tips and reviews each carry their month on the ribbon; the fruit-tree section shows chill-hour notes; the footer is the year closing back into fog.
- Motion idea: intro: a soft fog layer lifts off the mosaic (opacity and blur easing out) to reveal the four seasons. Scroll story: the almanac ribbon's marker travels month by month as you scroll and each section's color field cross-fades to its season.
- Tools: planting calendar for Modesto (hero-adjacent, "what to plant this month"), mature-size check for shade trees near ranch-house foundations, + ZIP checker + multi-step quote form.
- Photo mood: warm, hazy, nostalgic but honest: golden late light, valley haze or fog, orchard rows, blossom close-ups, backyard vegetable beds, old shade trees over modest ranch houses. Film-like warmth, never glossy.
