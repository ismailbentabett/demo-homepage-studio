▲ EL. 6,100 FT The ground under your slab

Three things in this dirt want your driveway gone. We plan for all three.

  1. Reading 01 · Clay

    It swells when wet

    Expansive clay lifts and drops a slab with every wet spring. We dig past it and lay a thicker, compacted base so it has less to push.

  2. Reading 02 · Frost

    It freezes, thaws, refreezes

    Sunny afternoons and frozen nights put the slab through the cycle again and again. Air bubbles in the mix give the ice room to grow.

  3. Reading 03 · Salt

    It rides in on your tires

    Magnesium chloride from the roads drips off every car all winter. A fall seal keeps it out of the surface.

▲ EL. 6,200 FT One January night

Why a driveway here flakes after one winter

Concrete that was fine in a warm state peels here. It happens in four steps, and the fix is built in on pour day.

  1. 01 · Melt

    Deicer melts the ice and pulls salty water into the top quarter inch of the slab.

  2. 02 · Freeze

    The night drops well below freezing. Water in the pores turns to ice and grows about nine percent.

  3. 03 · Scale

    With nowhere to go, the ice pops the top layer off in flakes and the stone shows through.

  4. 04 · Built in

    Air-entrained mix leaves millions of tiny voids for the ice. A fall seal keeps the salt water out.
