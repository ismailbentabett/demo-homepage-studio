# Strategy — junkremoval-A

## 1. The one job of this page
A Daytona Beach homeowner with a garage, estate or storm pile lands here and calls or texts within 10 seconds because they can see the price logic, trust that their keep-pile is safe, and believe the floor will be swept when the crew leaves.

## 2. The homeowner's first 5 seconds
- Trade + town: "Junk Removal · Daytona Beach" in the header and kicker.
- Way to act: phone number in the sticky mobile bar and a "Get a number" button above the fold.
- Reason to believe: the h1 promises "You point. We carry. The floor comes back." over a bright Florida garage photo with a diagonal ramp seam.
- Price logic visible immediately: a sample range chip ("Quarter truck · from {{proof.prices.quarter.from}}") sits under the lead.

## 3. The contractor's first 5 seconds
The clearing floor-plan signature: a top-down garage outline in the margin whose item outlines lift off section by section, revealing clean concrete. No competitor in Volusia County shows the *process* as a visual removal; they show trucks and logos. This is the thing the contractor points at on the sales call.

## 4. Objections and where each is answered
| Fear | Section |
|---|---|
| Price changes once it's on the truck | `#pricing` (sample ranges by truck share, labelled Sample) + Load Sizer tool |
| They'll dump everything, even the good stuff | `#where-it-goes` (donate / recycle / landfill sorter) + `#reviews` (donation proof) |
| Strangers scraping walls and floors | `#process` (the clearing floor plan, step-by-step carry-out) + guarantee line |
| They'll take what I meant to keep | `#keep-pile` (Keep-Pile Planner tool + blue-tape rule) |
| They won't show up when promised | `#hours` (open-now badge + arrival window note) + reviews mentioning timing |
| Storm debris is too wet / heavy for regular pickup | `#storm` (Storm Pile Check tool + dedicated service card) |
| Condo elevator / hallway damage | `#services` (Condo cleanout card with hallway promise) + review from beachside condo |

## 6. What this direction is NOT
- **Not B (tools-first):** B leads with the "Will you take it?" answer box as the hero and a three-column sort sheet as signature. A leads with the *physical process* and the floor coming back; tools support the story, they are not the hero.
- **Not C (local-story):** C uses garage-door panels and village names as its signature, dark eucalyptus palette, serif type. A uses the top-down floor plan, bright coastal teal, condensed grotesk. No village storytelling, no door-panel motif.
- **Not a franchise look:** No cartoon truck, no mascot, no lime-green/orange, no "We haul it all!" bubble font, no starburst phone. The palette is Intracoastal teal + sand paper + tailgate orange, the type is tall condensed + humanist sans, the hero is a real Florida garage with a ramp-angle seam.
