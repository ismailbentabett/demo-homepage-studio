March, the wind month

March wind lifts the tabs before hail ever falls.

Most March calls are a few lifted tabs or a ridge cap lying in the grass. Caught early, it's usually an afternoon's work.

Straight-line wind breaks the seal strip under a shingle. The tab flaps, the nail works loose, and the next front takes it. A lifted tab in March is a ceiling stain by May.

  • Tabs sitting up at the edges
  • Ridge cap pieces in the grass
  • A flapping sound in the attic on windy nights

July and August, the heat

Heat bakes what the hail started.

A July attic runs far hotter than the yard. Tired shingles curl and seal strips let go, so this is the season to choose what goes on next.

A Class 4 shingle dents where an ordinary one bruises. Many insurers in {{biz.stateName}} take something off the premium for it, so ask your agent.

September and October, build season

The calmest weeks of the year go to new roofs.

One tear-off, dawn to dark. Ask, and we'll show you the bare deck before anything covers it.

[[quote {{services.5.short}} btn-text | Get a quote for a new roof ]]

  1. Dawn

    Tear-off

    Old shingles and felt come off one slope at a time, with the dumpster backed up to the eave.

  2. Morning

    Deck check

    Every board walked and tapped; soft wood is cut out and replaced while you can still see it.

  3. Afternoon

    Underlayment & shingles

    Drip edge, ice-and-water at the valleys, then shingle courses from the eave up to the ridge.

  4. Before dark

    Ridge cap & magnet

    Ridge cap set last, then yard, drive and beds swept with a rolling magnet.

November to February, ice season

Ice brings the limbs down and finds every tired flashing.

An ice storm leaves two jobs: the limb on the roof and the leak it starts. We handle both, in that order.

Freeze and thaw open cracked pipe boots and old step flashing. When ice snaps a limb onto the roof, the limb comes off first; the deck is patched before a single shingle goes back.
