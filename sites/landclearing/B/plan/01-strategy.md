# Strategy — landclearing-B

## 1. The one job of this page
A Florida landowner with 1-40 acres gets a sample price per acre for their ground class before calling, so they trust the number enough to book a walk-through within 30 seconds.

## 2. The homeowner's first 5 seconds
Above the fold: four photo tiles of Florida ground classes (palmetto sand, oak hammock, pasture, pine) each labeled with its sample rate per acre in monospace; beside them, the headline "You know the number per acre before anyone drives out" on sun-bleached paper; the phone number and an open-now indicator; a ZIP checker input visible without scrolling.

## 3. The contractor's first 5 seconds
The density legend: hatch patterns that repeat as section backgrounds, chart keys, price rows and estimator map overlays. No other site in the niche uses a survey-sheet visual system tied to ground class.

## 4. Objections and where each is answered
- "They quote by the job then charge more when they find how thick it is" → hero mosaic shows rate per acre by density class; estimator tool breaks down sample ranges by ground type.
- "They'll bulldoze everything and I'll lose the big oaks" → keep/clear picker tool lets owner mark what stays; process section explains selective clearing.
- "Will it grow back next summer?" → regrowth calendar note in invasive removal service card; follow-up visit pricing shown.
- "Ruts and torn-up ground" → ground reader tool matches machine to soil/slope; caption notes tracked mulcher on soft sand.
- "Where does the debris go?" → debris answer explained in process section; mulch-in-place default stated.
- "Do they work in my town?" → ZIP checker in hero and area section; towns listed explicitly.

## 5. Conversion paths
Primary: "Price it" button → multi-step quote form (#contact) prefilled with selected service or estimator output. Secondary: tel: link always visible in header and mobile bar; SMS enabled. Tool hand-offs: estimator result includes "Send us your parcel to firm it up" → form prefilled with acreage and density; ground reader ends with call CTA.

## 6. What this direction is NOT
- Not A's dark forest + flagging ribbon process narrative (no orange tape, no step-by-step walk).
- Not C's red-clay Piedmont story with split-rail timeline (no diagonal seams, no seasonal rails).
- Not a bare-dirt moonscape gallery or equipment-brand showcase (clichés from brief §4).
