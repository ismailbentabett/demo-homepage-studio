L1 Roof deck

Icicles are heat leaving through the roof.

Warm attic air melts the snow on the upper roof. The water runs down to the cold eave, freezes, and builds a ridge of ice. The next melt backs up behind it and finds your ceiling. Bigger gutters don't change that. A sealed, evenly insulated attic does.

L2–L5 Attic to rim joist

Six steps, always in this order.

  1. 1 Look
  2. 2 Measure
  3. 3 Seal the leaks
  4. 4 Insulate
  5. 5 Keep the vents clear
  6. 6 Check again

More insulation over open gaps is a warmer blanket with holes in it. So step 3 comes first, and we work from the top of the house down.

  1. 01 L2 · Chimney chase

    Close the gap around the chimney.

    It is often a hand wide. We cover it with metal flashing and fire-rated sealant, never foam against the brick.

  2. 02 L2 · Bath fan

    Send the bath fan outside.

    A fan that blows into the attic frosts the roof boards. We run the duct out through a roof or wall cap.

  3. 03 L3 · Knee walls

    Block the floor behind the knee walls.

    Behind the short walls upstairs, the floor cavity is open to the cold. We block it, seal it, then insulate.

  4. 04 L4 · Wall tops

    Seal every hole in the top plates.

    Wires and pipes pass through the top of each wall. Each hole gets sealed from above before the blown-in goes down.

  5. 05 L5 · Rim joist

    Foam the rim joist.

    Cold air comes back in at the basement band joist. Closed-cell foam seals it, usually in a morning.

On a cold street, one roof loses its snow first. That house is leaking heat.

L1–L6 Every level

Each level of the house gets its own fix.

Spray foam goes on the rim joist. The attic gets sealing and blown-in. Hollow walls get dense-pack. We don't sell one material for the whole house.

Tool Season planner

When to book, month by month.

Blown-in work goes on all winter. Foam needs warm surfaces. In July the attic is brutal, so crews start at dawn. Here is what fits each part of the year.

January and February

Ice dams and frost in the attic. We can still seal and blow in: the attic is cold, the work isn't. If ice is leaking in now, we look this week and tell you what can wait for spring.

Lead time: short, except right after a deep-cold week.

Book for Jan–Feb

March and April

The thaw shows the damage: ceiling stains, wet insulation, dark roof boards. A good time for an attic look and a written plan before anything is replaced.

Lead time: moderate. Inspections fill up after the first warm week.

Book for Mar–Apr

May and June

Home sales season, when inspection reports say "thin attic insulation". Comfortable attics: the right months for big jobs like knee walls and dense-pack walls.

Lead time: usually the easiest months to get on the calendar.

Book for May–Jun

July and August

The attic is too hot by noon, so attic crews start at dawn. Basements stay cool: a good time for the rim joist and crawl spaces.

Lead time: short for basements, a little longer for attics.

Book for Jul–Aug

September to November

The rush. Everyone remembers last winter, and the first snow here often comes in October. Book the attic look early and the work lands before the cold.

Lead time: the longest of the year: weeks, not days.

December

Ice dam calls start again after the first big snow. We come back to houses we sealed in the fall and look again after the first cold snap.

Lead time: short between storms.

Questions Before the ladder

What people ask before we go up the ladder.

There is grey-brown gravel in my attic. Is it asbestos?

It may be vermiculite, which can contain asbestos. If we see it, we stop. Leave it alone, keep kids and storage out of the attic and get a sample lab-tested. We will not blow anything over it or disturb it until you know what it is.

Will someone's foot come through my ceiling?

We lay boards across the joists before anyone walks the attic, and we use drop cloths from the door to the hatch. The hose comes in through the hatch, not through your hallway carpet.

Won't more insulation block the vents and cause mold?

Only if it is done wrong. We put baffles at every eave so air can still move from the soffits to the roof vents, and we make sure the bath fan goes outside, not into the attic.

How do I know you actually put in what you said?

We staple depth rulers to the trusses before we blow, and photograph them after. You, or the next buyer's inspector, can read the depth from the hatch.

Can you work on an attic in January?

Yes. Sealing and blown-in go on all winter. Spray foam needs a warm surface, so on cold days we heat the basement before the rim joist goes on.

How long does an attic take?

Most attics are sealed and insulated in one or two days. Knee walls and dense-pack walls take longer; we tell you the number of days in the written plan.
