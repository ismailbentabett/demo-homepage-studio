## 2 Ice dams (L1 Roof deck)
- h2: Icicles are heat you paid for, leaving through the roof.
- Body: Warm attic air melts the snow on the upper roof. The water runs down to the cold eave, freezes, and builds a ridge of ice. The next melt backs up behind it and finds the ceiling. Bigger gutters don't change that. A sealed, evenly insulated attic does.
- Diagram labels: Warm attic air · Snow melts here · Refreezes at the cold eave · Water backs up under the shingles
- Check h3: Is your ice dam coming from the attic?
- Items: Icicles along the same eave every winter · Bare stripes on your roof while the neighbours' stay white · A brown stain on an upstairs ceiling near an outside wall · Slanted ceilings or short knee walls upstairs · Recessed lights or a bath fan on the top floor

## 3 The order (story)
- Kicker: How a crew works your house
- h2: Six steps, always in this order.
- Rail: 1 Look · 2 Measure · 3 Seal the leaks · 4 Insulate · 5 Keep the vents clear · 6 Check again
- Lead: More insulation on top of open gaps is a warmer blanket with holes in it. So we close the holes first, from the top of the house down.
- Steps:
  1. L2 · Chimney chase — The gap around the chimney is often a hand wide. We close it with metal flashing and fire-rated sealant, never foam against the brick.
  2. L2 · Bath fan — A fan that blows into the attic frosts the roof boards. We run the duct outside through a roof or wall cap.
  3. L3 · Knee walls — Behind the short walls upstairs, the floor cavity is open to the cold. We block it, seal it, then insulate.
  4. L4 · Wall tops — Wires and plumbing pass through the top plates of every wall. Each hole gets sealed from above.
  5. L5 · Rim joist — Cold air comes back in at the basement band joist. Closed-cell foam seals it in a morning.
- Final: Sealed top to bottom, the roof holds its snow evenly.
