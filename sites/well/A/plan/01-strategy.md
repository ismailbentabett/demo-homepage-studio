# Strategy: well-A (Static Level Water Wells)

## 1. The one job of this page
A homeowner on the South Plains with a failing well or a new rural lot lands here and calls or texts within 10 seconds because they see that Static Level knows the Ogallala under their specific town, shows the work as a drill log they can hold, and gives a sample price range before the truck rolls.

## 2. The homeowner's first 5 seconds
- **Trade + town:** "Water Well Drilling · Lubbock & South Plains" in the header and hero kicker.
- **Way to act:** Phone number visible in the header, sticky mobile bar, and hero CTA "Get a number".
- **Reason to believe:** A proof line under the h1: "22 years on the Ogallala · Sample rating 4.8 · Written drill log for every foot".
- **Visual anchor:** A dusk photo of a drill mast against wide West Texas sky, not an oil rig or a green lawn.

## 3. The contractor's first 5 seconds
The signature depth ruler running down the left edge of every section, counting feet from 0 at the hero to ~320 at the contact block, with strata bands behind each section and a single blue static-water line. No competitor in Lubbock visualizes the borehole itself; they all show pumps on tailgates. This makes the contractor say "nobody in my town shows the ground like that."

## 5. Conversion paths
- **Primary:** Call via `[[tel]]` in header, hero, mobile bar, and every service card. Form `#contact` with multi-step quote flow.
- **Secondary:** Text/SMS (biz.sms = true) via `sms:` link in mobile bar. Triage tool → prefill form with answers. Estimator → prefill form with selected scope.
- **Phone proximity:** Sticky mobile bar under 900px keeps phone one thumb-reach away at all times. Header phone visible above fold on desktop.

## 6. What this direction is NOT
- **Not B (Forty Sixty):** Not a gauge-first diagnostic tool site. That direction uses mono type and signal red; this one uses condensed industrial sans and earth tones. The tool here is the drill log, not the pressure dial.
- **Not C (Karst & Cypress):** Not a lush Florida limestone map. No teal, no serif, no canopy roads. This is dry West Texas dusk, caliche bone white, and Ogallala sand.
- **Not a softener franchise:** No "pure clean water" language, no blue gradients, no water-drop clip art. The palette is night-earth brown-black and caliche white, with water blue appearing only once the scroll reaches the water table.
