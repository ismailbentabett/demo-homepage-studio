p. 05At the door

You’ll know who’s coming. We’ll know it’s your door.

After you call, a text tells you who is on the way and when. When they arrive, they ask for your ID or a lease before opening anything.4

Why we ask for your ID

A locksmith who opens any door for anyone will open yours for someone else. So we check a licence, a lease or a bill with the address before the pick goes in. We’d want the same if it were our door.

What happens if picking fails

We stop and tell you the next step and its price. You decide. Nothing is drilled without a yes.

p. 06Rekey or replace

Most locks need new pins, not a new lock.

Rekeying swaps the small pins inside the lock you already have, so old keys stop working. If the hardware is fine, we’ll tell you a rekey will do.5

How do your locks turn?

If they turn smoothly Rekey them.

The hardware is fine. New pins, new keys, the old keys stop working. One key for every door if you want it.

If one sticks Rekey, and fix the sticky one.

Most sticking is a strike plate out of line or a dry cylinder. We fix that on the same visit before we talk about new hardware.

If one is broken Replace that one, rekey the rest.

A broken or worn-out lock gets replaced. The others are rekeyed to match the new one, so one key still opens everything.
