# Strategy — tile-C (Two-Flat Tile & Mosaic)

## 1. The one job of this page
A Chicago homeowner in a bungalow or two-flat lands here, sees their own house and their own tile problem reflected back, and either texts a photo or prices a job within 10 seconds.

## 2. The homeowner's first 5 seconds
- Trade + town: "Tile & mosaic work for Chicago bungalows and two-flats" in the kicker, h1 names vintage repair and new installs.
- A way to act: sticky mobile bar with Call and Price it; hero has both.
- One reason to believe: a warm photo of real vintage hex mosaic inside a hex mask, plus a proof line "Mon–Sat 7–4:30 · {{biz.areas|list}} · 4.9 Sample rating".
- ZIP check visible above the fold on desktop (peek of next band).

## 3. The contractor's first 5 seconds
The hex-mosaic service-area map: neighborhoods rendered as a field of small hexagons in brick/cream/green like an old bath floor. No competitor in Chicago visualizes their territory as the material they install. That is the signature.

## 4. Objections and where each is answered
| Fear | Section |
|---|---|
| Will it leak again behind the new tile? | process (layers under the tile) |
| Crooked lines / lippage on big tile? | craft (flatness, cuts, edges) |
| They'll disappear mid-job | timeline (named days) |
| Price doubles once walls open | estimator (sample ranges) |
| Dust everywhere | site care (contained cuts, daily sweep) |
| Does my old tile deserve saving? | repair-or-redo quiz |
| Do you come to my block? | area + ZIP checker + hex map |

## 5. Conversion paths
- Primary: `[[call]]` and `[[quote svc]]` → `#contact`.
- Secondary: text-a-photo CTA opens form with service prefilled; estimator hands off scope.
- Phone is always one thumb-reach away via the sticky mobile bar (hidden when form is visible).

## 6. What this direction is NOT
- Not A (Miami process cutaway): no layer-stack ruler, no wet-slate dark-led tone, no pinned shower wall.
- Not B (Oakland proof & tools): no gauge readouts, no mono-accent type, no raking-light large-format grid.
- This is warm, nostalgic, color-led, serif-headed, and anchored in Chicago housing stock and seasons.
