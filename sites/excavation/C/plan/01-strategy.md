# Strategy — excavation-C (Swale & Culvert Excavating)

## 1. The one job of this page
A Macon-area homeowner with water standing against their brick ranch or washing out a gravel drive lands here, sees that we know red clay and summer storms by name, and calls or texts within ten seconds to get a drainage fix priced and scheduled before the next thunderstorm.

## 2. The homeowner's first 5 seconds
- Trade + town in the kicker: "Drainage & excavation · Macon, GA".
- A headline that names the problem in their words: "Red clay holds the water until somebody gives it a way out."
- A horizontal filmstrip of five frames under the headline showing wet red clay, a straw-lined swale, a culvert mouth, a pine-edged yard, and a dry finished slope — proof this is their kind of yard, not a highway job.
- Two buttons above the fold: "Price my drainage" (to the quote form) and "Call now" (tel:).
- An open-now chip beside the phone reading the current hours from tokens, so they know someone answers.

## 3. The contractor's first 5 seconds
The signature system: every section is keyed to a soil color chip from a soil scientist's book ("Red clay · wet", "Red clay · dry", "Sandy loam · Byron", "Creek bottom gray"). The chip sets the section's background tone and reappears as the ZIP checker's answer. No other excavation site in Middle Georgia ties color to place like this; it reads as local knowledge, not a template.

## 5. Conversion paths
- Primary: multi-step quote form at `#contact`, prefilled by the drainage picker or gravel estimator. Phone button in sticky mobile bar and header.
- Secondary: "Text us a photo" link (`sms:`) in the machine-access section; ZIP checker hands off to the form with the area pre-selected.
- Tertiary: "Call now" tel: link repeated at the end of every major section and in the footer.
- The phone is always one thumb-reach away on mobile via the sticky bar; the form never asks more than four fields per step.

## 6. What this direction is NOT
- Not A (Batter Board, Ozark process): no grade stakes, no chert-white type, no dark-led night-blue palette, no diagonal-split hero. Direction C is color-led and serif, not industrial grotesk.
- Not B (Rod Reading, Fayetteville proof): no laser-red level line, no monospace readings, no tool-first hero, no field-book paper. Direction C tells a local story through soil color and weather, not numbers.
- Not a generic excavator site: no "Residential & Commercial" block, no orange-machine-on-blue-sky hero, no thirty-service list without explanation, no Facebook feed embed, no equipment logos as credentials. Every photo is residential scale, every service named in homeowner words, every price labelled Sample.
