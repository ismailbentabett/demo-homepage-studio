# Strategy — handyman-A

1. **The one job.** Homeowner with a repair list lands, sees a half-day visit priced and timed, and clicks "Price it" or calls within 8 seconds.
2. **Homeowner's first 5 seconds.** H1: "8:00 to 12:00. Your list, done." + kicker "Handyman visits in Grand Rapids". Stamped time card visual under headline. Two buttons: "Price my list" (primary) and "Call (616) 555-0125" (secondary). Proof line: "Mon–Sat 7:30–5 · Sample 4.9 rating · 138 reviews".
3. **Contractor's first 5 seconds.** The page is a time card: every section stamped with a punch-clock time (8:00, 8:10, 8:25…) connected by a vertical time rail. No other local site sells the visit as a clocked, predictable morning.
4. **Objections → sections.** "Nobody calls back for small jobs" → hero promise + half-day planner tool. "They'll say 8–5 and show at 4" → time-card stamps + arrival window in services. "Trip charge for one thing" → price-by-visit table with sample ranges. "Sloppy patch" → detail-clean-patch photo + review quoting invisible repair. "They'll try what they shouldn't" → fix-or-specialist triage tool + honest-limits copy. "When will they come" → open-now badge + hours in header/footer. "Too many items for one visit" → punch-list builder with hour bands.
5. **Conversion paths.** Primary: "Price my list" → multi-step quote form (#contact) with service prefilled from tool output. Secondary: sticky mobile bar with call button always visible below 900px; text link in hero proof line. Tool hand-off: half-day planner and punch-list builder both end with "Book this list" that scrolls to #contact with items prefilled.
6. **What this direction is NOT.** Not B's pegboard/tool-outline grid (no tool icons as navigation). Not C's neighborhood walk/street-sign narrative (no geographic stops). Not a generic service-grid template (no 30-icon grid, no "we do everything"). This direction is purely temporal: the visit as a clocked sequence.
