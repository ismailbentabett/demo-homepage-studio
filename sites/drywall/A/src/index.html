Four steps, in the order the mud allows.

Visit 1 of 3 · cut and backed

  1. Visit 1

    Cut it square and back it

    Damaged board is cut back to a clean square. A backer strip goes in behind, so the new piece can’t sink.

  2. Visit 1

    Tape every seam

    The new piece is screwed in and paper tape is bedded in mud on all four seams.

  3. Visits 2 and 3

    Three coats, dry between

    Each coat runs wider than the last, so the edge fades out. Mud has to dry, so a fist-size hole is three short visits, not one long one.

    [[video story-1 step__vid]]

    A knife laying a coat of mud across a wall.

  4. Last visit

    Sand under a side light, then prime

    Plastic goes up at the doorway first. Every coat is checked with a work light held flat to the wall, then primed for your painter.

Name your texture. We spray a test board before the patch.

Smooth

A thin skim coat over the whole patch area, sanded flat. The hardest to hide, because a raking light shows every ridge.

Blend: hard

Orange peel

Thinned mud sprayed in fine drops, like the skin of an orange. The drop size changes with the pressure, so we dial it in on a scrap board first.

Blend: medium

Knockdown

Bigger splatter, flattened with a wide knife a few minutes after it lands. Timing the knife pass is most of the match.

Blend: medium

Skip trowel

Mud laid on by hand with a curved trowel that skips across the wall. Done stroke by stroke, so the patch is blended out past its edges.

Blend: easier

Fix now or wait?

Winter cracks can often wait for spring.

  • Nail pops and seam cracks from this winter: dry heat shrinks the framing. Wait until spring unless they keep growing.
  • Plaster cracks: pin it, don’t just caulk it. Loose plaster gets screwed back to the lath first.
  • Stains: fix the leak, then fix the ceiling. A stain-blocking primer keeps it from bleeding back.
