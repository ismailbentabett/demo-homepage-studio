Three walls, three repairs

Every house here was built with the wall of its decade.

Before anyone says "gut it", we find out which wall you have. The repair follows from that.

  1. I

    [[frame era-1 win | | sizes="260px"]]

    Horsehair plaster on wood lath

    Wet plaster was pushed through the gaps between thin wood strips and curled behind them. Those curls, the keys, hold the wall up. A long crack and a hollow knock mean some keys have let go.

    Keep. Pin the loose plaster back to the lath with washers, tape the crack, skim it flat.

  2. II

    Gypsum plaster on rock lath

    Small gypsum sheets took the place of the wood strips, with two thin coats over them. The cracks run straight, along the edges of the sheets.

    Pin. Reset the sheet edges, tape the joints, skim. The wall stays where it is.

  3. III

    [[frame era-3 win | | sizes="260px"]]

    Board, paper tape and compound

    Screwed to the studs, taped at the seams, finished in three coats. Nail pops and seam cracks show up after the first dry winter.

    Replace only what failed. Reset the screws, re-tape the seam, refinish and prime.

The heating season

Cracks show up in January. Not all of them need fixing in January.

Dry heat shrinks the framing and opens cracks and nail pops. Ice dams leave stains when the thaw comes. Some repairs go in now; some wait for the spring list.

What showed up on your wall?

Nail pop: spring list

The framing is still drying out. Reset it in March and it stays reset. If the bumps keep multiplying before then, call and we will look sooner.

Put me on the spring list

Seam crack: spring list, unless it grows

A hairline along a taped seam usually settles by spring. If it widens week to week, the tape has let go and it is worth fixing now.

Put me on the spring list

Plaster crack: fix now

A long, branching crack in old plaster means keys are letting go, and winter loosens more. Pin it, do not just caulk it.

Ceiling stain: after the roof is dry

Get the ice dam or leak fixed first. Then we open the wet section, dry it, rebuild it and seal it so the brown never bleeds back.

Wall-era identifier

Plaster or drywall? Four questions tell you.

No tools needed, just a knuckle and a pushpin in a closet. Your answer picks the plate, and the plate picks the repair.

1 When was the house built?
2 Knock between the studs.
3 Push in a pushpin.
4 Long cracks that branch?

Answer above and your wall’s plate is picked here. The key:

Plate I Horsehair on wood lath

Before 1920, hollow knock, hard to tack, long branching cracks. Pin and skim.

Your hollow knock: some keys have let go. Plaster washers pull them back.

Plate II Gypsum on rock lath

About 1920 to 1969, solid knock, hard to tack, straight cracks at sheet edges. Reset, tape, skim.

Plate III Drywall board

1970 on, the tack goes in easily, cracks follow seams, nail pops in winter. Patch, re-tape, refinish.
