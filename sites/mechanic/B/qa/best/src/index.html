Before you call

What’s my car doing?

Three quick checks from the driveway. None replaces a test on the lift. All of them tell you how worried to be.

Which light is on?

Tap the light you see on the dash.

MIL · STEADY

Drive gently, book soon

Something the engine computer watches is out of range. Often minor, sometimes a loose gas cap.

We check first: the code, the freeze-frame data, then the sensor or circuit it names.

MIL · FLASHING

Stop and call

A flashing light usually means a misfire bad enough to overheat the converter. Pull over when it’s safe and call before driving far.

We check first: misfire counts per cylinder, then plugs, coils and injectors.

OIL PRESS · LOW

Stop and call

Low oil pressure can hurt an engine in minutes. Shut it off, check the dipstick, and call us.

We check first: oil level, the pressure sender, then real pressure with a gauge.

COOLANT TEMP · HIGH

Stop and call

The engine is running hot. Pull over, let it cool, and never open a hot radiator cap.

We check first: a pressure test for leaks, then the thermostat, fan and water pump.

CHARGE · LOW

Drive gently, book soon

The charging system isn’t keeping up. You may have one drive left, so skip the heated seats and come straight in.

We check first: a battery load test, alternator output, then a draw test overnight.

BRAKE · ON

Check one thing first

Make sure the parking brake is fully off. If the light stays on, the fluid may be low. A soft pedal means don’t drive it.

We check first: fluid level, pad thickness in millimeters, then the lines for leaks. See what each millimeter means [[i arrow]]

ABS · OFF

Drive gently, book soon

Your normal brakes still work. The anti-lock part is off, so leave more room on gravel and ice.

We check first: the ABS codes, then the wheel speed sensor they point to.

TPMS · LOW

Drive it in

One tire is low, or its sensor is. Check pressures at the next station. If the light stays on, we find which.

We check first: all four pressures, then each sensor’s reading.

Got a code from a parts-store scan?

P0300
Random misfire. More than one cylinder is missing. Cheapest first: plugs, then coils, then a vacuum leak or fuel.
P0420
Catalyst below threshold. Before a converter, we test the rear oxygen sensor and look for an exhaust leak.
P0171
Running lean. Too much air or too little fuel. Often a cracked intake hose or a dirty airflow sensor.
P0128
Warms up too slowly. Usually a thermostat stuck open. The heater blows lukewarm in January.
P0455
Large evaporative leak. Start with the gas cap. Then a smoke test finds the hose.

Your brakes, in millimeters.

You get the number, not “they’re getting low”.

10mm

  1. PAD 10 mm

    New.

    Nothing to do. We write the number down so the next reading means something.

  2. PAD 7 mm

    Half worn.

    Check again at your next oil change. No reason to spend on it today.

  3. PAD 3 mm

    A month or two left.

    Book it before it starts to grind. The rotors are usually still fine at this point.

  4. PAD 0 mm · METAL

    Grinding.

    Metal on the rotor. Drive straight to us, gently, and expect rotors too.
