# Strategy — autotuneup-B (Next Interval Tune-Up)

1. **The one job.** A Reno driver lands, types their mileage into the hero tool, sees what is due now versus later, and books a time or texts within 10 seconds.
2. **Homeowner’s first 5 seconds.** H1 “Car maintenance by mileage in Reno” + odometer input field + “See what’s due” button + phone number in header + proof line “Sample 4.8 ★ · 296 reviews · Most oil changes in about 30 min”.
3. **Contractor’s first 5 seconds.** The page *is* the service-interval odometer: every section is a mileage row that rolls to its interval and says “due” or “not yet”, proving honesty before the call.
4. **Objections mapped.**
   - “They’ll sell me an air filter every time” → Odometer rows show “not due” in grey with the next-due mileage.
   - “A quick oil change that takes two hours” → Service menu lists posted times; wait-or-drop planner in tools.
   - “Do I really need a flush?” → Brake fluid and coolant sections show the test-first policy and sample reading.
   - “Will they use the right oil?” → Oil type explainer tool + “your car’s spec wins” stated.
   - “They’ll find something wrong because I’m there” → Guarantee line + sample review quoting “didn’t charge me for the test”.
   - “I don’t know what 60k service means” → Hero tool output translates mileage into plain items.
5. **Conversion paths.** Primary: “Book a time” form at #contact, prefilled from mileage tool. Secondary: tel: and sms: in header + sticky mobile bar. Tool → form hand-off: mileage tool passes due items as checkboxes.
6. **What this direction is NOT.**
   - Not A’s stopwatch-station relay (no lap times, no color lanes).
   - Not C’s altitude-marker story (no elevation rail, no adobe/turquoise palette).
   - Not a coupon/starburst chain site (no discounts, no logos, no scare copy).
