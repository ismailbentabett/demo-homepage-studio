## 2 Plates
- Kicker: The field guide
- h2: Five things that end up in a {{biz.city}} gutter.
- Lead: Each one clogs a different way and falls in a different month. The bar under each plate is the year: filled months are when it lands on your roof.
- Index label: In this guide
- No. 1 Douglas fir · Pseudotsuga menziesii · Drops: all year
  - Does: Needles fall every month and mat into a wet felt that slows the water before it stops it.
  - Fix: Fine micro-mesh guards, plus one cleaning a year. Mesh helps; it doesn't end the ladder.
  - Link: Price fir-needle guards
- No. 2 Bigleaf maple · Acer macrophyllum · Drops: October to December
  - Does: Leaves the size of a dinner plate fill a run in three weeks and plug the drop outlet first.
  - Fix: A cleaning booked the week after the big drop, not before it. Downspouts flushed.
  - Link: Price a cleaning
- No. 3 Moss · Bryophyta · Grows: November to March
  - Does: Grows on shaded north roofs all winter and washes down in clumps with every rain.
  - Fix: Moss brushed off the lower shingles, the run cleaned on the same visit.
  - Link: Price moss removal
- No. 4 Cottonwood · Populus trichocarpa · Drops: June
  - Does: Seed fluff in June sticks to mesh and wet debris, so guards can clog under it.
  - Fix: A summer cleaning instead of a guard. We'll say so before you buy one.
  - Link: Price a cleaning plan
- No. 5 Ice · rare specimen · Some winters, December to February
  - Does: A heavy coat of ice and falling limbs pull runs off their hangers.
  - Fix: Sections re-hung on new hangers within days, fascia checked behind them.
  - Link: Price storm repair
- Legend: filled = falls · rust = book it · dashed = some years
