LayerWhat we checkWhy it matters in winter

  1. 01 Tear-off

    We check: Old siding comes off one side at a time, nails swept twice a day, painted trim on older houses handled as lead paint.

    In winter: Nothing goes back up over a wall we have not seen.

  2. 02 Sheathing

    We check: Every sheet pressed and photographed. Soft ones are marked, priced per sheet and shown to you before we replace them.

    In winter: Wet sheathing freezes, swells and pushes new siding out of line by spring.

  3. 03 Wrap

    We check: House wrap lapped top over bottom, seams taped, cut neat at every opening.

    In winter: Wind drives snow behind siding. Wrap sends that water down and out, not into the wall.

  4. 04 Flashing

    We check: Flashing at every window head, under every sill and where a wall meets a roof.

    In winter: Melt water runs along the eave and finds every gap. Flashing gives it a way out.

  5. 05 Starter

    We check: A level starter strip at the base, held off the ground and the deck.

    In winter: If the first course is out of level, every course above it shows it.

  6. 06 Courses

    We check: Each course hung, not nailed tight, so it can move. Butt joints staggered, never two on one stud.

    In winter: Vinyl shrinks in the cold. Panels nailed tight buckle when it warms up.

  7. 07 Trim

    We check: Corners, window and door trim, drip caps, vented soffit and wrapped fascia.

    In winter: Moving attic air keeps the roof cold, and a cold roof grows smaller ice dams.

How it will look

Pick the line your house will read in.

The exposure is how much of each board shows. It changes the face of the house more than the color does.

4 in. lap

Tight, busy lines, close to the clapboard on many older houses. More courses, more nail lines.

5 in. lap

The middle. Reads clean on a two-story without looking thin.

7 in. lap

Fewer, wider courses. Long low lines that suit a rambler.

Dutch lap

A cove at the top of each board throws a deeper shadow line. It reads strongest in low winter sun.

Board and batten

Vertical boards with narrow battens. Often used on a gable end over lap siding below.
