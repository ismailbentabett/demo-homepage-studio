## C-03 Layers
- Kicker: C-03 · The order we work in
- h2: Seven layers. You see every one.
- Lead: A wall in {{biz.stateName}} takes hail in June and ice at the eaves in January. Each layer is there for one of those days.
- Counter: Layer 01 of 07
- 01 Tear-off · Check: Old siding off one side at a time, nails swept twice a day, painted trim on older houses handled as lead paint. · Winter: Nothing goes back up over a wall we have not seen.
- 02 Sheathing · Check: Every sheet pressed and photographed. Soft ones are marked, priced per sheet and shown to you before we replace them. · Winter: Wet sheathing freezes, swells and pushes the new siding out of line by spring.
- 03 Wrap · Check: House wrap lapped top over bottom, seams taped, cut neat at every opening. · Winter: Wind-driven snow gets behind siding. Wrap sends that water down and out, not into the wall.
- 04 Flashing · Check: Flashing at every window head, under every sill and where the wall meets the roof. · Winter: Melt water runs along the eave and finds every gap. Flashing gives it a way out.
- 05 Starter · Check: A level starter strip at the base, set off the ground and the deck. · Winter: If the first course is out of level, every course above it shows it.
- 06 Courses · Check: Each course hung, not nailed tight, so it can move; butt joints staggered and kept off the same stud. · Winter: Vinyl shrinks in cold. Panels nailed tight buckle when it warms up.
- 07 Trim · Check: Corners, window and door trim, drip caps, vented soffit and wrapped fascia. · Winter: Moving attic air keeps the roof cold, which keeps ice dams smaller.

## C-05 Reveal
- Kicker: C-05 · How it will look
- h2: Pick the line your house will read in.
- Lead: The exposure is how much of each board shows. It changes the whole face of the house more than the color does.
- Tabs: 4 in. lap · 5 in. lap · 7 in. lap · Dutch lap · Board and batten
- 4 in.: Tight, busy lines. Close to the clapboard on many older houses. More courses, more nail lines.
- 5 in.: The middle. Reads clean on a two-story without looking thin.
- 7 in.: Fewer, wider courses. Long low lines that suit a rambler.
- Dutch lap: A cove at the top of each board throws a deeper shadow line. It reads strongest in low winter sun.
- Board and batten: Vertical boards with narrow battens. Often used on a gable end over lap siding below.
