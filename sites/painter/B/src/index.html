Prep is the job

Four steps before the first coat goes on. Each one is listed on your ticket.

[[frame process-wash frame--3x2 | class="story-step__img"]]

01 — Wash

Pollen, mildew and chalked paint washed off with low-pressure rinse. Surface must be clean before anything sticks.

Tool
Low-pressure washer
Dry time
24 h minimum
[[frame process-scrape frame--3x2 | class="story-step__img"]]

02 — Scrape

Loose and peeling paint scraped back to sound wood or masonry. Feathered edges so the new coat lays flat.

Tool
Carbide scraper + 80-grit
Note
Bare spots spot-primed before finish coat
[[frame process-prime frame--3x2 | class="story-step__img"]]

03 — Prime

Bare wood, patched drywall and stain spots sealed with bonding or stain-blocking primer. Two coats where needed.

Product
Bonding primer
Dry time
1–2 h
[[frame process-cut-in frame--3x2 | class="story-step__img"]]

04 — Cut-in

Edges brushed by hand along trim, ceilings and corners before the roller goes on. Straight lines, no tape bleed.

Tool
2½" angled sash brush
Coats
Two finish coats

Pick the right sheen for the room

Semi-gloss or satin

Wipeable against steam and splashes; semi-gloss shows less water spotting on trim.

Satin or eggshell

Handles grease wipes without shining up every wall imperfection.

Eggshell or matte

Hides drywall texture and patch marks; low glare in evening light.

Eggshell

Durable enough for scuffs, forgiving on older plaster walls.

Semi-gloss

Hard finish that wipes clean; contrasts with flatter walls.

Flat white

No sheen to catch overhead light; hides joint compound seams.
