    Step 1: Count the parts on a blanket.

    We open every box and count the parts on a blanket before a single screw goes in. A missing bag shows up now, not halfway up a wardrobe.

    Step 3: Set the cams by hand.

    Cam locks get turned until they bite, not until they spin. The last turn into particleboard is by hand, so the holes hold.

    Step 4: Hang and adjust the doors.

    Every hinge has three screws: in and out, up and down, side to side. We turn them until the gaps match from top to bottom.

Questions people ask before the day.

My building asks for an insurance certificate.

Send us the building manager’s email when you book. We’re insured [[sample detail]], and the certificate goes to them before the day, not on it.

Do I have to book the elevator?

Usually, yes, and your building sets the hours. Tell us the window it gives you and we plan the visit around it.

What do you bring?

Drill-driver, level, stud finder, hex keys, wall anchors, anti-tip straps and a blanket to lay the parts on. You don’t need to own a screwdriver.

What if a part is missing?

Counting comes first, so we find out before the build, not halfway. We tell you exactly which part to order and build everything else.

I started it myself and stripped a hole.

That’s common with particleboard. Most stripped holes can be fixed with a dowel and glue instead of a new piece of furniture.

Do I need to be home?

For the first ten minutes, so we can agree where each piece goes and which walls take a strap. After that, it’s up to you.
